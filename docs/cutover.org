* Cutover

The =cutover= module requires =ledger=. It registers =accounting_cutover= (a
batch) and =accounting_cutover_row= (one row per source row or created
record). =VentureCutoverService= owns preview, import, reconcile, activate,
rollback preflight and rollback; generic writes to either record are refused,
so no form, REST call or approved AI write can stamp a batch reconciled or
active. The Cutover nav item opens the batch list; every action is on the
batch page, at =POST /api/v1/accounting_cutovers/:id/:action=, and through
the CLI:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act accounting_cutover 1 import
venturectl act accounting_cutover 1 reconcile
venturectl act accounting_cutover 1 rollback_preflight
venturectl act accounting_cutover 1 activate
#+end_src

A batch moves =preview= → =imported= → =reconciled= → =active=, or to
=rolled_back= from any state before =active=. Import and rollback are each
one database transaction: a failure anywhere leaves no invoice, bill,
payment, credit, asset, account, company or journal behind.

** The opening posting rule

The cutoff is an instant; a date means midnight UTC at its start. Opening
balances are the source system's position at the end of the day before the
cutoff, and they enter the VENTURE ledger at the cutoff instant.

Every opening posting has the opening balance clearing account on its other
side: code =3900=, "Opening balance clearing", an equity account created
inside the import transaction when the chart lacks it (=clearing_account_code=
chooses another; it must be an active equity account). Nothing a migration
imports touches income, expense, sales tax payable or recoverable tax:

| Source row             | Document in VENTURE                        | Journal at the cutoff                  |
|------------------------+--------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------|
| open receivable        | issued invoice, source issue and due dates | Dr receivables / Cr clearing (total)   |
| paid part of it        | opening receipt allocated to the invoice   | Dr cash / Cr receivables, then Dr clearing / Cr cash |
| open payable           | approved bill, source bill and due dates   | Dr clearing / Cr payables (total)      |
| paid part of it        | opening payment allocated to the bill      | Dr payables / Cr cash, then Dr cash / Cr clearing |
| customer credit note   | unapplied credit note, source date         | Dr clearing / Cr receivables           |
| vendor credit note     | unapplied vendor credit, source date       | Dr payables / Cr clearing              |
| customer prepayment    | unapplied receipt (deposit) at the cutoff  | net Dr clearing / Cr receivables       |
| vendor prepayment      | unapplied payment (deposit) at the cutoff  | net Dr payables / Cr clearing          |
| bank balance           | bank account                               | Dr bank / Cr clearing (flipped when negative) |
| fixed asset            | in-service asset, schedule from the cutoff | Dr cost / Cr accumulated / Cr clearing |
| stock on hand          | inventory receipt and FIFO cost layer      | Dr inventory / Cr goods received not invoiced, then Dr GRNI / Cr clearing |
| trial balance          | none                                       | one journal per currency, see below    |

Why: the source system already recognised that revenue, expense and tax. An
open invoice from March issued as ordinary revenue in a July cutover would put
40,000 of last half-year's sales into this year's P&L and 3,200 of tax, which
may already have been filed, into this quarter's return. And once fiscal
periods exist, a March-dated posting is refused by the period guard; the
opening documents post at the cutoff, so a closed March does not stop them.

Migrated invoices, bills and credit notes are stamped with =opening_at=, the
cutoff their journal posted at. The stamp is service-owned: a generic write
cannot set, change or clear it. It is what the rest of the system reads:

- The =tax_liability= report and the US sales-tax filing adapter skip issue
  and void events of stamped documents, including a rollback's voids.
- Allocating cash to a stamped invoice, or refunding that allocation, creates
  no cash-report sale, so collecting a migrated invoice is not revenue twice.
  (Cash-basis statements already recognise the issue journal's non-receivable
  side, which is clearing, not income.)
- The period guard judges a stamped invoice or bill, on any later edit, at its
  =opening_at= rather than its source issue date.
- An ordinary void of a stamped invoice or bill is refused: it would reverse
  the opening journal into clearing long after the cutover balanced it. Write
  it off, credit it, or roll back its batch.

The services own the posting. =venture_settlement_service_issue_opening()=,
=venture_settlement_service_credit_opening()=,
=venture_payables_service_approve_opening()= and
=venture_payables_service_credit_opening()= write the draft, its lines, the
issue or approval event (with the source dates) and the clearing journal in
one transaction; =..._void_opening()= reverses it during rollback. The
subledger events keep the source dates, so aging, customer and vendor
statements show the document as issued in the source system. The general
ledger carries it from the cutoff.

** The trial balance

=trial_balance= rows post one journal per currency at the cutoff for every
account no subledger owns -- loans, credit cards, prepaids, tax payable,
retained earnings, equity -- with clearing as the balancing leg. Rows on a
control account are *not* posted; they are asserted at reconcile. The control
accounts are:

- receivables: the =receivables= control mapping, else code =1100=;
- payables: the =payables= control mapping, else code =2000=;
- every GL account a =bank_balances= row names;
- every asset cost and accumulated depreciation account an =assets= row names;
- inventory (the =inventory= control mapping, else code =1200=) when the
  payload has =inventory= rows.

Once the subledgers and the trial balance are in, clearing nets to zero in
every currency. That is the proof the subledgers agree with the source
ledger: a receivable missing from =open_ar= leaves clearing short by exactly
its amount. Without a trial balance, clearing holds the subledgers' opposite
side and reconcile reports it as a note; import the trial balance to move it.

* The payload

A batch is one JSON object. Every member except =source= and =cutoff= is
optional. Arrays of rows are the sections; a row names its =source_id=, the
stable identifier from the source system.

| Member                | Meaning |
|-----------------------+---------|
| =source=              | =zoho_books=, =quickbooks=, =xero= or =generic= |
| =cutoff=              | ISO date or timestamp; must not be in the future |
| =currency=            | default ISO code for amounts and JSON numbers without one |
| =decimal_separator=   | ="."= (default) or =","= |
| =thousands_separator= | none (default), =","=, ="."=, ="'"= or =" "=; must differ from the decimal separator |
| =clearing_account_code= | default =3900= |
| =account_map=         | object of source code → VENTURE code, applied to every account code in the payload |
| =chart=               | accounts to create when missing |
| =customers=, =vendors= | party details, by =source_id= |
| =open_ar=, =open_ap=, =credits=, =bank_balances=, =assets=, =inventory=, =trial_balance= | the opening balances |
| =items=               | accepted and listed as not imported; products are not opening balances |
| =unsupported=         | strings copied into the preview report |

** Amounts

An amount is a string =[-]1234.56 CUR=: an optional minus, digits, an
optional fraction no longer than the currency's minor unit, and a space and
three uppercase letters. The currency may be omitted when the row's
=currency= or the payload's =currency= supplies it. With
=thousands_separator= set, grouping is allowed and checked (=1,234,567.89=);
with =decimal_separator= =","=, =1.234,56 EUR= reads as 1234.56.

Anything else is refused with its row's location, never guessed:
=1,50 EUR= without separators, =€1.234=, =100.00 CR=, =1.005 USD=,
=1.5 JPY=, =12.34 usd=. The general money parser used for marketplace
imports is deliberately forgiving and is not changed; this grammar applies
to cutover payloads only.

Also accepted: the serialised money object (={"amount":10500,"currency":"USD","exponent":2}=)
and a JSON number when the row or payload names a currency. A JSON number
is binary floating point, so it is accepted only when the currency's minor
units print it exactly: =105.0= is 105.00, =0.1= is 0.10, =1.005= in a
two-decimal currency is refused.

Text fields accept a JSON integer (="number": 1001=); whole-number fields
accept a string of digits (="useful_life_months": "36"=); booleans accept
=true=/=false= or the strings =true=, =yes=, =1=, =false=, =no=, =0=.
Dates are ISO dates or timestamps only -- =now= and =today= are refused,
because in a migration file they would date history at the moment of import.

** chart and account_map

=chart= rows: =code=, =name=, =kind= (=asset=, =liability=, =equity=,
=income=, =expense=) and optional =active=. The code passes through
=account_map=. A missing account is created inside the import transaction;
an existing one is kept (a different kind is a preview warning). A code
appearing twice, or the clearing code, is refused.

Every account code elsewhere -- bank rows, bill lines, assets, trial balance
rows -- is mapped, then must exist in the chart or in =chart=. A code mapped
to the clearing account is refused.

** Parties

=customers= and =vendors= rows: =source_id= (required, unique), =name=,
=email=, =phone=, =website=, =address=, =external_id=, =terms= and
=tax_exempt=. A document row names its party by =customer_source_id= /
=vendor_source_id=, or by =customer_name= / =vendor_name= alone; one of them
is required.

A party is resolved, in order, to:

1. the company an earlier live cutover row created or matched for that source id;
2. the company whose external ID is the party's =external_id= (default: its source id);
3. the company with the same name, ignoring case and runs of whitespace;
4. for rows with a name and no source id, the one company this batch created for that name;
5. a new company, stamped with that external ID and carrying email, phone,
   website, address, tax exemption and =Payment terms (source): …= in its notes
   (companies have no terms field).

The cutover never invents a party. A source id that reaches step 5 must
name a =customers[]= / =vendors[]= entry with a =name=, because that entry is
where the company's name and details come from; a row whose id is missing,
empty, or names no such entry is refused with a rule code naming the row and
the field -- =open-ar-customer=, =open-ap-vendor= or =credit-party= -- and,
like every other row problem, the preview lists it on the row and import
refuses before its first write. An id the payload does not list is still fine
when it matches a company at step 1 or 2. (Issue #84: earlier versions
created an "Imported customer" or "Imported vendor" for such rows.)

More than one match at step 2 or 3 is a located error: give the companies
distinct external IDs. A company used as a vendor is marked a supplier,
which payables requires; preview warns when that changes an existing company.
A =customers= or =vendors= row no document references creates nothing: a
cutover imports balances, not a contact list.

** open_ar

| Field | |
|-------+-|
| =source_id= | required |
| =customer_source_id= / =customer_name= | one required; an id must name a =customers[]= entry unless it already matches a company (rule =open-ar-customer=) |
| =number= | required; invoice numbers are unique per organization |
| =date= | required, before the cutoff; an undated invoice would be issued today |
| =due_date= | optional; carried onto the invoice and its issue event for aging and dunning |
| =terms=, =memo= | stored on the invoice's terms and notes |
| =amount= | the gross document total |
| =net=, =tax= | income before tax and its frozen tax |
| =paid= | received before the cutoff |
| =balance= | optional; must equal the total less =paid= |
| =currency=, =tax_exempt=, =tax_exempt_reason= | |

One total rule serves import and reconcile: with =net=, the total is
=net + tax= and an =amount= given as well must equal it; with only
=amount=, the total is =amount= and =net= is =amount - tax=. A zero total is
refused. =paid= must be at least zero and below the total (rule
=open-ar-balance=); the open balance is total less paid.

A negative total is money owed back to the customer and imports as an
unapplied opening customer credit note of that size; it may not carry =paid=
or =tax=. No terms-to-due-date calculation is made: VENTURE has no payment
terms helper, so give =due_date=.

** open_ap

| Field | |
|-------+-|
| =source_id=, =number=, =date= | required; =date= before the cutoff; bill numbers are unique per organization, not per vendor |
| =vendor_source_id= / =vendor_name= | one required; an id must name a =vendors[]= entry unless it already matches a company (rule =open-ap-vendor=) |
| =due_date=, =memo=, =currency= | optional |
| =lines= | at least one (rule =open-ap-lines=): =description=, =amount= (net, positive), =tax= (frozen, nonnegative), =account_code= or =category= |
| =paid= | paid before the cutoff; nonnegative and below the lines' total (rule =open-ap-balance=) |
| =balance= | optional; must equal lines less paid |

A negative line is refused with a pointer: record a supplier credit under
=credits= with kind =vendor=. Two vendors' bill =1001= cannot both be stored
as =1001=; preview names both rows and suggests a distinct number.

** credits

| Field | |
|-------+-|
| =source_id=, =date= (before the cutoff), =amount= (positive) | required |
| =kind= | =customer= or =vendor=; omitted, a row with =vendor_source_id= is a vendor credit (rule =credit-identity=) |
| =type= | =credit_note= (default) or =prepayment= |
| party | =customer_source_id= / =customer_name= or =vendor_source_id= / =vendor_name=; an id must name a =customers[]= / =vendors[]= entry unless it already matches a company (rule =credit-party=) |
| =number= | stored as the credit's reference (default: the source id) |

A credit note stays unapplied for later settlements. A prepayment -- a
retainer received, or a deposit paid, with no document yet -- becomes an
unapplied receipt or payment dated at the cutoff (its reference keeps the
source date) with its cash offset against clearing, so it can later settle
an invoice or bill or be refunded like any deposit. A credit may not carry
=tax=: its tax was reported by the source system.

A credit larger than the open documents its party has in the payload is a
preview warning (rule =credit-within-open-balance=), not a refusal: a
customer's retainer with no invoice is an ordinary unapplied balance.

** bank_balances

=source_id= and =name= are required; =account_code= defaults to =1000=;
=amount= is signed. A negative balance -- a credit card owing, an overdraft
-- credits the account. More than one row without =account_code= is
refused as ambiguous, as is a repeated name. Several rows may name the same
GL account explicitly; they are reconciled as one per-account total. A bank
account record with the same name and GL account is reused, so a corrected
batch does not grow a second "Checking".

** assets

| Field | |
|-------+-|
| =source_id=, =cost=, =in_service_at= (before the cutoff), =useful_life_months= (1-1200) | required |
| =tag=, =name= | default to the source id; tags are unique per organization |
| =category=, =salvage= (0 to cost), =acquired_at= (on or before service) | optional |
| =method= | =straight_line= (default), =declining_balance= or =none= |
| =accumulated_depreciation= | 0 to cost less salvage (rule =opening-accumulated-within-basis=) |
| =asset_account_code=, =accumulated_depreciation_account_code=, =depreciation_expense_account_code= | required, must exist |

Placed through =venture_asset_service_import_opening()= against clearing.
History is not recomputed: one opening journal at the cutoff debits cost and
credits accumulated depreciation and clearing for net book value, and one
posted =depreciation_entry= for the month holding the last instant before the
cutoff carries the accumulated amount. That month and every earlier one are
history; the remaining depreciable amount (cost less salvage less
accumulated) is spread over the months of life left, from the month after, so
the first post-cutover run continues from the source register. An asset past
its life keeps its residual value until disposed. No tax schedule is
created.

** inventory

=source_id=, =sku=, =quantity= (a positive whole number) and =unit_cost=
(positive money) are required; =location= chooses between inventory items
sharing a SKU, and =currency= applies as elsewhere. The item must already
exist -- items belong to products, and products are not opening balances -- so
an unknown or ambiguous SKU is a located error.

Stock is received through =venture_inventory_service_receive()= at the cutoff,
creating a FIFO cost layer post-cutover sales consume like any purchase. The
receipt debits inventory and credits goods received not invoiced; a batch
journal moves that credit to clearing, because nothing was received from a
supplier. Reconcile compares quantity times unit cost with the receipt, and
the trial balance's inventory row with the ledger.

** trial_balance

=account_code= (required, mapped), optional =name=, =currency=, and =debit=
and/or =credit= (nonnegative, one currency). An account may appear once per
currency. Debits must equal credits in every currency; the difference is
reported. See [[The trial balance]].

** Modules

A non-empty section whose owner is switched off refuses the whole payload at
preview, rather than importing it partially: =open_ar= needs invoicing and
receivables, =open_ap= and vendor credits need payables, customer credits
need receivables, =assets= need the assets module and =inventory= needs
goods. List what cannot be imported under =unsupported=.

* CSV

Most source systems export spreadsheets. Each section has a CSV template,
and a set of CSVs becomes exactly the payload JSON above, previewed like any
other payload: every rule, located problem and the strict money grammar
still apply. The CSV path is a second way to write a payload, not a second
importer.

| Section         | Columns |
|-----------------+---------|
| =customers=, =vendors= | source_id, name, email, phone, website, address, external_id, terms, tax_exempt |
| =chart=         | code, name, kind, active |
| =account_map=   | source_code, venture_code |
| =open_ar=       | source_id, customer_source_id, customer_name, number, date, due_date, terms, currency, amount, net, tax, paid, balance, tax_exempt, tax_exempt_reason, memo |
| =open_ap=       | source_id, vendor_source_id, vendor_name, number, date, due_date, currency, paid, balance, memo, line_description, line_amount, line_tax, line_account_code, line_category |
| =credits=       | source_id, kind, type, customer_source_id, customer_name, vendor_source_id, vendor_name, number, date, currency, amount |
| =bank_balances= | source_id, name, account_code, currency, amount |
| =assets=        | source_id, tag, name, category, currency, cost, salvage, accumulated_depreciation, in_service_at, acquired_at, method, useful_life_months, asset_account_code, accumulated_depreciation_account_code, depreciation_expense_account_code |
| =trial_balance= | account_code, name, currency, debit, credit |
| =inventory=     | source_id, sku, location, quantity, unit_cost, currency |

Headers match as the generic CSV importer's do: case, spaces and dashes do
not matter (=Due date= is =due_date=). Files are RFC 4180, parsed by the same
parser as =/e/:type/import=. Every non-empty cell becomes a string member, so
the payload rules judge it. =open_ap= has one CSV row per bill line: rows
sharing a =source_id= are one bill, the bill columns taken from its first row;
a later row's bill columns must be empty or agree. An unknown column (a
misspelling would drop a figure silently), an unknown section, a row wider
than its header, an incomplete =account_map= row and more than 5,000 data
rows in a file are refused together, before preview.

| Route | |
|-------+-|
| =GET /cutover/template/:section=, =GET /api/v1/accounting_cutovers/template/:section= | the header row as =cutover-SECTION.csv= |
| =POST /cutover/csv= | multipart form from the batch page: =source=, =cutoff=, =currency= (and the separators and clearing code) plus one file per section; redirects to the new batch |
| =POST /api/v1/accounting_cutovers/csv= | JSON: the same settings plus =files= mapping section to CSV text; returns the previewed batch, or with ="build_only": true= the payload without creating a batch |

All require an editor and the cutover module, and preview into the selected
legal entity.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl cutover template open_ap > open_ap.csv
venturectl cutover csv source=quickbooks cutoff=2026-01-01 currency=USD \
  vendors=@vendors.csv open_ap=@open_ap.csv trial_balance=@tb.csv
venturectl cutover csv source=generic cutoff=2026-01-01 open_ar=@ar.csv build_only=true
#+end_src

The CLI reads the files and sends their text; the server parses and validates
them exactly as it would an upload.

* Preview: the write-free validation pass

Preview parses the whole payload once and collects every problem instead of
stopping at the first. A payload that cannot be read at all -- unknown
source, missing or future cutoff, bad separators, a section that is not an
array, a module switched off for a non-empty section, more than 5,000 rows
-- is refused and no batch is created. Everything row-shaped is recorded:

- one =accounting_cutover_row= per source row of =open_ar=, =open_ap=,
  =credits=, =bank_balances=, =assets=, =inventory= and =trial_balance=, status =preview=
  or =exception=, with its errors and warnings in =exception=;
- the batch report lists every problem located as
  =open_ap[12] bill-9: amount …=, followed by the unsupported fields.

Preview checks, besides every field rule above: duplicate =source_id=
within a section (a copy-pasted id silently imported one row and reconciled
against two); duplicate invoice or bill numbers and asset tags within the
payload and against existing documents (a rolled-back batch's numbers are
free, see [[Rollback]]); ambiguous party matches and parties the payload
cannot name (=open-ar-customer=, =open-ap-vendor=, =credit-party=); missing account codes;
rows another live batch already imported (warning: this batch will skip
them); the clearing account being usable; the credit cap (warning); and a
predicted reconcile of the trial balance: what each control account will
hold once the subledgers are imported, against the trial balance's figure
(warning, with both amounts).

Import re-runs the same parse and refuses before its first write while any
error remains, listing up to twenty located errors and a count.

The batch is limited to 5,000 rows across =chart=, =customers=, =vendors=
and the seven opening sections, the generic CSV importer's limit; the refusal
suggests splitting the migration into batches.

** No transactional dry run

A dry run of import and reconcile inside a transaction that is always rolled
back was considered and not shipped. Saving a record emits =audit= and
=entity-saved= synchronously inside the transaction: outbound webhooks start
their HTTP POST from the =audit= handler and automation pods fire on
=entity-saved=, and neither is deferred to commit. A dry run would announce
invoices and bills to external systems that the rollback then erased.
Preview's predicted reconcile covers the same ground without writes.

* Import

Import requires a =preview= or =imported= batch, re-validates, and then in
one transaction: creates the clearing account and =chart= accounts, imports
=open_ar=, =open_ap=, =credits=, =bank_balances=, =assets=, =inventory=
and the trial balance, and sets the batch =imported=.

For every row it first checks ownership. A source id this batch already
imported is done (re-running import is a no-op). A source id another live
batch imported is recorded here as a =skipped= row naming the owner, and is
left out of this batch's reconcile expectations. Rows record what was
written: =company= (=imported= or =matched=), =invoice=, =vendor_bill=,
=customer_credit=, =vendor_credit=, =payment= or =bill_payment= (opening
payments as =open_ar_payment= / =open_ap_payment=, prepayments under
=credit=), =bank_account=, =fixed_asset=, =inventory_txn= and the trial
balance =journal=.

* Reconcile

Reconcile compares figures *as of the opening*: every journal and
subledger event strictly before the cutoff instant, plus the opening
postings at it. Activity recorded in VENTURE at or after the cutoff is the
new books, not drift: a receipt dated on the cutoff day is January's cash,
so it is left out of both sides. At the cutoff instant a journal counts when
it was posted for a live batch -- the batch's own journals, and the issue,
approval, credit and payment postings of records a live batch imported.
Opening journals of a rolled-back batch and their reversals drop out, so a
corrected batch is not compared against the one it replaced.

Every total is kept per currency and never netted.

Subledger checks, over this batch's imported rows (rows owned by other
batches are listed and not counted):

- =open_ar CUR=: each row's open balance against the invoice's balance as of
  the opening (issue, less allocations before the cutoff and the opening
  receipt); a negative row against its credit's unapplied amount.
- =open_ap CUR=: the same for bills.
- =credits CUR=: each amount against the credit's or prepayment's unapplied
  amount as of the opening.
- =assets CUR=: cost less accumulated against the register's net book value
  (cost less depreciation posted on or before the cutoff).
- =inventory CUR=: quantity times unit cost against the opening receipt.

Ledger checks, over the whole payload against the opening ledger:

- =bank ACCOUNT CUR=: the sum of the bank rows mapped to each GL account.
- With a trial balance, =trial_balance ACCOUNT [(control)] CUR= for every
  row: a control account proves its subledger, any other account proves the
  trial balance journal.
- A control account holding a balance the trial balance does not name fails
  as =missing from the trial balance=.
- =clearing 3900 CUR= must be zero in every currency the trial balance or
  clearing uses. When everything passes, the report says
  "Trial balance ties at cutoff".

The report is appended, never overwritten: each run adds a dated block with
=PASS= or =FAIL= lines of expected, subledger or ledger, and difference, in
formatted amounts with their currency; up to the first failing rows under
each failing check (=open_ap[1] bill-2: expected 200.00 USD, subledger
100.00 USD, difference -100.00 USD=); rows owned by other batches; notes; and
=Result: reconciled= or =FAILED=. A failed run is saved before the refusal,
whose message names the first three failing checks, and a =reconciled= batch
that no longer reconciles returns to =imported=.

* Activate

Activation requires =reconciled=, re-runs reconcile against the books as
they are now (appending its block) and refuses -- returning the batch to
=imported= -- if anything drifted since. It then closes, in order, every open
fiscal period that ends at or before the cutoff, through the ordinary period
close (checklist and snapshots included), so nothing can later be posted
underneath the opening balances, and sets =active= with =activated_at=. An
active batch is the books and cannot be rolled back.

* Rollback

=rollback_preflight= walks the rollback without writing and lists every
blocker; the action appends them to the report as =BLOCKER= lines (or "No
blockers"). Rollback runs the same walk first and refuses with the whole
list, changing nothing, if any blocker exists. Repeating a completed rollback
is a no-op: it never reverses a reversal.

The walk, inside one transaction, in this order:

1. *Cash on imported documents.* Every cash allocation on an imported
   invoice or bill -- the opening receipt or payment, or a real one recorded
   after the cutoff -- is refunded for what is still applied, dated at the
   document's latest event (never before the cutoff): allocations are
   immutable, and a refund may not precede a later event on the same
   document. A real receipt or payment is then recorded again as an unapplied
   deposit on the same date, method and a =Cutover rollback: unapplied from
   NUMBER= reference: that cash really moved, and the bank statement still
   shows it.
2. *Imported payments.* An opening receipt, payment or prepayment applied to
   a document outside the batch is refunded there at that document's latest
   event; whatever is still unused is refunded at its latest allocation.
3. *Imported credit notes.* The opening posting is reversed at the latest
   date the credit was applied (never before the cutoff) and its remaining
   balance refreshed to zero, so it can never settle a later document.
4. *Void.* Every imported invoice and bill is voided through its service's
   opening void at its latest event, reversing its opening journal exactly as
   posted, and its number gets the suffix =-rb<batch id>=. An allocation from
   a credit note reversed in step 3 no longer blocks the void.
5. *Batch journals.* Every journal the batch posted itself -- bank balances,
   trial balance, payment offsets, asset openings -- is reversed at the
   cutoff.
6. *Assets and stock.* Depreciation (book and tax) posted after the cutoff is
   reversed at its own date; the asset is then retired through
   =venture_asset_service_rollback_opening()= at the latest of those dates,
   its remaining schedule skipped and its tag suffixed =-rb<batch id>=.
   Imported stock goes back out through =venture_inventory_service_restore()=
   at the item's latest movement, reversing inventory against goods received
   not invoiced; the batch journal of step 5 already moved that back from
   clearing.

Every row is marked =rolled_back= and the batch =rolled_back=. Nothing is
deleted; companies and bank accounts stay and are matched by a corrected
batch. With the numbers and tags freed, a corrected payload imports again as
a new batch.

Blockers, which only a person can resolve:

- the batch is =active=;
- a credit note or write-off *from outside the batch* applied to an imported
  document: an applied credit cannot be unapplied;
- an imported credit note applied to a document *outside the batch*, or
  refunded as cash;
- an imported prepayment or opening payment whose unused credit was refunded
  as cash;
- an imported asset no longer in service (disposed or written off);
- imported stock of which fewer units are on hand than were imported (sold
  or moved after the cutoff);
- any unwinding date in a closed or locked period, named with the period's
  refusal.

* Schema
