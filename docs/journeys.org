#+title: Journeys: sequences with branches, triggers and preferences

A journey is a sequence. There is one engine, the sequence sweep described
in [[file:sequences.org][Sequences]]; a journey is a sequence whose steps branch and jump, that
triggers can enrol people into, and that can honour the recipient's
marketing preferences. Everything a sequence does still holds: send
windows, suppression, exits on reply or a won deal, delivery through the
outbox, and the lazy, bounded sweep.

* Steps, waits and branches

Steps run in position order. Each step waits its own delay (days and hours)
after the step before it.

- An *email* step sends one message.
- A *wait* step only waits.
- A *call task* or *text task* step tells the enrollment's owner to call or
  text. Real texts arrive with the phone lane; until then a text step is a
  task. A later lane gives a channel its behaviour with
  =venture_sequence_channel_register()= (the channel hook).
- A *branch* step waits its delay, then looks at the enrollment's latest
  email and asks one question:
  - *opened* — did the person open it?
  - *clicked* — did they click a link in it?
  - *replied* — did anything come back from them after it (the one reply
    rule sequences already use)? Its "no" path is "no response within the
    wait".

  The answer is fixed at the end of the wait, by the branch's scheduled
  time, not when the sweep happens to run: an open an hour after the wait
  ended does not count even if the sweep is a day late. Opens and clicks
  count only when they are a person's: the same judgement campaigns use
  ([[file:deliverability.org][Deliverability]], "Machine opens") sets aside a mail scanner's or a
  prefetching proxy's open, keeps it as evidence with its reason, and it
  never takes a branch. A branch with no email before it answers no.

  *If yes, go to* and *If no, go to* name a step position; empty is the next
  step. The branch's history row says which way it went
  (=branch_result= yes or no).

Any step may also say *Then go to* a later position, and *End after this
step* finishes the journey there, which is how a path stops before the next
path's steps. Every jump goes forward — the save refuses a jump to the same
or an earlier position — so every journey ends. Steps a path passes over are
recorded as skipped, with the reason, the next time the enrollment runs.

The sequence's page draws the journey: every step in order, its wait, how
many enrollments reached it, and for a branch how many went each way.

Tracking must be on (the organization's =sequence_tracking= and the
sequence's =tracking=) for opened and clicked branches to see anything;
with it off they always answer no.

* Triggers

A =sequence_trigger= enrols a contact into its journey when something
happens:

| When                         | What it reads                                  | Narrowed by            |
|------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+------------------------|
| =form_submitted=             | a form response naming a contact (a personal link, or the contact its lead became) | =form_id=, empty is any form |
| =tag_added=                  | a save that adds the tag to a contact, any capitals | =tag=                 |
| =deal_stage_changed=         | a deal moving stage through the deal service   | =deal_stage= (lead, qualified, proposal, negotiation, won, lost), empty is any move |
| =quote_not_accepted=         | the business-event feed's =quote.sent=, once it is =days= old (7 when empty) and the quote is still sent | =days= |
| =invoice_paid=               | the feed's =invoice.paid=, for the invoice's contact (Attention of) | -- |
| =booking_made=               | the feed's =booking.created=, for the booked meeting's contact | -- |

Form, tag and deal triggers fire as the record is saved. Feed triggers are
read by the sequence sweep (=venture_sequence_service_run_triggers()=, called
first by =POST /api/v1/sequences/run=), at most 500 events per trigger per
sweep, each trigger keeping its own position in the feed. A new trigger
starts at the end of the feed: making one does not enrol everybody who ever
paid.

*Once per event per journey.* Each enrollment a trigger makes carries a
=trigger_key= — the journey, the event and the contact — unique in the
organization, deleted rows included. A re-run, a re-save, a restart or two
triggers on one journey cannot enrol anybody twice for the same event. A
later genuine event (the tag removed and added again, another invoice paid)
is a new event. The ordinary enrollment rules still apply: a suppressed
address is never enrolled, and somebody already active or paused in the
journey is not enrolled again; both are "not enrolled", not errors.

* Preferences

A sequence with a *marketing topic* is a marketing journey. Before each of
its emails it asks the recipient's preference centre
([[file:marketing.org][Marketing]], "The preference centre"): an email on a topic they turned
off, or more mail than the frequency they chose (weekly or monthly, counting
campaign mail and marketing journey emails), is skipped with the reason and
the journey carries on. A sequence without a topic is one-to-one outreach;
only stop-everything (suppression) applies to it, as before.

* Limits and defaults

| Setting                      | Default        | Limit                         |
|------------------------------+----------------+-------------------------------|
| Branch wait                  | the step's delay | 0 to 100 years, like any delay |
| Quote not accepted after     | 7 days         | 0 to 365 days                 |
| Feed events per trigger per sweep | --        | 500                           |
| Enrollments per sweep        | --             | 1000, as before               |

* Storage

Paired migration =000924_sequence_journeys.sql= checks the new step,
enrollment and delivery columns; existing steps have no branch and no jump,
so existing sequences run exactly as before, and existing opens carry no
machine reason and keep counting. =tests/test-journeys.c= covers branches
with a controlled clock, the machine judgement, validation, the channel
hook, every trigger and its idempotency, and preferences.
