#+title: Hosted enrollment and one-time guarantee credit

* Ownership and authority

The hosted client calls these operations with the same trusted workspace
administrator service token as hosted signup. Browser sessions, customer
members, viewer tokens and unscoped credentials do not acquire this authority.
Every HTTP response is =Cache-Control: no-store=.

A business has its own organization. The operator's configured billing
organization holds its plans, subscriptions, invoices and collection provider.
The business is represented there by a customer company. Its own customer
invoices and attribution remain in its own organization. A guarantee credits
the customer company in the billing organization; it never creates a refund.

* Enrollment and saved payment authority

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/enroll= accepts exactly =organization_id= and
=plan_price_id=. The price must be active, positive, and monthly or annual in
the configured billing organization. One business has one lifetime enrollment.
The first call returns 201; the same terms return 200 and the same subscription.
Different terms on a retry conflict. An existing subscription cannot enroll.
The =hosted:= receipt prefix is reserved from legacy subscription requests.

Enrollment creates a thirty-day setup window (=defer_days= 30) through the
billing service without changing the catalogue price or issuing an invoice.
Nothing is charged before the site goes live: the window gives the owner enough
to get set up, and at its end Lightsite switches the unpublished business off.
Lightsite owns that switch-off and its reminders; Venture only stops charging
and reports the date. The receipt retains the subscription, billing customer,
=enrolled_at=, =expires_at= (exactly =enrolled_at= plus thirty days, same ISO
format; there is no =charge_due_at=) and guarantee cap. The subscription gets
the reserved identity =hosted:<business>=, which keeps the renewal and
trial-expiry sweep from invoicing it until publication activates it. =/prepay=
is unchanged. The cap is three initial monthly prices, or one quarter of the initial
annual price using exact money rounding. Later catalogue changes cannot raise
it. The older =/subscriptions= operation retains its immediate-start behavior;
new deferred onboarding must use =/enroll=.

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/setup= accepts exactly =organization_id=.
It returns =awaiting_card= and a transient hosted URL, or =card_ready= for
already verified authority. Setup reuses the existing subscription authorization
service. It saves reusable payment authority and creates no invoice or charge.
The owner follows the hosted page; the client never handles card details.
The existing provider verification/webhook path activates permission.

* First charge

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/publish= accepts =organization_id= and an
optional =published_at=, the time the site actually went live. The trusted
client calls it when the business first goes live. =published_at=, or the
server receipt time without it, becomes the immutable =go_live_at=: the billing
clock starts at actual publication, not when the request arrives. A time in
the future or before enrollment is refused. Retrying never restarts the clock,
whatever time a later request carries. The C API is
=venture_lightsite_billing_publish()=.
Publication is recorded even when the owner has not completed payment setup.

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/first-charge= accepts the same object. The client
may retry either operation after a failure. Without publication it never issues
an invoice: before =expires_at= verified enrollment remains =building=, and
without verified authority it returns =awaiting_card=; at or after =expires_at=
it returns =expired= with =invoice_id= 0.

Only publication bills, before or after =expires_at=: the billing service's
=activate= action ends an uncancelled trial, anchors its first paid period now,
issues the first invoice and collection starts. A retained subscription event also lets the
operation adopt an invoice already created by the recurring scheduler.
A unique receipt identifies the first invoice, even after later renewals.
Collection uses the existing invoice-wide idempotent provider attempt. Network
uncertainty leaves =collection_pending= and a retryable invoice identity.
Only verified settlement makes the result =paid=; provider HTTP success alone
does not post cash. Collection problems do not suspend the business or its site.

* Made-back measurement

=GET /api/v1/lightsite/billing/:organization_id/made-back= returns =won=,
=invoiced=, =overlap= and =made_back= as exact money, together with =go_live_at=
and an exclusive =until=. The default end is the earlier of now and ninety days
after go-live. The C API can request an earlier end in that window.

Only acquisition bindings whose original submission retained a verified hosted
connection qualify. A later site connection, an unsigned public capture, or a
current CRM source label does not establish hosted provenance. The original
capture must fall in the measurement period, and conversion must precede the
financial event. A later signed enquiry cannot reclassify an originally
unsigned acquisition. Analytics consent is not required for a business enquiry.

Signed ordinary form relays retain the accepted response’s lead as acquisition
evidence, without copying its answers or creating another lead. Evidence uses
the immutable response identity: replay repairs an interrupted evidence write
without repeating form acceptance. Response-only fallback creates no acquisition.

Won value is the current management value of a won deal, dated at its close.
Invoiced value uses frozen invoice issue/void net evidence, after discount and
excluding tax and shipping. The event's recorded instant places it in the
window; a calendar book date at midnight must not exclude a same-day issue.
Explicit quote/deal links identify overlap: a won
job of 20.00 with a linked invoice of 15.00 contributes 20.00, not 35.00.
Currency mismatches, ambiguous acquisition and missing qualifying financial
amounts fail rather than producing a guessed total. The reader does not convert
currencies. The measurement must be retained by the caller at its decision time;
current CRM values are not a historical valuation archive.

* One-time account credit

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/guarantee= accepts exactly:

| Field | Meaning |
| organization_id | Enrolled business |
| shortfall | Nonnegative exact money, for example =10.00 USD= |
| eligible | Frozen checklist result, boolean |
| measurement | Opaque retained evidence identity, 1–128 identifier characters |

The trusted hosted client owns the checklist and the paid-versus-made-back
calculation. It must use actual settled payments, not invoices merely issued,
and retain its evidence under =measurement=. Venture enforces the ninety-day
minimum from first go-live, enrolled currency, initial cap and one lifetime
outcome. The credit is =min(shortfall, cap)= when eligible, otherwise zero.
An exact retry replays the original result. A different measurement, eligibility
or shortfall conflicts. Zero and ineligible outcomes also consume the one-time
measurement; they cannot become a later payout.

The ordinary settlement service posts the customer credit and ledger evidence.
The credit and immutable receipt commit together. Generic record writes,
deletes or purges cannot replace the receipt. Concurrent attempts either replay
or receive a transaction conflict to retry; they never pay twice.

* Fixed prepaid terms

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/prepay= uses the same trusted-service gate as
=enroll=. It accepts =organization_id=, an annual fixed =plan_price_id= and
=payment_path=: =single=, =installments=, =ach=, =wire= or =split=. Enrollment
freezes a 24-month total (twice the annual price) and issues nothing. Identical
retries return the same subscription; changed terms conflict. Tax additions,
metered prices and monthly catalogue prices are refused for this contract.

For =split=, optional =shares= contains two or three positive integer minor-unit
amounts whose sum is the total. Omitting it selects two equal shares, with any
rounding remainder on the last share. Each share requires a distinct saved card.
Other paths reject =shares=. Those minor units are scaled to Stripe charge units
before collection. ISK and UGX use two Stripe decimals even though their ISO
exponent is zero. A share that is not an integral charge unit is refused.
Owner notices format the stored charge units back into money.
A publication call fixes the start of the 24-month
term and creates exactly one invoice. Ordinary trial expiry and renewal sweeps
cannot collect this enrollment before that invoice. Once the term is due and
=cancel-at-period-end= is set, the renewal sweep ends the subscription and does
not issue another invoice.

The existing =setup= and =publish= routes return a prepaid result containing
=prepayment_id=, =invoice_id=, =state= and =payment_links= (slot and hosted URL).
Setup links collect provider-hosted consent without charging. Expired links are
replaced. No card or bank details pass through the hosted client. Bank debit
requires an enabled USD connection and a verified reusable mandate. Wire uses
USD customer-balance instructions; only a matching authenticated payment result
with the exact customer, currency and amount settles the invoice. Merely opening
instructions or returning from a browser never marks it paid.

Installments collect half at publication and half 30 days later. The invoice's
final due date is the second installment date. The persisted collector retries
definite declines daily and reconciles uncertain responses before another
mutation. Restarting the process preserves the schedule and payment identities.
Manual settlement that changes the expected outstanding balance pauses further
collection for reconciliation. Ordinary Checkout is unavailable while this
collection owns the invoice.

Split cards are all authorized before any capture. A definitive failure cancels
remaining holds and refunds any captured share. Reversal is asynchronous: the
state remains =compensating= until every cancellation or refund is verified,
then becomes =failed=. It is never reported as partial success. A lost refund
response is recovered from the original payment's refund history, including
when the provider no longer retains the request's idempotency key. Captures and
reversals remain in the private service-owned journal. Invoice allocations are
posted only after every share succeeds. A compensated failure does not silently
start another attempt. Unresolved authentication or contradictory evidence stays
unpaid and requires reconciliation.

=StripePrepayment= is a service-owned durable journal; ordinary record writes
cannot create or alter it. Migrations =000911_prepaid_collection.sql= and
=000912_prepaid_term_end.sql= are paired for SQLite and PostgreSQL and invent
no historical collections or term ends. The local
=test-prepaid-stripe= fixture proves all five paths, restart/retry, lost responses,
compensation, expired setup, payment identity checks and manual-payment conflicts.
=test-hosted-billing= covers the HTTP contract, authority, replay and frozen shares.
These fixtures do not claim live-provider payment verification.

* Money back

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/money-back= accepts exactly =organization_id=,
with the same trusted service authority as cancellation. It keeps the
thirty-day money-back promise: within thirty days of =go_live_at= it ends the
subscription now and refunds, in full, every settled payment received against
the subscription's invoices.

- The decision, the immediate cancellation and the list of payments commit
  together as the =money-back= receipt (=state: refunding=). Each card payment
  is then refunded through the provider with the idempotency key
  =hosted:<business>:money-back:<payment>=. An existing refund for the payment
  intent is looked up first, so a retry after a lost provider answer finds it
  instead of making a second one.
- Each refund is booked once against the payment's receipt, with reference
  =stripe:<refund id>=, and appears in the business's notifications as a
  =refund=.
- When every refund is made, a =money-back-done= receipt (=state: refunded=)
  records the refund ids. A retry returns it verbatim. A failure part way
  leaves =refunding=; the next request finishes the same refunds.
- A payment that did not come through the card provider is listed with
  =by_hand: true= for staff to return; it is not refunded automatically.
- Before go-live nothing was charged, and after thirty days the promise has
  ended: both are refused (=409=) and nothing changes. Later =publish= and
  =first-charge= requests return the money-back receipt, and the business
  cannot subscribe again.

=test-stripe /stripe/hosted-money-back=, =/stripe/hosted-money-back-lost= and
=/stripe/hosted-money-back-late= cover a settled monthly payment, a lost
refund answer, and day thirty-one. All provider calls use fixtures.

* Cancel an enrollment

=POST /api/v1/lightsite/billing/cancel= accepts exactly =organization_id=.
It uses the same trusted service bearer authority as enrollment. The hosted
client checks that the dashboard user owns that business before forwarding
this request; browser sessions, customer tokens and viewer tokens cannot call
this service boundary directly.

An unpublished enrollment with no invoice always ends immediately, inside the
setup window or after it expired, because nothing was charged. The response has =state: cancelled= and
=no_charge: true=, with =cancelled_at= and =effective_at=. The subscription and
immutable cancellation receipt commit together. An identical retry returns
the original response. Queued =publish= and
=first-charge= calls return that response without collection. New enrollment,
subscription changes and payment setup are refused for the cancelled business.
The normal renewal sweep cannot issue an invoice for the ended subscription.
A delayed prepaid setup completion becomes terminal =cancelled= without
provider calls or payment links, and the prepaid timer skips it thereafter.

Publication takes precedence over the unpaid trial. After publication, or once
an invoice exists, the response instead reports =cancel_at_period_end= and =no_charge: false=. Existing
monthly invoices and prepaid installment obligations remain; cancellation
creates no automatic refund or credit (money back, above, is the separate
request that refunds within thirty days of go-live). A published prepaid enrollment that
has not yet been invoiced stores =go_live_at= plus twenty-four months as the
subscription's period end and reports that stored date. Prepaid terms still
end after their original twenty-four months, with no renewal. Payment setup
and collection of those already committed terms remain available.

The built HTTP fixture =test-hosted-billing= covers early monthly and prepaid
cancellation, replay, delayed publication/collection, a later renewal sweep,
cancellation after the setup window expired, incurred monthly invoices, invalid requests and
authority. =test-prepaid-stripe= covers delayed setup completion after cancel
and a second installment retained after cancellation at term end.
=test-stripe /stripe/cancel-before-charge= verifies that an already verified
saved card makes zero payment requests after cancellation on day thirteen.
All provider calls in these proofs use fixtures.
