* Reporting

A report turns a period and some options into headline metrics and a table of
rows. =VentureReportResult= is the single shape every report produces, which
is what lets the web UI, the CLI, the AI and the CSV exporter render any
report — including one a plugin added this morning — without knowing anything
about it.

do", "how did each format do" and "how did each product do".

** Several currencies in one organization

An organization keeps its books in one currency -- its =default-currency=,
the answer =venture_posting_service_book_currency()= gives -- and may deal in
others: euros beside dollars, or a game's fair tickets beside its gold. The
operational reports read sales and expenses, not the ledger, and never
convert, so they keep one figure per currency:

- =pnl= shows its eight lines (gross, refunds, fees, shipping, net revenue,
  expenses, of which deductible, profit) once per currency, with a
  =currency= column. The book currency's block is always there and always
  first, zero when nothing was in it; any other currency's block appears
  when a sale or expense is in it. Profit is revenue less expenses *within*
  a currency.
- =ventures= has one row per venture per currency, the book currency's
  always; =sales= counts that currency's sales.
- =monthly= has one row per month per currency: the book currency's every
  month, so the series has no holes, and another only in a month that
  traded in it.

Metrics follow the convention =inventory_valuation= set: the book
currency's figure keeps the plain key (=revenue=, =expenses=, =profit=), and
another currency's adds its code (=revenue_TICKET=, labelled "Net revenue
(TICKET)"). A dashboard widget or the headline reading =revenue= keeps
reading the book currency however many other currencies appear beside it.
A report with more than one currency says, in a note, that they were kept
apart and which is the book.

This replaced a heuristic that carried every total in whichever currency
most records used. In a gold organization with three ticket sales and one
gold one, that dropped the gold sale, could not subtract a gold expense
from ticket revenue, and left profit blank; with no expenses at all the
empty expense total was made in USD and a gold-only organization showed no
profit. Neither happens now. Behaviour change: in a USD organization whose
sales are all in EUR, =revenue= is now 0 USD and the euros are
=revenue_EUR=, where =revenue= used to be the euro figure.

Who holds how much of each currency is a different question, answered by
=holdings=: one row per location and currency, from the holding accounts'
journal lines for a posted currency and their memo movements for a memo
one, the book currency first under each location, totals as =held= and
=held_<CODE>= metrics by the same convention.

=categories=, =tax=, =campaigns= and =pipeline= still show one figure per
column: it is carried in the book currency whenever any record is in it,
else in the currency most records use, an empty figure is zero in the book
currency, and whatever could not join is counted and said in a note.
Filter by =venture_id= for exact figures in one currency.

** What =cash_vs_booked= compares

The first question -- how much money is coming in -- has two answers that
drift apart, and this report puts them side by side per bucket and per
currency: *booked* is invoices issued in the bucket (the frozen issue
amount, tax included) less voids, credit notes and write-offs dated in it;
*received* is receipts dated in the bucket less refunds dated in it. Every
receipt -- manual, Stripe, or a matched bank credit -- is a =payment=
written by the settlement service, so receipts are counted once whichever
door they came through. The *gap* is booked less received, and the
*cumulative gap* runs it from the period start, so a month that collects
last month's invoices shows a negative gap and a shrinking total. Migrated
invoices and credits carrying the cutover =opening_at= stamp are neither
booked nor received here.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report cash_vs_booked this_quarter
venturectl report cash_vs_booked 2026-07-01..2026-07-31 bucket=week
venturectl report cash_vs_booked 2026 customer_id=7
curl '.../api/v1/reports/cash_vs_booked?period=2026&bucket=week&format=csv'
#+end_src

=bucket= is =month= (default) or =week=; weeks run Monday to Sunday, are
clipped to the period and labelled by their Monday. =customer_id= keeps one
customer's invoices and receipts. =venture_id= keeps the venture's invoices
and reaches cash through them: a receipt belongs to a customer, not a
venture, so receipts and cash credits *applied* to the venture's invoices
count as received on the application date, credit notes and write-offs
applied to them count against booked, and an unapplied deposit is nobody's
venture cash. =currency= (default the instance default) picks the currency
the total tiles are in; every other currency keeps its own rows and is
named in the tiles' note, never converted or added in. =as_of= stops the
evidence at that instant. Any other option, and a =bucket= other than
month or week, is refused with a validation error rather than ignored --
including =dimension=, which journal lines carry and invoices and receipts
do not, so a report pack cut by dimension cannot include this report.

The report has no rows of its own until something is dated in the period;
an empty period is one zero row per bucket in the report currency, so the
reader sees the months they asked about. The home page's Profit and loss
card carries a *Booked vs received* line taken from this report's own
=booked_vs_received= metric, so the card and the report cannot disagree;
with receivables off it reads =n/a=.

** What =receivables= ages against

The end of the period, or now, whichever is earlier.

For a period that has closed those are the same question and the end date
is the honest one: a receivables report for last month should age against
31 August rather than against whenever somebody happened to run it.

For the period you are standing in they are not. Its end date is in the
future, and aging against a date that has not happened yet reports invoices
as overdue before they are due --- run =report receivables this_month= on 3
September anchored to 30 September and an invoice due on the 13th lands in
the =1-30 days= bucket and joins the overdue total. That total is the
figure somebody chases a customer over, so it says debt, not forecast.

Invoices with no due date are their own bucket rather than overdue: undated
is unknown, not late. Only =sent= invoices count at all --- a draft is still
being written, and paid or void ones are settled history.

* The aggregate report

Every other report answers one question about one kind of record. =aggregate= answers the shape of question they all share --- /how much, how many, grouped by what, over which days/ --- for any business record type, a plugin's included, from the field table alone. It is what to reach for before totalling =venturectl list= output by hand, and what the assistant is told to prefer.

It belongs to the =core= module, so no module switch hides it; the record types it reads are hidden by their own modules instead. The implementation is =src/report/venture-aggregate-report.c=, and the same accumulator backs the dashboard's =sum= and =progress= widgets (see [[file:dashboards.org][Dashboards]]), so a card and the report behind it are one computation.

** Options

|------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------|

| =measure=        | the field to aggregate, a custom field by name, or =count= for the records themselves                  | =count=                          |
| =aggregate=      | =sum=, =avg=, =min=, =max=, =count=, =count_distinct= (the =VentureAggregate= enum's nicks)            | =sum= for a numeric measure, else =count= |
| =group_by=       | up to three comma-separated fields; =reference.field= follows one reference                           | none: one row                    |
| =category_depth= | roll category and location groups up to this level of their tree; =0= is the top                      | leaves                           |
| =date_field=     | the declared date or time field the period bounds and =bucket= reads                                   | none: the period bounds nothing  |
| =bucket=         | =day=, =week= (ISO, Monday), =month=, =quarter=, =year=                                               | none                             |
| =filter=         | a list page's query string: =status=open&priority__in=high,urgent=                                     | none                             |
| =per=            | =hour= or =day=: add a rate column                                                                     | none                             |
| =period=         | any period a report takes: =this_month=, =2026-03=, =2026-Q1=, =last_30_days=, =all=                   | =this_month=                     |

| =venture_id=     | narrow to one venture, for types that belong to one                                                    | all                              |

The options are described in full by the report's own parameter schema --- =GET /api/v1/reports= lists it, and it is what the assistant's =venture_report= tool and the MCP =venture_report= tool read.

** The rows

One row per bucket × group × currency, sorted by bucket, then by group label, then by currency. The columns are:

- =bucket= (when bucketed): =2026-03-02= for a day, =2026-W09= for an ISO week, =2026-03=, =2026-Q1=, =2026=.
- one column per =group_by=, keyed by the name as asked (=product_id.category_id=), headed by the field's label. A name that collides with the report's own columns is keyed =group_1= … instead.
- =currency=, when the measure is money.
- =records=: how many records the row holds (with a value for the measure, when there is one).
- =value=: the aggregate. Money for =sum=, =avg=, =min= and =max= of a money field; a number otherwise.
- =rate=: with =per=, below.

Two headline metrics: =records= (how many records were read) and =rows=.

*Money is never mixed.* A money measure is keyed by currency before anything is added, so gold and dollars are two rows and nothing below the key can add across them. There is no conversion: a total in a single currency is what =exchange_rate= and the ledger are for.

*An average of money is one exact division.* =avg= of a money field is the sum multiplied by 1/n through =venture_money_multiply_rational()=, rounded half to even: 0.01 and 0.04 average to 0.02, not the 0.03 rounding half up gives or whatever a double makes of 0.025. Integers sum exactly, and an overflow is refused rather than wrapped; doubles are doubles.

*A missing value is in no row, and said so.* A record whose money field (or custom field) is unset is not counted --- not as zero, and not in =records= --- and the result carries a note: "3 records had no gross and are in no row." A custom value that no longer parses is counted and noted the same way. =count= with a measure counts the records that have a value; =count_distinct= counts distinct values (for money, per currency).

** Groups

- A plain field groups by its value: a string as written, an integer, a boolean as =yes=/=no=, a date or time by the UTC calendar day it is.
- An enum groups by its nick and shows its declared label (or the nick made readable: =in_progress= is "In progress").
- A reference groups by id and shows the target's display name, so two targets that share a name stay apart. Nothing referenced is =(none)=; a target that cannot be read (gone, or outside what the caller may see) shows as =product #42= rather than its name.
- A reference to =category= or =location= shows the node's path, computed by =venture_category_path()= on every run and never stored, so renaming a parent renames the group. =category_depth=N= rolls each node up to its ancestor at level N (=venture_category_ancestor_at_depth()=): at depth 1 "Materials / Herbs / Rare" and "Materials / Herbs" are both "Materials / Herbs". A node shallower than N is its own group. =category_depth= with no tree group is refused rather than ignored.
- A custom field groups by the value stored in the record's attributes, under the name it was defined with; a custom reference behaves as a built-in one.
- =reference.field= follows one reference and groups by a field of the record it points at --- =product_id.category_id= groups sales by their product's category. One hop is the limit; a first part that is not a reference is refused.
- Long text and JSON fields are refused: every record would be its own group.

** Time: the period, buckets and rates

The period bounds =date_field= and nothing else. Without a =date_field= the period cannot bound anything, and rather than pretend, the report reads every matching record and says so in a note ("No date_field was named, so the period does not bound these records"). =created_at= and =updated_at= are accepted as date fields on every type. A custom date field is refused: it lives in the attributes, where the query cannot bound it.

Buckets are *UTC* calendar buckets, for the reason every period boundary is (see /Periods and calendar dates/ above): a date is stored as midnight UTC, and in a zone west of Greenwich local buckets would put the first of every month in the month before. A record with no date lands in a =(no date)= bucket, last.

*=per=* adds a rate: the row's value divided by the time elapsed in its window, in hours or days. The window is the bucket clipped to the period --- or, unbucketed, the period --- clipped again to now, so this month's rate is over the days gone, not the days it will have. February 2026 has 28 days, so 28.00 of sales is 1.00 a day; per hour it is 28.00 ÷ 672 = 0.0416…, rounded half to even to 0.04 in the currency. A rate of money is computed with =venture_money_multiply_rational()=, never through a double. A window that has not started has no rate (the cell is empty, not zero). =per= applies to =sum= and =count= only, and an all-time period with no bucket is refused: it has no length to divide by.

** Scope and what is refused

Rows are read through the ordinary query, which carries the organisation (=organization_id=, or the default), the caller's access scope (so a member sees only what the access policy lets them read, and an organisation scope set by the web layer, the API or the assistant holds), the module mask and =as_of=. Nothing here writes SQL.

Refused, before a row is read, with a message saying what to change:

- no =type=; a type that does not exist, or whose module is off (the message names the switch);
- a type that is not business data: users, API tokens, chat, the inbox and watch list are /personal/, forges and webhooks /platform/ machinery. This is derived from the type's declared authority (=venture_data_class_for_type()=: only =tenant= and =reference= types are read), so a plugin's type is covered the day it declares itself;
- a field that does not exist, and a *sensitive* field anywhere --- as the measure, a group, the date field, or in the filter: a total and a comparison are both ways of reading a value;
- =sum=, =avg=, =min=, =max= of a field that is not money, an integer or a double; =count_distinct= without a measure; an unknown aggregate;
- more than three groups; a bad =bucket=; =bucket= without =date_field=; a bad =per=;
- a =filter= that sets =limit=, =offset=, =page= or =order= (paging would quietly total a page);
- a question matching more than =VENTURE_AGGREGATE_MAX_ROWS= (20 000) records. The count is taken first and the question is refused, naming the count, rather than answered from a truncated set --- a smaller number presented as the same one. Narrow the period or the filter.

Every report that totals fetched rows shares this bound, and reads it with =venture_aggregate_get_max_rows()= rather than the macro so a test can lower it (=venture_aggregate_set_max_rows()=, process-wide, restored with 0) instead of seeding twenty thousand rows. Whatever narrows the question must be a filter in the query the bound counts: a filter applied to the rows after a capped fetch leaves a large organization refused however narrowly it asks, by a refusal that tells it to narrow.

** Worked examples

Sales gross by product category, per month, this year --- one row per month, category and currency:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report aggregate 2026 type=sale measure=gross \
    group_by=product_id.category_id date_field=occurred_at bucket=month
#+end_src

The same, rolled up to the top of the category tree, with a daily rate:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report aggregate 2026-Q1 type=sale measure=gross \
    group_by=product_id.category_id category_depth=0 \
    date_field=occurred_at bucket=month per=day
#+end_src

Tickets by status, all of them (no date field, so no period note matters):

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report aggregate all type=ticket group_by=status
#+end_src

Open external tickets by assignee and priority:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report aggregate all type=ticket group_by=assignee,priority \
    'filter=status__not_in=done,cancelled&kind=external'
#+end_src

The average sale per channel last month, and how many distinct products each sold:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report aggregate last_month type=sale measure=gross aggregate=avg \
    group_by=channel date_field=occurred_at
venturectl report aggregate last_month type=sale measure=product_id \
    aggregate=count_distinct group_by=channel date_field=occurred_at
#+end_src

Over HTTP the options are query parameters, and =format=csv= exports the table:

#+begin_src sh
curl '.../api/v1/reports/aggregate?period=2026&type=sale&measure=gross&group_by=channel&date_field=occurred_at&bucket=month&format=csv'
#+end_src

The page at =/reports/aggregate= draws a form for the options; asked with no type it shows the form above the refusal, so a person finishes the question there.

* Design notes

Reports aggregate in C wherever money is involved. A =SUM= over mixed
currencies would produce a confidently wrong number, so money is never added
across currencies. The reports that answer "how is the business doing" --
=pnl=, =ventures=, =monthly= -- keep one figure per currency, the
organization's book currency first; the rest carry a single figure per
column in the book currency (else the currency most records use) and
exclude — rather than convert — the others. An exclusion is never silent:
any report whose totals had to leave amounts out says how many in a note
attached to the result.

A figure is never presented as more complete than it is. The P&L shows the
deductible total beside the cash total and says why they differ; the tax
report counts unreviewed expenses as zero and says how many are waiting.

Metrics carry a direction. =venture_metric_set_higher_is_better(metric, FALSE)=
on a cost or an awaiting-review count means the UI colours a rise red rather
than green.

Every output format comes from one structure, so a report is written once and
appears correctly in the browser, the terminal, a CSV export and an org file.
CSV escaping defuses a leading =​== that a spreadsheet would otherwise execute
as a formula; HTML output escapes its content.

** =organization_id= is a question, not a grant

Every door takes =organization_id= from its caller: the query string on
=/reports/:name= and =/api/v1/reports/:name= (and so =venturectl report= and
the MCP tool, which go through the API), the assistant's tool input. None of
them checks membership before the report runs, and none needs to: the web
layer runs every authenticated request under the caller's access scope
(=venture_orgaccess_web_dispatch()=), and the assistant under its own
(=venture_ai_service_enter_scope()=), so every read the report makes --
=venture_database_get()= and =venture_database_find()= alike -- is refused a
row the caller may not read. A report that checks its organization exists
(=recipe_margin=, the goals, market and sessions reports) therefore answers
another organization's id with 404 /Organization #N not found/, exactly as if
it did not exist; the generic =aggregate= counts none of its rows; the
product and goal pickers on the report pages list only readable rows; and a
craft naming another organization's location refuses with "location #N",
never its name.

What would break this is a read under an internal scope
(=venture_access_policy_enter(policy, NULL)=) of a record whose id came from
the caller. Internal scopes exist for validators and machinery; a report, a
picker or a refusal message that names a caller-supplied record must read it
under the caller's scope. =/orgaccess/report-organization= in
=tests/test-auth.c= pins all of it with two organizations and a member of
one, and fails when a picker or the craft's location name is read under an
internal scope.

** Periods and calendar dates

A date typed without a time (=2026-03-01=, or =today=) is a calendar date, stored as midnight UTC on that day by =venture_time_from_string()=. Every period boundary =venture_date_range_parse()= builds is a midnight UTC for the same reason, whatever =locale.timezone= says; the configured zone only decides what /today/, /this month/ and /this year/ are, so at nine in the evening on 31 March in New York =this_month= is still March. The consequence is the one that matters in a ledger: an expense dated 1 March is in March's P&L, and one dated 1 January is in the same year as the tax filing, whose years were always UTC.

A bare date is that one day: =venturectl report pnl 2026-03-14= reports the
fourteenth. The month form used to be tried first and matched the date's
first seven characters, so a one-day P&L silently reported all of March. A
string shaped like a date that is not one (=2026-02-31=) is refused rather
than read as the month it begins.

Built in the configured zone instead, as periods once were, 1 March sat five hours before March began in New York, so every document dated the first of a month was reported in the month before. =/report/period-boundaries-are-calendar-dates= and =/headline/timezone/bare-date= pin this.

A true instant -- a payment recorded from a webhook at 21:00 on the last evening of a month in New York -- is judged against the same UTC boundaries and so falls in the next month. That is the smaller error of the two: it touches only events stamped in the last few hours of a period, where the old rule moved every calendar date on the first of one.

=venture_time_to_date_string()= shows a value at exactly midnight UTC as the calendar date it is, in any zone, and converts anything else to the zone it is given. A list page rendered in New York used to show 1 March as 28 February while the record page showed 1 March.

* Writing one

A report is a function:

#+begin_src C
static VentureReportResult *
my_report(VentureContext    *context,
          VentureDateRange  *period,
          JsonObject        *options,
          GError           **error)
{
	g_autoptr(VentureReportResult) result = NULL;

	result = venture_report_result_new("My report", period);

	venture_report_result_add_metric(result,
		venture_metric_new_money("total", "Total", amount));

	venture_report_result_add_column(result, "name", "Name",
	                                 VENTURE_REPORT_COLUMN_TEXT);
	venture_report_result_add_column(result, "value", "Value",
	                                 VENTURE_REPORT_COLUMN_MONEY);

	venture_report_result_begin_row(result);
	venture_report_result_set_text(result, "name", "A row");
	venture_report_result_set_money(result, "value", amount);

	return g_steal_pointer(&result);
}
#+end_src

Register it:

#+begin_src C
venture_report_registry_add(registry,
	VENTURE_REPORT(venture_func_report_new("mine", "My report",
	                                       "What it answers", my_report)));
#+end_src

It now appears in the CLI, the web UI, the REST API and the AI's report tool.

A plugin needing state can subclass =VentureReport= instead and override
=generate=; both end up in the same registry and are indistinguishable to
every consumer. Registering a name that already exists replaces it, so a
plugin can deliberately override a built-in with a better version for its
data.

* Sales pipeline analysis

The =pipelines= module adds =stage_duration=, =funnel=, =forecast=,
=loss_reasons= and the =overdue_deals= dashboard metric. All accept
=organization_id=, =pipeline_id= and =owner=. The existing =pipeline= report
also accepts =pipeline_id=. Definitions and currency separation are described
in [[file:pipelines.org][Sales pipelines]].
* Ledger financial statements

The =statements= module adds =balance_sheet=, =income_statement=, =cash_flow=,
=general_ledger=, =account_balances=, =pnl_reconciliation= and =year_end_pack=. These read posted
ledger evidence per exact legal entity and currency; they never choose a
majority currency. All accept =as_of= and =compare_to=, and general ledger also
accepts =account_id=. They use the normal report pages, CSV export and
financial close snapshots. See [[file:statements.org][definitions and the indirect cash-flow method]].
The older =pnl= remains operational sales/expenses reporting; differences are
explained by source document in =pnl_reconciliation=. Each currency's section
comes after the book currency's and carries a =books= label from the posting
rule (=Separate book: TICKET=, =Own book: EUR (no rate to GOLD)=); see
[[file:ledger.org][Currencies in the books]].

* Saved reports and dimensions

=saved_report= stores report name, period, JSON options and an optional
dimension. Execution always scopes =organization_id= from the saved row and
passes =dimension= into the shared ledger query so departmental packs do not
see another team's postings. =report_pack= groups saved reports for a
schedule. =venture_report_pack_service_run_due= dispatches due packs
(cron-like =0 8 * * *= daily, =0 8 1 * *= monthly) and stamps
=last-run-at=. Automation: =venture->report_packs_run("1")=.

* Scheduled report packs

Schedules accept =daily= or five space-separated numeric/=*= fields: minute,
hour, day of month, month, weekday (0 or 7 is Sunday). Evaluation uses UTC.
Unsupported expressions are refused instead of interpreted as daily. When
both day fields are specified, either may match. A delayed sweep catches up
once on the scheduled day; it does not replay missed historical days.

A successful run stores its result array in =report_pack.last_output= and
updates =last_run_at= in the same record save. Read that JSON through the
generic record API or CLI. A failed run preserves the previous output and
timestamp. No historical output archive is implied; mailing the output is
described under Delivery below.

A report pack may reference only saved reports in its own organization. The runner validates this boundary before generating each result, including scheduled packs; foreign saved-report ids fail instead of copying another organization's report into the pack output.

* The year-end pack

=year_end_pack= is the report an outside accountant pulls once a year: for
a fiscal year (=fy_2025=, or =fy= for the current one) it gathers, in
reading order, the trial balance at year end, the profit and loss
(=income_statement=), the balance sheet, the cash-flow statement, the
receivables and payables aging at year end, the general ledger detail, the
sales-tax return summary (=tax_liability=: frozen invoice and bill tax by
code, the same grouping the US sales-tax adapter files), a 1099 pack summary
(the =contractor_tax_pack= rows prepared for the calendar year the fiscal
year ends in — vendor, form, status and total; the frozen CSV with the TIN
is never copied) and the fixed-asset register with accumulated
depreciation. =organization_id=, =basis=, =currency= and =dimension= pass
through to every member so the statements agree with each other.

It is one =VentureReportResult= whose rows are the lines of every file:
=file=, =line=, =content=, followed by the lines of =index.txt=, which
names each file, its title and row count, carries each member's own note
(a currency exclusion, an empty ledger), and says in so many words when a
member was *omitted* because its module is off — a pack is never silently
incomplete. That shape is what lets the pack ride the scheduled-report
path unchanged: a =saved_report= naming =year_end_pack= with period
=fy_2025=, inside a =report_pack= with a schedule, is run by
=venture_report_pack_service_run_due=, retained in
=report_pack.last_output=, and readable back through the generic record API.

The zip is rebuilt from those rows — =venture_year_end_pack_zip()= from a
live result, =venture_year_end_pack_zip_from_output()= from a pack's
retained JSON — so the file an accountant downloads a year later is exactly
what ran, not the books as they stand now. On the web:

- =GET /books= is the Books page: the pack form, the retained packs with a
  download each, the statements and the other reports.
- =GET /books/year-end-pack.zip?period=fy_2025&basis=accrual= generates the
  pack for the selected entity under the caller's own read authority.
- =GET /books/packs/:id/year-end-pack.zip= is the retained output of a
  scheduled pack; a pack outside the caller's organisations is not found,
  and a pack whose output holds no year-end result is refused rather than
  answered with an empty archive.

Without libarchive the report still runs and the zip routes answer
=VENTURE_ERROR_UNSUPPORTED=. =test-accountant= covers the file list, the
index, the module-off note, the scheduled round trip and the downloads.
** Delivery

A pack can mail each run's output. Two fields on =report_pack= decide it:

| Field        | Meaning                                                                     |
|--------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =recipients= | Comma-separated addresses. Each is trimmed and must look like a mailbox     |
|              | (one =@=, a local part, a dotted domain, no whitespace or separators); an   |
|              | invalid one, or an empty entry from a trailing comma, refuses the save.     |
| =deliver=    | =none= (the default, written when the field is empty) or =email=. Anything  |
|              | else is refused, and =email= with no recipients is refused too.             |

When the sweep (=venture_report_pack_service_run_due=, the =report_packs_run=
automation) runs a due pack whose =deliver= is =email=, it first saves the
run --- =last_run_at= and =last_output= --- and then hands that output to the
transactional mail outbox (=venture_report_pack_service_deliver=). The message
goes to the pack's recipients with:

- the retained output as =<pack-name>.json=;
- a =<report-title>.csv= for every result that has columns and rows, built
  the way the web export builds it (formatted cells, CSV escaping that
  defuses a leading ===);
- a subject and body from the organization's =mail_template= named
  =report_pack=, or the built-in wording when there is none. The placeholders
  are ={name}= (the pack), ={period}= (the distinct period labels of its
  reports), ={run_at}= and ={reports}= (one line per report with its
  headline metrics).

Nothing is sent from the sweep: the outbox queues the message and its own
delivery sweep submits it, with the at-least-once, retry and uncertainty
rules of [[file:mail.org][Transactional mail]]. That is also where a relay
failure lives: on the =mail_message= row, not on the pack.

The pack records the hand-over. =last_delivered_at=, =last_delivery_message_id=
(the durable Message-ID) and =last_delivery_mail_id= (the outbox row, for
=venturectl mail retry=) are set when the message was queued;
=last_delivery_error= holds the reason when it was not, and the other three
are cleared. The run is never marked failed by its delivery: a refused send
--- the mail module off, no organization SMTP binding configured, a
policy plugin refusing the enqueue, no output retained yet --- leaves
=last_run_at= and =last_output= as the run wrote them, so the report is not
run again for it, and the sweep goes on to the next pack. The hand-over and
its record commit together; a refusal is recorded in a transaction of its own.

Re-send the last retained output at any time:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report packs deliver 7
curl -X POST .../api/v1/report_pack/7/deliver
#+end_src

Both require editor access and the =statements= module, refuse =--stage=, and
answer with the queued =mail_message= (202). They send what the last run kept;
they never run the report. Each hand-over gets its own idempotency key
(=report_pack:<uuid>:<delivery clock>=), so a deliberate re-send is a new
message rather than the queued one returned again.

Not covered: a year-end archive is not a registry report on this base, so no
zip is attached; when one lands, its file rides as one more entry in the
same attachment list the composer builds. =test-report-delivery= proves the
field rules, the queued message and its attachments, the refusals, and the
CLI and REST surfaces.

* The headline numbers

The =headline= module adds the numbers the business is run on, as ordinary
registry reports: =cac=, =customer_churn=, =ltv=, =ltv_cac= and
=customer_cohorts=. They serve the questions of issue #75: what a customer
costs, who is leaving, what a customer is worth, whether the first is worth
paying for the third, and how long customers stay. Each is written in plain
words here, refusals included, because a number whose formula you cannot say
is not one you can run a business on.

** Rules that hold across all of them

- *Currency.* Every amount is a =VentureMoney= carried in the organisation's
  book currency (=default-currency=, or the process default). An amount in
  another currency is counted, noted and left out, never converted.
- *Ratios.* Every ratio is integer arithmetic in basis points, rounded once,
  half to even, and becomes a fraction only when rendered: two of three is
  6,667 basis points (66.67%), not the 6,666 truncation gives. LTV:CAC and
  CAC payback are kept to hundredths the same way.
- *Refusals.* Every division names its zero and answers =n/a= rather than
  dividing. =n/a= is a text metric, so a dashboard cannot mistake it for 0.
- *The anchor.* A trailing window is measured back from the anchor: the
  period's end, now, or the =as_of= cutoff, whichever is earliest --- the
  same anchor the receivables aging uses. A period still in progress has an
  end that has not happened yet.
- *The trailing window* is the twelve months before the anchor, cut into
  twelve months counted back from it (=anchor - 12 months= to =anchor - 11
  months=, and so on). For a calendar month period these are calendar months.
- *Scope.* All accept =organization_id=, =venture_id= and =as_of=.
  =venture_id= keeps only cash sales, expenses, vendor bills, campaigns,
  leads, tickets, schedules (the =venture_id= in their template) and
  subscriptions (their plan's venture) of that venture; rows with no
  venture are then left out, as the P&L does. =as_of= hides deletions made
  after it and every dated movement after it. Both are passed to the =pnl=
  report the gross margin is read from; the billing reports ARPA and the
  home page read from take =as_of= but are organisation-wide, not per
  venture.
- *One read.* The reports read their records once, through a snapshot, and
  derive every period from it; the home page asks for two periods of four
  reports from one set of queries.

A =headline_setting= row per organisation tunes them: =hourly_rate= (what a
logged support hour costs), =minimum_customer_months= (zero means 12) and
=activity_days= (zero means 90), and the support rollup's
=support_hourly_rate= and =support_ticket_rate= (whole units of the book
currency; zero means the worklog rate, and nothing, respectively --- see
[[*The support rollup][The support rollup]]). =customer_churn= and =ltv=
accept =days= to override the last. The save refuses a second living row for the same
organisation, on every save --- moving a row to an organisation that already
has one is refused too --- and migration =000350= backs this with a unique
partial index, because a restore does not run save validators. That
migration keeps the lowest-id living row of any existing duplicates (the row
every reader already used) and soft-deletes the rest.

** Cash: what a customer paid

Cash is decided by the settlement service, not reconstructed here. The
service derives a cash =sale= whenever money a customer paid is applied to
one of their invoices, and a negative cash adjustment whenever an applied
receipt is refunded. So:

- a =payment_allocation= that names a sale is a receipt, and a =refund= that
  names a sale is a refund; the amount is that sale's =gross=, which for a
  foreign invoice paid in book currency is the book-currency cash;
- a deposit or overpayment is not cash until it is applied to an invoice,
  and then each application is a receipt on the day it was applied;
- a credit note or write-off applied to an invoice is not cash, and the
  refund of unapplied money is not a refund of cash (it was never counted).

Worked through: a $500 invoice paid with $600, $100 of it refunded, is $500
of cash. A $6,000 retainer applied to twelve $500 invoices is twelve $500
receipts. The receipt belongs to the invoice's company; a refund to the
refund's customer. The first version counted allocations funded by a payment
and subtracted every refund: the first example read $400, and the retainer
customer had no cash at all.

A company's *first cash* is its earliest positive receipt. That is when it
became a customer --- not when an invoice was marked paid, which a write-off
also does and a later refund undoes.

** =cac=: what a new customer cost

*Acquisition spend* in the period is:

- every expense and every vendor bill line flagged =acquisition=, or naming a
  =campaign_id=, dated in the period. A bill line counts at its bill's date,
  drafts and voids excluded, and costs its net plus any tax that cannot be
  recovered (the tax code's =recoverable= flag; tax with no code counts). The
  cash expenses the payables service projects from a paid bill line
  (external id =bill_line:...= or =bill_cash:...=) are never counted: the
  line already was, at its bill date.
- each outreach campaign's own =spend=, spread evenly over the time it ran,
  =[started_at, ended_at)= or =[started_at, now)= while it has no end, by
  VentureMoney allocation so consecutive periods sum back to the spend. A
  campaign with no duration belongs whole to the period of its start. Only
  campaigns no expense or bill line names are counted this way: once a
  campaign's costs are recorded as rows, its =spend= figure is a summary of
  the same money. A campaign still running is re-spread every day, so its
  earlier months' shares shrink as it runs on.

The =acquisition= flag is an ordinary boolean on =expense= and
=vendor_bill_line=; a =tax_category= marked =acquisition= turns it on for
new expenses and bill lines filed under it, and only for new ones ---
changing a category later does not rewrite what was already spent. The
=campaign_id= reference on both, and the =cost_of_revenue= flags described
under =ltv=, are ordinary fields too; migration =000351= checks each table
has its new columns (or, for a disabled module, none) and backfills nothing:
no historical row is inferred to be cost of revenue or a campaign's cost.

A *new customer* is a company whose first cash fell in the period. A company
paying in instalments is new at its first. =CAC = acquisition spend / new
customers=, one rational multiplication rounded half to even.

Metrics: =spend=, =campaign_spend= (campaign figures and rows naming a
campaign), =expense_spend= (other flagged rows), =new_customers=, =cac=.

The table breaks the new customers down by the *campaign* and *source* of
the lead they were converted from (the first converted lead, in id order); a
company with no lead is attributed to its own =source=, or =(unattributed)=.
A campaign's spend in the period is split across its source rows in
proportion to the customers each brought, by VentureMoney allocation, and
each row shows its share and its own CAC. The first version gave every row
the whole campaign spend.

*Refusal.* Zero new customers reports the spend and =n/a= for CAC, with a
note. Spend with nobody to divide it by is a cost, not a cost per customer.

** =customer_churn=: who is leaving

Two measures, because a business has two kinds of customer.

*Recurring churn* counts customers, not schedules. A customer is recurring
at an instant when any of their commitments is running then:

- an invoice =recurring_schedule= (kind invoice, the company in its
  template) runs from =start_at= until the earlier of =end_at= and its
  deletion; a paused schedule is paused whatever the date, because the flag
  carries none;
- with billing on, a =customer_subscription= is what its latest
  =subscription_event= before the instant left it: active and past_due run,
  trialing and paused are paused, cancelled and expired are gone.

The cohort is the customers running at the period's start. One is lost when
none of their commitments is running *or paused* at the anchor. Recurring
churn is lost over the cohort. Cancelling one of three schedules is not a
lost customer, replacing one schedule or plan with another is not churn, and
a pause is not churn. The rows list each lost customer, how their last
commitment ended (cancelled, lapsed, expired) and when. Bills, expenses and
journal schedules are costs, not customers, and are not counted.

*Monthly activity churn* is over paying customers, in the trailing window.
For each customer, from their positive receipts before the anchor:

- their *rhythm* is the gap between receipts they are expected to keep: the
  cadence of their latest receipt's invoice when it is known --- a billing
  price interval (31 days a month, 366 a year) or the recurring schedule that
  generated it (monthly 31, weekly 7, daily 1, yearly 366) --- else the median
  gap between their receipts on distinct days (the upper median for an even
  count), else 31 days;
- their *quiet threshold* is =max(days, ceil(1.5 x rhythm))= when the rhythm
  is evidence (a known cadence, or at least two receipts), else =days=;
- they are *covered* until their latest receipt plus their rhythm;
- they *churned* at their latest receipt plus their threshold, if that is
  before the anchor.

A *churn event* is a customer whose churn moment falls inside the window. A
customer is *at risk* in a month of the window when, at its first instant,
they had paid before (first receipt earlier) and were still covered. Monthly
activity churn is =events / customer-months at risk=. A customer who went
quiet and paid again is judged by their latest receipt, so only a final
departure is an event.

Worked through: fifty monthly customers, one leaving and one joining every
month, is 12 events over 600 customer-months: 2.00% a month. The first
version divided customers quiet at the end by everyone who paid in the year
times twelve (1.25%). Twelve annual customers renewing one a month is no
churn at all; the first version's single 90-day threshold called nine of
them churned.

Metrics: =active_at_start=, =churned= and =recurring_churn=;
=active_customers= (paid in the window), =inactive_customers= (churn events),
=customer_months= (at risk), =activity_churn= and =activity_churn_bps=;
=days=. The rows list each churn event with the date they last paid.

*Refusals.* No customer running at the start, or both the recurring and
billing modules off, makes recurring churn =n/a=. No customer-month at risk
makes activity churn =n/a=.

The billing module's =churn= report, titled /Subscription churn (billing)/,
answers a different question --- MRR and logos lost from an opening
subscription cohort --- and is described in [[file:billing.org][SaaS billing]].

** =ltv=: what a customer is worth

*Realised* lifetime value is each customer's cash before the anchor,
receipts less refunds. The report gives the mean, the median (the middle
customer, or the average of the two middle ones), the total, and the ten
largest customers as rows.

*Projected* lifetime value is
=revenue per customer-month x gross margin / monthly activity churn=, all
over the trailing window:

- *Revenue per customer-month* (=arpu=) is cash revenue in the window over
  the customer-months it was earned in. A receipt on a yearly cadence is
  split evenly across the twelve months starting with the one it arrived
  in; every other receipt belongs to its own month; a refund is spread like
  the receipt it refunds. Only the parts inside the window count, so an
  annual receipt from before the window still contributes its later months.
  A customer-month is one customer in one month of the window with a
  positive part that made it into the total --- a part left out as another
  currency adds no customer-month. Twelve annual customers at $1,200 are
  $14,400 over 144 customer-months: $100 (the first version said $1,200).
- *Gross margin* is =(revenue - cost of revenue) / revenue=. Revenue is the
  =pnl= report's net revenue over the window, scoped as this report is.
  Cost of revenue is every expense and vendor bill line flagged
  =cost_of_revenue= and dated in the window (bill lines costed as for CAC),
  plus the attributed support cost. A row flagged =acquisition=, or naming a
  campaign, is never cost of revenue: that spend is what CAC divides, and
  counting it here too charged it twice. Nothing else on the P&L ---
  salaries, rent, advertising --- is cost of revenue unless flagged. The
  =cost_of_revenue= flag is set like =acquisition=, and a =tax_category=
  marked =cost_of_revenue= defaults it on for new rows. With nothing flagged
  and no support cost the margin is 100%, and a note says so.
- *Support cost* is every =worklog= hour in the window on a ticket that
  names a company, priced at the organisation's =hourly_rate=, attributed to
  that customer (the table shows each customer's share). Hours are stored as
  a double; they are rounded once to hundredths and everything after is
  exact money times a rational. With no rate, support costs nothing, and the
  note says so. Do not also flag the salaries of the same support staff as
  cost of revenue, or they are counted twice.
- *Monthly activity churn* is as =customer_churn= defines it. Dividing by it
  is multiplying by =customer-months at risk / events=, one exact rational.

Worked through: revenue $120,000, hosting $20,000 flagged cost of revenue,
advertising $40,000 flagged acquisition, salaries and rent $70,000. Gross
margin is 83.33%. The first version subtracted every expense and got
-8.33%, a negative LTV and a negative LTV:CAC.

Metrics: =customers=, =realised_total=, =realised_mean=, =realised_median=,
=projected=, =arpu=, =revenue_12m=, =gross_margin= and =gross_margin_bps=,
=cost_of_revenue=, =monthly_churn=, =churn_events=, =risk_months=,
=customer_months= (of revenue) and =support_cost=.

*Refusals.* Below =minimum_customer_months= (default 12) customer-months of
revenue the projection is =insufficient data=, with the count and the
minimum in the note --- a lifetime projected from two customer-months is a
guess with a currency symbol on it. No P&L revenue in the book currency,
revenue at or below zero, or a margin at or below zero means =n/a= for the
margin and the projection, with the reason: a lifetime value projected from
a negative margin reads as a precise loss per customer rather than as the
absence of an answer. No churn event means an unbounded lifetime and =n/a=
--- good news, but not a number.

** =ltv_cac=: whether it is worth it

- =ltv_cac= is projected LTV over CAC for the same period, to hundredths
  rounded half to even, both sides being in the same currency. Whenever
  either side is =n/a= or =insufficient data=, so is the ratio, and the note
  says which. Sides in different currencies are refused rather than
  converted. =projected_ltv= and =cac= are shown beside it.
- =arpa= is average revenue per account per month. With billing in use (the
  module on and the organisation having subscription history) it is the
  billing =mrr= report's ARPA: contracted MRR at the period's end over the
  companies with positive MRR. Otherwise it is revenue per customer-month
  from =ltv=: the trailing twelve months' cash per paying customer per month.
  The note says which.
- =payback_months= is =CAC / (ARPA x gross margin)=: the months of gross
  profit a new customer takes to repay what acquiring them cost, to
  hundredths rounded half to even. It is =n/a= when CAC, ARPA or the margin
  is unknown, the margin is at or below zero, or they are in different
  currencies --- a customer who never repays has no payback period, not a
  very long one.

** =customer_cohorts=: how long customers stay

A cohort is the companies whose first cash fell in the period, grouped by
the calendar month of that receipt (in UTC, like every calendar date). Month /k/ of
a cohort (month 0 being its own) is the share of the cohort still paying: a
positive receipt covers that calendar month, received in it or on a yearly
cadence in the eleven months before. The period only chooses the cohorts:
they are followed up to now, or =as_of=, so last quarter's cohorts show how
they have done since. Months that have not started by then are blank, not
zero. =month_1=, =month_3=, =month_6= and =month_12= pool every cohort that
has reached that month: the companies still paying over the companies in
those cohorts; =n/a= when no cohort has.

* The cuts of the P&L

The =pnl_cuts= module adds the four cuts Ben runs the business on, as
ordinary registry reports: =revenue_by_customer=, =spend_by_vendor=,
=recurring_costs= and =cash_outlook=. They serve the P&L question of
issue #75 --- where the money comes from, where it goes, what it goes on
every month, and what the bank will look like in a few weeks --- and they
hang off the P&L card at =/= as its links. The module requires
=receivables= and =payables=; with =leads= off sources come from the
company's own field, with =recurring= off the run-rate is empty, with
=banking= off the outlook has no opening balance. Each case says so in a
note rather than showing a zero.

The three rules of the headline reports hold here too: every total is a
=VentureMoney= in the organisation's book currency, with rows in another
currency kept on their own row in their own currency and counted in a note
rather than converted; every ratio is an exact rational; and nothing is
invented --- an undated bill is listed as undated, a schedule with no
price in its template is listed unpriced.

All four accept =organization_id=. =revenue_by_customer= and
=spend_by_vendor= accept =by=; =cash_outlook= accepts =weeks=.

** =revenue_by_customer=: where the money comes from

Two figures per customer for the period, largest paid first.

*Paid* is what the customer actually handed over in the period: receipts
applied to their invoices (=payment_allocation= rows funded by a payment,
not by a credit) less refunds dated in the period. A credit note applied
to an invoice is not cash and does not count as paid.

*Issued* is what was invoiced in the period: the amount frozen on each
invoice's =issue= event dated in the period, less credit notes and
write-offs dated in the period, which are what was issued and then given
back or given up without cash moving. A voided invoice was never revenue
and is left out whenever it was issued; a draft has not been issued yet.

Each customer row shows its *source*: the =source= of the lead that
converted into the company (the first converted lead, as =cac= counts it),
else the company's own =source= field, else =(unattributed)=. =by=source=
groups the same figures by that source instead, so the report answers
"which channel pays" as readily as "who pays". The metrics are the paid
and issued totals and the number of customers with any movement.

*Refusals.* A =by= that is neither =customer= nor =source= is refused
with the choices named; falling back silently would answer a different
question. A customer who pays in two currencies gets two rows, one each.

** =spend_by_vendor=: where the money goes

Bills and expenses per vendor for the period, largest total first, with
the *acquisition* share beside the rest so the spend that CAC divides is
visible in the same table as the spend it does not.

*Bills* are the lines of vendor bills dated in the period that are past
draft and not void --- approved, part paid or paid --- valued as the
payables module values them (quantity times unit price plus tax), and
attributed to the bill's vendor company. *Expenses* are expenses dated in
the period, attributed to their free-text =vendor=, or =(no vendor)=.

Each row shows bills, expenses, the total, and that total split into
*acquisition* (the =acquisition= flag on the expense or bill line) and
*other*. =by=category= groups by the expense's or bill line's =category=
instead, =(uncategorised)= when it has none. The metrics are the five
totals.

*Refusals.* A =by= that is neither =vendor= nor =category= is refused.
With =payables= off only expenses are counted, and the note says so.

** =recurring_costs=: what it costs every month

Every running recurring schedule of kind =bill= or =expense=, priced from
its template the way the recurring service would read it --- the template
is loaded into an unsaved record of the document's own type, so an expense
template's =amount= and a bill template's lines (quantity times unit
price plus tax) are valued by the same code that would generate them ---
and scaled to a month: monthly as is, weekly times 52/12, daily times
365/12, yearly divided by 12, each one exact rational multiplication. The
row shows the schedule, its document kind, the vendor (the bill template's
company, or the expense template's vendor), the frequency, the amount per
occurrence, the amount per month and the next due date (=next_run_at=).
The metrics are the monthly run-rate, the annual run-rate (twelve times
it), the number of schedules counted and the number excluded.

*Excluded* are schedules that are cancelled (deleted), paused, or whose
=end_at= day has gone by. =end_at= is the /inclusive/ last occurrence
date, the way the recurring service reads it, so a schedule ending today
is still a cost today. Invoice schedules are revenue and journal
schedules are bookkeeping; neither is a cost and neither is counted or
excluded.

*Refusals.* A schedule whose template carries no price --- no =amount=,
no lines, a line without a unit price, lines in two currencies --- is
listed with no figure and counted in a note, never as zero. A schedule in
another currency keeps its row and is left out of the totals, with the
note saying so. With =recurring= off the run-rate is empty and says so.

** =cash_outlook=: what the bank will look like

The next =weeks= weeks (default 8, at most 104) of expected cash,
starting where the period does: today for =today= or =this_week=, the
first of the month for =2026-04=. All-time has no start, so the outlook
builds its own the way every period boundary is built --- the configured
calendar decides /which/ day it is, and the boundary itself is midnight
UTC, because that is how a due date is stored. Built at local midnight it
would sit hours after the due dates that share its day, and west of UTC
everything due on the start day would read as overdue.

*Cash out* is what is still owed on every approved or part-paid vendor
bill, by its due date, valued by the payables service (issued less paid
plus refunded). Drafts are not commitments; paid and void bills are
history. *Cash in* is what is still owed on every sent or part-paid
invoice, by its due date, valued by the settlement service.

The rows are *Overdue* (due before the start), *Week 1* to *Week N* (seven
days each, the *from* and *to* columns being the first and last day of
the week in the organisation's timezone), *Later* (due after the horizon)
and *Undated*. Each carries cash in, cash out and the net. The *opening*
figure is cash in the bank --- the sum of every bank account's last
statement balance, the same figure the P&L card shows --- and the
*balance* column runs it forward through Overdue and the weeks; the
*closing* metric is the balance after week N. The expected-in and
expected-out metrics cover the same rows.

*Refusals.* An undated invoice or bill is listed on the Undated row and
counted in a note; nothing is assumed about when it will be paid and it is
not in the projected balance. Later is shown but not projected either. No
statement balance means no opening figure, no running balance and a
closing of =n/a=, with the flows still shown; a bank balance in a
different currency from the book is likewise not run forward. =weeks=
below 1 or above 104 is refused. Amounts in another currency are counted
and noted, never added.

The name is =cash_outlook= rather than =cash_forecast= because the
=budgets= module already owns a =cash_forecast= --- the budget-driven one
over ledger balances --- and registering a second report under the same
name would replace it.
* Customer health: the list behind the churn number

Churn is a lagging number: by the time it moves, the customer has gone.
The =customer_health= module asks the same question early, per company,
so the answer is a list you can act on rather than a percentage you can
only regret. It requires =headline= and =activities=; =tickets=,
=dunning= and =mail_sync= make it better and are not required.

** =customer_health=: who is about to leave

One row per company of kind =customer=, worst first. Nothing is stored:
every row is read from the records the other modules already keep, as of
a moment --- the period's end, now if the period is still running, or an
explicit =as_of= --- against the thresholds the organisation has *now*.
A stored band would be wrong the morning after a threshold changed.

| Column              | What it is                                                         |
|---------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =owner=             | the username of the company's =owner_user_id=; blank when unowned  |
| =band=              | =green=, =amber= or =red=                                          |
| =last_touch=        | the latest interaction, completed activity or inbound mail (through the contact it was matched to), on or before the moment |
| =days_since_touch=  | days since then; since the company was added, if there has never been one |
| =open_deals=        | deals neither won nor lost                                         |
| =overdue_invoices=  | issued, unsettled invoices past their due date; undated is not overdue |
| =overdue_days=      | how far past due the oldest of them is                             |
| =open_tickets=      | tickets neither done nor cancelled; zero with =tickets= off        |
| =sla_breaches=      | of those, how many have missed a service level                     |
| =dunning_step=      | the furthest reminder step reached on any invoice still open; a step on an invoice since paid is history, not health |
| =revenue_12m=       | applied receipts less refunds in the twelve months before the moment, in the book currency, exactly as =ltv= counts them; another currency is left out, never converted |

*The three reasons.* A company is *quiet* when =days_since_touch= reaches
the touch threshold; *behind* when =overdue_days= reaches the overdue
threshold; *struggling* when =open_tickets= reaches the ticket threshold
or any open ticket has missed its service level. No reason is green, one
is amber, two or three are red. Each reason is written out in words
whether or not it counts, so a company page says where it stands on all
three, not only the ones that tripped.

*Thresholds* are three fields on the organisation's =headline_setting=
row, zero meaning the default: =health_touch_days= (30),
=health_overdue_days= (15) and =health_open_tickets= (3).

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report customer_health this_month
venturectl report customer_health this_month band=red          # the at-risk list
venturectl report customer_health this_month owner=alice sort=-overdue_days
venturectl update headline_setting 1 health_touch_days=45 health_open_tickets=5
#+end_src

=band= keeps one band; =owner= keeps one account owner; =sort= names a
column, with a leading minus for descending (=sort=-revenue_12m=). A band
or column that does not exist is refused rather than ignored. The
=red=, =amber=, =green= and =customers= metrics always count the whole
organisation, filtered or not, so the churn card and a filtered page
cannot disagree about how many are at risk. The report page carries a
filter row for the three options.

** The sweep: red becomes somebody's next action

#+begin_src sh
venturectl customers health-sweep [as_of=DATE] [organization_id=N] [limit=N]
curl -X POST '.../api/v1/customers/health/sweep?organization_id=1&limit=50'
#+end_src

For each red company the sweep creates one planned =activity= ---
=check in: <company>=, a high-priority follow-up for the account owner,
due at =as_of=, related to the company, with the three reasons in its
body. A company that already has a planned check-in is left alone, so
running the sweep twice creates nothing the second time; once that
check-in is done or cancelled, the next sweep may raise another if the
company is still red. The sweep is bounded (=limit=, default 100, at most
1000 red companies visited), synchronous --- there is no background
thread --- and one transaction: a save refused part-way through rolls
back every check-in the sweep made, so a rerun starts clean. It answers
={"created": N}=. An unowned company still gets its
check-in, with no owner, so it appears on the activity list rather than
nowhere. The route needs the editor role and, like every whole-organisation
action, a working membership (owner, admin, editor, finance or sales) in
the organisation it names; it is refused when =customer_health= or
=activities= is off.

** Where it shows

The churn card on the home page carries an *At risk* line --- the red
count, as of the period's end, linking to the report filtered to red.
A customer's own page shows its band and the three reasons under
*Health*. See [[file:dashboards.org][Dashboards]].
* The money calendar

The =money_calendar= module adds =money_calendar=: every dated money event
in the period (recurring schedules expanded forward, bills and invoices on
their due dates with overdue ones carried to =as_of=, dunning steps,
payroll runs, tax packs) with per-day and per-ISO-week nets per currency,
never folded across them. It accepts =from= and =to= as an explicit span,
=as_of=, =kind=, =customer_id= or =vendor_id=, =venture_id= and
=currency=. The same events feed =/money/calendar= and
=/money/calendar.ics=; see [[file:money-calendar.org][the money calendar]].
* The support rollup

=support_rollup= answers question 5 of issue #75 --- how do I support
existing customers --- with a number per customer rather than a list of
tickets. It is an ordinary registry report of the =tickets= module, so it
has the report page, =GET /api/v1/reports/support_rollup=, the CSV export,
=venturectl report support_rollup= and =venturectl support rollup=, and a
=metric=, =report= or =chart= widget can read it. The Support card on the
home page and the Support block on a company's page read their figure from
the same function, =venture_support_rollup_cost()=, which runs this report
and hands back its =support_cost= metric, so the card, the page and the
table cannot disagree.

** The rows

One row per company, for the tickets *raised* in the period --- the same
rule as the =support= report: "the tickets of March" is what somebody
means. A ticket that names no company is counted under =(no company)=, so
the total is the whole desk. Each row has:

- =tickets= raised, =closed= (done or cancelled) and =open= now;
- =breaches=: tickets the service-level sweep marked breached;
- =response= and =resolution=: the *median* whole minutes from the ticket
  being raised to its first visible reply and to its resolution, over the
  tickets that have one (the middle sample, or the mean of the two middle
  ones rounded half up). A median rather than the =support= report's mean
  because one ticket left open for a month should not move a customer's
  figure by weeks; blank when no ticket has one;
- =csat=: the share of ratings that were =good=, over the ratings given,
  exactly as the =support= report scores it; blank when nobody rated;
- =minutes=: agent minutes from the worklogs on those tickets, each worklog
  rounded once to whole minutes;
- =support_cost=: what those tickets cost, below.

The figures above the table are the same over the whole scope, with
=companies= (or =products=) counting the rows listed.

** What it costs

Two integer rates on the organisation's =headline_setting= row, in whole
units of the book currency:

| Field                 | Means                                                    | Zero means                                            |
|-----------------------+----------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| =support_hourly_rate= | the cost of one agent hour logged on tickets             | the worklog =hourly_rate= (money) the LTV margin uses |
| =support_ticket_rate= | a flat cost for a ticket with no logged minutes          | such tickets cost nothing                             |

A row's cost is its minutes at the hourly rate --- =rate x minutes / 60=,
multiplied then divided once, rounded half to even --- plus the per-ticket
rate for each of its tickets with no minutes, and the total is the sum of
the rows. With neither rate the cost is =n/a= and the note says which
field to set; a desk that logs no time sets the per-ticket rate and gets a
number, a desk that logs time sets the hourly one. A worklog =hourly_rate=
in a currency other than the book currency is not converted: it is left
out and the note says so. Nothing is stored: the figure is computed from
the tickets and worklogs at the moment of asking.

** Options

- =sort=[-]column=: any column key, =-= for descending; the default is
  =-support_cost=. A column that does not exist is refused, as is any
  option this report does not take.
- =min_tickets=N=: list only rows with at least =N= tickets. The figures
  above the table still cover every row, so hiding small customers never
  changes the total; the note says how many were hidden.
- =company=ID=: one company's tickets only. The total is then that
  company's cost, which is what its page shows.
- =product=NAME=: only tickets about that product.
- =group_by=product=: one row per product instead of per company, the same
  columns, with tickets that name no product under =(no product)=.

A ticket's product is a custom field called =product= on the ticket (the
=custom_fields= module; =venturectl fields define ticket product string=),
or a =product_id= field when the ticket type has one. The core ticket has
neither, so without the custom field the product breakdown is one row and
a note saying how to add it.

=organization_id=, =venture_id= and =as_of= scope it as they do every
report. The reads are gated as every report is: the viewer role over the
API and the report page, and with the =tickets= module off the report is
not registered, so asking for it is a 404 rather than an empty table.

** Organization mail readiness

A report pack's email delivery prepares its actual organization transport
before enqueueing retained output. An unconfigured or revoked binding records
a delivery error while keeping the completed report run. A prepared SMTP
client is also supported; having no legacy installation configuration does
not make that client unconfigured. No SMTP submission occurs during this
preflight. The outbox resolves and durably binds the current account at the
first real delivery attempt, so queued reports still honor later rotation
or disconnect. The pack's delivery stamp records acceptance into the outbox,
not relay acceptance or arrival in a recipient's inbox.
