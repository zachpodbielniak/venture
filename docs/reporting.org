* Reporting

A report turns a period and some options into headline metrics and a table of
rows. =VentureReportResult= is the single shape every report produces, which
is what lets the web UI, the CLI, the AI and the CSV exporter render any
report — including one a plugin added this morning — without knowing anything
about it.

* The built-in reports

| Name         | Answers                                                      |
|--------------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
| =pnl=        | revenue, expenses, profit; deductible shown separately       |
| =ventures=   | sales, revenue, expenses and profit per venture              |
| =categories= | sales grouped by a product field; genre by default           |
| =inventory=  | quantity on hand and carrying value; reorder flags           |
| =tax=        | expenses by category with deductible amounts                 |
| =tax_liability= | frozen invoice and bill tax grouped by tax code            |
| =campaigns=  | spend, attributed revenue, return                            |
| =pipeline=   | open deals by stage, probability-weighted                    |
| =monthly=    | revenue, expenses and profit month by month                  |
| =ideas=      | ideas ranked by opportunity and confidence against effort    |
| =receivables= | outstanding invoices bucketed by how far past due           |
| =releases=   | releases out in the period, with tickets, builds, deployments |
| =lead_time=  | days from a ticket being raised to its release going out     |
| =incidents=  | incidents in the period, hours to resolve, the mean          |
| =cac=        | acquisition spend over customers with a first cash receipt; =n/a= if none |
| =customer_churn= | recurring churn in customers and monthly activity churn (=days=) |

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report                       # list them
venturectl report pnl this_quarter
venturectl report categories fy         # by genre over the fiscal year
curl '.../api/v1/reports/tax?period=2026&format=csv'
#+end_src

=categories= takes =group_by=, so the same report answers "how did each genre
do", "how did each format do" and "how did each product do".

** What =receivables= ages against

The end of the period, or now, whichever is earlier.

For a period that has closed those are the same question and the end date
is the honest one: a receivables report for last month should age against
31 August rather than against whenever somebody happened to run it.

For the period you are standing in they are not. Its end date is in the
future, and aging against a date that has not happened yet reports invoices
as overdue before they are due --- run =report receivables this_month= on 3
September anchored to 30 September and an invoice due on the 13th lands in
the =1-30 days= bucket and joins the overdue total. That total is the
figure somebody chases a customer over, so it says debt, not forecast.

Invoices with no due date are their own bucket rather than overdue: undated
is unknown, not late. Only =sent= invoices count at all --- a draft is still
being written, and paid or void ones are settled history.

* Design notes

Reports aggregate in C wherever money is involved. A =SUM= over mixed
currencies would produce a confidently wrong number, so totals are carried in
the majority currency of the records and exclude — rather than convert — the
minority ones. An exclusion is never silent: any report whose totals had to
leave amounts out says how many in a note attached to the result.

A figure is never presented as more complete than it is. The P&L shows the
deductible total beside the cash total and says why they differ; the tax
report counts unreviewed expenses as zero and says how many are waiting.

Metrics carry a direction. =venture_metric_set_higher_is_better(metric, FALSE)=
on a cost or an awaiting-review count means the UI colours a rise red rather
than green.

Every output format comes from one structure, so a report is written once and
appears correctly in the browser, the terminal, a CSV export and an org file.
CSV escaping defuses a leading =​== that a spreadsheet would otherwise execute
as a formula; HTML output escapes its content.

** Periods and calendar dates

A date typed without a time (=2026-03-01=, or =today=) is a calendar date, stored as midnight UTC on that day by =venture_time_from_string()=. Every period boundary =venture_date_range_parse()= builds is a midnight UTC for the same reason, whatever =locale.timezone= says; the configured zone only decides what /today/, /this month/ and /this year/ are, so at nine in the evening on 31 March in New York =this_month= is still March. The consequence is the one that matters in a ledger: an expense dated 1 March is in March's P&L, and one dated 1 January is in the same year as the tax filing, whose years were always UTC.

Built in the configured zone instead, as periods once were, 1 March sat five hours before March began in New York, so every document dated the first of a month was reported in the month before. =/report/period-boundaries-are-calendar-dates= and =/headline/timezone/bare-date= pin this.

A true instant -- a payment recorded from a webhook at 21:00 on the last evening of a month in New York -- is judged against the same UTC boundaries and so falls in the next month. That is the smaller error of the two: it touches only events stamped in the last few hours of a period, where the old rule moved every calendar date on the first of one.

=venture_time_to_date_string()= shows a value at exactly midnight UTC as the calendar date it is, in any zone, and converts anything else to the zone it is given. A list page rendered in New York used to show 1 March as 28 February while the record page showed 1 March.

* Writing one

A report is a function:

#+begin_src C
static VentureReportResult *
my_report(VentureContext    *context,
          VentureDateRange  *period,
          JsonObject        *options,
          GError           **error)
{
	g_autoptr(VentureReportResult) result = NULL;

	result = venture_report_result_new("My report", period);

	venture_report_result_add_metric(result,
		venture_metric_new_money("total", "Total", amount));

	venture_report_result_add_column(result, "name", "Name",
	                                 VENTURE_REPORT_COLUMN_TEXT);
	venture_report_result_add_column(result, "value", "Value",
	                                 VENTURE_REPORT_COLUMN_MONEY);

	venture_report_result_begin_row(result);
	venture_report_result_set_text(result, "name", "A row");
	venture_report_result_set_money(result, "value", amount);

	return g_steal_pointer(&result);
}
#+end_src

Register it:

#+begin_src C
venture_report_registry_add(registry,
	VENTURE_REPORT(venture_func_report_new("mine", "My report",
	                                       "What it answers", my_report)));
#+end_src

It now appears in the CLI, the web UI, the REST API and the AI's report tool.

A plugin needing state can subclass =VentureReport= instead and override
=generate=; both end up in the same registry and are indistinguishable to
every consumer. Registering a name that already exists replaces it, so a
plugin can deliberately override a built-in with a better version for its
data.

* Sales pipeline analysis

The =pipelines= module adds =stage_duration=, =funnel=, =forecast=,
=loss_reasons= and the =overdue_deals= dashboard metric. All accept
=organization_id=, =pipeline_id= and =owner=. The existing =pipeline= report
also accepts =pipeline_id=. Definitions and currency separation are described
in [[file:pipelines.org][Sales pipelines]].
* Ledger financial statements

The =statements= module adds =balance_sheet=, =income_statement=, =cash_flow=,
=general_ledger=, =account_balances= and =pnl_reconciliation=. These read posted
ledger evidence per exact legal entity and currency; they never choose a
majority currency. All accept =as_of= and =compare_to=, and general ledger also
accepts =account_id=. They use the normal report pages, CSV export and
financial close snapshots. See [[file:statements.org][definitions and the indirect cash-flow method]].
The older =pnl= remains operational sales/expenses reporting; differences are
explained by source document in =pnl_reconciliation=.

* Saved reports and dimensions

=saved_report= stores report name, period, JSON options and an optional
dimension. Execution always scopes =organization_id= from the saved row and
passes =dimension= into the shared ledger query so departmental packs do not
see another team's postings. =report_pack= groups saved reports for a
schedule. =venture_report_pack_service_run_due= dispatches due packs
(cron-like =0 8 * * *= daily, =0 8 1 * *= monthly) and stamps
=last-run-at=. Automation: =venture->report_packs_run("1")=.

* Scheduled report packs

Schedules accept =daily= or five space-separated numeric/=*= fields: minute,
hour, day of month, month, weekday (0 or 7 is Sunday). Evaluation uses UTC.
Unsupported expressions are refused instead of interpreted as daily. When
both day fields are specified, either may match. A delayed sweep catches up
once on the scheduled day; it does not replay missed historical days.

A successful run stores its result array in =report_pack.last_output= and
updates =last_run_at= in the same record save. Read that JSON through the
generic record API or CLI. A failed run preserves the previous output and
timestamp. No email delivery or historical output archive is implied.

A report pack may reference only saved reports in its own organization. The runner validates this boundary before generating each result, including scheduled packs; foreign saved-report ids fail instead of copying another organization's report into the pack output.

* The headline numbers

The =headline= module adds the numbers the business is run on, as ordinary
registry reports: =cac=, =customer_churn=, =ltv=, =ltv_cac= and
=customer_cohorts=. They serve the questions of issue #75: what a customer
costs, who is leaving, what a customer is worth, whether the first is worth
paying for the third, and how long customers stay. Each is written in plain
words here, refusals included, because a number whose formula you cannot say
is not one you can run a business on.

** Rules that hold across all of them

- *Currency.* Every amount is a =VentureMoney= carried in the organisation's
  book currency (=default-currency=, or the process default). An amount in
  another currency is counted, noted and left out, never converted.
- *Ratios.* Every ratio is integer arithmetic in basis points, rounded once,
  half to even, and becomes a fraction only when rendered: two of three is
  6,667 basis points (66.67%), not the 6,666 truncation gives. LTV:CAC and
  CAC payback are kept to hundredths the same way.
- *Refusals.* Every division names its zero and answers =n/a= rather than
  dividing. =n/a= is a text metric, so a dashboard cannot mistake it for 0.
- *The anchor.* A trailing window is measured back from the anchor: the
  period's end, now, or the =as_of= cutoff, whichever is earliest --- the
  same anchor the receivables aging uses. A period still in progress has an
  end that has not happened yet.
- *The trailing window* is the twelve months before the anchor, cut into
  twelve months counted back from it (=anchor - 12 months= to =anchor - 11
  months=, and so on). For a calendar month period these are calendar months.
- *Scope.* All accept =organization_id=, =venture_id= and =as_of=.
  =venture_id= keeps only cash sales, expenses, vendor bills, campaigns,
  leads, tickets, schedules (the =venture_id= in their template) and
  subscriptions (their plan's venture) of that venture; rows with no
  venture are then left out, as the P&L does. =as_of= hides deletions made
  after it and every dated movement after it. Both are passed to the =pnl=
  report the gross margin is read from; the billing reports ARPA and the
  home page read from take =as_of= but are organisation-wide, not per
  venture.
- *One read.* The reports read their records once, through a snapshot, and
  derive every period from it; the home page asks for two periods of four
  reports from one set of queries.

A =headline_setting= row per organisation tunes them: =hourly_rate= (what a
logged support hour costs), =minimum_customer_months= (zero means 12) and
=activity_days= (zero means 90). =customer_churn= and =ltv= accept =days= to
override the last. The save refuses a second living row for the same
organisation, on every save --- moving a row to an organisation that already
has one is refused too --- and migration =000350= backs this with a unique
partial index, because a restore does not run save validators. That
migration keeps the lowest-id living row of any existing duplicates (the row
every reader already used) and soft-deletes the rest.

** Cash: what a customer paid

Cash is decided by the settlement service, not reconstructed here. The
service derives a cash =sale= whenever money a customer paid is applied to
one of their invoices, and a negative cash adjustment whenever an applied
receipt is refunded. So:

- a =payment_allocation= that names a sale is a receipt, and a =refund= that
  names a sale is a refund; the amount is that sale's =gross=, which for a
  foreign invoice paid in book currency is the book-currency cash;
- a deposit or overpayment is not cash until it is applied to an invoice,
  and then each application is a receipt on the day it was applied;
- a credit note or write-off applied to an invoice is not cash, and the
  refund of unapplied money is not a refund of cash (it was never counted).

Worked through: a $500 invoice paid with $600, $100 of it refunded, is $500
of cash. A $6,000 retainer applied to twelve $500 invoices is twelve $500
receipts. The receipt belongs to the invoice's company; a refund to the
refund's customer. The first version counted allocations funded by a payment
and subtracted every refund: the first example read $400, and the retainer
customer had no cash at all.

A company's *first cash* is its earliest positive receipt. That is when it
became a customer --- not when an invoice was marked paid, which a write-off
also does and a later refund undoes.

** =cac=: what a new customer cost

*Acquisition spend* in the period is:

- every expense and every vendor bill line flagged =acquisition=, or naming a
  =campaign_id=, dated in the period. A bill line counts at its bill's date,
  drafts and voids excluded, and costs its net plus any tax that cannot be
  recovered (the tax code's =recoverable= flag; tax with no code counts). The
  cash expenses the payables service projects from a paid bill line
  (external id =bill_line:...= or =bill_cash:...=) are never counted: the
  line already was, at its bill date.
- each outreach campaign's own =spend=, spread evenly over the time it ran,
  =[started_at, ended_at)= or =[started_at, now)= while it has no end, by
  VentureMoney allocation so consecutive periods sum back to the spend. A
  campaign with no duration belongs whole to the period of its start. Only
  campaigns no expense or bill line names are counted this way: once a
  campaign's costs are recorded as rows, its =spend= figure is a summary of
  the same money. A campaign still running is re-spread every day, so its
  earlier months' shares shrink as it runs on.

The =acquisition= flag is an ordinary boolean on =expense= and
=vendor_bill_line=; a =tax_category= marked =acquisition= turns it on for
new expenses and bill lines filed under it, and only for new ones ---
changing a category later does not rewrite what was already spent. The
=campaign_id= reference on both, and the =cost_of_revenue= flags described
under =ltv=, are ordinary fields too; migration =000351= checks each table
has its new columns (or, for a disabled module, none) and backfills nothing:
no historical row is inferred to be cost of revenue or a campaign's cost.

A *new customer* is a company whose first cash fell in the period. A company
paying in instalments is new at its first. =CAC = acquisition spend / new
customers=, one rational multiplication rounded half to even.

Metrics: =spend=, =campaign_spend= (campaign figures and rows naming a
campaign), =expense_spend= (other flagged rows), =new_customers=, =cac=.

The table breaks the new customers down by the *campaign* and *source* of
the lead they were converted from (the first converted lead, in id order); a
company with no lead is attributed to its own =source=, or =(unattributed)=.
A campaign's spend in the period is split across its source rows in
proportion to the customers each brought, by VentureMoney allocation, and
each row shows its share and its own CAC. The first version gave every row
the whole campaign spend.

*Refusal.* Zero new customers reports the spend and =n/a= for CAC, with a
note. Spend with nobody to divide it by is a cost, not a cost per customer.

** =customer_churn=: who is leaving

Two measures, because a business has two kinds of customer.

*Recurring churn* counts customers, not schedules. A customer is recurring
at an instant when any of their commitments is running then:

- an invoice =recurring_schedule= (kind invoice, the company in its
  template) runs from =start_at= until the earlier of =end_at= and its
  deletion; a paused schedule is paused whatever the date, because the flag
  carries none;
- with billing on, a =customer_subscription= is what its latest
  =subscription_event= before the instant left it: active and past_due run,
  trialing and paused are paused, cancelled and expired are gone.

The cohort is the customers running at the period's start. One is lost when
none of their commitments is running *or paused* at the anchor. Recurring
churn is lost over the cohort. Cancelling one of three schedules is not a
lost customer, replacing one schedule or plan with another is not churn, and
a pause is not churn. The rows list each lost customer, how their last
commitment ended (cancelled, lapsed, expired) and when. Bills, expenses and
journal schedules are costs, not customers, and are not counted.

*Monthly activity churn* is over paying customers, in the trailing window.
For each customer, from their positive receipts before the anchor:

- their *rhythm* is the gap between receipts they are expected to keep: the
  cadence of their latest receipt's invoice when it is known --- a billing
  price interval (31 days a month, 366 a year) or the recurring schedule that
  generated it (monthly 31, weekly 7, daily 1, yearly 366) --- else the median
  gap between their receipts on distinct days (the upper median for an even
  count), else 31 days;
- their *quiet threshold* is =max(days, ceil(1.5 x rhythm))= when the rhythm
  is evidence (a known cadence, or at least two receipts), else =days=;
- they are *covered* until their latest receipt plus their rhythm;
- they *churned* at their latest receipt plus their threshold, if that is
  before the anchor.

A *churn event* is a customer whose churn moment falls inside the window. A
customer is *at risk* in a month of the window when, at its first instant,
they had paid before (first receipt earlier) and were still covered. Monthly
activity churn is =events / customer-months at risk=. A customer who went
quiet and paid again is judged by their latest receipt, so only a final
departure is an event.

Worked through: fifty monthly customers, one leaving and one joining every
month, is 12 events over 600 customer-months: 2.00% a month. The first
version divided customers quiet at the end by everyone who paid in the year
times twelve (1.25%). Twelve annual customers renewing one a month is no
churn at all; the first version's single 90-day threshold called nine of
them churned.

Metrics: =active_at_start=, =churned= and =recurring_churn=;
=active_customers= (paid in the window), =inactive_customers= (churn events),
=customer_months= (at risk), =activity_churn= and =activity_churn_bps=;
=days=. The rows list each churn event with the date they last paid.

*Refusals.* No customer running at the start, or both the recurring and
billing modules off, makes recurring churn =n/a=. No customer-month at risk
makes activity churn =n/a=.

The billing module's =churn= report, titled /Subscription churn (billing)/,
answers a different question --- MRR and logos lost from an opening
subscription cohort --- and is described in [[file:billing.org][SaaS billing]].

** =ltv=: what a customer is worth

*Realised* lifetime value is each customer's cash before the anchor,
receipts less refunds. The report gives the mean, the median (the middle
customer, or the average of the two middle ones), the total, and the ten
largest customers as rows.

*Projected* lifetime value is
=revenue per customer-month x gross margin / monthly activity churn=, all
over the trailing window:

- *Revenue per customer-month* (=arpu=) is cash revenue in the window over
  the customer-months it was earned in. A receipt on a yearly cadence is
  split evenly across the twelve months starting with the one it arrived
  in; every other receipt belongs to its own month; a refund is spread like
  the receipt it refunds. Only the parts inside the window count, so an
  annual receipt from before the window still contributes its later months.
  A customer-month is one customer in one month of the window with a
  positive part that made it into the total --- a part left out as another
  currency adds no customer-month. Twelve annual customers at $1,200 are
  $14,400 over 144 customer-months: $100 (the first version said $1,200).
- *Gross margin* is =(revenue - cost of revenue) / revenue=. Revenue is the
  =pnl= report's net revenue over the window, scoped as this report is.
  Cost of revenue is every expense and vendor bill line flagged
  =cost_of_revenue= and dated in the window (bill lines costed as for CAC),
  plus the attributed support cost. A row flagged =acquisition=, or naming a
  campaign, is never cost of revenue: that spend is what CAC divides, and
  counting it here too charged it twice. Nothing else on the P&L ---
  salaries, rent, advertising --- is cost of revenue unless flagged. The
  =cost_of_revenue= flag is set like =acquisition=, and a =tax_category=
  marked =cost_of_revenue= defaults it on for new rows. With nothing flagged
  and no support cost the margin is 100%, and a note says so.
- *Support cost* is every =worklog= hour in the window on a ticket that
  names a company, priced at the organisation's =hourly_rate=, attributed to
  that customer (the table shows each customer's share). Hours are stored as
  a double; they are rounded once to hundredths and everything after is
  exact money times a rational. With no rate, support costs nothing, and the
  note says so. Do not also flag the salaries of the same support staff as
  cost of revenue, or they are counted twice.
- *Monthly activity churn* is as =customer_churn= defines it. Dividing by it
  is multiplying by =customer-months at risk / events=, one exact rational.

Worked through: revenue $120,000, hosting $20,000 flagged cost of revenue,
advertising $40,000 flagged acquisition, salaries and rent $70,000. Gross
margin is 83.33%. The first version subtracted every expense and got
-8.33%, a negative LTV and a negative LTV:CAC.

Metrics: =customers=, =realised_total=, =realised_mean=, =realised_median=,
=projected=, =arpu=, =revenue_12m=, =gross_margin= and =gross_margin_bps=,
=cost_of_revenue=, =monthly_churn=, =churn_events=, =risk_months=,
=customer_months= (of revenue) and =support_cost=.

*Refusals.* Below =minimum_customer_months= (default 12) customer-months of
revenue the projection is =insufficient data=, with the count and the
minimum in the note --- a lifetime projected from two customer-months is a
guess with a currency symbol on it. No P&L revenue in the book currency,
revenue at or below zero, or a margin at or below zero means =n/a= for the
margin and the projection, with the reason: a lifetime value projected from
a negative margin reads as a precise loss per customer rather than as the
absence of an answer. No churn event means an unbounded lifetime and =n/a=
--- good news, but not a number.

** =ltv_cac=: whether it is worth it

- =ltv_cac= is projected LTV over CAC for the same period, to hundredths
  rounded half to even, both sides being in the same currency. Whenever
  either side is =n/a= or =insufficient data=, so is the ratio, and the note
  says which. Sides in different currencies are refused rather than
  converted. =projected_ltv= and =cac= are shown beside it.
- =arpa= is average revenue per account per month. With billing in use (the
  module on and the organisation having subscription history) it is the
  billing =mrr= report's ARPA: contracted MRR at the period's end over the
  companies with positive MRR. Otherwise it is revenue per customer-month
  from =ltv=: the trailing twelve months' cash per paying customer per month.
  The note says which.
- =payback_months= is =CAC / (ARPA x gross margin)=: the months of gross
  profit a new customer takes to repay what acquiring them cost, to
  hundredths rounded half to even. It is =n/a= when CAC, ARPA or the margin
  is unknown, the margin is at or below zero, or they are in different
  currencies --- a customer who never repays has no payback period, not a
  very long one.

** =customer_cohorts=: how long customers stay

A cohort is the companies whose first cash fell in the period, grouped by
the calendar month of that receipt (in UTC, like every calendar date). Month /k/ of
a cohort (month 0 being its own) is the share of the cohort still paying: a
positive receipt covers that calendar month, received in it or on a yearly
cadence in the eleven months before. The period only chooses the cohorts:
they are followed up to now, or =as_of=, so last quarter's cohorts show how
they have done since. Months that have not started by then are blank, not
zero. =month_1=, =month_3=, =month_6= and =month_12= pool every cohort that
has reached that month: the companies still paying over the companies in
those cohorts; =n/a= when no cohort has.
