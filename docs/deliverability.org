#+title: Deliverability: bounces, replies, the domain check, warm-up and honest numbers

What happens to campaign mail after your mail server accepts it, whether
your domain is set up to be believed, how fast a new domain may send, and
what each number on a send's report means. Everything here feeds the
evidence rules in [[file:marketing.org][Marketing]] ("Bounce and complaint evidence",
"Observations and report meaning"): there is one suppression list, one
reply rule and one report.

* Where bounces and complaints come from

Three doors, one rule. Each records evidence through
=venture_marketing_service_feedback()=, so a hard bounce or a complaint
suppresses the address and stops pending outreach, a temporary bounce is
recorded without suppression, and a replayed event is recorded once.

1. *An operator* uses a recipient's *Record delivery feedback* action, as
   before.
2. *A bounce in a synced mailbox.* When mail sync files a new message from
   a shared (not private) mailbox, it is read as a delivery status
   notification (RFC 3464, =multipart/report; report-type=delivery-status=)
   or an abuse report (RFC 5965, =report-type=feedback-report=). Only the
   machine-readable part decides, never the prose:
   | Action                    | Status  | Recorded as        |
   |---------------------------+---------+--------------------|
   | =failed=                  | 5.x.x   | hard bounce        |
   | =failed=                  | 4.x.x or none | temporary bounce |
   | =delayed=                 | any     | temporary bounce   |
   | =delivered=, =relayed=    | 2.x.x   | delivered          |
   | abuse report (=Feedback-Type: abuse=) | -- | complaint     |
   The message it is about is found by Message-ID: the per-recipient
   =Original-Message-ID= if the reporting server wrote one, else the
   returned message's own =Message-ID= (from its =message/rfc822= or
   =text/rfc822-headers= part), else the report's =In-Reply-To=. It must be
   a campaign message this organization's outbox sent, and the report's
   =Final-Recipient= must be the address it was sent to: a forwarded copy
   bouncing elsewhere is not evidence about the person you wrote to. The
   replay identity is the report's own Message-ID and the recipient.
3. *A signed provider feed.* =POST /hooks/deliverability/ORG/PROVIDER=, per
   organization, with no session. See below.

The evidence time is the event's own, kept between the message's
acceptance and now, so a provider clock running ahead, or a report dated
before the message existed, cannot place evidence where the rules refuse
it. Events about mail this organization did not send are /ignored/, not
refused, so a provider that posts everything does not see errors.

** The signed feed

Set the organization's signing secret on =/marketing/deliverability=
(*Make a signing secret*; it is shown once) or with
=POST /api/v1/marketing/feedback-secret= ={"organization_id": N}=
(optionally ="secret"=, 32 to 128 printable characters). Owners and
administrators only. It is sealed as an integration credential (provider
=marketing_feedback=), never shown again, and a new one replaces the old
at once. An organization with no secret refuses every delivery.

Each delivery carries:

- =X-Venture-Timestamp=: Unix seconds.
- =X-Venture-Signature=: hex HMAC-SHA256, keyed with the secret, over the
  timestamp, a newline (=\n=) and the exact body bytes. A =sha256= prefix
  (=sha256=...=) is accepted, the same shape outbound webhooks send.

A delivery is refused (401) when either header is missing or malformed,
the signature does not match (compared in constant time), or the timestamp
is more than 300 seconds from the server's clock -- the replay window.
A refused signature is logged as a warning naming the organization. Bodies
are capped at 256 KiB and 100 events.

The =generic= adapter reads this shape:

#+begin_src json
{"events": [
  {"id": "evt-1", "type": "bounce", "bounce": "hard",
   "message_id": "<message id as sent>", "recipient": "reader@example.net",
   "occurred_at": "2026-10-18T12:00:00Z", "status": "5.1.1",
   "detail": "550 5.1.1 mailbox does not exist"}
]}
#+end_src

=type= is =delivered=, =complaint= or =bounce= (with =bounce= =hard= or
=soft=). =id= is required and is the replay identity (scoped by
provider). =message_id= may keep or drop its angle brackets;
=recipient=, =occurred_at= (ISO 8601 with a zone), =status= and =detail=
are optional. The answer is ={"accepted": n, "ignored": n, "results":
[{"id", "result"}]}=, where =result= is =recorded= or why it was ignored.

*Adding a provider.* A provider with its own event shape is an adapter: a
=VentureDeliverabilityAdapter= (=id=, =label=, =parse=) registered with
=venture_deliverability_adapter_register()=. The signature check runs
before =parse= and is the same for every adapter, so an adapter only turns
a verified body into =VentureDeliverabilityEvent=s. Its id is the last
part of the feed address. Recorded example payloads live in
=tests/fixtures/deliverability/=.

* Replies

A campaign reply is counted the way sequences count one
(=venture_sequence_interaction_is_reply()=): an inbound interaction -- not
outbound -- for the contact or lead. Mail sync files a reply as exactly
that, matched to the contact by address, so there is no second matcher.
It counts once, for the most recent campaign message the relay accepted for
that person before the reply and within 30 days. Like the sequence rule,
any inbound interaction counts, a logged call back too; an out-of-office
answer from the person's address counts as well. An outbound interaction,
or one dated before the message was sent, never counts.

* The sending domain check

=/marketing/deliverability= (owners and administrators) and
=GET /api/v1/marketing/domain-health?organization_id=N&domain=D&selector=S=
check three DNS records and say, in plain words, what each means and what
to change:

| Check | Looked up at               | Pass                                   | Weak                                                | Missing                     |
|-------+----------------------------+----------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------|
| SPF   | the domain                 | one =v=spf1= ending =~all= or =-all=   | =+all=, =?all=, no =all=, or two SPF records         | no =v=spf1= record          |
| DKIM  | =SELECTOR._domainkey.DOMAIN= | a key (=p==) is published              | --                                                  | no key, an empty (revoked) key, or no selector set |
| DMARC | =_dmarc.DOMAIN=            | =p=quarantine= or =p=reject=, pct 100 | =p=none= (monitoring only), pct below 100, two records, no valid =p= | no =v=DMARC1= record |

A lookup DNS could not answer is =error= ("could not check"), not a
verdict about the domain. The overall result is the worst of the three.
Save the domain and its DKIM selector as a =marketing_sending_domain=
record and the page checks it every time it opens (at most ten domains).

DNS goes through an injectable resolver (=VentureDnsResolver=); the default
is GResolver's TXT lookup on a private main context with a five-second
deadline, so the server's own loop is not driven underneath a request.
Tests use a fake and never touch the network. *Limits:* this reads public
DNS only. It cannot see what your relay actually signs or whether SPF
covers the server that really sends; send yourself a test message and
look for =spf=pass=, =dkim=pass= and =dmarc=pass= in its
=Authentication-Results= header.

* Warm-up

Mailbox providers distrust a new domain that suddenly sends a lot, so
campaign delivery starts small and grows. The limit is per organization
and per UTC day: Venture sends an organization's campaign mail through its
one SMTP account. It counts campaign messages queued that day (cancelled
ones aside), across all its sends.

The default schedule (=VENTURE_DELIVERABILITY_DEFAULT_RAMP=):

| Day of sending | Most campaign messages that day |
|----------------+---------------------------------|
| 1-2            | 50                              |
| 3-4            | 100                             |
| 5-6            | 250                             |
| 7-9            | 500                             |
| 10-13          | 1,000                           |
| 14-20          | 2,500                           |
| 21-29          | 5,000                           |
| 30 on          | no limit                        |

Day one is the organization's first =marketing_sending_domain= record's
=warmup_started_on=, or, without one, the day its first campaign message
was accepted -- so an organization that has been sending through Venture
for a month is already past the warm-up, and one that has never sent
starts at day one. Set =ramp_schedule= on that record to change it:
=day:limit= pairs in increasing day order starting at day 1, each limit
holding until the next pair, 0 for no limit (=1:20,4:100,15:0=), or =off=.
A malformed schedule is refused when the record is saved.

*Execute bounded delivery* enforces it: once today's limit is reached the
send's next enqueue moves to the next UTC midnight and nobody is skipped;
the next run after midnight continues with the same recipient. The
per-send pacing interval (1-86,400 seconds, default 60) still applies on
top, so one send alone reaches at most 1,440 a day at the default.

* The send report and what each number means

=/marketing/sends/ID/report= and =GET /api/v1/marketing/sends/ID/report=
(owners, administrators, editors and sales members) show one send;
=marketing_performance= has the same numbers per send (pass
=send_id= in its options for one). They count people, once each:

| Number          | Counted from                                                                 |
|-----------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Accepted        | the outbox message is =sent=: your relay took it. Not proof of an inbox.      |
| Delivered       | a provider or DSN said the receiving server accepted it (only when one tells us) |
| Opened          | a tracked open not judged a machine's (=opened_at=)                         |
| Machine opens   | the recipient's only opens were judged a machine's (=machine_opened_at=)    |
| Clicked         | a tracked click not judged a machine's                                      |
| Replied         | a reply, as above                                                           |
| Unsubscribed    | the one-click unsubscribe was used                                          |
| Bounced         | hard bounce evidence for this recipient                                     |
| Soft bounced    | temporary bounce evidence                                                   |
| Complained      | complaint evidence                                                          |

The *click map* lists every tracked link in the message with the people
who clicked it and, beside them, the recipients whose clicks on it were
judged a machine's. Links are shown without their query string or
fragment, which can carry a personal value. Accepted and bounced overlap:
accepted mail can bounce later.

** Machine opens, honestly

An open (or click) is judged a machine's, kept as evidence with the reason,
and kept out of Opened and Clicked when:

- the request's User-Agent names a known prefetching proxy (Yahoo's), a
  link or attachment scanner (Barracuda, Mimecast, Proofpoint, Safe Links
  and similar) or a crawler or scripted client (=bot=, =python-=, =curl/=,
  headless browsers); the User-Agent is read and dropped, only the reason
  is kept; or
- it arrived within 10 seconds of the relay accepting the message, faster
  than a person opens mail (=venture_marketing_service_set_machine_seconds()=).

The judgement is per observation, and part of its daily identity: a
proxy's fetch on arrival does not swallow the person's own open later that
day. What it cannot do, and does not claim to:

- Apple Mail Privacy Protection fetches every image from Apple's servers
  with an ordinary browser User-Agent, often minutes or hours after
  delivery. Venture collects no IP address, so those opens count as
  opens. Gmail's image proxy fetches when the reader opens, so it is a
  real open and is not flagged.
- "Opened but never clicked" is not used as a machine signal: most people
  who open never click, and the rule would move them out of Opened.

So Opened is an upper bound and Machine opens a lower one. Treat clicks,
replies and unsubscribes as the signals of a person. Observations recorded
before this judgement existed keep counting as opens.

recipient columns (=delivered_at=, =replied_at=, =complained_at=,
=machine_opened_at=) after field-table reconciliation, plus
=marketing_event.machine_reason= and the new =marketing_sending_domain=

sent before them.
