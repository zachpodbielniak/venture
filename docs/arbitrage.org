* How it fits together

Arbitrage is the last stage of a pipeline that starts with somebody else's prices and ends in the general journal. Each stage is its own module with its own document, and each hands the next one plain data through a registry or a record, never through a call into the next stage's internals -- which is why a plugin can add to any stage without touching the others, and why a stage can be switched off without breaking the ones before it.

1. *Plugins* bring the outside in. A native or crispy plugin calls =venture_plugin_register()= and adds providers, fee models, strategies and export formats to the context's registries with plain functions; an exec plugin is a program the server runs, declared in a manifest, and can provide a data source provider (the supplier's price list is one) but not a fee model, a strategy or an export format, which are C functions with no manifest kind. A registered name is looked up exactly like a built-in one, and never twice, so nothing a plugin adds can replace a built-in. See [[file:plugins.org][Plugins]], "The reference plugins" for the three that ship for this pipeline.
2. *Feeds* decide when and from where. A =data_source= record names a provider and its settings; the =feeds= module freezes it on the main thread -- credentials included, never in a record -- and hands it to the worker. See [[file:market-data.org][Market data]], "Feeds".
3. *The series worker* fetches, one thread and one far end at a time, and writes what the provider returns into the source's store. It never touches the database, which is why everything it needs is frozen first. See [[file:market-data.org][Market data]], "The worker thread".
4. *The series store* is a SQLite file per source, not entity tables: millions of rows of prices that only ever need indexed, precomputed reads. Every figure the scan judges by -- a venue's lowest price, its region's median and deal price, its sale rate, a bookmaker's odds -- is a column there. See [[file:market-data.org][Market data]], "Market data".
5. *Marketdata* turns store keys into records a trade can name: a =venue= (with its fee model, its transfer cost and the location whose holding its money moves through) and an =instrument= (with the =product_id= that links it to stock), made by /promotion/ from what the store already knows. It also answers "what is this worth" (the price oracle) and drives the Trading pages, watchlists and alerts. See [[file:market-data.org][Market data]], "Venues, instruments and the price oracle" and "Browsing: the Trading pages".
6. *Arbitrage* -- this page -- reads the stores through one strategy at a time, prices every venue's fees through its fee model, judges every candidate by the same filters and offers the rest as opportunities; the one a person chooses is planned again from the prices as they are now and recorded through the =record= action as a trade and its legs. See [[*Finding opportunities][Finding opportunities]] and [[*Recording an attempt: =record=][Recording an attempt]].
7. *The ledger* makes the trade's money real. Every leg is a registered posting source, so any save of a leg through any door posts, reverses or does nothing exactly as the ledger decides ([[file:ledger.org::*Posting on save: the source registry][Ledger: the source registry]]); a stock leg moves units once, through the inventory service, when it is executed ([[file:inventory.org::*Bought and sold outright: =receive_from= and =issue_to=][Inventory: bought and sold outright]]); a craft between the buy and the sell is production's ([[file:production.org][Production]]); and the close takes what is left on the positions account to the gains account, one journal per currency. The results reach =arbitrage_performance=, =pnl=, =ventures=, =monthly= and the ledger's own statements ([[file:reporting.org][Reporting]]).

* What the module is for

Arbitrage is buying where something is cheap and selling where it is dear, or backing every outcome of an event so that whichever happens pays more than the stakes cost. The =arbitrage= module records each such attempt and makes it reach the books correctly, in any domain: herbs bought on one game realm and sold on another, a supplier's SKU drop-shipped through a marketplace, a surebet across two bookmakers, materials bought at three venues and crafted into something that sells at a fourth.

It requires =marketdata= (a leg names a venue and an instrument) and =ledger= (a leg posts journals), and suggests =production= (a craft between a buy and a sell) and =goods= (stock bought and sold by a leg carries its cost). Because =ledger= requires =finance=, the module's types are financial in the organization role matrix: an organization's finance member records and closes trades, an editor member does not. The module is on by default and switches off like any other; its types, its report and its actions disappear with it.

The first half of this document is the records and the books; the second, [[*Finding opportunities][Finding opportunities]], is the scan -- its strategies, the arithmetic of odds and flips, fee models, presets, the calculators and how an opportunity becomes a trade.

=make demo= seeds one of each, and four worked examples rebuild them by hand with the exact commands (listed in [[*Where to read next][Where to read next]]).

* The records

** =arbitrage_trade=

One attempt.

| Field              | Kind      | For                                                                                        |
|--------------------+-----------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =name=             | text      | what you call it                                                                           |
| =strategy=         | text      | =spread=, =transform=, =deal=, =cover=, =back_lay= or a plugin's; trades of one are compared |
| =venture_id=       | reference | optional: the venture it was for                                                           |
| =status=           | enum      | =planned= (the zero value), =open=, =closed=, =abandoned=                                  |
| =opened_at=        | datetime  | derived: stamped when it opens                                                              |
| =closed_at=        | datetime  | derived: stamped when it is closed or abandoned                                            |
| =expected=         | text      | technical: the scan's snapshot, a JSON object                                              |
| =close_journal_id= | reference | technical: the first journal Close posted                                                  |
| =tags=, =notes=    | text      |                                                                                            |
| =data_source_id=   | reference | technical: set by Record flips, the source whose ledger the trade was matched from          |
| =external_ref=     | text      | technical: set by Record flips, =<source uuid>:<sale key>:<n>=, unique in the organization |

=planned= and =open= move freely by hand. =closed= and =abandoned= are reached only through the Close and Abandon actions, and left only through Reopen, because each of those posts or reverses; a status typed into a form would do neither, and is refused with the action to use instead. =opened_at= and =closed_at= follow the factory's rule for a derived date: stamped when empty, kept when given, cleared on leaving the state, refused when typed onto a trade never in it. A finished trade's =closed_at= is the action's: a hand edit is put back (a form posts every field back), because the close journals carry that date and the operational P&L counts the trade in its period. Reopen and close again to move it. A trade never closes before it opened.

=expected= is what the scan that found the trade expected, verbatim. The report reads one member of it, =profit=: a money string (="12.00 GOLD"=), an array of them, or an object of currency code to amount. Anything that does not name its currency is left out rather than guessed into one.

** =arbitrage_leg=

One movement at one venue.

| Field               | Kind      | For                                                                                       |
|---------------------+-----------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =venue_id=          | reference | where it happened; first, so a bare amount reads the venue's currency                     |
| =trade_id=          | reference | the trade; required, in the leg's own organization                                         |
| =location_id=       | reference | optional: the place whose holding the money moves through (the character who bought or sold), instead of the venue's |
| =kind=              | enum      | =buy= (the zero value), =sell=, =fee=, =transfer=, =stake=, =payout=, =refund=, =write_off= |
| =status=            | enum      | =planned= (the zero value), =executed=, =failed=, =cancelled=; only executed posts         |
| =instrument_id=     | reference | optional: what was traded                                                                  |
| =inventory_item_id= | reference | optional, buy or sell: the stock the units go into or out of                               |
| =quantity=          | integer   | how many units; at least one with stock                                                    |
| =unit_price=        | money     | optional; with a quantity it fills an empty amount                                         |
| =amount=            | money     | the money that moved, before fees                                                          |
| =fees=              | money     | optional: what the venue charged, in the amount's currency                                 |
| =occurred_at=       | datetime  | when; stamped when it is executed with none                                                |
| =cost=              | money     | technical: what the units issued cost, first in first out (in the leg's currency when they cost several) |
| =cost_detail=       | text      | technical: what the units issued cost in every currency, as JSON ={"GOLD": "0.6667", "TICKET": "34"}= |
| =inventory_txn_id=  | reference | technical: the stock movement Execute made                                                 |
| =notes=             | text      |                                                                                           |

The direction of the money is derived from the kind and never stored: a buy and a stake take money out of the venue into the position; a sell, a payout and a refund bring it back; a fee is a charge on its own. A transfer between two venues is two legs, one leaving the first (a positive amount) and one arriving at the second (a negative amount); they net to nothing on the positions account, and a transfer whose arrival is not recorded yet is money in transit, on the position. =write_off= legs are written by the Abandon action only.

*Bare amounts.* An amount typed without a currency is read in the currency of the first referenced record that names one. =venue_id= is the first reference in the field table for exactly this reason: "100" at a gold auction house is 100 GOLD, not dollars. A venue that names a currency refuses an amount in another ("is in TICKET, and Argent Dawn AH trades in GOLD").

*What a leg refuses*, in the order a person would fix it:

- writing its stamps (=inventory_txn_id=, =cost=, =cost_detail=), or a =write_off= leg, by hand;
- any change to a leg that moved stock -- including cancelling it. The units are on a shelf or gone; a correction is a counter-leg (a sell for a buy, a buy for a sell);
- a financial change to an executed leg of a closed or abandoned trade, and a new executed leg on one: the closing journals were posted from them. Reopen first. A planned leg may still be added;
- a trade missing, or in another organization;
- stock on anything but a buy, a sell or a write-off, and stock with a quantity under one;
- a negative amount on anything but a transfer, negative fees, fees without an amount, and fees or a unit price in another currency than the amount;
- an executed leg with no amount; and a stock leg marked executed by hand -- the Execute action does that, because it moves the units first;
- a venue, instrument or stock in another organization, and stock holding a different product from the instrument's.

The leg validator never writes the trade. A derived write would bump the trade's version under whoever is editing it.

* Posting

Every movement of a trade passes through one asset account, *arbitrage positions*, so that what is left on it when the trade is over is exactly its profit or loss.

| Leg                                    | Stock side (Execute)                      | The leg's rule (on save)            |
|----------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| buy, stake, transfer out, no stock     | --                                        | Dr positions / Cr venue cash         |
| sell, payout, refund, transfer in      | --                                        | Dr venue cash / Cr positions         |
| buy with stock                         | Dr inventory / Cr venue cash, at what was paid | fees only                     |
| sell with stock                        | Dr positions / Cr inventory, at FIFO cost | Dr venue cash / Cr positions         |
| write_off (Abandon)                    | Dr positions / Cr inventory, at FIFO cost | nothing                             |
| fee                                    | --                                        | Dr arbitrage fees / Cr venue cash    |
| every leg's =fees=                     | --                                        | Dr arbitrage fees / Cr venue cash    |

** The rule and the registry

=arbitrage_leg= is a registered ledger source (see [[file:ledger.org][the general journal]], "Posting on save: the source registry"): rule =arbitrage_leg=, postable when the leg is executed and has an amount (written, or a unit price and a quantity to make one), dated by =occurred_at=, flags none. So every save of a leg, through any door, is a posting:

- a planned, failed or cancelled leg posts nothing;
- executing it posts its lines;
- saving it again with the same figures posts nothing -- editing a note does not churn history;
- changing an executed leg's amount, fees, venue or date reverses what it posted and posts the new version;
- cancelling an executed leg (or moving it back to planned) reverses its journals and clears its memo holding movements. The holding floor judges that removal: a cancelled payout that funded a later spend is refused.

The rule emits its lines in a fixed order -- principal, then fees -- because the ledger compares the stored version's lines with the new version's in order to decide whether anything changed. It is pure: it reads the leg, the venue and the account mappings, and the only thing it ever writes is an account found or made on first use. It never calls the inventory service. The stock side of a leg is done once, by Execute, and stamped on the leg; a rule that moved stock would move it again on every save.

** Venue cash

Where a leg's money moves through: the holding at the leg's own =location_id= when it names one -- the character who actually bought or sold, which a venue several characters trade at cannot say -- else the holding at the venue's location (=venture_holdings_account_for_location()=, made on first use) -- a character's purse, a stall's till -- else the venue's own =account_id=, else the organization's cash (the =cash= control map, else the chart's 1000, else one made under =<org>:1000=). A venue with a location is a holding, so every rule of holdings applies: one truth per currency, and the floor.

** Currencies

Every journal goes through the posting service's one currency rule (see [[file:currencies.org][Currencies]] and [[file:ledger.org][the general journal]]): a book-currency leg posts in the book journal; a valued currency with a rate on the leg's date is converted into it, the line keeping its original amount; a separate-book currency (or a valued one with no rate) posts a journal of its own; a memo currency posts no journal at all -- its venue-cash line on a holding becomes a =holding_txn= movement, and its positions line is dropped. Nothing is ever added across currencies.

** The holding floor

A leg spending from a holding is judged like any other spend, from the leg's date onward: a buy dated 1 March from a purse first funded on 10 March is refused although the purse holds enough today ("Aria holds 0 TICKET on 2026-03-01 ..."). A memo leg is judged by the movement's validator, a posted one by the posting service's guard. A leg that is refused is not saved at all.

* Stock legs and Execute

A leg naming =inventory_item_id= moves stock as well as money, and only the =execute= action may execute it. In one transaction, inside the whole-operation posting boundary (begun before the transaction, as the boundary requires):

1. a *buy* is received through =venture_inventory_service_receive_from()=: a purchase movement referenced =arbitrage_leg:<id>=, cost layers that split the amount over the units exactly (100.00 for seven units is four layers at 14.29 and three at 14.28, one lot), and Dr inventory / Cr venue cash. Goods received not invoiced (2010) is never touched: no bill will come to clear it. The leg's =cost= is its amount;
2. a *sell* is issued through =venture_inventory_service_issue_to()=: first in, first out, Dr positions / Cr inventory at the layers' cost -- not cost of goods sold (5000), one journal pair per currency the cost is in (none for a memo currency). The leg's =cost= is that cost in the leg's currency (else the first currency it is in), and =cost_detail= every currency's part;
3. the leg is stamped with the movement and saved executed, which posts its money and fees through the rule.

So for a flip, the cash paid becomes inventory; the units leave into the position at cost; the proceeds come out of the position into the venue's cash; and what stays on the position is the profit. Each unit is posted once, by the movement that moved it.

Execute also works on a leg without stock: it stamps the time and saves it executed, which is all the rule needs. A planned trade becomes open, opened at the leg that started it.

Executed stock legs are irreversible. Changing or cancelling one is refused with the counter-leg message. A sell whose units cost some other currency (made by a craft from gold inputs and sold for tickets) carries its cost in that currency on the position and in the leg's =cost=. Units whose first-in-first-out cost spans two currencies -- a craft from gold and ticket inputs, whose sibling layers are one lot, or two lots bought in different currencies -- leave with every currency's share at once, on a sell and on an abandon's write-off alike, and the leg keeps each part in =cost_detail=. The figures read the detail, so each currency's cost of stock is counted in that currency: the gold part is what the issue put on the positions account and what the close takes to gains, and a memo currency's part (tickets) is a loss in that currency in the figures while, being memo, it posts no journal. (These were refused until the detail existed: one recorded cost dropped a currency the positions account carried.) A leg that executed as cash cannot be given stock afterwards; only Execute moves units.

* Close, reopen, abandon

These are record actions, not rules that run on save. A rule built over a trade's sibling legs would always look unchanged to the ledger's comparison, because the trade itself did not change.

** Close

=venture_arbitrage_close()= -- the =close= action, optional parameter =closed_at= (now by default, never before the last executed leg):

1. gathers every journal the trade touched: its legs' (deleted legs included -- a deletion is not a correction), their stock movements', and its own earlier closes and their reversals;
2. adds up what those journals put on the positions account, *per book section* (journal currency), in book amounts;
3. posts, for each section with a balance, one journal in that section's own currency: a credit balance (a profit) is Dr positions / Cr arbitrage gains, a debit balance (a loss) is Dr gains / Cr positions. A loss is a debit on the gains account rather than a separate loss account, so one account reads the net result of every trade;
4. stamps the trade closed, with =closed_at=, =close_journal_id= (the first of the journals) and, when empty, =opened_at= (its first executed leg).

Each closing journal is keyed =arbitrage_close:<trade>:<n>:<CODE>=, where =n= counts the closes the trade has posted, so a reopened trade closes again under a new key. A trade with a gold section and a dollar section posts two journals, never one: gold is not added to dollars.

Sections are journal currencies, not original currencies. A euro leg converted into the dollar book at a rate sits in the dollar section at its booked value, and is closed at exactly that, so the section ends at zero; the gain therefore carries the exchange difference between the legs' rates. A section kept in a separate book (gold) is closed in that book. Each closing journal's lines are all in its header's currency, so it is posted with the core post and nothing is converted. A section whose currency has since become memo cannot be held by any journal, and the close is refused saying so.

** Reopen

=venture_arbitrage_reopen()= -- the =reopen= action: reverses every closing journal the trade still has posted (dated now, or the close's own date when that is later), clears =closed_at= and =close_journal_id=, and sets it open. Its executed legs are editable again; stock written off by an abandon stays written off.

** Abandon

=venture_arbitrage_abandon()= -- the =abandon= action, parameters =goods= (=keep=, the default, or =write_off=) and =abandoned_at=:

- =write_off=: for each stock the trade's executed legs (deleted ones included, as close counts them) bought into and did not sell out of, the remaining units -- never more than are on hand, because units that left the shelf some other way are not the trade's to lose -- are issued at FIFO cost into the positions account through a =write_off= leg (stock movement kind =write_off=, referenced =arbitrage_trade:<id>=). The close that follows takes their cost to gains as a loss;
- =keep=: the units stay in stock at their cost and nothing moves.

Then the trade is closed as above, with the status abandoned.

** Deletion

Deleting a leg or a trade removes nothing from the books (see [[file:ledger.org][the general journal]]): its journals and movements stay posted. A deleted leg still counts in its trade's position and figures, and the trade page marks it deleted rather than hiding it.

* Recording an attempt: =record=

=venture_arbitrage_record()= -- the type-level =record= action on =arbitrage_trade= -- creates a trade and its legs in one transaction, inside the posting boundary. It is what the scan's "Record attempt" calls.

| Parameter         | Kind      | Notes                                                                    |
|-------------------+-----------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =organization_id= | integer   | the organization it is judged and filed in; a member must name one       |
| =name=            | string    | required                                                                 |
| =strategy=        | string    |                                                                          |
| =venture_id=      | reference |                                                                          |
| =expected=        | json      | an object, kept as the trade's snapshot                                  |
| =notes=           | text      |                                                                          |
| =legs=            | json      | required: 1 to 200 leg objects                                           |

Each leg object may carry =kind=, =status=, =venue_id=, =location_id=, =instrument_id=, =inventory_item_id=, =quantity=, =unit_price=, =amount=, =fees=, =occurred_at= and =notes=, in their wire spelling; any other member (a stamp, an id, a trade) is refused by name. Legs are created first, then those asked for as executed are executed in the order given -- a buy before the sell of the same units -- exactly as Execute does, which opens the trade.

The action is stageable: a staged record holds its parameters, and approval performs it afresh, recounting stock and re-reading purses as they are then. Like every type-level action, it is judged in the organization it names (=venture_action_prepare_target()=, =venture_action_require_organization()=): its finance member records, an editor member is refused, a member who names no organization is told to name one, and a global administrator who names none records in the default organization.

venturectl act arbitrage_trade 0 record organization_id=1 name="Peacebloom flip" \
  strategy=spread expected='{"profit":["40 GOLD"]}' \
  legs='[{"kind":"buy","venue_id":3,"amount":"100","status":"executed"},
         {"kind":"sell","venue_id":4,"amount":"150","status":"executed"}]'
#+end_src

* Accounts

| Classification        | Kind    | Unmapped, made on first use as |
|-----------------------+---------+--------------------------------|
| =arbitrage_positions= | asset   | =<org>:1460= Arbitrage positions |
| =arbitrage_gains=     | income  | =<org>:4960= Arbitrage gains     |
| =arbitrage_fees=      | expense | =<org>:6960= Arbitrage fees      |

Map them with an =accounting_control_map= (see [[file:setup.org][Accounting setup]]) to use your own chart's accounts. =venture_arbitrage_account()= resolves one; the codes are organization-scoped so they can never take a number a chart already uses. Only a writer makes one -- the leg posting rule inside the save that posts, executing a leg, closing, writing off. Every read (the trade page, =venture_arbitrage_trade_summary()=, =venture_arbitrage_position()=, the reports) asks =venture_arbitrage_find_account()=, which answers 0 when the account does not exist yet; nothing was posted to an account that does not exist, so the position is empty. Opening a trade is therefore never a write to the chart. Remapping =arbitrage_positions= while trades are open leaves their balance on the old account: close them first.

* The trade page

A trade's page shows its own block before the generic panels: the result per currency (money out, back, into stock, cost of stock out, fees, realised), what is left on the positions account (a profit or a loss, per section), links to every journal, and the legs in order with venue, instrument, units, amount, fees, status and an Execute button beside each planned or failed leg. The legs are not listed again under Related. All of it is =venture_arbitrage_trade_summary()=, the arithmetic the report runs.

* The =arbitrage_performance= report

How finished trades did -- closed or abandoned, in the period they finished. One row per group and currency, the book currency first in each group, never a sum across currencies.

| Option            | Values                                                         |
|-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =group_by=        | =strategy= (default), =venue_pair=, =instrument=, =month=      |
| =strategy=        | only trades of this strategy, matched exactly                  |
| =venture_id=      | only this venture's trades                                     |
| =organization_id= | the legal entity; the default organization otherwise          |

Columns: trades, profitable, hit rate, realised, capital deployed, ROI, fees, average hold (days), expected, and realised less expected.

- *Realised* is what came back, less what went out, less the cost of stock that left (sold or written off), less fees. Money that became stock still held is not counted as spent: =returned - (capital - stock_bought) - stock_cost - fees=, per currency, from executed legs only (deleted ones included). A leg's stock cost is its =cost_detail=, every currency it is in, when the action wrote one, else its =cost=.
- *Capital deployed* is money out, stock bought included. *ROI* is realised over it; nothing deployed is no ROI, not an infinite one.
- *Hit rate* is the share of a currency's trades that realised more than nothing.
- *Average hold* is opened to closed, over the group's trades; it belongs to the group and repeats on each currency row.
- *Expected* and *realised less expected* count only trades whose snapshot named an expected profit in that currency, so the comparison is like for like.
- =venue_pair= labels a trade by the venue its money first went out at and the venue it first came back at ("Market → Supplier"), =instrument= by the first leg that names one, =month= by the UTC month it finished.

The strategy, venture and organization filters narrow the query before its row bound is counted. Past =venture_aggregate_get_max_rows()= finished trades the report refuses, naming the bound, rather than total a truncated set.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report arbitrage_performance this_month group_by=venue_pair strategy=spread
#+end_src

The option reaches every door: the web API, the report page (with a form for group, strategy, venture and period), the CLI, MCP's =venture_report= and the assistant's report tool.

* In the profit and loss: =pnl=, =ventures= and =monthly=

The ledger's =income_statement= always had the trades -- the close journals' Arbitrage gains and the legs' Arbitrage fees. The operational reports, which total records rather than journals, now count them too, through one function, =venture_arbitrage_realised_totals()=: every trade that finished (closed or abandoned) in the period, by =closed_at=, with exactly the arithmetic of the trade page and =arbitrage_performance=. A report's =as_of= reaches them as it reaches the sales and expenses: a trade deleted after the cutoff still counts in a P&L asked as of it.

- =pnl= adds three lines to each currency's block, after the expenses: /Arbitrage gains/ (what the trades made before fees, which is what their close journals moved to the gains account), /Less arbitrage fees/ and /Arbitrage result/ (their realised result). /Profit/ is net revenue less expenses plus the arbitrage result. Metrics: =arbitrage= (and =arbitrage_<CODE>= for another currency) and =arbitrage_trades=.
- =ventures= adds an /Arbitrage result/ column for each venture's own trades (a trade's =venture_id=), and =monthly= one per month, by the month a trade finished; both add it to the row's profit.
- The figures are kept per currency and never converted, the book currency first: a gold trade in a dollar organization is a gold block (or row) of its own. The income statement converts a valued currency at its rate; these reports, like every operational report, do not.
- A trade's legs are neither sales nor expenses, so nothing is counted twice -- the reports total sales and expenses as before and add the trades beside them.
- The venture filter (=venture_id=) and the organization narrow the trades as they narrow the sales.
- The lines and the column appear only when the period finished at least one trade, so an organization that never trades reads exactly as it did. With the =arbitrage= module off there are none, and no error.

Fees are dated with the trade's close here, so the three lines of one trade always fall in the same period and agree with its page; the ledger dates each fee when its leg was executed, so a trade spanning a month end can put its fees in one month's income statement and its gains in the next.

* Flips from an external ledger

A data source that pushes the operator's own ledger (see [[file:market-data.org::*Operator accounts][Market data: operator accounts]]) has its flips -- buys matched to the sales that followed them -- on the Accounts pages. With the source's =books: trades= setting, =record_flips= records them as trades: closed =arbitrage_trade= records whose executed legs post through the books like any other. (=books: daily= posts the same ledger a day at a time instead; a source books one way. See [[file:market-data.org::*The external ledger in the books][Market data: the external ledger in the books]].)

** One trade per sale

The matching is the flips report's own walk, one take at a time (=venture_series_store_flip_pairs()=): per item and currency, first in first out, a sale taking units from the oldest buy before it (a buy and a sale in the same second: the buy first), each take's cost a share of the buy and its proceeds a share of the sale, split so the shares add back to exactly the buy and the sale. Each sale with matched units becomes one trade:

- a *buy leg* for every buy the sale drew on: its units, the share of the buy's cost, dated when it was bought, at the buyer's venue and with =location_id= the buyer's place (the character's purse);
- one *sell leg*: the matched units and their share of what the sale brought (after the venue's cut, as the source records it -- so there is no fee leg), dated when it sold, at the seller's venue and place;
- strategy =flip=, closed at the sale. Its =expected= profit is what the ledger says it made, so the performance report shows no slippage for it.

One trade per sale, not per lot, because a sale is complete the moment it happens: a lot partly sold would otherwise wait open for units that may never sell, and the trades of a window add up to exactly the flips report's matched cost, proceeds and profit (when the walk and the report's window start at the same place). Units a sale sold with no earlier buy (farmed, crafted, bought before the books) are in no trade.

The legs carry no stock: the units were in a game's bags, not in VENTURE's inventory. Buys debit arbitrage positions from the buyer's purse, the sale credits it into the seller's, and the close moves what is left -- the profit -- to arbitrage gains, in the source's currency's own book.

** Once, and the rest later

What each ledger row gave is kept in the trade's =expected= (=source=: the sale's key, account, time, units and proceeds, and each buy's key, account, time, units and cost), and the trade carries =data_source_id= and =external_ref= =<source uuid>:<sale key>:<n>=. The next run reads every recorded trade of the source -- deleted ones too: a deletion leaves the journals posted, so what it took stays taken -- and walks again from what each row has left. So recording again records nothing, a sale the source later merged larger is recorded for the units it gained under =:2=, and a lot is never costed twice, even when the ledger grows behind what was recorded. Because the next run reads them, a recorded flip's =data_source_id=, =external_ref= and =expected= are Record flips' alone: a hand edit to any of them is refused (=expected= compared as JSON, so a form posting it back re-indented is not a change), or the rows an edited snapshot no longer named would be matched, and the sale recorded, again. The walk starts at the source's =books_from= (the whole ledger without one); =from= narrows which sales are recorded, never earlier than =books_from=, and the earlier sales still take their units from the lots before them.

A flip touching a day already posted a day at a time -- its sale's or any buy's account and day -- is left out and counted (=skipped.posted_days=), and a day whose flips are recorded is left out of the daily posting: whatever the setting says now, nothing is booked twice.

** Purses the ledger did not fund

The buyer's purse is judged by the holding floor at the buy's time, and the ledger rarely shows the gold that paid for it arriving (mail from another character, a balance from before the ledger). Before each buy leg is executed, =venture_holdings_floor_shortfall()= asks what would have to be there; the shortfall is posted first, dated the buy, as Dr the purse / Cr =trading_capital= (rule =external_ledger_capital=), and counted in the answer's =capital=. An account allowed to go negative needs none.

** The action

=record_flips= is a type-level action on =arbitrage_trade=, so the organization role matrix judges it as financial work (an organization's finance member records, an editor member does not); stageable; one transaction inside one whole-operation posting boundary.

| Parameter | Kind | Notes |
|-----------+------+-------|
| =organization_id= | integer | the organization; a member must name one |
| =data_source_id= | reference | required: the source |
| =from=, =until= | date | which sales: from =books_from=, before now |
| =instrument= | text | one item's key |
| =min_profit= | money | only flips that made at least this, with its currency ("10.0000 GOLD") |

The answer is the data source with a one-time =result=: what was recorded (each trade's reference, id, units, cost, proceeds, profit and the capital its buys needed), what was left out and why, and how many are left for the next call.

* Finding opportunities

The other half of the module: reading the market data stores (see [[file:market-data.org][Market data]]) for an attempt worth making, and handing the one you choose to =record=. A /scan/ asks one /strategy/ one question; the strategy finds candidates and the scan judges them with the same filters, sorts them and cuts them to =top=. Everything is read-only until "Record attempt", which plans the opportunity and performs the =record= action -- the only path from an opportunity to the books.

Three rules hold everywhere:

- *Nothing adds across currencies.* A sell side in another currency than the buy side is converted through the organization's =exchange_rate= table on the scan's date, exactly as the posting service values a currency -- never an inverted or guessed rate. With no rate the candidate is skipped and a note counts it ("No exchange rate from EUR to USD on file: 3 candidates skipped"). A filter amount (=min_profit=, =max_capital=) in another currency than a row is converted the same way, or the row is left out.
- *Nothing unquoted is read as zero.* An input with no price, a venue whose fee model is not loaded, an outcome no allowed bookmaker quotes: the row is kept with =net=, =capital= and =roi= null, and =missing= names what is unquoted. A blank row survives only while no numeric bound is asked (it cannot be judged against one), and it has no "Record attempt".
- *An opportunity reaches the books only through its plan and =record=.* The page posts the question and the opportunity's key; the server runs the question again, finds the key in what the data says now, and performs the action. An opportunity that moved away is refused ("no longer there"), never recorded from what a page showed a minute ago.

** The question

Every door takes the same names: the =arbitrage_scan= report's options, the query string of =/arbitrage= and its JSON twin, a preset's filters, an =opportunities= widget's options. An unknown name is refused, naming it -- a misspelt filter is a filter not applied. Ratios travel as strings, money as ="10.00 GOLD"= (an amount that names no currency is refused). No option is called =limit=.

| Option           | Kind               | For                                                                                                         |
|------------------+--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =strategy=       | name               | =spread= (default), =transform=, =deal=, =cover=, =back_lay= or a plugin's                                  |
| =preset_id=      | integer            | load an =arbitrage_strategy= preset; anything given beside it wins, a blank value does not clear it         |
| =data_source_id= | integer            | only this source's store; every source of the organization (at most 20) otherwise                           |
| =buy_venues=     | keys, comma        | venues to buy (or back) at; every venue when empty                                                          |
| =sell_venues=    | keys, comma        | venues to sell (or lay) at; every venue when empty                                                          |

| =group_key=      | text               | only venues in this group (a region)                                                                        |
| =category_path=  | text               | only instruments under this store category path                                                             |
| =kind=           | text               | only instruments of this kind                                                                               |
| =instrument=     | key                | one instrument; for =cover=, one event                                                                      |
| =recipe_id=      | integer            | =transform=: one recipe; every active recipe (at most 200) otherwise                                        |
| =recipe_category_id= | integer        | =transform=: only recipes filed in this =category= or beneath it -- a profession, an expansion, a section of its book |

| =sell_basis=     | choice             | what a unit sells for: =min= (the venue's lowest listing, the default), =market=, =sale_avg=, =region_median= (the default for =deal=), =bid= |
| =total_stake=    | money              | =cover= and =back_lay=: the stake; otherwise 100 in the bookmakers' currency, else the book currency        |

| =top=            | integer, 1-500     | how many rows, 50 by default                                                                                |

FlippingPal's "profit at sale avg" is =sell_basis: sale_avg= (the region's 14-day average sale price instead of the lowest listing). Item level is a game's attribute, not a column the store indexes; a plugin's strategy can read it from the instrument's attrs.

A plugin's strategy may declare options of its own (=venture_arbitrage_strategy_registry_add()='s =options=); they are accepted for that strategy only and travel as given. A common name cannot be redeclared.

** Strategies

A strategy is a registered scan function: it reads the stores through the scan it is handed, finds candidates, and adds each as an opportunity; the scan then judges every candidate the same way (see [[*The answer][The answer]]). The five built-ins declare no options of their own -- each reads the common options above, and the list under each strategy says which ones change what it does. Every opportunity carries a =key= that names it uniquely within its strategy's answer, so a fresh run of the same question can find it again; the key's shape is the strategy's, and is shown under each.

| Strategy    | Label (the page's tab) | Reads from the store                                            | Default =sell_basis= |
|-------------+------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------|
| =spread=    | Spread                 | current rows, cheapest against their region first               | =min=                |
| =deal=      | Deals                  | current rows at or under their group's deal price               | =region_median=      |
| =transform= | Crafting               | recipes, their products' instrument records, every input's book | =min=                |
| =cover=     | Surebets               | events with quotes, newest first, and their outcomes' back odds | --                   |
| =back_lay=  | Back and lay           | instruments with a lay quote, newest first, and their back odds | --                   |

=GET /api/v1/arbitrage/registries= (=venturectl arbitrage registries=) lists every registered strategy with its label and description, a plugin's included.

*** =spread=


/Only plausible sales./ A sell price read from listings -- the =min= basis (the default), =market= or =region_median= -- counts only where the instrument sells (once the store knows anything of sales there: units sold, or a sale rate of 2%, its own or the source's) and is not over twice the group's median or the venue's market value (=venture_series_row_sell_plausible()=, shared with the Deals page). Unguarded, the best "opportunities" were a copper bought and a 9,999,999g troll listing sold to: a hundred million percent. The =bid= and =sale_avg= bases are sales already and are not held to it. Nor is a spread whose sell price is over twenty times its buy price (=VENTURE_SERIES_SELL_MAX_MARKUP=): trolls cancel and relist, so their listings vanish like sales and hold a market's median at their price, but the gap between buying and selling is the one thing relisting cannot fake. Real cross-realm flips run to a few times.

Buy where an instrument is cheap, sell where it is dear: cross-realm flipping, drop shipping, retail and crypto spreads. Candidates are the in-stock rows at the buy venues, cheapest against their region first (the store's indexed =pct_vs_region=), at most 500 instruments per source. For each, every source's venues quoting the same instrument key (=venture_series_store_other_venues()=, one indexed read per source) give the buy side -- the N cheapest in-stock venues per currency, N being =buy_sources= -- and the sell side, the best other venue per currency on =sell_basis=. Each buy and sell pair is priced as a flip (below). An instrument is the same instrument across two sources when its key is the same, which is how a supplier's price list and a marketplace's prices meet.

The buy is priced by walking the venue's book for =units= (=venture_series_store_bulk_cost()=): a book too thin prices the units it has and remembers =requested_units=; a store that keeps only statistics prices at the lowest price with the depth unknown.

- Options that shape it: =buy_venues=, =sell_venues=, =group_key=, =category_path=, =kind=, =instrument= (one instrument), =units=, =buy_sources=, =sell_basis=, =share=, =max_age_hours=; and =max_buy_pct= (or =sell_basis: sale_avg=) makes it read the buy venue's region averages to fill =buy_vs_sale_avg=.
- Key: =spread:<buy source>:<buy venue>><sell source>:<sell venue>:<instrument>=, e.g. =spread:1:thornmere>1:silverfen:moonsteel-bar=.
- Legs it plans: a buy at the buy venue, a sell at the sell venue with that venue's fees, and a =fee= leg at each end whose venue has a =transfer_cost=, because the lot moves.

*** =deal=

Undermine's deal: a venue's price at or under its group's deal price -- the median of the venues' lowest prices, or the 33rd percentile when 15 or more venues offer it (the store's =deals_only= filter) -- bought there and sold back at the same venue at =region_median= unless =sell_basis= says otherwise. No transfer; the venue's own fees. Every deal row is its own candidate (a spread keeps one per instrument), and the venue must be allowed on both sides: =buy_venues= and =sell_venues= both have to admit it.

- Options that shape it: the same as =spread= less =buy_sources=.
- Key: =deal:<source>:<venue>><source>:<venue>:<instrument>=, the same venue on both sides.

*** =transform=

Crafting arbitrage (TSM's crafting cost). For each recipe, the output's best venue on =sell_basis= fixes the currency; each input is priced at every allowed buy venue of every instrument its product is quoted as (instrument records with =product_id=), by walking that venue's book for the units needed, converted into the output's currency; the cheapest wins. A reusable component (a tool) is needed once, at its quantity, however many batches -- never multiplied, the =goal_materials= rule. A venue whose book cannot fill the units, or whose currency has no rate, is passed over; none left leaves the input unquoted and the row blank, naming it. The cost is the sum of the inputs, the output's gross is its price times =output_quantity= times the batches, and moving the lots costs each input venue's transfer cost and the output venue's once. The opportunity carries =inputs= -- each line's venue, units, unit price and cost -- which =craft_arbitrage= prints as a shopping list.

- It needs the =production= module: with it off the scan answers no rows and a note says so. An inactive recipe is skipped unless =recipe_id= names it.
- Options that shape it: =recipe_id= (one recipe, else every active one, at most 200 by name), =recipe_category_id= (the recipes filed in a category and beneath it, narrowed in the recipe query itself, so the 200 counts only them), =units= (batches), =buy_venues= (where inputs may be bought), =sell_venues= (where the output may be sold), =group_key=, =sell_basis=, =max_age_hours=.
- /A region-wide venue is open from every venue./ A venue whose store attributes carry ="region_wide": true= -- a game's commodity market, which every realm of a region trades on -- may be bought from and sold to whatever =buy_venues= and =sell_venues= say (=venture_arbitrage_scan_venue_region_wide()=). Narrowing the reagents to one realm must not leave every commodity reagent unquoted: the person on that realm buys them from the region's market without leaving it. Only =transform= reads it; =spread= and =deal= keep their sets exactly (a commodity market is its own group's only venue, so every commodity there would read as a deal).
- /How old each price is./ Each input carries =taken_at=, =age_seconds= and =stale= beside its venue, the =sell= object the same, and the row =buy_taken_at=, =buy_age_seconds=, =buy_stale= (the oldest input: one realm whose feed stopped greys the craft) and =sell_taken_at=, =sell_age_seconds=, =sell_stale= -- the members Deals carries, judged against =series.stale_minutes= the same way.
- /Margin and fees./ A priced row carries =margin=, the net over the gross (what is left of each unit of sales), and =recipe_category_id= when its recipe is filed. Its =warnings= name the venue the output sells at when that venue has no record or a record with no fee model: its cut and deposit are not counted, and the craft reads better than it is (the =none= model is a model: a venue that really is free names it).
- Key: =transform:<recipe id>:<output source>:<output venue>=, or =transform:<recipe id>:0= when no venue quotes the output.
- Legs it plans: a buy per input at its venue, then the output's sell, then a =fee= leg for each venue a lot moves from and for the output venue, at its =transfer_cost= in its own currency (the net subtracted them, so a recorded craft pays them too). Every transfer cost, the output venue's included, is converted into the row's currency for the net; one with no rate is named in =missing=. The craft itself is not a leg: it is the recipe's =craft= action, performed between them (see [[file:examples/arbitrage-crafting.org][the crafting example]]).

*** =cover=

A surebet across bookmakers for one event. Events are the parents of quoted instruments (=venture_series_store_list_quoted_parents()=, newest first, at most 500 per source); an event's outcomes are its children with any quote. For each outcome, the best back odds at an allowed venue that takes the stake's currency, net of that venue's commission. An outcome no allowed venue quotes blanks the row and names it: two of three outcomes are not an event. With every outcome quoted, the stakes are split (below); =net= is what the rounded stakes are sure to make, =payout= what the worst result pays, and =ideal_profit= and =payout_ideal= the unrounded figures beside them.

*An event that has started is left out.* An odds provider stores an event's start as the =commence_time= attribute of its instrument (the-odds-api's plugin does, and so does the demo's JSONL). An event whose =commence_time= is at or before the scan's time is under way or over, and its quotes -- books stop moving a finished match's odds -- would read as a surebet nobody can take; the scan leaves it out and a note counts how many. An event that names no =commence_time= is kept, and a note says how many were and that =max_age_hours= is then the only guard.

- Options that shape it: =instrument= (one event, by its key), =buy_venues= (the bookmakers that may be backed at), =total_stake= (the money split over the outcomes), =max_age_hours= (prices older are not read, and events quoted only before it are not listed at all).
- A bookmaker whose venue record names a currency other than the stake's is passed over; one with no record or no currency may take it. Odds are quotes with no currency; a quote that carries one is a price, not odds, and is not read.
- Key: =cover:<source>:<event>=.
- Legs it plans: a =stake= per outcome at its bookmaker, for that outcome's rounded stake. A row also carries =outcomes= (each outcome's venue, odds, effective odds, stake and payout), =sum=, =overround=, =is_surebet= and =residual=.

*** =back_lay=

A back at one venue matched by a lay of the same outcome at an exchange. Instruments with a lay quote (=venture_series_store_list_quoted_instruments()=); the best back (net of its venue's commission) at the buy set and the cheapest lay, counting the exchange's commission, at the sell set, at two different venues. =net= is the worse of the two results; capital is the back stake plus the lay's liability. An outcome whose event has started (the outcome's own =commence_time=, else its parent event's) is left out as =cover= leaves the event out.

- Options that shape it: =instrument= (one outcome, by its key), =buy_venues= (where to back), =sell_venues= (where to lay), =total_stake= (the back stake; zero is refused, as for =cover=), =max_age_hours=. A venue whose fee model cannot price the stake is left out and named in the answer's notes.
- Key: =back_lay:<source>:<instrument>:<back venue>><lay venue>=.
- Legs it plans: a =stake= of the back stake at the back venue, and a =stake= of the liability at the exchange (what it holds while the lay is matched). A row also carries =back_odds=, =lay_odds=, =commission=, =rating=, =back_stake=, =lay_stake=, =liability=, =if_back_wins=, =if_lay_wins= and =ideal_profit=.

** The answer

=venture_arbitrage_scan_run()= -- behind =GET /api/v1/arbitrage/scan=, the page, the report, the widget and the CLI -- answers one JSON object:

| Member      | What                                                                                                                                |
|-------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =available= | false when there is nothing to scan: no SQLite in the build, =feeds= off, or no data source in the organization (a note says which) |
| =strategy=  | the strategy that ran                                                                                                               |
| =options=   | the question as the scan read it: normalised, the preset merged in, every value in its one spelling                                 |
| =examined=  | how many candidates the strategy offered                                                                                            |
| =excluded=  | how many were left out, by reason (below)                                                                                           |
| =truncated= | true when more rows passed than =top= let through                                                                                   |
| =rows=      | the opportunities, sorted, at most =top=                                                                                            |
| =notes=     | what the scan wants a person to know: bounds reached, rates missing, events started, sources off                                    |

*Judging.* Every candidate any strategy offers is judged by the same function, in this order, and the first reason that applies is the one counted in =excluded=:

| Reason           | Left out when                                                                                                |
|------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =stale=          | its oldest price is older than =max_age_hours= (applies even to =craft_arbitrage=)                           |
| =unquoted=       | something in it is unquoted (=missing=) and any numeric bound was asked; with none asked a blank row is kept |
| =unprofitable=   | its =net= is zero or less                                                                                    |
| =no_rate=        | =min_profit= or =max_capital= is in another currency and there is no =exchange_rate= to the row's on the day |
| =min_profit=     | =net= is under the bound                                                                                     |
| =max_capital=    | =capital= is over the bound                                                                                  |
| =min_roi=        | =roi= is under the bound, or unknown                                                                         |
| =min_sale_rate=  | =sale_rate= is under the bound, or unknown                                                                   |
| =max_buy_pct=    | =buy_vs_sale_avg= is over the bound, or unknown                                                              |
| =min_confidence= | =confidence= is under the bound                                                                              |
| =never_sells=    | the sell venue's sale rate is zero: infinitely many relists, every deposit lost (counted by the flip itself) |

A strategy also drops prices before they become candidates: a price older than =max_age_hours= is never read (a note counts them), and a sell side in a currency with no rate to the buy side's is skipped (a note per currency pair counts them, "No exchange rate from EUR to USD on file: 3 candidates skipped. Record an exchange_rate to compare them."). Neither is in =excluded=, because neither became a row.

*Sorting.* =sort= orders what passed. =profit= (by =net=) and =ev= are money, and money in two currencies has no order: rows group by currency, the book currency first and the rest by code, and order by amount within each. The ratio sorts (=roi=, =roi_per_day=, =annualized=, =confidence=) cross currencies freely. A row missing the figure sorts last whichever way; ties go by key, so the same data always gives the same order -- which is what lets =venturectl arbitrage record ROW= number rows.

*Cutting.* =top= (50 by default, at most 500) keeps the first rows and sets =truncated= when it dropped any. Recording runs the same question with =top= at 500, so an opportunity a page showed on row 50 is still found.

*A row.* The members every strategy writes: =strategy=, =key=, =title=, =instrument_key=, =instrument_name=, =data_source_id=, =currency=, =net=, =capital=, =roi=, =ev=, =confidence=, =age_seconds=, =venues=, =warnings= (a venue with no record, a sale rate unknown with a deposit at stake), =missing= (what is unquoted, when anything is), =narrow= (the options that find it again: its =instrument=, or for =transform= its =recipe_id=) and =legs= (kind, source, venue key, instrument key, quantity, unit price, amount, fees -- the plan's raw material). A flip (=spread=, =deal=) adds =units=, =requested_units= (a book too thin), =buy= and =sell= (each side's venue, unit price, amount, fees, and the sell side's =deposit=), =transfer_cost=, =listing_loss=, =relists=, =lock_days=, =roi_per_day=, =annualized=, =sale_rate=, =sold_per_day= and =buy_vs_sale_avg=; =transform= adds =inputs=; =cover= and =back_lay= add the members listed under them. Money is the usual money JSON; ratios are fractions (=0.15= is 15%). The page alone adds =trend= and =trend_currency= to each row -- the buy venue's lowest price over fourteen days, for its sparkline (=venture_arbitrage_add_trends()=); the JSON twin, the report and the export answer without them.

** The arithmetic

All of it is =venture-arbitrage-math.h=: pure, money in minor units, ratios as doubles, rounding half to even once where a ratio meets an amount (=venture_arbitrage_round_minor()=), and an amount past 2^53 minor units refused rather than rounded wrong. Where odds multiply money they do so as integers at the store's odds scale (a million), so 100 x 1.13 pays 113, not the 112 a double would floor to.

*** Odds

- Decimal odds d. American +A is 1 + A/100, -A is 1 + 100/A (a moneyline is at least 100 in magnitude); fractional a/b is 1 + a/b; "evens" (or "evs") is 2.0. Odds of one or less pay nothing and are refused.
- =format= is =auto= (the default), =decimal=, =american= or =fractional= (=venture_arbitrage_odds_format_from_string()=). Auto reads a slash as fractional, a leading sign as American, "evens" as fractional and anything else as decimal -- which is why "+200" needs no format but "200" given as a moneyline does: auto would read it as decimal odds of 200.
- Implied probability p = 1/d. Overround = sum(1/d_i) - 1. No-vig probability p_i = (1/d_i) / sum(1/d_j).

*** Surebet

With the best odds d_i for every outcome, S = sum(1/d_i). It is a surebet when S < 1 (by more than 1e-12: three outcomes at 3.0 sum to one in binary too, and that is no edge).

- Stake_i = T x (1/d_i) / S, rounded half to even to the currency's minor unit.
- Payout_i = stake_i x d_i, rounded down: a bookmaker never pays part of a minor unit. The odds themselves are taken to the store's millionths downward first, never to the nearest: -117 is 217/117, and rounding it to the nearest millionth claimed a payout of 109266 on a stake of 58913 that truly pays 109265.99.
- Staked = sum(stake_i); the /residual/ is T - staked (rounding can leave a unit over or take one).
- /Payout/ = min(payout_i): what the rounded stakes are sure to return, whichever outcome happens. /Profit/ = payout - staked: what they are sure to make. That is the opportunity's =net=.
- /Ideal/ payout T/S and ideal profit T(1/S - 1), rounded half to even, are shown beside them as =payout_ideal= and =profit_ideal= (a scan row's =ideal_profit=), never instead of them: 1000 yen split over 2.5 and 1.8 stakes 419 and 581, pays 1047 and 1045, so the payout is 1045 and the profit 45, against an ideal 1047 and 47. A calculator that answered the ideal as the payout promised a unit of money no bookmaker pays.

*** Back and lay, with commission

For a back stake B at odds d (already net of the back venue's commission c_b: d = 1 + (d_raw - 1)(1 - c_b)) and a lay at odds L at an exchange keeping c of net winnings:

- Effective back odds 1 + (d - 1)(1 - c); effective lay odds 1 + (L - 1)/(1 - c).
- Lay stake = B x d / (L - c), rounded half to even; liability = lay stake x (L - 1), rounded up -- the exchange holds all of it. Lay odds go to millionths upward for this, back odds downward, so a non-terminating price (-333 is 433/333) cannot shave a unit off the liability.
- If the back wins: B(d - 1), rounded down as every payout is, less the liability. If the lay wins: lay stake x (1 - c), rounded down, less B.
- /Worst/, the lesser of the two, is what the pair is sure to make: every rounding in it goes against the person, so it is a guarantee and not an estimate. Rounding half to even had shown 3.0 backed against 2.9 laid at 5% as 0.00 and 0.01 when the rounded lay stake costs a cent whichever way it goes.
- Both equal B x d(1 - c)/(L - c) - B before rounding, the /ideal/, shown beside the worst; the /rating/ d(1 - c)/(L - c) above one is a profit.

*** Flips

For units bought at one venue and sold at another (or the same), in one currency:

- Expected relists = 1/sale_rate - 1. A sale rate of zero is "never sells" -- infinitely many relists, every deposit lost -- and the candidate is left out and counted in a note. An unknown sale rate counts no relist (and the row warns when there is a deposit).
- Listing loss = relists x deposit when a sale refunds its own deposit, (relists + 1) x deposit when nothing is refunded.
- Net = units x sell price - sell fees - buy cost - buy fees - listing loss - transfer cost.
- Capital = buy cost + buy fees + one deposit + transfer cost.
- Transfer cost, when the lot moves (the buy and sell venues differ), is the buy venue's =transfer_cost= plus the sell venue's, each converted into the flip's currency; a lot sold where it was bought (=deal=) moves nowhere and costs nothing.
- ROI = net / capital; nothing tied up is no ROI, not an infinite one.
- Lock days = units / (sold per day x share) + transit days (both venues' =transfer_hours=, added, / 24, when the lot moves); unknown without a pace of sales.
- ROI per day = ROI / lock days. Annualised = (1 + ROI)^(365 / lock days) - 1; a total loss stays -1, and a hold too short for the figure to be a number has none.
- EV = P(fill) x net - (1 - P(fill)) x unwind loss, with P(fill) the sale rate and the unwind loss a deposit and the move back (in a scan; the =flip= calculator, which has no venues, counts only its =transfer=). With no sale rate there is no EV. A surebet or a back and lay is quoted on both sides now: its EV is its net.

*** Confidence

A number in [0, 1], the product of four factors (=venture_arbitrage_confidence()=):

| Factor    | Formula                                                                 | Unknown |
|-----------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+---------|
| freshness | 1 - (age - I) / 3I, clamped to [0, 1]; I the venue's learned interval (an hour by default), age the oldest price used | --      |
| breadth   | min(1, venues quoting / 3)                                              | --      |
| stability | 1 / (1 + sigma/mu) of the sell venue's price                            | 0.8     |
| depth     | the share of the units on offer at the price                            | 0.5     |

Fresh data at three venues with a steady price and a deep book is 1; two intervals old is two thirds of that; four is nothing. No evidence of a steady price is not evidence of one, hence 0.8 and not 1.

** Fee models

A venue names a model in =fee_model= and gives its parameters in =fee_params= (YAML, or JSON, a mapping). A venue with no record, or no model, charges nothing, and a row says so in its =warnings= ("realm-c has no venue record: no fees counted there").

*The registry.* Fee models live in a =VentureFeeModelRegistry= on the context (=venture_context_get_fee_models()=), the built-ins first, a plugin's added by name with =venture_fee_model_registry_add()=. A model is two plain functions:

- *compute* (=VentureFeeModelComputeFunc=) is handed the venue's parameters, the side (=VENTURE_FEE_SIDE_SELL= or =VENTURE_FEE_SIDE_BUY=), the gross amount for all the units, the units (at least one), a reference amount for all the units (in a scan, the sell venue's market value times the units; =NULL= when nobody knows one), and the instrument's *attributes* as its source stored them (=NULL= in a calculator, or when the instrument has none). It fills a =VentureFeeQuote=: =fee= (left =NULL= it is zero), an optional =deposit= and whether it is =deposit_refundable=, and a =commission= share for an exchange. It runs on the main thread and reads no database: everything it may judge by is in its arguments.
- *validate* (=VentureFeeModelValidateFunc=), optional, judges the parameters when a venue writes them. A model with no validator accepts only empty parameters.

=venture_fee_model_registry_compute()= is the one caller: an empty name is =none=, a name not registered is =VENTURE_ERROR_NOT_FOUND= ("The fee model wow_auction is not loaded"), and a failure clears the quote. A scan calls it through =venture_arbitrage_scan_fees()=, which finds the venue's record and parameters and reads the instrument's attributes from the store by the side's instrument key (cached for the scan); a failure there does not fail the scan: a flip or a surebet is blanked with =fees: <the reason>= in =missing=, and =back_lay= passes over the venue whose model failed.

| Model         | Where               | Parameters                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
|---------------+---------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =percent=     | built in            | =cut_percent= of the sale (at most 100); =fixed_per_unit=, =fixed_per_order= on top; at least =min_fee=; =deposit_percent= of the listing price (=deposit_basis: price=, the default) or of a reference (=deposit_basis: reference=, the sell venue's market value), refunded on a sale unless =deposit_refundable: false=; a =buy:= mapping with the same keys (but no =buy:= of its own) prices buying, which is free without one                                          |
| =commission=  | built in            | =rate_percent= of net winnings (an exchange), under 100; charged only on a positive sell amount, and handed to the odds strategies as the quote's =commission=, which they apply through the effective odds                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| =none=        | built in            | nothing; takes no parameters                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                 |
| =wow_auction= | the Blizzard plugin | =cut_percent= (5), =duration_hours= (12, 24 or 48; 48 by default), =deposit_factor_percent= (the classic 15/30/60% of the vendor price by duration otherwise); the deposit is the instrument's =vendor_sell= attribute (copper) times the units, refundable, and absent while the item's details are unfetched; the amount must have four decimal places (=GOLD=). Buying is free. See [[file:examples/wow-auction-house-feed.org::*Fees: =wow_auction=][the Blizzard feed]] |

Percents are read exactly (4.75% is 47500 parts per million, at most four decimal places); an amount with no currency is in the sale's, and one naming another currency is refused rather than converted. Booleans read =true=/=false=, =yes=/=no=, =on=/=off= or =1=/=0=.

*Judged when written.* A venue's model and parameters are checked against the registry when either is written -- an unregistered model is refused naming the registered ones, a parameter the model does not take is refused by name (=cut_percnt= read as no cut is the fee nobody charged). A name kept from before is never judged again, so a venue saved under a plugin's model stays editable after the plugin is gone, and with the arbitrage module off nothing is judged at all: nothing loaded is not evidence that a name is wrong. The built-in models are always registered, so a name written while a plugin is not loaded is refused -- write it once the plugin is there. A scan at a venue whose model is missing blanks the row and names the model.

** Bounds

The scan runs on the main thread of a single-threaded server, so every read is bounded and uses the store's precomputed, indexed columns:

- at most 20 data sources, 500 candidate instruments (or events, or lay-quoted instruments) per source, 2000 store rows read to choose them, 200 recipes, 100 instruments per product, 64 outcomes per event;
- each candidate costs one indexed read per source (=other_venues=), one book walk per buy venue and one reference read when the question needs a sale average;
- events and lay quotes are enumerated by the store with a =LIMIT= over the current quotes (one row per venue, instrument and side), never the quote history.

Past a bound the answer says so in a note ("narrow the question with a category, a venue set or an instrument"); nothing is truncated silently.

** Presets

*** A venue group

=venue_group= narrows a scan the way Deals' venue group picker narrows Deals: both sides only at the group's venues -- the user's banking realms, say. The group is read with =venture_marketdata_venue_group_venue_keys()=, which gives the keys it stands for in every source of the organization, each connected realm whole (a group naming one realm takes every source's keys for its connected realm; see "Deals" in [[file:market-data.org][Market data]]), and the scan intersects =buy_venues= and =sell_venues= with them: no list is the whole group, a list keeps what is in the group, and a list with nothing in it -- left over from the group asked before -- is set aside for the whole group with a note. A strategy reading candidates from the index filters by =venture_arbitrage_scan_get_buy_venues()=, never by the =buy_venues= text. A group that is not the organization's is refused (NOT_FOUND), never ignored; =venue_group_name= in the answer names the one used. =/arbitrage= offers the same picker as Deals; the CLI, the reports, presets (as an option) and the =opportunities= widget take it as an option. Each row's =buy= and =sell= sides say how old their price is (=age_seconds=, =stale=; see "How old a price is" in [[file:market-data.org][Market data]]), and the page greys a stale side.

An =arbitrage_strategy= record is a saved question: =name=, =strategy=, =data_source_id=, =buy_venues=, =sell_venues= and =options= -- the rest of the question as YAML (=min_profit: 10.00 GOLD=, =min_roi: 15=, =top: 20=). =preset_id= loads one; the record's fields and options fill the question and anything asked beside it wins. The save validator reads the options with the scan's own reader and holds the strategy to the registry (when written, as a venture type is), so a preset that saved is a preset that runs; a preset cannot load another. =/arbitrage= lists the organization's presets to load and saves the current question as one through the generic record form.

** Recording an opportunity

Each opportunity carries a =key= (unique within its strategy's answer), a =narrow= mapping (the options that find it again cheaply: its instrument, recipe or event) and =legs= in store terms: kind, source, venue key, instrument key, quantity, unit price, amount, fees. "Record attempt" -- the page's button, =POST /api/v1/arbitrage/record= with the question and the key, or =venture_arbitrage_record_opportunity()= -- runs the question again with =top= at its most, finds the key, and asks the strategy's plan for the =record= request. The default plan (=venture_arbitrage_plan_legs()=) promotes each leg's venue and instrument to records (idempotent; a deleted one is restored), writes every leg =planned= in its venue's currency, and fills =expected= with the profit (as a money string the performance report reads), capital, ROI, confidence, data age and key, and -- for the flip planner -- the sell side's =deposit=, the =listing_loss= relisting is expected to cost, and the =question= that found it: the scan's normalised options less =top= and =preset_id= (a preset edited since must not change what was planned), with the row's =narrow= over them. The request goes through the =record= action, so the organization's roles, a second actor's approval and the action's consent operation apply as they do by hand. A blank opportunity cannot be planned.

=POST /api/v1/arbitrage/record?stage=1= (and =venture_arbitrage_stage_opportunity()=) plans the same way and stages the =record= action with the plan instead of performing it: the answer is =202= with the pending confirmation, exactly as the generic action route answers a staged action, and approval performs the same parameters. Planning still promotes the venues and instruments the legs name -- a leg must name a record, and promotion is idempotent -- but the trade and its legs wait for the approval.

| Strategy   | Legs                                                                         |
|------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =spread=, =deal= | buy at the buy venue; sell at the sell venue with its fees; a fee for each venue's transfer cost when the lot moves |
| =transform= | a buy per input at its venue; the output's sell                             |
| =cover=    | a stake per outcome at its bookmaker                                         |
| =back_lay= | a stake at the back venue; the liability as a stake at the exchange          |

** Export

=GET /arbitrage/export?format=NAME&...= (and its API twin, and =venturectl arbitrage export FORMAT [-o FILE]=) runs the question and hands its rows -- exactly the rows the page would show, after the filters, the sort and =top= -- with the normalised options to the format named, through the export format registry (=venture_context_get_export_formats()=, =venture_export_format_registry_export()=). The answer is a download, =arbitrage-<format>.<extension>=, with the format's content type and =Cache-Control: no-store=; a format nobody registered is NOT_FOUND, listing the ones that are. The page links every registered format beside the results.

| Format          | Where               | Content type                | What                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |
|-----------------+---------------------+-----------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =csv=           | built in            | =text/csv; charset=utf-8=   | one line per row: =strategy=, =key=, =title=, =instrument=, =buy_venue=, =sell_venue=, =units=, =currency=, =capital=, =net=, =roi=, =roi_per_day=, =annualized=, =confidence=, =sale_rate=, =age_seconds=, =missing=. | =shopping_list= | built in            | =text/plain; charset=utf-8= | every buy and stake leg of the rows, by venue, the same instrument at the same venue added up, a total per venue per currency (never across two)                                                                                                                               |

An export function (=VentureExportFunc=) is handed the rows and the options and returns =GBytes=; it runs on the main thread after the scan, and reads nothing else. It computes nothing of its own -- every figure is the strategy's row -- and adds only what a page needs to read it.

** The flip planner

=/arbitrage/plan= (=GET /api/v1/arbitrage/plan=, =venture_arbitrage_planner()=) is everything planned, as a shopping list. A plan is not a second record type: a planned =arbitrage_trade= /is/ a line of the plan -- what "Record attempt" and "Add to plan" (=POST /arbitrage/plan/add=, the same record, which answers with the planner) write from Deals, Arbitrage and Crafting -- and executing its legs is how it is carried out. Deals' "Add to plan" posts the =spread= question that finds exactly that flip (its item, bought at its realm, sold at the deal's sell realm, at the lowest listing) with the spread's key, so the flip is priced again with each venue's own fee model where Deals took a flat cut; one those fees turn into a loss is refused as no longer there.

The planner reads every trade still =planned= in the organization, newest first, with its legs -- all of them or a refusal: past 500 (=VENTURE_ARBITRAGE_PLANNER_MAX_TRADES=) it answers CONFLICT rather than total part of a plan -- and regroups the planned legs (=venture_arbitrage_planner_build()=, plain data in and out):

| Section  | What                                                                                                                |
|----------+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =buy=    | one group per venue the buy (and stake) legs name, by name; each instrument once, its units added, =unit_price= the dearest planned -- the most a unit may cost -- and the amount and fees added |
| =sell=   | one group per venue of the sell (and payout) legs; each instrument /and price/ once -- what to post is a price as well as a count |
| =fees=   | the fee and transfer legs (moving a lot), per venue                                                                 |
| =totals= | one block per currency, never added across two: =trades=, =outlay= (every buy, its buy fees and every fee leg), =gross= (the sales), =cut= (the sell legs' fees), =deposit= (what posting ties up, refunded on a sale; from each trade's =expected=), =listing_loss= (the deposits relisting is expected to lose), =revenue= (gross less cut less listing loss) and =profit= (revenue less outlay) |

Only planned legs count: an executed one is the books' now, a cancelled or failed one is not happening. Because the totals add the same legs, cut and expected lost deposits the scan's flip subtracted, a plan's profit is exactly the sum of what its trades promised.

=?reprice=1= asks each trade's own question again (its =expected.question=; at most 100 trades, =VENTURE_ARBITRAGE_PLANNER_MAX_REPRICE=, one scan per distinct question) with losses kept, finds its key and says how it stands in =reprice=: =ok=, =stale= (still there, a buy or sell price is old), =unprofitable= (still there, now a loss), =unquoted= (a price or fee is missing now), =gone= (the question no longer finds it) or =unknown= (recorded before questions were kept, or by hand) -- with the =net= now beside the =planned_net=.

=GET /arbitrage/plan/export?format=csv= is the list as CSV -- =section= (buy, fees, sell), venue, instrument, key, quantity, unit price, amount, fees, then a =total= line per currency and figure -- defused as the scan's CSV is; =format= may also name a registered export format other than the scan's =csv= and =shopping_list=, which is handed the buy lines as rows ({=instrument_key=, =instrument_name=, =data_source_id=}, each item once): with the Blizzard plugin, =format=tsm= is a TradeSkillMaster group-import string of what to buy. =POST /arbitrage/plan/remove= (=trade_id=; JSON on the API twin) takes a trade off the plan -- its legs and the trade deleted in one transaction (=venture_arbitrage_planner_remove()=), refused for a trade under way or finished (close or abandon it instead) and for an executed leg. A planned trade posted nothing, so deleting it removes nothing from the books.

The page: links to Deals, Crafting and Arbitrage; "Price again" and the exports; the totals per currency; Buy (a heading per realm over its lines and its total, in =<tfoot>=), Moving lots, Post; and the planned trades with their planned profit, their standing when priced again, and Remove.

** Calculators

| =back_lay= | =back_odds=, =lay_odds=, =format=, =commission= and =back_commission= (percents), =stake=               | lay stake, liability, both results, =worst= (sure), =ideal=, the rating     |
| =flip=     | =buy=, =sell= (unit prices, one currency), =units=, =cut= (percent), =fixed= (per order), =deposit= (percent of the sale), =refundable=, =sale_rate= (percent), =sold_per_day=, =share= (percent), =transfer= (an amount), =transit_hours= | net, capital, ROI, ROI a day, annualised, listing loss, EV, days tied up |

*Surebet.* The odds are split on commas, semicolons, spaces or new lines, so "2.10, 2.05" and "+150 -120" both read; at least two outcomes, at most 64. =payout= is the least any outcome's rounded payout returns and =profit= is that less what was staked -- what a person is sure to get -- and =payout_ideal= (T/S) and =profit_ideal= (T(1/S - 1)) are the unrounded figures beside them; =stakes=, =payouts=, =staked= and =residual= (the total less what was staked) show where rounding went. On the demo's 2.10 and 2.15 with 100.00 USD: stakes 50.59 and 49.41, each paying 106.23, so =payout= 106.23 and =profit= 6.23 against an ideal 106.24 and 6.24. Each outcome also carries its =implied= and =no_vig= chance; the answer has =sum=, =overround=, =margin= and =is_surebet=. The page shows "Sure profit" ("Sure loss" when the odds are no surebet), "Sure payout" and "Ideal profit"; the CLI's table Payout, Profit, Ideal payout and Ideal profit.

*Back and lay.* =back_commission= turns the back odds into effective odds before anything else (an exchange used to back); =commission= is the lay exchange's. =worst= is the lesser of =if_back_wins= and =if_lay_wins=, every rounding against the person, so it is sure; =ideal= is the unrounded result. The page shows them as "Sure result" and "Ideal".

*Flip.* The sell side's fees are the =percent= fee model's arithmetic -- =cut=, =fixed= as =fixed_per_order=, =deposit=, =refundable= -- exactly as a venue naming that model would charge. =buy= and =sell= in different currencies are refused (the calculator has no exchange rate to use), and so is a =transfer= in another currency.

** From the command line

=venturectl arbitrage= is a thin client of these routes and of the actions: =scan [STRATEGY] [option=value ...]= (the table numbers its rows), =record ROW|KEY [STRATEGY] [option=value ...]= (the same question plus the row's key and =narrow=, posted to the record route), =record name=... legs=JSON|@FILE= and =close|reopen|abandon TRADE_ID=, =execute LEG_ID= (the =act= verb, typed from each action's schema), =calc surebet|back-lay|flip= (=--stake AMOUNT=), =export FORMAT [-o FILE]= and =registries=. Every writing verb takes =--stage=. =organization_id=N= on =scan=, =record= and =export= asks for that organization (the routes read it; see "Which organization" below); the CLI refuses =venture_id=, =as_of=, =key=, =format= and =calc= there (exit 2), which the scan's routes would ignore or drop. =--stake= is accepted only by =calc= and =-o= only by =export=. Whole-number options (=preset_id=, =data_source_id=, =recipe_id=, =units=, =buy_sources=, =max_age_hours=, =top=, =organization_id=) are sent as numbers and refused when they are not; everything else travels as text for the server to judge. A row past the end, a key no longer there or an unknown export format is exit 3. See [[file:cli.org][venturectl]].

** Which organization

Every =/api/v1/arbitrage= route answers for the organization its =organization_id= names -- in the query string, or for the record route in the JSON body with the question -- and for the active organization when it names none, which for an API token is always the default one. The organization is judged before anything is read: its own row is read under the caller's access scope, which every member may read and nobody else, so a stranger is told NOT_FOUND, exactly what reading its records would say. A recorded trade is then judged again by the =record= action, in that organization. The pages ignore the parameter and answer for the sidebar's pick. The feeds and market routes follow the same rule ([[file:market-data.org][Market data]]).

** Plugins: strategies, fee models and export formats

All three registries hang off the context, hold the built-ins first and take a plugin's additions with plain functions -- no GObject subclass -- from =venture_plugin_register()=. A name is lower case letters, digits and underscores, never registered twice, so a plugin cannot replace a built-in. The database is handed the context's fee model and strategy registries when the module installs (the last context over a database wins), which is what the venue and preset validators read.

A fee model is a compute function and an optional validator. The compute function is handed the venue's parameters, the side, the gross amount, the units, a reference (the sell venue's market value for all the units, in a scan) and the instrument's *attributes* as its source stored them -- what a model keyed on the thing itself reads. A scan looks the attributes up by the side's instrument key in that source's store (=venture_arbitrage_scan_fees()= takes the key); a calculator passes none. A shortened version of the Blizzard plugin's model:

#+begin_src C
/* 5% of the sale, and a refundable deposit of 60% of the item's vendor
 * price (48 hours listed), read from the instrument's attributes. */
static gboolean
wow_auction_compute(JsonObject *params, VentureFeeSide side, const VentureMoney *amount,
                    gint64 units, const VentureMoney *reference, JsonObject *attrs,
                    VentureFeeQuote *out, gpointer user_data, GError **error)
{
	gint64 vendor_sell;

	if (VENTURE_FEE_SIDE_BUY == side)
	{
		out->fee = venture_money_new(0, venture_money_get_currency(amount),
		                             venture_money_get_exponent(amount));
		return TRUE;
	}

	out->fee = venture_money_multiply_rational(amount, 5, 100, error);
	vendor_sell = (NULL != attrs) ? json_object_get_int_member_with_default(attrs, "vendor_sell", -1) : -1;

	if (vendor_sell >= 0)
	{
		g_autoptr(VentureMoney) vendor = venture_money_new(vendor_sell * units,
			venture_money_get_currency(amount), venture_money_get_exponent(amount));

		out->deposit = venture_money_multiply_rational(vendor, 60, 100, error);
		out->deposit_refundable = TRUE;
	}

	return NULL != out->fee;
}

venture_fee_model_registry_add(venture_context_get_fee_models(context), "wow_auction",
	"The auction house's 5% cut and its listing deposit",
	wow_auction_compute, NULL, NULL, NULL, error);
#+end_src

The real one, with its parameters (=cut_percent=, =duration_hours=, =deposit_factor_percent=) and their validation, is =plugins/blizzard-auctions/blizzard-plugin.c=; its =tsm= export format is beside it.

A strategy is a scan function and an optional plan. The scan reads the stores through the scan it is handed and adds what it finds; =venture_arbitrage_scan_add_flip()= prices a buy at one venue against a sell at another with every venue's fees, the transfer, the conversion, the relists, the figures, the confidence and the legs, so most strategies are a candidate search and one call. A crispy script:

#+begin_src C
#include <venture/venture.h>

gboolean venture_plugin_register(VentureContext *context, GError **error);

/* "vendor_flip": items whose lowest listing is under what a vendor pays
 * (the instrument's own attrs would say; here, a fixed table). */
static gboolean
vendor_flip_scan(VentureArbitrageScan *scan, gpointer user_data, GError **error)
{
	GPtrArray *sources = venture_arbitrage_scan_get_sources(scan);
	guint i;

	for (i = 0; i < sources->len; i++)
	{
		gint64 source_id = venture_entity_get_id(g_ptr_array_index(sources, i));
		VentureSeriesStore *store = venture_arbitrage_scan_open_store(scan, source_id);
		g_autoptr(VentureSeriesRow) row = NULL;
		g_autoptr(VentureMoney) price = NULL;
		g_autoptr(VentureMoney) vendor = NULL;
		VentureArbitrageSide buy;
		VentureArbitrageSide sell;
		VentureArbitrageMarket market;

		if ((NULL == store) ||
		    !venture_series_store_get_current(store, "realm-a", "2770", &row, error))
			continue;

		if ((NULL == row) || (row->quantity <= 0))
			continue;

		memset(&buy, 0, sizeof(buy));
		market.sale_rate = NAN;
		market.sold_per_day = NAN;
		market.dispersion = NAN;
		market.venues = 1;
		price = venture_money_new_for_currency(row->min_price, row->currency);
		vendor = venture_money_new_for_currency(150, row->currency);
		buy.data_source_id = source_id;
		buy.venue_key = row->venue_key;
		buy.instrument_key = row->instrument_key;
		buy.units = 1;
		buy.unit_price = price;
		buy.taken_at = row->taken_at;
		buy.depth = 1.0;
		sell = buy;
		sell.unit_price = vendor;

		if (!venture_arbitrage_scan_add_flip(scan, "vendor_flip:2770", "Copper Ore to a vendor",
		                                     &buy, &sell, &market, NULL, error))
			return FALSE;
	}

	return TRUE;
}

gboolean
venture_plugin_register(VentureContext *context, GError **error)
{
	return venture_arbitrage_strategy_registry_add(
		venture_context_get_arbitrage_strategies(context), "vendor_flip", "Vendor flips",
		"Items listed under what a vendor pays", NULL, vendor_flip_scan, NULL, NULL, NULL,
		error);
}

* Traps

- *Never make the leg rule touch stock.* It is built twice on every save; a rule that received or issued units would do it again each time. Execute does the stock side once and stamps it.
- *Never post a stock leg's principal from the rule.* A buy's cash was paid when its units were received; posting it again from the rule would spend it twice.
- *Never close by original currency.* Close per journal currency in book amounts, or a converted section never reaches zero.
- *Never write the trade from a leg validator.* Use the actions, which are the writers allowed to.
- *Tests that create a trade with executed legs from a purse must fund it first, and before the legs' dates* -- the floor is the running balance.
- *Never compare or add a scan's amounts across currencies.* Convert through =venture_arbitrage_scan_convert()= (the =exchange_rate= table) or skip with a note; a sort by profit groups by currency first.
- *Never read an unquoted price or fee as zero.* Blank the row and name it in =missing=; a blank row has no plan.
- *Never record an opportunity by any path but its plan and the =record= action.* A plan is made from a fresh run of the question, so what is recorded is what the data says now.
- *A route that drops or ignores a field must not be reached with it.* The scan's routes drop =key= and =format= and ignore =venture_id= and =as_of=; the calculator reads =format= by name, and the CLI refuses the others by name rather than send a question the route will answer differently. =organization_id= is read by the routes (=venture_web_request_organization()=), not by the scan.
- *Never show an =_ideal= figure as what a person gets.* A calculator's =payout=, =profit= and =worst= are rounded against the person; the unrounded ones say =ideal= in their name.
- *Never add a trade's legs to a sales or expense total.* The operational reports add finished trades through =venture_arbitrage_realised_totals()=, beside sales and expenses.
- *Every scan option is a report option.* A new one goes into the option table in =venture-arbitrage-scan.c=, the =arbitrage_scan= schema, and all five doors in the same commit.
- *A fee model or strategy name is judged when written, never when kept,* and only while the module is on.
- *Bound every read a strategy makes.* Choose candidates by an indexed query with a limit; never walk a region's rows.
- *Crafting and the planner compute nothing of their own.* Crafting is the =transform= row; the planner adds up the planned legs and the =expected= deposit and listing loss the scan wrote. A margin, a fee or a profit computed in either, or in a page, is a second formula that will disagree with the trade the plan records.
- *A plan is planned trades, not a record type of its own.* "Add to plan" is the =record= path; a parallel plan type would be a second way into the books that skips the plan and the action.
- *A recorded flip's =expected= is what the next run subtracts.* Never record a flip without it, never match again from the raw ledger (a sale merged larger later would book its lots a second time), and never let it be written by hand: the trade validator refuses a change to it on any trade with a =data_source_id=, outside the module's permit.
- *Flip totals are checked, never wrapped.* A sale's units, cost and proceeds, its profit, what recorded trades took from a row and the call's totals all add through =books_add()=; a sum past 64 bits refuses the call, and nothing is written.
- *A source books one way.* =record_flips= leaves out a flip touching a day posted a day at a time, and =post_ledger= a day whose flips are recorded; keep both checks on the data, not only on the =books= setting, which can change.
