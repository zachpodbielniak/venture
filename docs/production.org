* Stock that becomes other stock

Some ventures buy things and sell them. Others turn what they bought into
something else first: a workshop builds a lamp from a shade, a base and a
cord; a kitchen turns flour, butter and eggs into a dozen pastries; a
crafting profession in a game turns two herbs and a vial into a potion. The
=production= module keeps that step, generically, in two record types and
one action:

- =recipe= :: a bill of materials. One batch makes =output_quantity= units
  of one product.
- =recipe_component= :: one line of it: a product, how many one batch
  takes, and whether it is used up or only has to be there (=reusable=: a
  tool, a mould, a catalyst).
- =craft= :: the action on a recipe that does it -- consumes the
  components, produces the output, in one transaction, through the
  inventory service. The made units carry what the consumed components
  cost, so selling them later books the right cost of goods sold.

And one report, =recipe_margin=: what a batch costs and makes at a chosen
price source, profit and margin, cost per unit, and how many batches the
stock on hand allows now.

The same records describe:

- *A manufacturer or workshop*: a recipe is a build sheet; =reusable=
  marks the jig and the mould; the output lands in the finished-goods
  location.
- *A kitchen or bakery*: a recipe is a recipe card with a batch size; the
  margin at the supplier's prices says which items are worth baking.
- *A crafting profession in a game*: a recipe is a recipe; locations are
  characters and banks; prices come from the auction house's observed
  market value (the [[file:market.org][market module]]), and the margin
  report is the "is this worth crafting" sheet.

The module is =production=. It requires =sales=, which owns products,
inventory items and inventory transactions; switching =sales= off switches
=production= off with it. It suggests =market= (observed prices for the
margin report) and =goods= (the FIFO cost layers a made unit's cost comes
from). See [[file:modules.org][Modules]].

* Recipes

** Fields

| Field               | Kind      | For                                                              |
|---------------------+-----------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =name=              | string    | what you call it; required                                       |
| =venture_id=        | reference | optional: the venture that makes it                              |
| =output_product_id= | reference | the product one batch makes; required                            |
| =output_quantity=   | integer   | units one batch makes; at least 1                                |
| =category_id=       | reference | optional: where it is filed ([[file:taxonomy.org][Taxonomy]]); the category's =applies_to= must be empty or =recipe= |
| =active=            | boolean   | can be crafted; an inactive recipe keeps its history             |
| =notes=             | text      |                                                                  |

=active= has no default an API call can supply: a recipe created through
the API or =venturectl= without =active=true= is inactive, is not offered a
Craft button, is refused by the action and is left out of =recipe_margin=.
The form starts the box ticked, as it does for every =active= field.

** What the save refuses

The validator (=src/production/venture-production.c=) runs for every writer
-- the form, the API, an approved staged change, the assistant:

- no output product (=output_product_id= 0). It is declared =NOT_NULL=,
  but the generic check covers strings and times only;
- a batch size below 1;
- an output product or venture in another organization (only when the
  reference is being written, the rule every reference follows);
- an existing recipe changed to make a product it already takes as a
  component.

* Components

** Fields

| Field        | Kind      | For                                                           |
|--------------+-----------+---------------------------------------------------------------|
| =recipe_id=  | reference | the recipe the line belongs to; required                      |
| =product_id= | reference | what the recipe takes; required                               |
| =quantity=   | integer   | units one batch needs; at least 1                             |
| =reusable=   | boolean   | a tool or catalyst: must be on hand, is not used up           |
| =notes=      | text      |                                                               |

The flag is named for the exception on purpose. A boolean has no default,
so its zero value must be the safe one: a line whose box nobody ticked is
*consumed*, and stock goes down. Spelled the other way round (=consumed=,
false meaning "a tool"), every forgotten tick would be free materials.

** What the save refuses

- no recipe or no product (the =NOT_NULL= integers read 0 unset);
- a quantity below 1;
- a line in another organization than its recipe, or a product in
  another organization;
- the recipe's own output: a craft would consume what it makes;
- a second line for a product the recipe already takes. It is refused
  rather than merged: merging would silently change a quantity somebody
  typed, and which of two lines' =reusable= flags wins would be a guess.
  The message names the existing line and says to change its quantity.

Deeper loops (A takes B, B takes A) are allowed: each craft is one level,
and neither can be made without the other already on the shelf.

The recipe's page lists its components under related records, with New
to add one; there is no bespoke components block. The Craft form is in
the page's Actions card, generated from the action's parameters.

* Crafting

** What one craft does

=craft= is a record action on =recipe=. Its parameters:

| Parameter     | Kind                 | Meaning                                                              |
|---------------+----------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =times=       | integer, 1–1 000 000 | how many batches; 1 when left out                                    |
| =location_id= | reference (location) | where to take components from and put the output                    |
| =occurred_at= | datetime             | when it was made; now when left out                                  |

In one database transaction:

1. The recipe must be live and =active=, and must have at least one
   component (something from nothing is stock arriving; record it as an
   adjustment).
2. Every component's inventory item is found (below). A consumed
   component needs =quantity × times= on hand, unless its item allows
   negative stock. A reusable one needs its =quantity= on hand -- once,
   whatever =times= is: one still makes as many batches as the herbs allow
   -- and *always* has to be there, whatever its item allows:
   =allow_negative= is for counts that lag behind the shelf, and a tool
   nobody has is not a tool that has not been counted yet.
3. The output's inventory item is found the same way. None is refused,
   naming what to create; the craft does not invent an item (with no
   SKU, venture or reorder point) on your behalf.
4. =venture_inventory_service_produce()= writes a negative =production=
   transaction per consumed component, at its FIFO cost, and one positive
   =production= transaction for =output_quantity × times= units of the
   output, then gives the output cost layers carrying exactly what was
   consumed. Reusable components are not touched.

Every transaction's =reference= is =recipe:<id>=, so "everything recipe 12
ever made or used" is one filter. A refusal, or a failure at any step --
the last write included -- rolls every write back: nothing is consumed
without something being made.

The action returns the output's =inventory_txn=. From the page, a
successful craft lands on that transaction.

** Which inventory item

=venture_production_find_stock()= picks the one item holding a product in
the recipe's organization:

- with =location_id=, the item for that product *at exactly that
  location* -- not its children: "the bank" and "a bag in the bank" are two
  places;
- without it, the only item for that product there is.

None is refused ("No stock of Copper Bar is kept at Bank; create an
inventory item for it there (product_id=… location_id=…)"); more than one
is refused naming every location and its id, so the fix is one parameter.
One =location_id= applies to every component and to the output: a craft
happens in one place. To craft from the bank into a bag, craft at the
bank and transfer.

** Cost

The made units carry what the consumed components cost, taken FIFO from
their cost layers:

- *Split exactly.* 10.00 over 6 units is 4 units at 1.67 and 2 at 1.66,
  two layers that sum to 10.00. A single layer at the rounded average
  would leave a cent behind (or invent one) every craft, and the cost of
  goods sold when the potions sell would drift from what went into them.
  The output transaction shows the rounded average for reading; the layers
  are what the books count.
- *No journal.* Stock moved from inventory to inventory; account 1200 is
  unchanged and =inventory_valuation= is the same before and after.
- *Units with no layer cost nothing.* Stock typed in by hand as an
  adjustment has no cost layer; with =allow_negative=, stock below zero has
  none either. Those units are consumed at no cost, and the output carries
  only what was recorded. If no consumed unit carried a cost at all, the
  output still gets a zero-cost layer (in its item's unit-cost currency,
  else the organization's book currency) -- an issue refuses units with no
  layer, so without one the made units could never be sold.
- *Every currency the inputs cost.* Inputs costing GOLD and TICKET make
  units that cost both, never one number added across them (no rate was
  given to make it one). The output gets *sibling layers*, the exact split
  of each currency, all naming the craft's output transaction as their
  lot; selling a made unit later takes its share of every currency at
  once, so 3 charms from 1 GOLD and 50 TICKET sell for 0.3334 GOLD + 17
  TICKET, then 0.6666 GOLD + 33 TICKET. Zero-cost inputs (a posted
  session's goods) add no layer beside costed ones. The output transaction
  shows no unit cost when it cost several currencies; its notes say what
  each came to. See [[file:inventory.org][Inventory]], "Costing in several
  currencies". (Before lots, such a craft was refused.)
- *Without the goods module* there are no cost layers: a craft moves
  quantities only, as every other stock movement does on such an install.

** Staging

=craft= is stageable. Unlike the factory's rollback (two writes the queue
would have to hold as records), a staged action holds only its parameters,
and approval performs the whole craft afresh in one transaction --
recounting stock as it stands at approval, not as it stood when proposed.
A proposal that was affordable when made and is short by the time it is
approved is refused at approval, and nothing is written.

** Organization

A record action is judged in its record's organization, so the recipe
places the craft: the access policy checks the recipe, and every item is
looked up in the recipe's organization. No =organization_id= parameter is
needed, or accepted.

* Reports

** =recipe_margin=

One row per active recipe: what one batch costs and makes, at a price
source.

| Option            | Meaning                                                                          |
|-------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =price_source=    | price at the latest observation from this source, matched exactly; any source when left out. Needs the market module. =series:<basis>[@<venue or group>]= prices from market data feeds instead (below) |
| =as_of=           | prices and stock as they stood at this date; now by default                     |
| =currency=        | only prices observed in this currency count; left out, the book currency's price wins wherever the product was seen in it, else the newest in any. See [[file:market.org][Market]] |
| =venture_id=      | only this venture's recipes                                                      |
| =category_id=     | only recipes filed in this category *or beneath it*                             |
| =organization_id= | the legal entity; the default organization otherwise                             |

*Pricing.* With the market module on, every figure comes from
=venture_market_latest_price()= -- the newest observation at or before
=as_of= from =price_source=, in the =currency= option's currency when one
is named and otherwise in the book currency wherever the product was seen
priced in it (=venture_market_price_preferring()=), so a gold organization's
margin is not worked out in tickets because tickets were seen last. Nothing else: falling back to a recorded cost
for a product the source never priced would mix two kinds of number in one
margin without saying which. With the market module off, the recorded
figures: a component at its first inventory item's =unit_cost=, else the
product's =cost=; the output at the product's =list_price=. =price_source=
with the market off is refused rather than ignored. The =priced_by= column
says which.

*Pricing from market data.* A =price_source= of the form =series:<basis>[@<venue or group>]= -- =series:min@argent-dawn=, =series:region_median@eu=, =series:market_14d= -- is not an observation source: it is parsed first, and asks the price oracle for each product through the instruments that name it (see [[file:market-data.org][Market data]], "The price oracle"). The basis is a price basis (=min=, =market=, =market_14d=, =historical_60d=, =region_median=, =region_p33=, =region_market_avg=, =sale_avg=); the figure is as of =as_of=, strictly in the =currency= option's currency when one is named and otherwise preferring the book currency, and never converted. The never-fall-back rule holds here too: a product no instrument prices is named in the note and its figures left blank, even when an observation of it exists. It needs the marketdata and feeds modules (in a build with SQLite); without them the report is refused, naming what is off.

*Where to buy, not what it costs.* =recipe_margin= prices every component on one basis at one place. Which venue each input is cheapest at, after fees, transfer costs and the depth of each venue's book, is a different question, and the arbitrage module answers it: its =transform= strategy and the =craft_arbitrage= report walk every allowed venue for each input and price the output where it sells best, treating a reusable component as bought once exactly as =goal_materials= does. See [[file:arbitrage.org::*=transform=][Arbitrage: transform]] and [[file:examples/arbitrage-crafting.org][the crafting walkthrough]].

| Column          | What                                                                     |
|-----------------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =recipe=        | the recipe's name                                                        |
| =makes=         | the output product                                                       |
| =batch=         | =output_quantity=                                                        |
| =currency=      | the one currency every figure is in                                      |
| =cost=          | Σ consumed components' unit price × quantity, per batch                  |
| =value=         | output unit price × batch                                                |
| =profit=        | value − cost                                                             |
| =margin=        | profit ÷ value; empty when value is zero                                 |
| =cost_per_unit= | cost ÷ batch, one division rounded half to even                          |
| =craftable_now= | the fewest whole batches any consumed component allows, 0 when a tool is missing |
| =priced_by=     | the source, "prices seen, any source", or "recorded costs"               |
| =note=          | missing prices, mixed currencies, no components                         |

*A missing price is named, not zero.* A component nobody priced is not
free and an output nobody priced is not worthless: the note names them and
the figures that depend on them are blank. What can still be said is --
the cost when only the output is unpriced, the value when only an input
is.

*One currency per row.* A recipe whose components and output are priced in
two currencies gets "priced in GOLD, USD; no margin across currencies" and
no money figures at all, never a sum of both.

*Reusable components* are not in the cost (they are not used up) but gate
=craftable_now=. On-hand counts every location; a craft itself takes from
one, so a recipe whose herbs are split across two characters may read 3
here and still need a transfer first.

A worked example. Herbs seen at 1.50, a vial at 0.40, a potion at 2.00;
the recipe takes 2 herbs, a vial and a still (reusable), and makes 3
potions:

| Figure        | Arithmetic                  | Value  |
|---------------+-----------------------------+--------|
| cost          | 2 × 1.50 + 1 × 0.40         | 3.40   |
| value         | 3 × 2.00                    | 6.00   |
| profit        | 6.00 − 3.40                 | 2.60   |
| margin        | 2.60 ÷ 6.00                 | 43.3%  |
| cost per unit | 3.40 ÷ 3, half to even      | 1.13   |
| craftable now | min(10 herbs ÷ 2, 4 vials ÷ 1), still on hand | 4 |

** Where it is asked from

=/reports/recipe_margin= (with a form for =price_source= and =as_of=),
=GET /api/v1/reports/recipe_margin?…=, =venturectl report=, the assistant's
and MCP's =venture_report= tool. =price_source= and =category_id= were
added to all five doors in the same change.

It refuses, naming the bound, more than =VENTURE_AGGREGATE_MAX_ROWS=
active recipes rather than answering for some. The bound counts the
question, not the organization: =active=, =venture_id= and =category_id=
(as an =IN= over =venture_category_descendants()=) are filters in the
query, so an organization with more recipes than the bound is answered
once it is narrowed under it. They once ran in C over a capped fetch of
every recipe, and a large organization was refused whatever it asked --
told to narrow what it had narrowed. =/production/margin/bound= pins this
with the bound lowered through =venture_aggregate_set_max_rows()=.

* API and CLI

#+begin_src sh
venturectl describe recipe
venturectl describe recipe_component

# The bill of materials
venturectl create recipe name="Healing Potion" output_product_id=14 \
    output_quantity=3 active=true
venturectl create recipe_component recipe_id=3 product_id=11 quantity=2
venturectl create recipe_component recipe_id=3 product_id=12 quantity=1
venturectl create recipe_component recipe_id=3 product_id=13 quantity=1 reusable=true

# Crafting: act types each argument from the action's schema
venturectl act recipe 3 craft times=5
venturectl act recipe 3 craft times=5 location_id=2 occurred_at=2026-03-14T18:00:00Z
venturectl --stage act recipe 3 craft times=20     # propose; approval crafts
curl -X POST .../api/v1/recipe/3/actions/craft -d '{"times":5,"location_id":2}'

# Everything recipe 3 made or used
venturectl list inventory_txn reference=recipe:3

# The margin sheet
venturectl report recipe_margin all price_source="market value"
venturectl report recipe_margin all category_id=4 as_of=2026-03-01
#+end_src

On the wire the fields use underscores (=output_product_id=,
=output_quantity=); a POST with dashed keys is silently ignored field by
field. Action parameters are typed: ={"times":"5"}= is refused, and
=venturectl act= and the page's form send an integer.

The sidebar files *Recipes* under Operations.

* Traps

- *=reusable=, not =consumed=.* The zero value must be the safe one; a
  forgotten tick consumes. Do not add a =consumed= alias.
- *A tool must really be on hand.* =allow_negative= does not apply to a
  reusable component. And it is needed once, not once per batch.
- *One line per product.* Duplicates are refused, not merged.
- *The craft is one transaction, and so is the produce underneath it.*
  Do not issue components through =venture_inventory_service_issue()=: it
  posts cost of goods sold for stock that is still on the shelf, as a
  potion.
- *The output's layers are split exactly.* Never replace them with one
  layer at the rounded average, and never add two currencies into one
  layer: a two-currency craft's layers share =lot_txn_id=, and FIFO takes
  a lot's units out of every currency together.
- *The craft does not create the output's inventory item.* It refuses and
  says what to create.
- *=recipe_margin= never falls back.* Market on means observed prices
  only; a missing one is named, not replaced by a recorded cost or zero.
- *An API-created recipe needs =active=true=.* Otherwise it is invisible to
  the report and refused by the action.
