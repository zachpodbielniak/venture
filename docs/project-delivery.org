#+title: Sales to project delivery
#+description: Retained scope, ordinary tickets, acceptance and shared invoicing

* Operator workflow

Enable Projects and the existing Quotes, Tickets and Invoicing capabilities.
On an accepted quote or won deal, choose *Start client project*, entering its
name, responsible username and agreed scope. A quote creates a fixed-price
project; a won deal creates a time-and-materials project. The sales document
must identify a live customer company and an explicit nonnegative amount.
The owner is a responsibility label, not an organization permission grant.

The project and retained =project_scope= are created together. Its original
sales identity, currency and customer cannot be edited through generic forms.
Repeating an identical handoff returns that project; changed parameters under
the same sales identity are refused. Use project actions for later decisions.
Once a deal has a project, handing off its linked quote cannot create another
one. Choose the quote handoff for a fixed contract, or the won-deal handoff for
time-and-materials delivery; that choice is retained rather than silently
changing the project's billing model.

*Plan delivery* takes a stable request key, title, agreement ID, due date and
optional fixed amount. It creates an ordinary ticket and =project_deliverable=
link atomically. Use the ticket page for description, assignment, worklogs and
status. A repeated key with identical parameters returns the same delivery;
reusing it for different work is a conflict. There is no second task engine.

Finish the ticket, then *Accept delivery* with the acceptance evidence.
Acceptance stores time and audit actor and cannot be rewritten. It is
staff-recorded evidence, not a new customer portal permission. Reopening a
ticket does not erase historical acceptance. Ordinary escaped record rendering
displays the text; the service never executes it or fetches embedded links.

For a progress-billed fixed agreement, *Invoice delivery* uses the accepted
slice and the existing progress/settlement services. Replay returns the same
invoice, never a second invoice or a receipt. Quotes accepted with full
billing already have an invoice: use that invoice. Handoff associates prior
full/progress invoices with the project. Subsequent quote-side progress
invoices create the same project allocation inside their transaction.
If an accepted slice was invoiced directly from its quote, use *Associate
issued progress invoice*. It verifies the agreement, organization and exact
amount and refuses an invoice already allocated to another delivery. The
association creates no invoice or accounting entry. A retained project
agreement cannot be newly invoiced with Projects disabled; enable it first
so the allocation and invoice remain one transaction.

Approved time/cost remain actual-cost evidence on fixed-price projects; the
time-and-materials billing action refuses them to prevent charging both the
contract and its labour. Time projects use existing rates/time approval and
*Invoice approved work*. Each project has one billing model; fixed change
orders require a fixed project. Retainer/retention accounting remains in the
existing progress service, with explicit links. Receipts, credits, bank
reconciliation and period close retain their existing services and controls.

* Changes and completion

*Add accepted change order* takes a separately accepted quote for the same
customer and currency. Scope and value are appended, increasing budget once;
replay cannot add the price again or transfer the quote to another project.
It is an additive agreement, not replacement of the original. Reductions and
corrections use existing credit/correction accounting and retain signed scope.

*Update delivery ownership and status* records owner, status and reason; audit
history keeps previous decisions. Active projects accept planning, changes and
acceptance; paused projects do not. Completion requires at least one delivery
and acceptance of every delivery. Completed/cancelled projects are terminal:
new work needs another project. Already accepted work can still be invoiced
and existing invoices remain payable. Cancellation neither reverses books nor
silently writes off debt.

* Reporting and retention

The [[file:projects.org][profitability contract]] applies. Fixed-project unbilled amounts are accepted
delivery slices dated by =accepted_at=, excluding those invoiced before period
end. Labour's sales rate is not added again. Labour/pass-through actual cost
keeps its occurrence date and explicit unknown-cost behavior. Billed amounts
are immutable allocations dated by invoice issue, whether invoiced through
the quote or delivery. Full-billed scope is not counted again as unbilled.

Scope, delivery and billing rows are service-owned evidence. Generic create,
edit, delete, restore and purge are refused. Sales-linked projects retain their
identity and history. Migration =000480= adds metadata columns and checks
their presence; it guesses no historical scope, owner, acceptance or link.

* Shared actions and limits

Buttons, authenticated REST, CLI and staged proposals use the same registered
actions. Approval rechecks permissions and current state. Inspect
=venturectl describe TYPE= for schemas; REST uses
=POST /api/v1/TYPE/ID/actions/NAME= with typed JSON and underscore names.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act quote 12 handoff name=Website owner=alex 'scope=Launch agreed design'
venturectl act client_project 3 plan_work key=design title=Design scope_id=4 due=2026-10-01 'amount=400 USD'
venturectl act project_deliverable 8 accept 'evidence=Customer approved design'
venturectl act project_deliverable 8 bill
venturectl act project_deliverable 9 link_invoice invoice_id=21
venturectl act client_project 3 change_scope quote_id=15 'scope=Add reporting'
venturectl act client_project 3 manage owner=alex status=completed 'reason=All deliveries accepted'
#+end_src

Names/titles are bounded to 256 UTF-8 bytes, usernames/keys to 128, and
scope/reason/acceptance text to 65,536. Dates and money use existing parsers;
money never passes through floating point.

ordinary tickets, acceptance refusal, exact invoicing and additive budget.
Initial handoff, planning, management and change tests each failed on missing
actions. The fixed-delivery report failed at unbilled 0 versus 40000; the direct
progress test failed at billed 0 versus 25000. These pin business outcomes.
