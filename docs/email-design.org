#+title: Designed email

A designed email is a mail template built from blocks, in the business's
own brand, instead of typed-in HTML. The assistant can design one from a
few plain words; a person can fine-tune it block by block. It is the same
=mail_template= record and the same template renderer every sender
already uses -- invoices, reminders, report packs and campaigns all send
what the design says, with no second template system beside it.

Everything here is in the =mail= module and is changed only by an owner
or administrator of the organization. Other members can read it.

* Brand settings

One =email_brand= record per organization (=/e/email_brand=):

| Field | What it is |
|-------+------------|
| Business name | Shown in the header when there is no logo, and in the footer |
| Logo | One of the organization's hosted images |
| Primary colour | Headings and buttons, written =#rrggbb= |
| Accent colour | A second button colour |
| Text and background colours | The words, and the surround of the message |
| Dark-mode colours | Primary, text and background in dark mode; left empty, they are worked out from the light ones (the primary is lightened until it reads on the dark card) |
| Font | =sans=, =serif=, =rounded=, =system= or =mono=: each a stack of faces every computer has, ending in a generic fallback, because mail apps cannot load web fonts reliably |
| Website | The business's own address, used for the header logo's link and offered to the designer |
| Postal address | Shown in the footer |

An organization with no brand gets a plain look: neutral colours, a
sans-serif stack and its name in place of a logo.

After a brand or a shared section changes, a template picks it up the
next time it is saved. To rebuild every designed template at once:
=venturectl act mail_template rebuild_designs organization_id=N=.

* Hosted images

Pictures in email have to be on a public address: a mail app fetching
one sends no cookie and no password. *Upload an image* on the builder
page (or =POST /email-design/images=, multipart, fields =file=, =alt=
and =organization_id=) stores it and gives it an address like

#+begin_example
https://books.example.com/i/1/ghjk...wxyz.png
#+end_example

- PNG, JPEG, GIF and WebP only, up to 5 MB and 8000 pixels on a side.
  What the file is, and its width and height, are read from its bytes --
  never from the name or the type the browser declared. SVG is refused:
  it can carry script.
- The last part of the address is 32 random characters (160 bits) and is
  the only thing that makes an image findable. It works only under its
  own organization's number; another number answers /not found/.
- Served with =Cache-Control: public, max-age=31536000, immutable= and
  =X-Content-Type-Options: nosniff=. An image never changes under its
  address -- a new upload is a new address -- and a deleted one stops
  being served.
- The bytes are kept in the ordinary attachment store as a document; the
  =hosted_image= record holds the token and what was read from the file,
  and nothing but the upload can make or change one.

A product's =image_url= can be a hosted image's address, which is how the
designer finds photos of what the business sells.

* Blocks

A design is JSON on the template's =design= field:

#+begin_src json
{"preheader": "Book a tune-up by Nov 15 and save 10%.",
 "blocks": [
   {"type": "header"},
   {"type": "text", "heading": "Get ready for fall", "body": "First paragraph.\n\nSecond."},
   {"type": "button", "label": "Book now", "url": "https://brightside.example.com/book"},
   {"type": "footer"}]}
#+end_src

| Block | Fields |
|-------+--------|
| =header= | =show_logo=, =tagline=, =align= -- the logo, or the business name |
| =text= | =heading=, =body= (blank line between paragraphs), =align= |
| =image= | =image_id= (a hosted image) or =src= (an address), =alt= (required), =width= (16-600), =link= |
| =button= | =label=, =url=, =size= (small, medium, large), =align=, =color= (primary, accent) |
| =columns= | =columns=: two or three, each ={"blocks": [...]}= of text, image, button or divider blocks; they stack on a phone |
| =divider= | -- |
| =social= | =links=: up to eight ={"network", "url"}= (facebook, instagram, x, linkedin, youtube, tiktok, pinterest, website) |
| =video= | =url= (the page where it plays), =poster_image_id= or =poster_src=, =alt=, =caption= |
| =footer= | =text=, =address= (the brand's address when left out) |
| =section= | =section_id= -- a shared section, put in place |

Validation is strict: an unknown block type or field, a missing required
field, a size out of bounds, more than 40 blocks, more than 64 KB, or any
address that is not =http= or =https= with a host is refused with a
sentence saying which block and why. Text may hold placeholders such as
={first_name}=, filled from the record when the email is sent; a literal
brace is written ={{=.

** What the HTML is

The renderer writes for the mail apps people use: table layout, every
style inline, a body 600 pixels wide that narrows on a phone, buttons that
are padded links (they work with images off and in Outlook), alt text and
a width on every image, a =color-scheme= meta tag and a
=prefers-color-scheme: dark= block with the brand's dark colours (and the
same rules for Outlook.com's own darkening), and Outlook conditional
comments where they are cheap. There is no script, no form and no linked
style sheet. The plain-text alternative is written from the same blocks:
headings, paragraphs, every button and link as =Label: address=.

** Video

No mail app plays video inside a message. A video block is a poster -- a
picture with a play button drawn over it -- that links to the page where
the video plays, with the caption as a text link beneath it. Outlook for
Windows, which cannot draw a picture behind a button, gets the poster as
an ordinary linked picture.

** Dark mode

The dark block applies in apps that honour =prefers-color-scheme=. Some
apps ignore it and some invert a message's colours on their own; no
email can control that. A logo, usually dark ink on nothing, sits on a
light chip in dark mode so it does not disappear.

* Shared sections

An =email_section= is a saved group of blocks -- a header, a footer, a
signature -- that designs name with ={"type": "section", "section_id": N}=
and that is rendered in place. Sections may name sections, four deep. A
section that would include itself, through any chain, is refused when it
is saved; so is a design naming a section the organization does not have.

* Versions and undo

Every change to a design -- by hand, by the designer from a brief, or by
an instruction -- is saved as an =email_design_version=: its number, the
design exactly as it was, what made it (=manual=, =ai=, =ai-edit=) and the
brief or instruction. *Undo the last change* puts the template back to the
version before its current one, byte for byte, and its bodies are rebuilt
to exactly what they were. Versions stay in the history; the next change
is numbered after the newest one. Only the design service writes versions.

* The builder

=/email-design= lists designed templates and starts a new one;
=/email-design/ID= is the builder: a brief box, a box for changes in
words, *Undo*, the blocks in order with up, down, remove and an editor for
each block's JSON, *Add a block*, image upload, and the live preview --
the same frames and checks as [[file:mail.org::*Previews and pre-send checks][the mail preview]], phone width in light and
dark. Each action is an htmx post that swaps the builder in place; without
script it is an ordinary form. The full preview, every width in both
schemes, is =/mail/preview/email-design/ID=.

A new block starts from what the organization has -- its newest image,
its website. A button or social block with no website to point at is
refused rather than filled with an address nobody gave.

* The AI designer

Write what the email is for in plain words -- "fall furnace tune-up, 10%
off, ends Nov 15" -- in the builder's brief box, or ask the assistant,
which is offered the =mail_template= actions =design_email=,
=edit_design= and =undo_design= (always staged for a person to approve).

The designer gathers the organization's brand (name, colours, font,
website, address, whether it has a logo), its hosted images and up to
twenty products with their prices, links and photos, and sends them to
the configured AI provider as data, on the toolless path, between =BEGIN
RECORDS= and =END RECORDS=. What comes back must be a subject and a design;
it is then held to the following, in code:

- The same strict validation as a design typed by hand.
- Every address in it must appear in the brief or the records, and every
  image must be one of the organization's.
- *The grounding rule.* Every number in its words -- a price, an amount,
  a percentage, a date -- and the word "free" must appear in the brief
  or the records. A sentence in a paragraph or footer that breaks it is
  removed, and the builder says which and why; one in a heading, a
  button, a caption, alt text or the subject refuses the whole design and
  nothing is saved. The check does no arithmetic: "10% off $129.00"
  written as "$116.10" is a number nobody gave, so it goes. That is the
  safe way to be wrong.

With no logo the design uses the plain look, and the designer asks once
for a logo -- once per organization. After it has asked, or after *No
logo, thanks*, or once a logo is chosen, it never asks again.

Changes by words ("make the button bigger", "swap the photo", "shorter")
send the current design with the instruction and come back under the
same rules as a new version, so *Undo* reverses any of them.

* Rendering in mail apps: what is and is not checked

No test runs Gmail, Outlook or Apple Mail. =tests/test-email-design.c=
holds every rendered message to a structural check instead: table layout,
inline styles present, no script, form or linked style sheet, nothing
wider than 600 pixels, alt text and a width on every image, the dark-mode
block and colour-scheme tag, Outlook conditionals, and a plain-text
alternative of real length. It says the message is built from what every
major client renders; it cannot say how a given client draws it. Look at
the preview's frames, and send yourself a test, before a large send.
