* Who sent whom

The =referrals= module requires =crm= and =leads=. It owns four record
types, and everything about them -- tables, REST resources, CLI commands,
forms, lists, audit -- derives from their field tables:

| Type | What it is |
|------+------------|
| =referral_program= | The reward an organization gives, and the page a shared link points at |
| =referral_code= | A customer's or contact's shareable code, and its link |
| =referral= | One person sent by one referrer, following their lead to a won customer |
| =referral_reward= | What the referrer was given for one referral, or why it was not |

It suggests =receivables= and =billing=, which pay the two kinds of reward,
and =forms= and =attribution=, whose captures carry a code in. Switching
the module off hides all four types and the =referrals= report; a lead
saved with a code then saves exactly as it did before, and nothing is
attributed.

* Programs and codes

A program belongs to one organization. Its =reward_kind= is =none= (track,
give nothing -- the zero value, so a program written without choosing
pays nobody), =credit= (a credit note of =reward_amount=, which must be
positive) or =free_period= (the referrer's next subscription invoice is
credited in full). =landing_url= is an http or https address; a code's
link is that page with =ref=CODE= added. Only an =active= program's codes
attribute anything.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl create referral_program name=Friends organization_id=1 active=true \
  reward_kind=credit reward_amount='25 USD' landing_url=https://shop.example/join
#+end_src

A code names its referrer -- a customer (=company_id=), a contact
(=contact_id=), or both -- and a program. Left blank, the code is made:
eight characters with no 0/O or 1/I to misread. A typed code is trimmed
and stored in upper case; it is 3 to 32 letters, digits, hyphens or
underscores, and unique within the organization, counting deleted codes.
Arriving codes are matched without regard to case. With no program named
and exactly one active program in the organization, that one is used;
otherwise the save says to name one. A =retired= code attributes nothing;
unticked is the zero value, so a new code works. The =link= is derived on
every save, so a program whose page moved updates its codes the next time
each is saved.

Each customer gets their code from the =referral_code= action on the
company or the contact, which returns the existing code or makes it:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act company 12 referral_code
venturectl act contact 40 referral_code program_id=3
#+end_src

* A lead arriving with a code

A lead carries =referral_code=, the code as it arrived. Every writer of
leads sets it the same way, because it is a lead field and a capture
input:

- the generic API, CLI and CSV import (=venturectl create lead ... referral_code=AB3K9QZT=)
- a lead form's public capture (=POST /f/TOKEN= with =referral_code=), subject
  to the form's =fields= list
- a form with =create_lead= on, from a question whose =maps_to= is
  =referral_code= -- usually a hidden question keyed =ref= with
  =allow_prefill= on, so a program whose =landing_url= is the hosted form
  fills it from the shared link's =?ref==, with nothing typed
- the signed Lightsite form relay, as =referral_code= beside =answers=
  (see [[file:forms.org][Forms]]), which a mapped question's answer outranks
- the Lightsite site capture, as a mapped =referral_code= field (see
  [[file:attribution.org][Attribution]])

When the lead is saved, a code that matches a live, unretired code in an
active program of the lead's organization makes a =referral= naming the
lead, the code, its referrer and its program, in the same transaction as
the lead. A code that matches nothing is kept on the lead as it arrived
and attributes nothing: refusing it would lose the inquiry. A capture that
merges into an existing lead fills that lead's empty code and never
replaces one -- whoever sent them first sent them -- and a capture that
matches an existing contact or company attributes nothing, since they are
already known.

A referral may also be recorded by hand, for a customer who never was a
lead: name a referrer (a company, a contact, or a =code_id=, which then
decides the referrer and program) and who was referred (=lead_id=,
=company_id= or =contact_id=). A customer cannot be recorded as referring
themselves, and a lead is referred at most once.

* Status follows the lead

With a lead, a referral's =status= is the lead's: =pending= while the lead
is new, working or recycled, =qualified= when it is qualified, =lost= when
unqualified and =won= when it converts. A status typed on the referral
that disagrees is put back, not refused, because a form posts every field
back. Conversion fills the referral's =company_id= and =contact_id= with
the customer and contact it made. =won_at= is stamped when the referral is
won, kept when given and cleared on leaving won. Without a lead the status
is whatever was recorded.

* Rewards

A referral that becomes =won= in a program with a reward gets exactly one
=referral_reward=. It names the referrer (=company_id=; for a referring
contact, the contact's company) and the customer won
(=referred_company_id=), so it appears on both records, and the referral it
was for. The organization-scoped unique index on =referral_id=, counting
deleted rows, is what makes it once only.

- =credit= writes a =customer_credit= of kind =credit_note= for
  =reward_amount= to the referrer, through receivables' ordinary posting and
  approvals, and records it as =credit_id=.
- =free_period= credits the referrer's oldest subscription that will renew
  (trialing, active or past due, not ending at renewal) with what its next
  period will charge -- price, seats and discount -- through the billing
  service. The credit is carried as the subscription's pending adjustment,
  so the renewal invoice is issued and paid by a credit note in the same
  step. The reward records the =subscription_id= and the amount.

The reward is given after the transaction that won the customer commits,
in a transaction of its own. A credit note can be refused by the books or
an approval rule, and winning the customer must not depend on paying the
referrer: a conversion whose reward cannot be given still converts. Such a
reward is saved with =status= =failed= and =failure= saying why -- no
subscription, the module off, an approval required. Fix the cause and run
its =apply= action, which re-reads the row and refuses a reward that was
given already:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act referral_reward 7 apply
#+end_src

A given reward cannot be changed or deleted; what it gave is in the books,
and a correction goes there.

* Report

=referrals= covers referrals made in the period, one row per referrer:
referrals, pending, qualified, won, lost, the conversion rate (won as a
share of all their referrals) and the rewards given for those referrals
with their value. A referrer rewarded in two currencies gets a row per
currency with the counts repeated; the metrics keep the book currency's
value under =reward_value= and every other currency's under
=reward_value_CODE=. Past the aggregate row bound it refuses and says to
choose a shorter period.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report referrals --period this-year
#+end_src
