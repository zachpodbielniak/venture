#+description: Personal and household finance as a slimmed-down venture type

* Status

from the responsive household walkthrough. It does not complete or close the
epic. Publication scope is "household finance workflows, children 1–9";
use =Refs #201=, never =Closes #201= for this delivery.

* What a household venture is

A venture of type =household=: the same ledger, accounts and reports as any
other venture, with navigation and words for a home instead of a business.
Business ventures in the same organization are unchanged.

* Built from what exists

- Accounts and the ledger ([[file:ledger.org][ledger]]) hold checking, savings, cards, cash, loans and investments.
- Bank feeds ([[file:bankfeed.org][bankfeed]]) and file import bring transactions in; categorisation rules sort them.
- Budgets ([[file:budgets.org][budgets]]) become monthly category or envelope budgets with roll-over.
- Recurring entries ([[file:recurring.org][recurring]]) and the money calendar ([[file:money-calendar.org][money calendar]]) become bills and subscriptions.
- Goals ([[file:goals.org][goals]]) become savings goals tied to accounts.
- Documents ([[file:documents.org][documents]]) hold receipts and warranties.
- Dashboards ([[file:dashboards.org][dashboards]]) give the household home screen.

* Household type and slim view (child 1)

Create a venture through Settings / Ventures and choose *Household* as its
Type. The optional "What we are planning for" field is a note, not a required
setup step. The declarative definition is =data/venture-types/household.yaml=;
it uses the existing type registry and attribute storage, so no migration is
needed. A =general= venture whose Kind says household is unchanged; change
its Type to =household= explicitly to use this view.

Pick its entity in the sidebar, then pick the household under *Venture view*.
The view opens Spending (or the first available destination when finance is
disabled). An entity with no household keeps its ordinary selector. The menu
has these destinations, in order:

| Area      | Destinations                     |
|-----------+----------------------------------|
| Money     | Income, Spending, Accounts, Bank feeds |
| Bills     | Bills, Pay bills                 |
| Budgets   | Budgets                          |
| Goals     | Goals                            |
| Documents | Documents                        |
| Reports   | Reports                          |

Income and Spending reuse the ordinary sale and expense records; Bills reuse
payables, and Documents is the ordinary document list, where receipts can be
filed. These are existing pages, not new accounting operations. New offers
Spending and Bill. The list and new-entry headings use household words. The
command palette uses the same household destinations and omits business
record shortcuts. Plugin shortcuts, saved-view shortcuts and custom dashboard
shortcuts are omitted from this menu. Links belonging to disabled modules
stay hidden, including their otherwise empty headings.

Pick a business venture or *Full view* to restore normal navigation. A
household selection does not switch off any module or change a business
venture's type, stored data or navigation. Accountant-only access keeps its
Books menu. The menu still folds on a phone using the existing Menu button.

This child changes navigation, not accounting scope: the existing pages and
reports still answer for the selected entity. Use separate entities when
household and business books must be separate. Household account kinds and imports are described in child 2 below;
budgeting and household-specific reports are later children. Selecting this
view does not create a data-access boundary. Direct URLs,
the CLI and API retain their existing permissions and record contracts.

=GET /view/venture/ID= requires a signed-in session and a live readable venture
in the currently selected entity. =/view/venture/all= restores the full view.
The =venture_view= cookie carries only the chosen ID (HttpOnly, SameSite=Lax).
Every request rechecks its record and type under the caller's access scope;
a forged, deleted, moved or retyped household cannot keep an obsolete view.
Selecting All entities uses the full menu. The cookie never grants access.

The household destination table lives in =src/web/venture-web-household.inc=.
Later children append their destinations there; the business table stays
separate. No migration is consumed by child 1; upstream's highest migration
at implementation was =000920=.

Tests: =test-plugin /venture-type/shipped-definitions-load= checks the shipped
type. =test-auth /auth/household-navigation= checks selection, plain labels,
hidden business links in the sidebar and palette, every offered page, malformed
IDs and switching back to a business. =/auth/household-view-scope= checks
foreign selections, forged cookies and retyping. Both initial regressions
were observed on the epic baseline before implementation.

* Child 2: accounts, imports and transfers

In the household Money menu, *Bank accounts* holds accounts that can receive
statements: checking, savings, credit cards, cash wallets, loans and
investments. *Accounts* remains the underlying chart of ledger accounts.
Create a ledger account first, then a bank account linked to it, with its
currency and account kind. Checking, savings, cash and investments require
an asset ledger account; cards and loans require a liability. An investment
account here records money movements, not security lots or market valuations.
Existing accounts keep their identity and start as =unspecified=; choose a
kind deliberately. The kind appears in the normal generated form, REST and
CLI, and is checked at the shared save boundary.

Accounts still belong to the selected entity, not the browser's venture-view
cookie. Keep household and business books in different entities when their
money must be separate. This child does not change access permissions.

** Bring in statements

Open a bank account and use its Banking actions to map columns and import
CSV, OFX or QFX statement contents. New shortcuts on statements, statement
lines and transfers return to the account list to choose the account whose
actions create that evidence. Set the statement period and opening and
closing balances. CSV supports either signed amounts or separate debit and
credit columns; OFX/QFX uses FITID. Overlapping imports skip previously seen
identities and an invalid new line rolls back the batch. See
[[file:banking.org][Banking]] for the exact format and CLI examples.

For a provider connection, enable the optional =bankfeed= module and open
*Bank feeds*. Link the same bank account and configure that connection's
settings. Sync imports statement evidence; it does not book spending.
[[file:bankfeed.org][Bank feeds]] describes encrypted settings, provider support and sync.
File import works without provider credentials. The implementation tests use
local fake providers, never a real bank connection.

** Review and learn categories

*Statement lines* shows imported evidence. Use =create= for new spending,
=match= for a document already entered, and =correct= to change a spending
category. A correction reverses its old bank-created expense and creates its
replacement atomically. It saves a rule for this account and merchant, with
a real preview of matching historical statement lines. Learned spending rules
match debits in the same currency; a merchant refund remains for review. In *Category rules*, review
that preview, then =enable= the rule. The account's =inbox= previews suggestions;
=bulk= applies unique enabled suggestions. Ambiguous matches remain for review.
A corrected rule is disabled until reviewed and enabled again.

Corrections do not rewrite another account's rule or a broader merchant,
split or amount-bounded rule. Blank descriptions do not create catch-all
rules. Reversals and corrections retain audit history and obey closed periods
and reconciled statement locks.

** Move money without counting it as spending

Use =transfer= on the source bank account for a move to savings, investment
funding, a cash withdrawal, card repayment or loan principal. Enter the
destination bank account ID, amount and date. Both ends must use distinct
asset or liability ledger accounts in the same entity and currency. The
service checks the current ledger classes again before posting; a legacy
account cannot send transfer principal to income or spending.

If imported lines represent the movement, enter their source and destination
statement line IDs in the transfer form. Their bank, direction and amount
must agree exactly. The service marks both as transfer evidence in the same
transaction as the paired journal, so subsequent categorisation leaves them
alone. Do not create expenses from those lines. A duplicate transfer is
refused. *Transfers* lists the saved movements.

An optional fee is separate spending on the source account; only the
principal moves to the destination. Enter loan interest as separate spending,
not principal. Different-currency transfers require explicit exchange
accounting and are refused by this action.

Migration =000921_household_account_kinds.sql= adds no invented financial
history: field metadata adds the kind column and the paired migrations mark
previously unset kinds as unspecified, including when banking was disabled
after use. Tests cover account-kind persistence and class refusal, populated
upgrade/restart, correction isolation, imports, fake feed sync and transfers.

* Monthly envelopes (child 3)

Open Budgets in the household view to see this month's category amounts,
rolled-over balance, spending, left to spend and status. Create an active Budget
and add Budget lines with Monthly envelope enabled, a first month and a monthly
amount. Choose Roll over separately for each category. Unspent money and
overspending both carry forward when it is on; when it is off each month starts
with its own allocation. The report warns at 80% used, then says Used up or
Over budget. Warnings appear on the page, with no notification delivery.

Each envelope uses one expense account, matching the expense-category posting
map. Transfers between owned accounts do not consume envelopes; posted fees and
interest do. Refunds restore room to spend. Each account has one live envelope
per organization. Changing a plan recalculates past months; these are live plans,
not locked monthly snapshots. See [[file:budgets.org][Budgets]] for setup, calculation
rules, the CLI/API and migration 000922.

* Child 4: bills and subscriptions

The Bills menu includes *Due calendar*, *Subscriptions* and *Repeating bills*.
The calendar combines open bills, outgoing recurring schedules and possible
repeat charges. In the household browser view it shows only these outgoing
items; the business view keeps its other sources. Dates and amounts come
from the same calendar source used by reports, the planner and the ICS feed.

** Confirm a subscription

Open Repeating bills and create a bill or expense schedule. Set its start,
frequency, timezone and template as described in [[file:recurring.org][Recurring work]].
For an expense, a template such as ={"amount":"12.00 USD"}= supplies the
forecast amount. Check *Subscription* to include it in the yearly list.
Check *Forecast only* when charges already arrive through your bank: the
schedule then produces dates, never expenses, bills or ledger entries.
Ordinary recurring schedules keep their existing accounting behavior.

To replace a detected suggestion, set *Paid from* to its bank account and
copy the merchant's exact statement description into *Statement description*.
Matching ignores letter case and outer spaces, but does not guess merchant
aliases. This also suppresses detection while that schedule is paused, so a
cancelled subscription does not reappear as a new suggestion. Pause or set
an inclusive End date when payments stop. Neither operation cancels a vendor
contract; it changes this forecast.

** Detection and yearly cost

A possible repeat charge requires three consecutive equal negative charges
in one entity, bank account, merchant and currency, at an exact weekly,
monthly or yearly cadence. Month-end dates clamp independently. Changed
amounts, irregular intervals, excluded transactions, refunds and linked own
account transfers are not evidence of a subscription. If the next expected
payment is already past the report's as-of day, the suggestion is stale and
omitted. A missed or shifted bank date can therefore hide a real subscription;
create a schedule when its due date is known.

Suggestions are labelled *Possible repeat charge*, marked tentative in ICS,
and excluded from cash nets. They never create a bill, expense or payment.
Reads do not save a classification. Detection fails visibly rather than
silently truncating history beyond the configured aggregate row limit.

Subscriptions you pay for lists confirmed schedules separately from possible
subscriptions, with the next date, payment amount and cost over twelve months
from the selected period's start. It counts actual calendar occurrences,
respects start/end dates and pauses, and keeps currencies separate. Confirmed
schedule costs include contractual occurrences even if their documents have
already been generated; this is a forecast of the contract, not another
spending total. The same report is available at
=/api/v1/reports/household_subscriptions?period=2026-01= and through
=venturectl report household_subscriptions 2026-01=.

** Reminders

Set *Remind days before* to 1–28 and choose *Remind person* on an outgoing
schedule. Zero disables reminders. Visiting the due calendar or inbox runs a
lazy sweep; =venturectl calendar remind= also runs it through the existing
calendar reminder endpoint (planner enabled). There is no background timer,
email or automatic payment. A due date is notified at most once, with the
notification and its technical stamp saved in one transaction. The recipient
must still be active and authorized to read the schedule. Paused schedules
and already generated occurrences do not send schedule reminders. For bank
subscriptions use Forecast only so the schedule retains its forecast dates.
Polling ICS or reading reports never sends reminders.

Migration =000923_household_subscriptions.sql= (SQLite and PostgreSQL) keeps
existing schedules non-subscription, accounting-enabled and reminder-free;
its optional-table guard supports installations without recurring schedules.
Tests cover detection, stale and changed charges, tenant separation, suppressed
suggestions, yearly totals, web/API/CLI, reminders, forecast-only sweeps and
upgrade/restart behavior.

* Net worth and debt payoff (child 5)

Household Reports now links to *Net worth* and *Debt payoff*. Both reuse the
statement engine, report web page, API, CLI, MCP and assistant report tools.
They answer for one exact organization; currencies remain separate. They
require =statements= and its ledger/period dependencies. Debt planning also
requires =banking=. Turning a module off removes its registered reports.
These exploratory reports are not captured in period-close statement snapshots.

** Net worth over time

=/reports/net_worth?period=2026= lists each month's assets, liabilities and
net worth (assets minus liabilities). Balances include all earlier posted
entries and stop at the month end, or the earlier =as_of= cutoff. The Through
column shows that precise cutoff. Parent accounts never multiply totals;
unposted transactions and statement imports alone do not change net worth.
Use the existing posting and reconciliation workflows to bring those into
the ledger. These are book values, not live investment or property prices.
Current account classifications apply to historical rows. Requests are
limited to 600 months. Choose a shorter period for a closer look.

** Configure and compare debts

On *Money / Bank accounts*, edit each liability account to plan:

- Turn on *Include in debt planner*.
- Enter *APR in basis points*: 100 means 1% per year; 0 is interest-free.
- Enter a positive *Fixed monthly minimum* in the account's currency.
- Optionally enter an *Extra monthly contribution*. Contributions from all
  selected accounts of the same currency form one shared extra-payment pool.

Open =/reports/debt_payoff?period=2026-01=. The starting amount is each selected
liability account's direct posted balance at the end of January (or earlier
=as_of=), never a copied statement balance or a parent subtotal. Select each
ledger account at most once per currency. Unselected debts are excluded, and
credit balances count as zero debt. A selected account with no debt still
contributes its configured minimum and extra to the strategy budget; turn
it off to remove that contribution. Edit the settings to compare scenarios.

Each currency gets debt rows and an all-debts summary for three projections:

| Projection | Payment rule |
|------------+--------------|
| minimum | Each debt's fixed minimum only; retired payments stop. |
| snowball | All minimums, then remaining budget to the smallest balance. |
| avalanche | All minimums, then remaining budget to the highest APR. |

The latter two retain the sum of initial minimums plus extra contributions
throughout the projection, recycling unused and retired minimums. Priorities
are chosen after that month's interest and minimum payments; ties follow
bank-account record order. The summary shows the initial monthly budget;
individual debt rows show their minimum, not a promise of constant payment.
Interest saved compares each completed projection with minimum-only, including
the effect of both extra contributions and recycled payments.

Payments start one month after the balance cutoff. Dates remain anchored to
that cutoff's day, clamped in shorter months (January 31 gives February 28,
then March 31). Each month charges APR/12 on its opening balance, rounded
half to even in the currency's minor unit, then pays. Rates and fixed minimums
stay constant; there are no fees, new charges or daily accrual. These are
estimates, not lender payoff quotes, and the report creates no payments.

The horizon is 600 months and at most 200 selected debts. An unfinished debt
shows its remaining balance and interest accrued through that horizon, with
no payoff date. Savings are blank whenever that projection or its minimum-only
baseline is unfinished. Invalid currency, duplicate debt selection, invalid
APR/payment or money overflow returns an error instead of a partial estimate.

Both reports accept =period=, =organization_id=, =currency= and =as_of= through
existing report surfaces. They require accrual balances and reject cash basis,
comparative periods and dimension filters, which would misstate whole-account
wealth. For example:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report net_worth 2026 currency=USD
venturectl report debt_payoff 2026-01 currency=USD as_of=2026-01-31
#+end_src

Migration =000924_household_wealth.sql= is paired for SQLite and PostgreSQL.
Metadata reconciliation adds the bank-account fields; the guarded migration
backfills planning off and APR zero, preserving existing accounts and payments.

* Linked savings goals (child 6)

Under *Goals*, create or edit a goal and choose exactly one source:

- *Savings asset account*: an asset ledger account in the same organization.
  The report reads its direct posted balance in the target currency, without
  adding child-account balances or imported statements that have not posted.
- *Savings rollover envelope*: a monthly envelope with rollover enabled in an
  active budget in the same organization. The report reads its unspent amount,
  including carried surplus or overspending. This is a budget allocation,
  not a claim about cash held in a bank.

Set a positive *Savings target*, optionally set *Due*, and enter *Planned
monthly savings* in the same currency. Leave the ordinary numeric Start,
Current and Target fields alone: linked savings use exact money, not those
manual numbers. No due date leaves the amount needed per month blank; a
missing or zero planned contribution leaves the future forecast blank.

Open *Goals / Savings progress* (=savings_goals=). Choose one calendar month
and optionally a currency or goal ID. It reports saved funds, target, remaining
amount, progress, monthly amount needed and estimated target date. Balances
are at that month's end in UTC, consistently for both source types. The same
calculation is available through the API, CLI, MCP and assistant report tools:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report savings_goals 2026-01 currency=USD
venturectl report savings_goals 2026-01 goal_id=7
#+end_src

The API is =/api/v1/reports/savings_goals?period=2026-01=. Options are
=organization_id=, =goal_id= and =currency=. A year or partial-month period,
or an =as_of= override, is refused: use the calendar month whose closing
balance you want. Target presence and currency are filtered before the goal
row limit, so ordinary goals and goals in other currencies do not exhaust a
narrowed request. Too many matching savings goals still refuse; narrow by
=goal_id=. Invalid linked sources still refuse rather than disappear.
Current goal and envelope settings apply to past periods.

Contributions start one month after the cutoff and stay anchored to its day,
clamped in shorter months: January 31 gives February 28 and March 31;
February 28 gives March 28. Monthly needed is the remaining amount divided
by the number of these contributions on or before the due date, rounded up
to the smallest currency unit. If no contribution date remains, the full
remaining amount is needed now. Negative balances increase the shortfall;
progress can be negative or above 100%, while remaining never falls below zero.

The forecast uses the planned *net* monthly contribution, with no additional
withdrawals, interest, fees or currency changes. For an envelope, enter the
amount expected to remain after future spending, not necessarily its gross
monthly allocation. Forecasts stop at 1200 months; a longer result stays
blank with an explanation. Paused, achieved and abandoned goals get no future
contribution forecast. A source that already covers the target shows the
selected cutoff as covered, not a claim about when it was first achieved.

Several goals may refer to the same source; each sees its whole balance.
These are alternative views of funds, not reservations or amounts to add
together. The report makes no transfers and does not change goal status or
manual progress. Missing, deleted, moved or invalid sources are errors rather
than zero balances. Ledger and the source's optional module must be enabled.
Ordinary goals keep their manual progress behavior; =goal_progress= directs
linked savings goals to =savings_goals= instead of showing stale numeric values.

Migration =000925_household_savings.sql= is paired for SQLite and PostgreSQL.
Metadata reconciliation adds the money and reference fields; the guarded
migration leaves old goals unlinked and preserves their existing progress.

* Household walkthrough repairs

A linked savings goal's own page shows the same exact-money progress as the
Savings progress report for this month, including the amount still needed,
monthly contribution and forecast date. It labels the month-end cutoff and
never substitutes the unused manual Start/Current/Target values. If the
source cannot be read, the page shows the refusal instead of zero progress.
Its steps remain available. The report link selects this goal and entity.

Savings progress offers This month and Last month shortcuts; use its Month
field for another calendar month. Quarter and year shortcuts are omitted
because the calculation requires exactly one month. Other reports retain
all their period shortcuts, which wrap inside the screen on a phone.

The due calendar's month and week grids scroll within a labelled, keyboard

weekly-total columns from widening the phone page.

* Members, account sharing and settling up (child 7)

Personal accounts are private to their owner unless that owner explicitly
checks *Share with household*. While unshared, other household members,
including household administrators, cannot see the account, statements,
receipts, reports, exports or underlying settlement evidence derived from it.
When shared, all of those follow the account and become visible
to the household's authorized finance members. Each member uses a separate
login and an active membership in the household's organization.

Settlement disclosures contain only the shared amounts the reader is a
party to. They must never identify the private funding account, statement,
receipt or journal behind those amounts. Installation administrators retain
the existing service administration authority; household membership must not
be implemented by granting that global role.

This is the child-7 acceptance contract. Keep its policy tests together in
=test-orgaccess= so a later policy decision can change the contract in one
place. The existing optional personal-owner metadata is the enforcement
primitive; a household navigation selection is never an access boundary.

** Separate logins and personal accounts

Use the existing user and organization membership screens to give each adult
an individual login and an active membership in the household's entity.
Use the organization Finance role for bookkeeping. Do not grant global Owner
or Admin to a household member merely so they can enter spending: those are
installation administration roles with access to all data.

Open *Money / Accounts*. When creating an asset or liability ledger account,
choose its *Private owner*
for a personal account, or leave it empty for a joint account. The Bank account
inherits that choice from its Ledger account. Existing accounts remain shared;
ownership cannot be reassigned after saving. The owner can check or clear
*Share with household* on the ledger account. Sharing includes historical
activity; clearing it hides the account and its derived records again. It
cannot recall exports someone already downloaded while sharing was enabled.
Create a new personal account for future activity on a previously joint account. Spending categories stay shared; the purchase amounts inherit
the funding account's privacy.

Statement imports, statement lines, matches, category rules, feed connections,
reconciliations, spending, income, receipts, savings goals and repeating bills
inherit privacy through their declared references. Changing a reference within
the same ownership remains possible; changing it to publish another person's
private data is refused. Private postings hide both journal sides and their
ledger projections, including from reports and record exports. Retained report
packs and period-close snapshots contain shared records only, even when a
scheduled service runs them. Live reports also include the reader's own
personal records under the normal request authority.

** Transfers involving a joint account

A personal-to-joint transfer, or its reverse, creates two balanced journals
through the shared *Household contributions* equity account. The journal for
the joint account contains its amount and that equity account, never the
personal account or the private transfer reference. Its statement match links
to this shared journal. Each journal names only its own bank account; the transfer keeps both
sides for authorized readers. Taken together the journals have the same asset/liability
movement as a direct transfer; principal never becomes spending. A fee remains
spending on the side that paid it. Transfers between joint accounts keep the ordinary single journal. Any
personal account uses two journals even while shared, so stopping sharing
later preserves the joint-side balance.

The shared contribution account uses code =HH-CLEAR-<entity id>= and must
remain shared equity. Its balance reflects the shared side of contributions
when the other side is private to somebody else. Nothing here converts
currencies or allows a member to use another member's personal account.

** Who paid and settling up

Open *Money / Settle up*. Add a shared purchase, choose *Who paid* and *Who owes
a share*, enter only the amount to repay and the purchase date. The people
picker lists active members by login name without opening their user records.
Only those two members can read, export or edit the entry; organization
administration alone grants no access. The label must describe the shared
purchase only. There is no funding account, statement or private receipt field.

After repayment happens, either participant can check *Repaid*. The date is
recorded if left empty. A repaid entry keeps its amount, participants and dates;
remove a mistaken entry and record its correction, with audit history retained.
This is a record of an agreed obligation and an external repayment, not a bank
payment or another spending entry. Import and reconcile actual bank movements
through Money as usual so a shared purchase is never counted twice.

Migration =000926_household_privacy.sql= preserves all old accounts as shared
on SQLite and PostgreSQL. New reference metadata adds no invented account
owners or historical repayments. The settlement table is created only when
banking is enabled.

Shared bill payments and invoice collections use a shared funding account.
To fund a shared bill personally, first transfer the contribution to the joint
account, then pay the bill there. This keeps its shared payable balance and
payment evidence consistent without disclosing a personal account. A personal
receipt cannot be allocated to a shared invoice. These refusals leave the
operation unchanged; ordinary shared business accounting is unchanged.

Personal subscription schedules use Forecast only and import their actual
charges. Personal capture items inherit the original document's privacy;
automatic conversion into a shared bill or expense is refused. Enter the
purchase from its personal account and attach the receipt to that purchase.

* Year-end and warranties (child 8)

** Tag spending for tax

Open a purchase under *Spending* and set *Deductibility*:

- /Full/: deductible in full.
- /Partial/: deductible at the *Business use* percentage.
- /Capital/: spread over several years rather than claimed at once.
- /Review/: not decided; it shows as "Ask your tax preparer" and counts
  as zero until someone decides.
- /None/: not for tax; the default for ordinary spending.

** The year-end export

*Reports / Year-end for taxes* opens last year's purchases tagged for tax,
oldest first, with what, who was paid, category, the tax treatment, the
amount, the deductible part and the titles of the receipts attached. A
purchase with nothing attached says "No receipt attached", and the
*Without a receipt* figure counts them. Pick another period with the period
buttons; download it as CSV for whoever does the taxes. The business
=year_end_pack= is unchanged.

** Receipts and warranties on a purchase

Attach a document to the purchase (its *Expense* field). For a warranty or
guarantee, also set:

- *Warranty ends*: the last day it covers.
- *Remind person*: who gets an inbox reminder.

*Documents / Warranties* lists every document with an end date, soonest
first, with the days left or "Ended". The reminder arrives once per end date,
in the last 30 days, when the inbox or planner sweeps reminders; changing
the end date reminds again. Migration =000927= gives existing documents no
reminder person.

** Privacy

The same contract as child 7. Every read runs as the member asking, so a
purchase on another member's unshared personal account, its receipts and its
warranties are absent from that member's export and list, and appear once
the owner shares the account. A private purchase's warranty can remind only
its owner; a reminder is also skipped if its person can no longer read the
document, for example after the owner stops sharing.

Tests: =tests/test-venture-household-yearend.c= (both privacy directions,
reminder timing and once-only stamping, and the reminder person check).

* Household dashboard (child 9)

Picking a household under *Venture view* now opens *Overview*: each member's
own household dashboard, made from the =household= template the first time
they open it (a read-only member who has none yet gets *Spending by
category* instead). It is personal, so another member's copy shows their
own figures. Two columns on a desk, one stacked column on a phone:

- *Spent this month* and *Money in this month*.

Rearrange or remove widgets like any dashboard; *Dashboards / New* can

** The reports behind it

=household_spending= groups the period's spending by category, biggest
first and separately per currency, with each category's share. Transfers
between your own accounts are not spending, as in child 2.

=household_cash_forecast= starts from today's balance in checking, savings
and cash accounts (posted ledger evidence), then adds what the due calendar
says comes in and goes out, week by week from this week's Monday. Options:
=as_of= (a date) and =weeks= (1 to 53, default 13). It is a guide, not a
promise: spending nobody scheduled is not in it.

=household_months= is the =monthly= series in household words: money in
(income), spent and left over per month.

** Privacy

The same contract as child 7: every figure is computed as the member
asking, so another member's unshared personal account, its spending and its
balance stay out until the owner shares it.
