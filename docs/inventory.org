#+title: Inventory to the general ledger
#+description: FIFO cost layers, COGS on issue, and a valuation that ties to account 1200

* Quantity and cost

Quantity on hand is still the sum of signed =inventory_txn= rows, never
a stored field. FIFO cost lives on =inventory_cost_layer=, written by
=VentureInventoryService= when goods are received. Issue consumes the
oldest remaining quantity and posts Cost of goods sold 5000 against
Inventory 1200 through =VenturePostingService=. The inventory control
account is resolved with setup role =inventory=, or code 1200.

=allow-negative= on =inventory_item= is false by default; a movement
that would take stock below zero is refused. Transfers move layers
between two items of the same product (two locations) without hitting
COGS.

* Production

A craft (see [[file:production.org][Production]]) turns stock into other
stock through =venture_inventory_service_produce()=, in one transaction:
each input leaves as a negative =production= transaction, consuming its
FIFO layers; the output arrives as a positive =production= transaction and
receives cost layers that carry exactly what was consumed -- split into at
most two layers (=r= units at one minor unit more, the rest at the floor)
so they sum to the consumed cost to the minor unit. No journal is posted:
the value moved from inventory to inventory, so account 1200 and
=inventory_valuation= are unchanged by a craft, and cost of goods sold is
booked when the made units are sold.

Units with no cost layer (an adjustment typed in by hand, or stock taken
below zero on an item that allows it) are consumed at no cost. Inputs whose
costs are in two currencies give the output sibling layers, one set per
currency (see "Costing in several currencies" below). Without the =goods=
module there are no layers and a craft moves quantities only.

* Session yields

Posting a session (see [[file:sessions.org][Sessions]]) calls the same
=venture_inventory_service_produce()= with *no draws*: each goods yield
arrives as one positive =production= transaction, referenced
=session:<id>= and dated the session's end, and -- because nothing was
consumed -- receives one *zero-cost* layer, in the item's unit-cost
currency or else the organization's book currency. The yield's
=unit_value= is a valuation and is never written into a layer: a layer at
market value would book the profit when the goods were gathered and none
when they sold. The zero layer still matters, because the issue path
refuses units that have none. No journal is posted, so account 1200 does
not move, and =inventory_valuation= counts the units at zero.

Posting is idempotent: a yield stamped with its transaction is never
posted again, and a posted yield (and its session) cannot be deleted --
correct its stock with an adjustment instead.

* Bought and sold outright: =receive_from= and =issue_to=

Two variants of receive and issue serve stock that is paid for and sold on the spot rather than through a purchase order and a bill -- an arbitrage leg (see [[file:arbitrage.org][Arbitrage]]). Each changes which account the other side of the journal hits; layers, lots and FIFO are untouched.

- =venture_inventory_service_receive_from(self, item, quantity, total_cost, date, reference, credit_account_id, actor, &txn, error)= -- a purchase movement whose cost is credited to the account the money left (a venue's holding or cash), never to goods received not invoiced (2010): no bill will come to clear it. It takes the *total* cost and splits it over the units exactly, like a craft's output (100.00 for seven units is four layers at 14.29 and three at 14.28, one lot), because a rounded unit cost would leave a cent behind on every flip. Dr inventory, Cr the account paid from.
- =venture_inventory_service_issue_to(self, item, quantity, date, reference, debit_account_id, kind, actor, &txn, &costs, error)= -- an issue whose first-in-first-out cost is debited to the given account (the arbitrage positions account) instead of cost of goods sold (5000), with movement kind =sale= or =write_off=. Each currency the layers cost is posted as its own pair, as an ordinary issue does.

Both begin the whole-operation posting boundary, then one transaction (joining the caller's), and hand back the movement so the caller can stamp it. Account and item must be in one organization. Each unit is posted once: the arbitrage leg's own posting rule never repeats the stock side.

* Where stock is

=inventory_item.location_id= names a =location= record: a place that can
nest inside another (a bin in a warehouse, a bag on a character) and be
renamed once for every item in it. The older free-text =location= column is
kept, labelled "Location (text)"; upgrading made one location per distinct
value and linked each item to it (migration =000695=). Items also carry
comma-separated, searchable =tags=. See [[file:taxonomy.org][Taxonomy]].

* Costing in several currencies

One organization may buy stock in more than one currency: a game's gold
and its fair tickets, or dollars and euros. A cost layer holds one
currency; everything that adds layers up keeps *one total per currency* and
never adds across them, so nothing about stock is ever refused for mixing
currencies and nothing is silently dropped.

** Totals per currency

=venture_money_totals_new()= and =venture_money_totals_add()= (in
=src/boxed/venture-money.h=) are the set every inventory sum uses: an array
of =VentureMoney=, one per currency, into which an amount is added like to
like. A zero amount starts a zero total; it never clashes with anything, so
a zero-cost layer (a posted session's goods) consumed beside a TICKET layer
is simply "0 GOLD and 3 TICKET". =venture_money_totals_lookup()= finds a
currency's total and =venture_money_totals_sort()= puts the book currency
first.

The public surface that returns money about stock returns such a set:

| Call                                        | Returns                                                      |
|---------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
| =venture_inventory_service_issue()=         | =costs=: what the issued units cost, per currency              |
| =venture_inventory_service_produce()=       | =out_costs=: what the inputs cost, per currency (NULL: no layer consumed) |
| =venture_inventory_service_valuation()=     | the remaining layers' cost, per currency, book currency first; one zero in the book currency when nothing is on hand |
| =venture_inventory_service_item_value()=    | one item's remaining layers per currency, with the average unit cost of the units carrying each |

** Posting an issue

An issue (a sale, a fulfilment, a write-off) posts one cost of goods sold
/ inventory pair per currency its layers cost, all in *one* call to
=venture_posting_service_post_by_currency()=. The ledger's rule (see
[[file:ledger.org][the general journal]], "Currencies in the books") then
decides each: a book-currency cost, or a valued currency with a rate on the
date, lands in the book journal; a separate-book currency, or a valued one
with no rate, posts a balanced journal of its own; a memo currency posts
nothing. Every pair balances in its own currency, so no clearing is
needed. Zero amounts post nothing.

A gold sale of a unit bought with tickets therefore posts GOLD cash and
sales (the sale's own journal) and TICKET cost of goods sold against TICKET
inventory (the issue's) -- or, once a TICKET-to-GOLD =exchange_rate= is on
file, the cost valued into a GOLD journal whose lines keep the 50 TICKET as
their original amount. Neither is refused, and the cost is never missing.

** Lots: a unit that cost two currencies

A craft whose inputs cost 1 GOLD of dust and 50 TICKET of tokens makes
units that cost both. The output receives *sibling layers*: for each
currency that cost anything, the exact split (a layer one minor unit higher
and a floor layer), all naming the output transaction in =lot_txn_id=.
Three charms from those inputs get GOLD layers of 1 x 0.3334 and 2 x 0.3333
and TICKET layers of 2 x 17 and 1 x 16.

FIFO walks *lots*, not layers. A lot's layers in one currency add up to the
lot's units; taking a unit out of a lot takes it out of every currency of
the lot, each from its own layers in order. Selling one charm gives up
0.3334 GOLD and 17 TICKET; selling the other two gives up 0.6666 GOLD and
33 TICKET -- exactly what went in, in each currency. A layer with no lot
(every layer written before lots existed) is a lot of its own, which is the
old behaviour exactly. Receipts and transfers name their movement as the
lot too; a transfer re-splits every currency exactly at the destination
instead of writing one layer at a rounded average.

Zero totals make no layers when anything else cost something: a craft
drawing a zero-cost herb and three TICKET herbs gives its potion TICKET
layers only. When nothing cost anything the output still gets one zero
layer -- in the first zero's currency, else the item's unit-cost currency,
else the organization's book currency -- because an issue refuses units
with no layer.

A movement's transaction shows a unit cost only for reading: the average
when it moved one currency. A movement that moved several leaves
=unit_cost= empty and says in =notes= what each currency came to ("Cost
1.0000 GOLD + 50 TICKET for 3 units"); the layers are what the books count.

** Memo currencies

A memo currency's cost is *tracked on the layer and posts nothing*: a
receipt at 5 TICKET writes a TICKET layer and no journal, an issue reports
that it cost 5 TICKET and posts nothing, and the valuation counts the
TICKET still on hand. That keeps a memo token's stock costed (a margin or a
holdings view can still read it) without putting a unit that is not money
into any book.

** Recorded costs in other reports

=recipe_margin=, =goal_materials= and =session_performance= price with
market observations or, with the market module off, the typed
=inventory_item.unit_cost= (else the product's =cost=). Those are single
amounts, so a component "costed in two currencies" there is two components
in two currencies: =recipe_margin= notes it and gives no money figures,
=goal_materials= totals each currency on its own row. They do not read the
FIFO layers.

* Autojournal

When a product has an =inventory_item=, automatic sale journals skip
the product-cost COGS legs. For a product without one, the COGS pair is in
the currency of the product's recorded =cost=, which need not be the
sale's: a gold sale of an item that cost tickets posts gold cash and sales
and ticket cost of goods sold, each by its currency's treatment. The inventory service owns those journals
so a sale cannot double-post inventory and COGS.

* Reports

=inventory_valuation= as-of is remaining FIFO layers received at or before
the period's end, *one row per currency*: =currency=, =valuation= and
=books=, which says how that currency reaches the ledger on that date --
"book currency", "valued into the book currency", "separate book" or "memo,
not posted" -- because only the first reconciles directly to the inventory
account in the book currency. A separate book reconciles to the inventory
account's balance in that currency; a converted one was posted at the rate
on each receipt's date, so its book value is the history of those rates,
not today's. The book currency's figure is the =valuation= metric (the key
every dashboard reads); each other currency is =valuation_<CODE>=. Layers
in several currencies, of one product or of many, never fail the report.

=inventory= lists each item with its on-hand count (signed transactions up
to the period's end), its location -- the =location= record's path, "Bank /
Vault", else the older free text -- and its carrying cost. The cost is the
FIFO layers still on hand, *one row per currency* they cost, with
=currency=, =unit_cost= (the average cost of the units carrying that
currency) and =value=. An item with no costed layer left (stock typed in by
hand, or the goods module off) falls back to its typed =unit_cost= times
the count, on one row. Totals are per currency: =value= is the book
currency's (0 GOLD in an empty GOLD organization, never $0.00), each other
currency =value_<CODE>=. Like =inventory_valuation=, a past period leaves
out layers received since but cannot put back what was consumed since.

=reorder_worklist= lists items at or below their reorder point.
