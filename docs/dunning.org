* Dunning

The =dunning= module requires =receivables= and =mail=. Its two records are
field tables: the same declarations produce the database tables, forms, REST
resources, CLI commands, AI schemas and audit entries. Switching the module
off hides both records, the =collections= and =dunning_worklist= reports and
the sweep, retry and test-send actions together; invoices, customers and the
mail outbox are untouched, and a reminder it had already queued is cancelled
at delivery (see [[*Stopping and cancelling][Stopping and cancelling]]).

| Record | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =dunning_policy= | A named cadence: steps at day offsets relative to the due date, each with a =mail_template=; whether the last step escalates and to whom when nobody owns the invoice; when it was adopted |
| =dunning_event= | One step of one policy for one invoice: queued, sent, cancelled, suppressed with a reason, failed, or escalated; the mail message or next action it produced; the exact balance it rendered |

=steps= is a JSON array, ordered by strictly increasing offset:

#+begin_src json
[{"offset": -3, "template_id": 12},
 {"offset": 7, "template_id": 13},
 {"offset": 14}]
#+end_src

A negative offset is before the due date; =-3= becomes eligible on the third
calendar day before it and never earlier. Offsets are calendar days in UTC.
The save validator refuses anything it would otherwise have to guess at:

- =offset= must be a JSON integer, within 365 days either side of the due
  date. ="abc"=, =null=, =7.9=, =7.0= and =true= are refused; they used to be
  coerced into 0, 0, 7, 7 and 1.
- =template_id= must be a JSON integer naming a live =mail_template= of the
  policy's organization. Only the last step of a =final_escalation= policy
  may omit it, because that step sends no email.
- At most 20 steps.
- An =is_default= policy needs at least one step; an empty default is every
  invoice in the organization silently leaving dunning.
- =last_sweep= is the sweep action's answer (below) and is never stored.

Exactly one policy per organization may be =is_default=; a second is refused
naming the first. A company may name =dunning_policy_id= to override the
default for every invoice it is billed, and an invoice may name its own to
override both. A level naming a deleted policy falls through to the next --
the invoice's to the company's, the company's to the default -- and the sweep
warns once per missing policy (=g_warning= and the answer's =warnings=), rather
than the invoice quietly leaving dunning. =invoice.dunning_disabled= names no
policy at all; the invoice is simply skipped. =company.dunning_opt_out= is
different: every email step that falls due is recorded as =suppressed= with
reason =company_opt_out=, so the decision not to chase is visible on the
invoice and in the report, and the escalation step still creates its internal
task.

** Pausing: promise to pay

| Field | On | Meaning |
|-------+----+---------|
| =dunning_paused_until= | =invoice=, =company= | No reminder before this calendar day; the company's pause covers all of its invoices |
| =dunning_pause_reason= | =invoice=, =company= | Why, for the next person to read it |

A pause is not recorded as an event: nothing was decided. When it ends, the
current step goes out and the superseded rule keeps it from being a burst. A
pause set after a reminder was queued cancels that reminder at delivery with
reason =paused=.

When the =recurring= module is on, an invoice with a =collection_case= that is
=held= or =disputed=, or whose =promised_at= is still in the future, is held the
same way (cancel reason =collection_case=): two modules chasing one invoice is
how a customer receives two different letters on the same morning.

* The sweep

=VentureDunningService.sweep_detailed(organization, as_of, limit, dry_run)=
is the one implementation behind =POST /api/v1/dunning_policy/0/actions/sweep=,
=venturectl dunning sweep=, the assistant's action tool and the
=venture->dunning_sweep= automation handler. It is bounded and synchronous;
there is no background thread. Schedule it like the mail delivery sweep, after
business transactions commit (=data/examples/dunning.pod=):

#+begin_src text
pod dunning_daily = cron->new("0 8 * * *");
dunning_daily->on_schedule => venture->dunning_sweep("", "1", "100");
#+end_src

The handler's positional arguments are =as_of=, organization and limit; an
empty string takes the default (now, the default organization, 100). A limit
outside 1–1000 or an unparsable organization fails the handler rather than
being guessed. The pipe result's =count= is reminders queued plus escalations
and its =summary= is the sweep's JSON answer.

** Which invoices, which steps

The sweep reads only issued invoices (=sent= or =partially_paid=) whose due
date is on or before =as_of= minus the earliest first-step offset of any valid
policy in the organization; the rest of the ledger is never loaded. For each,
cheapest test first, it resolves the policy, skips the invoice when no step's
offset has been reached, loads what has already been recorded, and only then
checks holds (pause, collection case) and computes the open balance to refuse
an invoice that is paid, void, disputed (=workflow_state=disputed=) or has no
open balance. The default policy is looked up once per sweep.

The identity that is never repeated is =(invoice, policy, step offset)=,
pinned by the per-organization unique =dunning_key=
(=dunning:inv:<id>:policy:<id>:offset:<days>=). The offset, not the position:
prepending a step to a policy moves every position, and a position-keyed
history then re-sent one reminder and skipped another. =step= stays on the
event as the position when it was recorded, for display. Rows recorded before
this change are keyed by position (=...:step:<n>=) and are not rewritten;
recorded-ness is read from their =offset_days=, which they already carry, so
they still count. Moving an invoice to another policy starts that policy's
cadence. Rerunning the sweep for the same day produces nothing, which
=test-dunning= proves.

Every step whose offset is at or below the highest offset already recorded
for the invoice counts as done, whatever its own row says. When the newest
due step fails and its transaction rolls back, the older steps it superseded
roll back with it; without this rule they came due again the next day and a
7-day reminder went out after the 14-day one had been attempted
(=/dunning/failed-latest=).

When several steps fell due since the last sweep (a policy adopted late, a
sweep that was not run for a fortnight, a pause that ended) the customer
receives the current step only; the earlier ones are recorded as =suppressed=
with reason =superseded= rather than sent as a burst.

** Adoption and catch-up

=adopted_at= (the policy's creation time when unset) is when the policy began
to govern. Enabling dunning over an existing ledger used to supersede every
step of every old invoice and escalate each of them at once: a collect task
per overdue invoice and not one email. The rule now is:

1. Due steps that fell due before the adoption day are recorded as
   =suppressed= with reason =before_adoption=, except the current (latest due)
   step, which is handled normally.
2. A catch-up never escalates straight away. When the current step is the
   escalation and no email step has ever been decided for the invoice under
   this policy (anything recorded other than =superseded=/=before_adoption=
   counts, including an opt-out or no-recipient suppression), the latest
   email step is sent instead and the escalation is left pending. If the
   policy has no email step, it escalates.
3. After a catch-up reminder, the next step keeps the policy's spacing
   counted from the adoption day: a step at offset S following a catch-up at
   offset O becomes eligible on adoption + (S − O). Catching up is an email
   today and the escalation a policy-gap later, not tomorrow. The spacing is
   measured from adoption rather than from the day the catch-up was queued;
   a first sweep run well after adoption therefore gets a shorter gap.

=/dunning/adoption= proves it: a policy adopted 50 days after the due date
sends the 7-day step, records the -3 step as =before_adoption=, creates no
activity, and escalates seven days later.

** Rendering and the pay link

The current step renders its template with the invoice's public fields plus
={customer_name}=, ={contact_name}=, ={due_at}= and ={issued_at}= as dates,
={open_balance}= (display form) and ={open_balance_exact}=, ={days_overdue}=,
={days_until_due}=, ={step}=, ={offset_days}=, ={policy_name}= and
={pay_link}=. The recipient is the invoice contact's email, or the customer's
when the invoice names no live contact; with neither the step is recorded as
=suppressed= (=no_recipient=). A template naming an unknown placeholder, or a
missing template, records the step as =failed= with the error; see
[[*Retry][Retry]].

A soft-deleted contact or company is treated as absent: the event, the
activity and the recipient use only rows that load live. The event used to
copy the deleted row's id, the save's reference check refused it, the
failure record was refused the same way, and the sweep stopped on that
invoice every day (=/dunning/deleted-contact=).

={pay_link}= is the customer's portal link, =server.base_url= + =/portal/= +
a live =customer_portal_access= token, from which Stripe Checkout opens for
the exact outstanding balance. The token is a bearer credential, so it is
used only when

- the access is unrevoked and was issued to the reminder's recipient
  (compared case-insensitively, surrounding space ignored) -- an access
  issued to someone else at the customer is their credential; and
- =server.base_url= is a public HTTPS URL without credentials, query or
  fragment, exactly the rule the portal service applies before it sends an
  invitation.

Otherwise the placeholder is empty. =base_url= is bound to the running
configuration, so a corrected URL applies to the next reminder. The rendered
bodies containing the link go only into the message's sensitive
=private_text_body= and =private_html_body=; the transport sends the private
text in place of the public text and the private HTML in place of the public
HTML (a private text body with no private HTML drops the public HTML, as
before), so an HTML-only template keeps its link. The stored public bodies
render ={pay_link}= empty. Reminders never charge a card.

** One transaction per invoice

Each invoice is one transaction: its events, the outbox row, the activity and
the audit entries commit together or not at all. If the transaction fails it
is rolled back, the attempt is recorded on its own as a =failed= event on the
current step with the error (so a step that reached the outbox is never
mistaken next sweep for one that was never due), the invoice is added to
=failed_invoice_ids=, a =g_warning= names it, and *the sweep goes on to the
next invoice*. It used to return -1 at the first failing invoice, so every
invoice behind it went unreminded. If even the failure record cannot be
written, that is logged too. =/dunning/atomic-attempt= pins both.

Delivery itself is the outbox's at-least-once contract described in
[[file:mail.org][Transactional mail]]: the sweep enqueues, =mail deliver=
submits. The event's =delivery_status= mirrors the outbox row (=queued=,
=sent= with =sent_at=, =failed=, =dead=, =uncertain=, =cancelled=), so the
invoice page, the timelines and the reports read one column. =queued_at= is
the real time the sweep recorded the step, whatever =as_of= it was run for.

Only the message recorded on the event (its =mail_message_id=) moves that
status or is rechecked before sending. A message's =related_type= and
=related_id= are ordinary columns anybody who may write mail can fill in; a
forged message pointing at a reminder once flipped a sent event back to
queued, and a forged failure made a =retry= -- a second email to the
customer -- legal. =/dunning/forged-message= pins this.

** Limits, the future and the answer

=as_of= defaults to now. A sweep more than one day ahead of now is refused:
it would send next month's reminders today and spend those steps. A dry run
may look ahead freely. =limit= is invoices acted on per call, 1–1000, default
100, including invoices whose due steps were only recorded as suppressed and
invoices that failed; quiet invoices do not count.

The action returns an unsaved =dunning_policy= whose =last_sweep= is the JSON
answer (a type-level action returns a record; the answer rides on it the way
a financial batch carries =last_result=):

| Member | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =organization_id=, =as_of=, =dry_run= | What was swept |
| =invoices= | Invoices acted on, against =limit= |
| =queued=, =escalated=, =suppressed=, =failed= | Steps by outcome (for a dry run, what would happen) |
| =failed_invoice_ids= | Invoices whose transaction failed and were skipped |
| =warnings= | Missing or invalid policies, and "No mail transport is configured" when the outbox has no mailer |
| =plan= | Dry run only: one entry per step with =invoice_id=, =invoice_number=, =step=, =offset=, =outcome= (=send=, =escalate=, =suppress=, =fail=), =reason=, =recipient=, =subject=, =owner= |

=dry_run=true= plans each invoice exactly as the real sweep would (resolving
the recipient and rendering the subject) and writes nothing.
=venture_dunning_service_sweep()= remains for C callers and returns queued
plus escalated.

* Stopping and cancelling

Settlement, void or a dispute stops every later step: the sweep skips the
invoice. Partial payment does not stop reminders; the balance in the next one
is the remaining amount.

A reminder already queued but not yet submitted is rechecked by the outbox's
=before-send= signal, judged by the delivery run's own clock (the claim's
lease minus =VENTURE_MAIL_LEASE_SECONDS=), and cancelled with the reason on
the event. A queued reminder used to be rechecked only for settlement, so
with mail unconfigured or the relay down step 1 and step 2 went out together
on recovery. The reasons, in the order they are checked:

| Reason | When |
|--------+------|
| =module_disabled= | The dunning module was switched off after queueing |
| =invoice_gone= | The invoice was deleted |
| =settled=, =not_issued=, =disputed=, =invoice_disabled=, =company_opt_out= | The invoice left dunning (=/dunning/settlement=, =/dunning/void=, =/dunning/dispute-after-queue=, =/dunning/optout=) |
| =paused= | The invoice or its customer is paused past the delivery day |
| =collection_case= | A held or disputed collection case, or a promise still in the future |
| =superseded= | A later step for the same invoice and policy has been recorded |
| =no_longer_due= | The step's offset is beyond the days overdue, because the due date moved |
| =balance_changed= | The open balance differs from =rendered_balance=, the exact balance the reminder states |
| =stale= | Queued more than 7 days (=VENTURE_DUNNING_STALE_DAYS=) before delivery |

A cancelled row spends no outbox attempt and is never retried automatically.
Rows queued before =rendered_balance= existed skip the balance check.

* Escalation

When =final_escalation= is set, the last step creates an =activity= of kind
=followup=, subject =collect: <invoice number>=, due on the sweep day,
related to the invoice, instead of another email. Its owner is the first of:
=invoice.owner=; the username of the customer's =owner_user_id=; the policy's
=escalation_owner=. Invoices created before =owner= existed used to escalate to
nobody. The event is =escalated= and names the activity. A settled invoice
never escalates; a customer who opted out of reminders, or who has no email
address, still gets the internal task. With the activities module off the
step is recorded as =failed=. Late fees are not applied anywhere; if they are
ever wanted they belong on the escalation step as a credit-note-shaped
adjustment through =VentureSettlementService=, not in the reminder.

* Retry

A failed step used to be final: a fixed template could never send the step
it broke. =dunning_event= has a =retry= action (editor, stageable), allowed on
=failed=, =dead= and =uncertain= rows:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act dunning_event 57 retry
#+end_src

It runs that step again for the invoice now -- escalate, suppress for an
opt-out, or render and enqueue -- under the original key with =:retry:N=
appended, so the attempt is its own idempotent outbox message and the
original row stays as evidence. It is refused when a later step or another
attempt at this one is already recorded (retrying would send an older
reminder), when the invoice has left dunning or is on hold, or when the
policy no longer has a step at that offset. Retrying an =uncertain= row may
duplicate a message the relay did accept; that is the operator's call.

* Test send

=dunning_policy= has a =test_send= action (editor, not stageable) taking
=invoice_id= and an optional =offset=:

#+begin_src sh
venturectl act dunning_policy 2 test_send invoice_id=42 offset=7
#+end_src

It renders the step (by default the invoice's next email step under this
policy, else the policy's first email step) with that invoice's values and
enqueues it to the acting user's own email only, subject prefixed =[TEST]=,
with a random idempotency key. It writes no =dunning_event= -- nothing about
the invoice is decided -- and the customer's pay link is replaced with
=[pay link withheld from test sends]=, because the link is the customer's
credential. A request with no signed-in user (an API token), a user with no
email address, or an escalation step is refused with a message saying so. It
is not stageable because an approval would mail the approver's colleague a
day later.

* Where reminders show

Every event appears as a =reminder= entry on the invoice's timeline and on
the timelines of its contact and company ("reminder sent 2026-01-07 (step 1)",
"reminder step 2 suppressed: company_opt_out", "collection escalated to the
owner"), from the same =venture_desk_activity()= the pages and
=GET /api/v1/activity/:type/:id= render. The invoice's timeline also carries
one computed entry, never written: "next reminder: step 2 on 2026-01-17",
"next: collection escalation on <date> (step 3)" or "reminders paused until
<date>" (=delivery_status= =scheduled=, =id= 0). The customer portal's invoice
list shows only what reached the customer: "reminder sent <date>".

Reminder history cannot be deleted, restored or purged
(=venture_dunning_check_write()=): a deleted event is a step the sweep still
counts as recorded while no page shows it, and a purged one lets a step be
sent twice.

* Reports

** =collections=

One row per policy step, matched by offset: reminders sent, still queued,
failed (=failed=, =dead=, =uncertain=), escalations, suppressed (suppressed or
cancelled), invoices paid within 7 days of that step's send, and the average
days from issue to payment for invoices issued before and after the policy's
=adopted_at= (its creation time when unset). The step counts cover events
queued in the report's period (the API's default period is this month;
=all= for everything). The averages attribute an invoice the way the sweep
resolves it: the invoice's own policy, else its customer's, else the default.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl -f csv report collections all
#+end_src

** =dunning_worklist=

One row per open invoice that is overdue or already has a reminder, most
overdue first: invoice number, customer, days overdue, aging bucket
(=current=, =1-30=, =31-60=, =61-90=, =90+=), open balance, the last step with
its status (and reason) and date, what happens next and when ("step 2 (offset
7)" with its date, "escalation (...)", "escalated", "exhausted", "paused until
<date>", "held by a collection case", "disputed", "no reminders"), and the
escalation owner.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report dunning_worklist
#+end_src

* Relationship to =recurring= collections and =billing dunning=

The =recurring= module's =collection_policy= predates this one and remains:
it sends plain-text notices and monthly statements and keeps a
=collection_case= worklist. =dunning= is the templated, per-customer,
escalating cadence with an effectiveness report; run one or the other for
an organization, not both. Where both are on, a held, disputed or promised
=collection_case= holds dunning for that invoice. =billing dunning= is
subscription past-due recovery and unrelated to invoices.

The sequences engine was considered and not reused: a sequence is enrolled
per contact and paced from enrollment with its own delivery rows, while a
reminder is paced from an invoice's due date, stops on settlement of that
invoice and must be unique per invoice step. Binding enrollments to invoices
would have meant restructuring sequences. What is shared is the mail outbox,
the settlement service's balance and the activities record.

* Operator commands

#+begin_src sh
venturectl create mail_template name=Reminder subject='Invoice {number}' \
  text_body='Hello {customer_name}, {number} for {open_balance} was due {due_at}. {pay_link}'
venturectl create dunning_policy name=Standard is_default=true final_escalation=true \
  escalation_owner=alice adopted_at=2026-01-01 \
  'steps=[{"offset":-3,"template_id":1},{"offset":7,"template_id":1},{"offset":14}]'
venturectl update company 7 dunning_opt_out=true
venturectl update invoice 42 dunning_policy_id=2 owner=alice
venturectl update invoice 43 dunning_paused_until=2026-02-01 dunning_pause_reason='promised by phone'
venturectl dunning sweep as_of=2026-01-17 organization_id=1 limit=100 dry_run=true
venturectl dunning sweep as_of=2026-01-17 organization_id=1 limit=100
venturectl mail deliver --limit 100
venturectl list dunning_event invoice_id=42
venturectl act dunning_event 57 retry
venturectl act dunning_policy 2 test_send invoice_id=42
venturectl report dunning_worklist
#+end_src

=organization_id=, =limit= and =dry_run= are sent as JSON numbers and a
boolean; as text the action refused them. With organization access on, a
member who is not a global owner or admin must pass =organization_id=, and
only an owner, admin or finance member of that organization may sweep it:
reminder policies are financial records. The sweep runs in exactly the
organization it was authorised for. Without =organization_id= a member is
answered =organization_id: Send due reminders runs in one organization=; a
global owner or admin still sweeps the default organization. The sweep and retry actions are
stageable: =venturectl --stage dunning sweep= proposes the sweep for approval
like any other action.

* Migrations

=000320_dunning= verifies the two tables are present together or absent.
=000330_dunning_hardening= verifies =escalation_owner= and =last_sweep= on
policies, the three =rendered_balance= money columns on events and the pause
columns on invoices and companies, each group wholly present or wholly absent;
it invents nothing and does not rewrite legacy position keys.
=000331_private_mail_html= verifies =private_html_body= on =mail_messages=.
All three succeed with the module disabled (=/dunning/migrate-disabled=).

* Verification

=test-dunning= covers the default-policy uniqueness and invoice opt-out,
cadence and idempotent resweep, settlement, void and dispute stops including
a dispute after queueing, customer opt-out with the recorded reason and a
cancelled outbox row that spent no attempt, override resolution, superseded
bursts, the sweep limit on suppressed writes, render failure, transaction
rollback with the recorded attempt and the sweep continuing past it, the pay
link staying out of public bodies, escalation to the owner, the timelines and
portal text, module switch-off, migration with the module disabled, the REST
action's answer and the CLI verb with typed and dry-run arguments against a
private server with a logging mailer. The hardening adds: a deleted contact
(=/dunning/deleted-contact=), the pay link's recipient and HTTPS rules with a
live base URL (=/dunning/pay-link-guard=), the private HTML body
(=/dunning/private-html=, and =/mail/smtp-private-html-wire= on the wire),
policy edits and legacy position keys (=/dunning/policy-edit=), each delivery
cancellation (=/dunning/queue-superseded=, =/dunning/queue-balance=,
=/dunning/queue-due-moved=, =/dunning/queue-stale=, =/dunning/pause=,
=/dunning/module-off-queued=), retry, adoption catch-up, escalation owners
and opt-out escalation, a failed latest step, collection cases, dry runs and
the future =as_of= refusal, the worklist, the next-reminder timeline entry,
test sends, deleted-policy fallback, step validation, the report's period and
attribution, and history that cannot be deleted. =test-automation= covers
=venture->dunning_sweep=. No test sends mail.
