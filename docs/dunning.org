* Dunning

The =dunning= module requires =receivables= and =mail=. Its two records are
field tables: the same declarations produce the database tables, forms, REST
resources, CLI commands, AI schemas and audit entries. Switching the module
off hides both records, the =collections= report and the sweep action
together; invoices, customers and the mail outbox are untouched.

| Record | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =dunning_policy= | A named cadence: steps at day offsets relative to the due date, each with a =mail_template=; whether the last step escalates; when it was adopted |
| =dunning_event= | One step of one policy for one invoice: queued, sent, cancelled, suppressed with a reason, failed, or escalated; the mail message or next action it produced |

=steps= is a JSON array, ordered by strictly increasing offset:

#+begin_src json
[{"offset": -3, "template_id": 12},
 {"offset": 7, "template_id": 13},
 {"offset": 14, "template_id": 14}]
#+end_src

A negative offset is before the due date; =-3= becomes eligible on the third
calendar day before it and never earlier. Offsets are calendar days in UTC.

Exactly one policy per organization may be =is_default=; a second is refused
naming the first. A company may name =dunning_policy_id= to override the
default for every invoice it is billed, and an invoice may name its own to
override both. =invoice.dunning_disabled= names no policy at all; the invoice
is simply skipped. =company.dunning_opt_out= is different: every step that
falls due is recorded as =suppressed= with reason =company_opt_out=, so the
decision not to chase is visible on the invoice and in the report.

Invoices carry an =owner= (a username, like a deal owner). The final step
escalates to that person.

* The sweep

=VentureDunningService.sweep(organization, as_of, limit)= is the one
implementation behind =POST /api/v1/dunning_policy/0/actions/sweep=,
=venturectl dunning sweep= and the assistant's action tool. It is bounded
and synchronous; there is no background thread. Schedule it like the mail
delivery sweep, after business transactions commit:

#+begin_src text
pod dunning_daily = cron->new("0 8 * * *");
dunning_daily->on_schedule => venture->act("dunning_policy", "0", "sweep");
#+end_src

For each issued invoice (=sent= or =partially_paid=) the sweep resolves the
policy, refuses to continue when the invoice is paid, void, disputed
(=workflow_state=disputed=) or has no open balance, and computes the steps
whose offset has been reached and which have no =dunning_event= yet. The
identity that is never repeated is =(invoice, policy, step)=, pinned by the
per-organization unique =dunning_key=; moving an invoice to another policy
starts that policy's cadence. Rerunning the sweep for the same day therefore
produces nothing, which =test-dunning= proves.

When several steps fell due since the last sweep (a policy adopted late, a
sweep that was not run for a fortnight) the customer receives the current
step only; the earlier ones are recorded as =suppressed= with reason
=superseded= rather than sent as a burst.

The current step renders its template with the invoice's public fields plus
={customer_name}=, ={contact_name}=, ={due_at}= and ={issued_at}= as dates,
={open_balance}= (display form) and ={open_balance_exact}=, ={days_overdue}=,
={days_until_due}=, ={step}=, ={offset_days}=, ={policy_name}= and
={pay_link}=. The recipient is the invoice contact's email, or the customer's
when the invoice names no contact; with neither the step is recorded as
=suppressed= (=no_recipient=). A template naming an unknown placeholder
records the step as =failed= with the error and is not retried; fix the
template and later steps proceed.

={pay_link}= is the customer's portal link, =server.base_url= + =/portal/= +
their live =customer_portal_access= token, from which Stripe Checkout opens
for the exact outstanding balance. The token is a bearer credential, so the
rendered body containing it goes only into the message's sensitive
=private_text_body=, which the transport sends in place of the public bodies;
the stored public bodies render ={pay_link}= empty. With no portal access or
no =base_url= the placeholder is empty. Reminders never charge a card.

Each invoice is one transaction: the event, the outbox row and the audit
entries commit together or not at all. If the transaction fails after the
first write it is rolled back and the attempt is then recorded on its own as
a =failed= event with the error, so a step that reached the outbox is never
mistaken next sweep for one that was never due; =/dunning/atomic-attempt=
pins this. Delivery itself is the outbox's at-least-once contract described
in [[file:mail.org][Transactional mail]]: the sweep enqueues, =mail deliver=
submits.

The event's =delivery_status= mirrors the outbox row (=queued=, =sent= with
=sent_at=, =failed=, =dead=, =uncertain=, =cancelled=), so the invoice page,
the timelines and the report read one column.

* Stopping

Settlement, void or a dispute stops every later step: the sweep skips the
invoice, and a reminder already queued but not yet submitted is cancelled by
the outbox's =before-send= recheck with the reason recorded on the event
(=/dunning/settlement=, =/dunning/dispute-after-queue=). The same recheck
enforces a customer opt-out that arrives after a reminder was queued
(=/dunning/optout=). Partial payment does not stop reminders; the balance in
the next one is the remaining amount.

* Escalation

When =final_escalation= is set, the last step creates an =activity= of kind
=followup=, subject =collect: <invoice number>=, owned by =invoice.owner=,
due on the sweep day, related to the invoice, instead of another email. The
event is =escalated= and names the activity. A settled invoice never
escalates. Late fees are not applied anywhere; if they are ever wanted they
belong on the escalation step as a credit-note-shaped adjustment through
=VentureSettlementService=, not in the reminder.

* Where reminders show

Every event appears as a =reminder= entry on the invoice's timeline and on
the timelines of its contact and company ("reminder sent 2026-01-07 (step 1)",
"reminder step 2 suppressed: company_opt_out", "collection escalated to the
owner"), from the same =venture_desk_activity()= the pages and
=GET /api/v1/activity/:type/:id= render. The customer portal's invoice list
shows only what reached the customer: "reminder sent <date>".

* The collections report

=collections= has one row per policy step: reminders sent, escalations,
suppressed, invoices paid within 7 days of that step's send, and the average
days from issue to payment for invoices issued before and after the policy's
=adopted_at= (its creation time when unset). The default policy's averages
cover invoices naming no policy; a named policy's cover those naming it.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl -f csv report collections
#+end_src

* Relationship to =recurring= collections and =billing dunning=

The =recurring= module's =collection_policy= predates this one and remains:
it sends plain-text notices and monthly statements and keeps a
=collection_case= worklist. =dunning= is the templated, per-customer,
escalating cadence with an effectiveness report; run one or the other for
an organization, not both. =billing dunning= is subscription past-due
recovery and unrelated to invoices.

The sequences engine was considered and not reused: a sequence is enrolled
per contact and paced from enrollment with its own delivery rows, while a
reminder is paced from an invoice's due date, stops on settlement of that
invoice and must be unique per invoice step. Binding enrollments to invoices
would have meant restructuring sequences. What is shared is the mail outbox,
the settlement service's balance and the activities record.

* Operator commands

#+begin_src sh
venturectl create mail_template name=Reminder subject='Invoice {number}' \
  text_body='Hello {customer_name}, {number} for {open_balance} was due {due_at}. {pay_link}'
venturectl create dunning_policy name=Standard is_default=true final_escalation=true \
  adopted_at=2026-01-01 'steps=[{"offset":-3,"template_id":1},{"offset":7,"template_id":1},{"offset":14,"template_id":1}]'
venturectl update company 7 dunning_opt_out=true
venturectl update invoice 42 dunning_policy_id=2 owner=alice
venturectl dunning sweep as_of=2026-01-17 organization_id=1 limit=100
venturectl mail deliver --limit 100
venturectl list dunning_event invoice_id=42
#+end_src

=as_of= defaults to now; =limit= is invoices acted on per call, 1–1000,
default 100, including invoices whose due steps were only recorded as
suppressed. The sweep action is stageable: =venturectl --stage dunning sweep=
proposes it for approval like any other action.

* Verification

=test-dunning= covers the default-policy uniqueness and invoice opt-out,
cadence and idempotent resweep, settlement, void and dispute stops including
a dispute after queueing, customer opt-out with the recorded reason and a
cancelled outbox row that spent no attempt, override resolution, superseded
bursts, the sweep limit on suppressed writes, render failure, transaction
rollback with the recorded attempt, the pay link staying out of public
bodies, escalation to the owner, the timelines and portal text, module
switch-off, migration with the module disabled, the REST action and the CLI
verb against a private server with a logging mailer. No test sends mail.
