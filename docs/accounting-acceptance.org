#+title: Synthetic accounting acceptance scenarios
#+description: Reproducible opening, trading, close and recovery evidence

* Scope

=test-accounting-acceptance= composes the existing cutover, settlement,
payables, banking, reporting, close and backup services. All companies,
amounts and provider-independent bank rows are synthetic. The scenarios
exercise the same repository and services used by the application; they do
not simulate a deployment, browser onboarding, external bank feed or an
accountant's independent approval.

The opening-import and trading scenarios have separate empty databases.
Together they cover brought-forward books and a current trading period;
they are not evidence that one customer migration has been reconciled.
Currency arithmetic in this pack is USD integer cents. The wider cutover,
settlement and statement suites cover foreign currency and credit/refund
cases; their existence does not imply those cases were exercised in this
pack.

* Opening import and settlement

=/accounting-acceptance/opening-import= supplies a structured synthetic
QuickBooks-format opening payload to =VentureCutoverService=. The format
selection exercises the existing importer, without contacting QuickBooks.
The 1 January 2026 opening balances are independently specified:

| Control | Debit | Credit |
|---------+-------+--------|
| Cash | 500.00 | |
| Accounts receivable | 105.00 | |
| Accounts payable | | 50.00 |
| Opening equity | | 555.00 |

The outstanding invoice includes 100.00 net and 5.00 historical tax. Import
must bring forward its 105.00 receivable without recognizing that old sale
or its tax again in January. The test checks income, sales-tax control and
opening clearing are zero, asks the cutover service to reconcile, and
checks its trial-balance evidence. Repeating the payload must leave one
invoice and one bill.

A 105.00 receipt on 5 January and a 50.00 vendor payment on 6 January settle
those imported documents through their ordinary services. Cash becomes
555.00, AR and AP become zero, January profit remains zero, and the balance
sheet difference is zero. Historical export and restore must preserve
those statements and every archived public field and relationship after
identity remapping.

* Trading, bank reconciliation, close and correction

=/accounting-acceptance/full-cycle= generates the fiscal calendar and
records an invoice for 100.00 plus 5.00 tax on 10 January, a partial 40.00
receipt on 20 January, a 50.00 vendor bill on 12 January, and its 50.00
payment on 22 January. It also calls the existing dated tax-allocation
correction service. Correctly allocated tax must not be posted twice.

The bank CSV contains the two actual cash movements, with durable
synthetic external identities. Closing a statement while those rows are
unmatched must fail. The operator then explicitly matches each row to its
existing receipt/payment; this does not depend on heuristic automatic
matching. Reconciliation must report a zero difference.

| January result | USD |
|----------------+-----|
| Revenue | 100.00 |
| Expenses | 50.00 |
| Profit | 50.00 |
| Cash | -10.00 |
| Outstanding receivable | 65.00 |
| Outstanding payable | 0.00 |
| Total assets | 55.00 |
| Sales-tax liability | 5.00 |
| Balance-sheet difference | 0.00 |

Negative cash is an intentional synthetic opening assumption: this trading
fixture begins with zero cash and is independent of the funded opening
fixture. It is not silently filled with an unexplained opening balance.

The close service checks the subledger tie-out, completes its checklist,
records distinct preparer and reviewer signatures, and closes January.
The close pack contains the trial balance. A backdated receipt is refused
with =VENTURE_ERROR_CONFLICT= at the actual posting boundary.

An owner then reopens the period. An explicit balanced journal reclassifies
5.00 from account 6900 to 6400, preserving the original bill, payment and
net profit. A fresh check alone must not reuse the previous signatures:
completion returns =VENTURE_ERROR_VALIDATION= until both preparer and
reviewer sign again. The corrected period is closed and exported. Restore
preserves both the
historical evidence and the corrected statements; the source organization's
cash and receivable remain unchanged.

* Failure and retry

=/accounting-acceptance/rollback-retry= injects a failure at the customer
credit save while collecting a 40.00 invoice. The failed transaction must
leave no receipt and no additional journal. After removing the injected
failure, a freshly constructed retry creates exactly one receipt and the
three linked receipt/credit/allocation journals. AR becomes zero and cash
becomes 40.00. Reusing the failed mutable record is intentionally avoided.

* Running the evidence

#+begin_src sh
make DEBUG=1
make DEBUG=1 test-one T=test-accounting-acceptance
#+end_src

To run the same scenarios against a dedicated PostgreSQL test database,
set =VENTURE_TEST_ACCOUNTING_POSTGRES_URI= to its connection URI before
running the test command. The common accounting fixture creates a unique
schema per scenario and drops it after a successful run. A failed run
retains its schema as evidence. Use a disposable test database and a role
allowed to create schemas; do not use production or personal-data stores.
Keep credentials outside shell history and published logs.

The archive helper compares complete public field values after ID/UUID
remapping, exact record counts, ledger balances and invoice/bill balances.
This is the existing version 4 accounting archive, not a tenant or complete
installation backup: attachments, credentials and other excluded resources
remain outside its contract. See [[file:backup.org][historical accounting recovery]].

* Readiness evidence still required

Passing this pack demonstrates these executable scenarios on the backend
actually used. It does not establish M1 controlled parallel-use readiness,
M2 replacement readiness, independent accounting review, PostgreSQL coverage
unless separately run, or hosted tenant recovery. Those decisions require a
recorded deployment exercise, source-to-destination reconciliations and
resolution of any remaining workflow or infrastructure gaps.
