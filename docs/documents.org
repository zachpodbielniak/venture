#+title: Guided invoice and quote editor
#+description: One page to add lines, tax and send

Draft invoices and composed quotes do not require =post=/=pay= approval. Composing and sending an invoice together does: the complete header, lines and send command are one proposal, and the second actor repeats the exact input before any rows are created.

The guided editor reuses invoice and quote field tables and
=VentureQuoteService=. =POST /api/v1/invoices/compose= and
=/api/v1/quotes/compose= create a header, lines and optionally send in one
transaction. Numbers are allocated per organization when omitted. Invoice
quantities keep thousandths precision; quote quantities remain whole units
until a later commercial change. =venturectl compose invoice|quote JSON=
calls the same routes.

Every line must be an object with a positive numeric quantity and an exact money price. Fractional quote quantities are refused rather than truncated; invoice quantities retain their existing fractional support. Validation or send failures roll back the document and all its lines.

The browser editor is laid out as the document: the customer and "Attention of" at the top, a line table rows are added to, and the totals beside it as you type. API and CLI callers continue to use the same JSON composer.

* Invoice dates

The invoice sheet shows editable invoice and due dates beside the customer.
The defaults are today and thirty days later, stored as calendar dates at
midnight UTC. The invoice date is set before saving a draft, so configured
fiscal periods validate it normally. Sending uses that same date for issuance
and its journal; it does not silently replace a date the operator chose.
When issuance needs a second actor, the proposal includes the resolved dates.
Retrying a relative date on another day cannot reuse consent for yesterday.

The JSON composer accepts optional =issued_at= and =due_at= date strings.
Without =due_at=, =due_days= remains supported (default 30), counted from the
invoice date. Zero or negative =due_days= keeps the existing no-due-date behavior.
An explicit due date takes precedence over =due_days=. Invalid date strings,
wrong JSON types, overflowing offsets and a due date before the invoice date
are refused before any rows are saved. Closed or uncovered fiscal periods
remain refused by the ordinary accounting guard.

* Tax on an invoice

An invoice line is taxed by picking a *tax rate* -- a =tax_code= record --
from the line's Tax menu. "No tax" is first and chosen. Each rate shows its
percent; the preview adds it up, and the saved invoice freezes the rate's
exact fraction at issue. The JSON composer takes the same thing as
=tax_code_id= on a line. A quote sheet has the same Tax menu and a quote
line the same =tax_code_id=, so a quote asks for exactly what the invoice
it becomes will; a whole =tax_percent= is still accepted from the API for
lines that name no rate.

A tax rate is made at =/tax-rates/new= from a name and a percent ("8.875"
is stored as 8875 over 100000, never as a float); the code the return
files it under is made from the name. Tax rates are under Books in the
sidebar.

An exempt customer -- a non-profit, a reseller, a government body -- is
marked on the customer: =tax_exempt=, =tax_exempt_reason= and
=tax_exemption_number=. Choosing such a customer on the invoice sheet
ticks "This customer doesn't pay tax" and fills in why and the
certificate. Ticking it by hand asks for both and, unless "Remember for
this customer" is cleared, saves them onto the customer so the next
invoice fills itself in. The invoice freezes =tax_exempt= and a reason
such as "Non-profit (501(c)(3)), certificate EX-12"; the JSON composer
takes =tax_exempt= and =tax_exempt_reason=.

* Attention of

"Attention of" must be somebody at the invoice's customer. The field is
declared =VENTURE_COLUMN_FLAG_SAME_PARENT=: the save refuses a contact
whose company is not the invoice's, from every writer, and the form
offers only the chosen customer's people. The same holds for a quote.
See the record view documentation for how the flag finds the parent.

Remembering a customer's tax exemption and saving the invoice or repeating
schedule is atomic. If the sheet is refused, the customer's previous tax
treatment and audit history remain unchanged.

Remembering a customer's exemption is part of the invoice transaction, after
any required second-actor consent. A proposal or a failed save changes neither
the customer nor the invoice. The JSON composer carries this intent as
=remember_tax_exemption=true= with =tax_exemption_kind= and
=tax_exemption_number=; these values are included in issuance consent. Repeating
schedules keep their customer update and schedule creation in one transaction.

Browser composition strips identity fields from temporary form records before
building the command. Retrying the same form for approval therefore compares
its business inputs, not new construction timestamps.
