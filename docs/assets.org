#+title: Fixed assets and recurring journals
#+description: Book depreciation, prepayments and accrual releases
#+created: [2026-09-13]

* Assets

Voiding a source invoice cancels its recognition deferrals atomically: future entries become skipped and previously posted recognition journals receive linked reversals. Posted schedule entries retain their original journal references as evidence; the cancelled parent cannot settle as a completed accrual.

The =assets= module requires =ledger= and =periods=. Its four field tables
are =fixed_asset=, =depreciation_entry=, =deferral= and =deferral_entry=.
Their registers, forms, JSON, REST, related panels, assistant schemas and
ordinary CLI access come from the entity registry. Turning the module off
hides the records and both reports; service actions are refused.

A fixed asset records its organization, optional venture, name, tag,
category, acquisition date, exact cost and salvage value, useful life in
months, method, account choices, lifecycle dates and source expense.
Tags are unique within one organization, including retained deleted drafts.
Another organization may use the same tag.

Deleted drafts retain their tag for uniqueness but are excluded from the fixed-asset report and its category totals.

The asset's profile must supply active accounts in that organization:
=asset_account_id=, =accumulated_depreciation_account_id= and
=depreciation_expense_account_id=. A sale of the asset also needs
=proceeds_account_id= and may select =gain_loss_account_id=. A write-off
uses the depreciation expense account when no separate loss account is
selected. This module adds no seeded accounts.

=venture_asset_service_create_from_expense()= accepts a saved capital
expense and returns a draft with its cost, acquisition date, venture and
source link filled. Select the remaining profile fields before placement.
Creating the draft does not rewrite the expense journal. Placement reads
its current posted debit accounts and transfers the matching cost into the
selected asset account through a new posting-service journal. A profile
that already capitalized the purchase needs no transfer. Multiple active
source journals, mismatched amounts or a purchase already placed under
another asset are refused. An asset entered without a source expense must
already have its acquisition recorded through an explicit posting-service
journal or the installation's acquisition policy.

* Placing an asset in service

=VentureAssetService= owns the transition from =draft= to =in_service= and
creates the entire schedule in one transaction. A missing service date means
now. Dates before acquisition, invalid lives, negative amounts, salvage
above cost, mixed currencies and unusable accounts are refused. Lives are
bounded to 1-1200 months.

If the requested service date is closed, placement chooses the first open
fiscal period and stores the explanation in =schedule_note=. It never posts
into the closed period. A calendar that does not cover the starting date,
or has no later open period, must be corrected first. Future schedule rows
can extend beyond the current fiscal year; posting them still requires
calendar coverage when their month is run.

| Method | Monthly calculation |
|--------+---------------------|
| =straight_line= | Split cost less salvage equally; move residual cents to the last month |
| =declining_balance= | Double-declining book rate =2 / useful_life_months= on remaining carrying value; cap at salvage and release the remaining depreciable amount in the final month |
| =none= | No depreciation entries |

All calculations use =VentureMoney=. Straight-line and deferral splits use
=venture_money_allocate_evenly()= and redistribute its residual minor units
to the final installment, preserving the exact original total. There is no
floating-point money, currency conversion or daily proration.

Book and tax conventions are independent. =book-convention= and
=tax-convention= are =full_month= (the historical schedule) or
=half_year= (first installment halved, residual on the last month).
=tax-useful-life-months= and =tax-method= build a =tax_depreciation_entry=
schedule at placement. That schedule does not post to the book GL until
=assets run-tax-period=, which writes journals with =tax-book= set.
Statements ignore tax-book journals.

These tax schedules are not jurisdictional elections. No bonus, section 179
or MACRS table is inferred.

** A worked laptop

A $1,100 laptop with zero salvage and a 36-month life, placed in January
2026, has 35 charges of $30.55 and a final December 2028 charge of $30.75:
=35 * 3055 + 3075 = 110000= cents. January's posted journal debits
depreciation expense and credits accumulated depreciation by $30.55.
Its January net book value is $1,069.45.

** Disposal and write-off

=dispose= removes original cost and accumulated posted depreciation,
debits proceeds to the chosen cash account, and recognizes the difference
as gain or loss. =write-off= performs the same operation with zero proceeds.
The disposal date cannot precede service or posted depreciation, and its
period must be open. The journal, terminal asset state and skipped future
schedule rows commit together. Posted history is retained.

Generic writes cannot change an asset's lifecycle status or rewrite a
placed asset. They name =VentureAssetService= in the refusal. Deletion,
restoration and purge cannot remove placed assets or schedule history.
The removal check reads the stored state, not a caller's proposed state.

* Prepayments and accruals

=VentureDeferralService= creates the source and full exact-sum schedule in
one transaction. A deferral has its organization, kind, description, total,
start date, number of months, source and target account IDs, optional source
expense/invoice references and derived active/complete status.

For a prepayment without a source expense, supply =funding_account_id=:
creation debits prepaid and credits funding. If =source_expense_id= is
supplied, its organization and amount must match; creation instead debits
prepaid and credits the selected expense account to reclassify that cost.
The selected target must be the expense account used by the source's
posting policy. Each release debits target expense and credits prepaid.

An expense accrual has no initial journal. Each release debits target
expense and credits the accrued liability. Once every release is posted,
=operation=settle= with a =settlement_account_id= posts debit accrued /
credit settlement cash, records =settled_at= and =settlement_journal_id=,
and refuses a second settlement. A missing settlement date means now;
settlement cannot precede any release. Generic edits to schedule amounts,
journal links or derived state are refused.

When the target is an income account, the legs are reversed: an accrued
income release debits accrued assets and credits income; settlement debits
cash and credits accrued assets. A deferred-income source debits its funding
account and credits deferred income, then releases debit deferred / credit
income. Source references must belong to the same organization.

The deferral report's remaining balance means the amount still to release;
it is zero once all installments are posted. An accrued liability awaiting
cash settlement is visible in its ledger account.

* Running a month

#+begin_src sh
venturectl assets run-period 2026-01 organization_id=1 --dry-run
venturectl assets run-period 2026-01 organization_id=1
venturectl assets run-tax-period 2026-01 organization_id=1
venturectl asset place 12
venturectl asset dispose 12 'proceeds=500 USD' disposed_at=2026-07-15
venturectl asset write-off 12 disposed_at=2026-07-15
#+end_src

The period is exactly =YYYY-MM=. An omitted organization selects the
installation's default legal entity. The sweep posts every scheduled
asset and deferral entry in that month, dated at the end of the UTC month.
It checks the period guard even if no entries remain. A closed or locked
period returns the guard's error; the sweep itself never silently moves a
requested month.

One transaction spans the entire run. If any entry fails after another has
posted, every journal, entry update and audit write from that run rolls
back. Successful entries retain their journal IDs and source links;
running the same month again posts nothing. Dry-run checks the schedule,
parent states, accounts and period without creating journals.

** Scheduling with an automation rule

The registered =assets_run_period= handler accepts a month and optional
organization ID as strings. =last_month=, or an omitted month, selects the
previous calendar month when the handler runs:

#+begin_src
pod monthly_assets = cron->new("0 8 1 * *");
monthly_assets->on_schedule => venture->assets_run_period("last_month", "1");
#+end_src

This calls the same service on the automation engine's main thread and
records the automation actor. Keep the prior month open until the run has
succeeded, then close it. Retries are safe. Preview with the CLI before
turning on a new monthly rule.

* Surfaces and approval

=POST /api/v1/fixed_assets/:id/place-in-service=, =/dispose= and
=/write-off= require an editor and accept a JSON object containing the
action's dates and =disposal_proceeds=. Add =?stage=1= to propose the action.
The asset page has the same controls; its related-record panel lists the
schedule through the declared =asset_id= reference.

=POST /api/v1/assets/run-period= accepts
={"period":"2026-01","organization_id":1,"dry_run":false}= and returns
={"entries":N,"dry_run":false}=. It uses the same editor and module guards.

The assistant can stage an ordinary =fixed_asset= update with
=operation=place=, =dispose= or =write-off=, or a =deferral= update with
=operation=settle=. The command appears in the approval diff. Approval
calls the same service and rechecks the current state and period.
=operation=run-period:YYYY-MM= on an asset runs its organization's whole
month and can also be staged. Status remains service-owned.

Both services expose a =transition= signal with =G_SIGNAL_RUN_LAST=.
It receives a detached source snapshot and action name inside the transaction,
before financial writes. Snapshot edits are ignored; the first owned
=GError *= returned by a handler vetoes the operation. External consumers
needing committed journal effects should use the posting service's
=posted= signal.

* Migrated assets

=venture_asset_service_import_opening()= places an asset a cutover migrates
without recomputing its history (see [[file:cutover.org][accounting cutover]]);
the cutover passes its opening balance clearing account as the equity side.
=venture_asset_service_rollback_opening()= retires such an asset after the
caller reversed its opening journal and any depreciation posted after the
cutoff: remaining scheduled entries become skipped, the asset is written off
at the given date without a second journal, and a tag suffix frees the tag
for a corrected import (tags are unique per organization, retired assets
included).

* Reports and upgrades

=fixed_assets= shows cost, accumulated depreciation and net book value per
asset, plus category totals separated by currency. Disposal removes carrying
value on its effective date. =deferrals= shows total, released and remaining
per source. Both honor =organization_id=, =currency= and =as_of= with the
reporting period's exclusive endpoint. Later releases do not rewrite earlier
balances. Both are financial reports and are captured at period close.

The paired =000190_assets.sql= migrations verify that the module is either
absent or has all four metadata-created tables. They do not infer asset
history from old expenses. Upgrade and restart preserve existing records;
a partial table set fails transactionally. PostgreSQL uses the current
schema, matching isolated-schema installations.

=test-assets= covers schedules, lifecycle refusals, source conversion,
settlement, historical reporting, atomic/idempotent sweeps, dry-run,
staged approval, migration failure/retry and lifecycle vetoes. HTTP auth
coverage lives in =test-auth=.
