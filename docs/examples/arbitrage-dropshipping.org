* What this is

A dropshipper lists a supplier's goods on a marketplace and buys each one only when it has sold, shipped straight from the supplier to the customer. The margin is the marketplace's price less its fee less the supplier's landed cost, and the question every day is which of the supplier's lines are worth listing. This page is how =make demo= seeds that into its default organization (=seed_trading_desk= in =tools/venture-demo.sh=): a venture, two data sources, two venues, the =spread= scan across them, and one order through the books.

The buy side is the reference exec plugin, walked through on its own in [[file:dropship-supplier-feed.org][a dropshipping supplier's price list]]; the rules are in [[file:../arbitrage.org][Arbitrage]].

* Turning it on

The demo runs with:

#+begin_src yaml
feeds:
  enabled: true
  file_roots: /path/to/build/demo/market    # the generated files; nothing else
plugins:
  allow_exec: true
  paths: [/path/to/build/demo/plugins]      # a copy of the repository's plugins/exec
#+end_src

(as =VENTURE_FEEDS_ENABLED=, =VENTURE_FEEDS_FILE_ROOTS=, =VENTURE_PLUGINS_ALLOW_EXEC= and =VENTURE_PLUGINS_PATHS= in =start_server=). The plugin directory is a copy so that the supplier's snapshot -- dated by its price list's modification time -- is today's rather than the day the repository was cloned. =feeds.allowed_origins= stays empty: neither source reaches the network.

* The venture and its sources

#+begin_src sh
VENTURE=$(new venture name="Kitchen dropship" slug=kitchen-dropship venture_type=general \
    status=active priority=normal \
    description="Kitchen goods listed on ShopMart and shipped by Acme Wholesale; no stock held.")

SUPPLIER=$(new data_source name="Acme Wholesale" provider=supplier_csv schedule=manual currency=USD \
    venue_namespace=supplier instrument_namespace=sku \
    settings=$'venue: acme\nvenue_name: Acme Wholesale\ngroup: us')
MARKETPLACE=$(new data_source name="ShopMart listings" provider=file_jsonl schedule=manual currency=USD \
    venue_namespace=marketplace instrument_namespace=sku settings='file: usd/marketplace.jsonl')

venturectl feeds sync $SUPPLIER --wait
venturectl feeds sync $MARKETPLACE --wait
#+end_src

=new= creates a record and prints its id (see [[file:game-economy.org::*Before you start][the game economy]]). The supplier's program reads =sample-price-list.csv= in its own directory: six SKUs (=DS-1001= to =DS-1006=), one out of stock, each a listing at its landed price -- price plus shipping, added as decimal strings. The marketplace file is the generator's: fourteen days of hourly snapshots of two competing sellers per SKU on ShopMart, under the *same keys* -- which is what lets a scan put a supplier's =DS-1001= against ShopMart's.

* Venues and fees

These sources belong to the default organization, so promotion needs no =organization_id= (another organization's source would be promoted with =organization_id=N= after the key):

#+begin_src sh
ACME=$(venturectl -f json market promote $SUPPLIER venue acme | python3 -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["id"])')
SHOPMART=$(venturectl -f json market promote $MARKETPLACE venue shopmart | python3 -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["id"])')

venturectl update venue $ACME fee_model=none transfer_hours=72
venturectl update venue $SHOPMART fee_model=percent fee_params=$'cut_percent: 13\nfixed_per_order: 0.30 USD'
#+end_src

ShopMart keeps thirteen percent of a sale and thirty cents an order. Neither venue has a location, so the trade's money moves through the organization's cash account, which is not a holding and has no floor.

* The scan

#+begin_src sh
venturectl arbitrage scan spread buy_venues=acme sell_venues=shopmart units=1
#+end_src

#+begin_example
Bamboo cutting board: Acme Wholesale → ShopMart             8.15 USD   97.6%
Ceramic pour-over cone "V60" style: Acme Wholesale → ShopMart   7.64 USD  111.5%
Silicone baking mat, set of 2: Acme Wholesale → ShopMart    6.41 USD  160.3%
...
#+end_example

=spread= reads candidates from every source of the organization and, for each, the same key at every other venue of every source: Acme's =DS-1001= at 8.35 landed against ShopMart's lowest at about 19.50, less 13% and 0.30, is about eight dollars a board. The trivet is out of stock at Acme and never a candidate. Numbers move a little with the hour the demo ran: ShopMart's sellers reprice every day or so.

The demo saves the question as a preset, and the Trading desk dashboard (=/dashboards/trading=, imported from =data/examples/demo-trading.json=) runs it:

#+begin_src sh
new arbitrage_strategy name="Dropship: Acme to ShopMart" strategy=spread \
    buy_venues=acme sell_venues=shopmart options=$'units: 1\nmin_profit: 2.00 USD\nsort: roi'
#+end_src

=arbitrage record 1 spread buy_venues=acme sell_venues=shopmart units=1= would record the first row as a planned trade, with exactly the legs the scan priced.

* An order, end to end

Three boards sold on ShopMart, bought from Acme and shipped by it. No stock is held, so the legs carry money only (no =inventory_item_id=):

#+begin_src sh
BOARD=$(venturectl -f json market promote $SUPPLIER instrument DS-1001 | python3 -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["id"])')

ORDER=$(venturectl -f json arbitrage record name="Three bamboo cutting boards" strategy=spread \
    venture_id=$VENTURE expected='{"profit": "25.00 USD"}' legs='[
  {"kind":"buy","status":"executed","venue_id":'$ACME',"instrument_id":'$BOARD',"quantity":3,
   "unit_price":"8.35 USD","occurred_at":"2026-10-01T07:00:00Z","notes":"6.40 and 1.95 shipping, each"},
  {"kind":"sell","status":"executed","venue_id":'$SHOPMART',"instrument_id":'$BOARD',"quantity":3,
   "unit_price":"19.49 USD","fees":"7.90 USD","occurred_at":"2026-10-01T11:00:00Z",
   "notes":"13% of 58.47 and 0.30 for the order"}]' \
    | python3 -c 'import json,sys; print(json.load(sys.stdin)["id"])')
venturectl arbitrage close $ORDER closed_at=2026-10-01T12:00:00Z
#+end_src

The demo dates the buy 70 hours before its anchor and the sale 66. =DS-1001= is promoted once, from the supplier's source; the marketplace's =DS-1001= has the same namespace and key, so promoting it there returns the same record.

| Leg   | Journal                                                                 |
|-------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| buy   | Dr Arbitrage positions 25.05 / Cr Cash 25.05                            |
| sell  | Dr Cash 58.47 / Cr positions 58.47; Dr Arbitrage fees 7.90 / Cr Cash 7.90 |
| close | Dr positions 33.42 / Cr Arbitrage gains 33.42                            |

Realised: 58.47 - 25.05 - 7.90 = *25.52 USD*, ROI 102% on 25.05, slippage 0.52 against the 25.00 expected.

* Traps

- *Matching keys are the whole trick.* The supplier's SKU and the marketplace's listing must be the same instrument key, in sources of the same organization, or the scan never pairs them. A marketplace that names products its own way needs a mapping before it is a source -- a =csv= source's =fields= or a script.
- *An exec plugin runs as the server's user.* =plugins.allow_exec= is off by default; turn it on only where nobody else can write a plugin directory ([[file:../plugins.org::*Security model][Plugins: security model]]).
- *A dated snapshot is a dated snapshot.* The supplier's taken-at is its file's modification time; a list emailed last month reads as a month old on the Venues page, and =max_age_hours= on a scan leaves it out.
- *No stock means no =inventory_item_id=.* A leg with one is a stock leg and needs units on the shelf; a dropship order never has any.
