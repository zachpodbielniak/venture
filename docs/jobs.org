* What a job is

A home-services business works at its customers' addresses: a water heater
replaced, a furnace serviced, a drain cleared. A =job= is one such piece of
work. Before it existed the nearest thing was an activity of kind
"meeting", which has no customer address, no technicians, no promised
window and no link to the estimate or the bill.

The =jobs= module requires =crm= and suggests =quotes= and =invoicing=. It
owns five record types, all derived from their field tables like every
other: a list, a form, REST, CLI, JSON and the assistant's tools.

| Record | What it holds |
|--------+---------------|
| =job= | title, number, status, customer (=company_id=), contact on site, service address, problem, arrival window (=window_start=, =window_end=), estimate (=quote_id=), invoice (=invoice_id=), the times it was set off for, arrived at and finished, notes |
| =job_technician= | a job and a user sent to it; =lead= marks the one the customer deals with |
| =property= | a customer's place: name ("Home", "14 Oak St rental"), customer, address, how to get in (=access_notes=: gate code, key, pets, parking), who is there (=contact_id=), notes |
| =equipment= | a thing at a property that gets serviced: what it is, make, model, serial number, year installed, warranty end, notes |
| =maintenance_plan= | a visit bought on a schedule: plan name, customer, property, equipment, what each visit is (=visit=), months apart (=every_months=, 12 when blank), next visit due, when it ended, notes |

The technician picker names people by display name, else username, as the
access forms do; so does the day view below. Everywhere else a user is
named by display name, else e-mail, never "User #3" while either is set.
A job also names its =property_id=, and =maintenance_plan_id= when it was
booked from a plan. Properties, Equipment and Jobs are
under Customers in the sidebar, after Contacts. A property's page lists
its equipment and its jobs. A new property opened from a customer
(=/e/property/new?company_id=N=) starts with the customer's address, and
the property picker says whose each one is: "Home (Example household)".

A customer's page has a Work card: their properties, jobs, quotes and
invoices, each with a count and a button to start one for that customer.
A job's page has The visit: who is on it (the lead marked), how to get in
from the property, and the equipment there with make, model, year and
serial. Saving any new record opens it, rather than the list.

* What the save holds to

- A job saved without a number takes the next free =J-= number in its
  business; deleted jobs count, so a number is never reused. A typed
  number is kept, trimmed, and is unique within the business.
- The customer is required and must be in the job's business. A contact on
  site must be too, and must not work for another customer.
- An arrival window ends after it starts. An end with no start is refused;
  a start alone is allowed ("from 8").
- The estimate and the invoice must be the job's customer's own. With
  =quotes= or =invoicing= switched off, naming one is refused with the
  switch named.
- An =invoiced= job names its invoice.
- A technician is an active user who can sign in, on a job in the same
  business, once per job.
- A property belongs to a customer in its business and has an address.
  Its contact, when named, is in the same business.
- Equipment is at a property in its business. Its year installed is blank
  ("nobody knows") or a year from 1900 to next year, so its age can be
  read from it. Make, model and serial number are searchable.
- A maintenance plan is its customer's, at one of their properties, for
  equipment at that property (naming only the equipment fills in its
  property); a blank visit takes the plan's name, and the interval is 1 to
  120 months. A job's plan is its own customer's.
- A job's property is its own customer's. A job that names a property and
  has no service address of its own takes the property's address; a typed
  service address is kept.

* Through the day

The job's page offers only the buttons that apply to where it is. Each is
an ordinary record action (=POST /api/v1/job/:id/actions/<name>=,
=venturectl act job ID <name>=), needs an editor, and stamps when it
happened:

| Button | Action | From | To | Stamps |
|--------+--------+------+----+--------|
| On my way | =on_the_way= | booked | on the way | =left_at= |
| Arrived | =arrive= | booked, on the way | on site | =arrived_at= |
| Done | =complete= | booked, on the way, on site | done | =completed_at= |
| Invoiced | =invoiced= | done | invoiced | |
| Cancel job | =cancel= | booked, on the way, on site | cancelled | |

=invoiced= takes an optional =invoice_id=. Without one it uses the
invoice the job already names, else the invoice its accepted estimate
became, and refuses when there is neither. A button pressed from a status
it does not apply to is refused with the status named, so a finished job
cannot be put back on the road by a stale page.

*Send a technician* (=assign=, =user_id=, =lead=) is on the job's Actions
card while the job is still to be done. It adds the person, or changes
whether they lead; making one the lead stops the previous lead leading.

An estimate billed after the work (see [[file:quotes.org][Quotes]]) left a
draft invoice; =invoiced= issues it, dated today and due in thirty days,
which is when reminders can start.

The status is an ordinary field as well, so an owner can correct it on
the form; the save rules above still apply.

* A technician's day

=/jobs/day= (Day, under Customers in the sidebar) lists one technician's
jobs whose arrival window starts on one day in the business's time zone,
earliest first. Each shows its window, status, the job, the customer with a
tap-to-call link (the contact's phone, else the customer's), the service
address with a Directions link (a =geo:= address the phone's map opens),
how to get in from the property, the problem, and who is on it. Cancelled
jobs and other days are left out.

Without =tech= it is the signed-in person's day; =tech=ID= is one
technician's and =tech=all= everyone's; =date=YYYY-MM-DD= picks the day,
today by default. The arrival window is typed as a date and a time of day
on the job's form; see [[file:data-model.org][the data model]] for how a
date-and-time box is read.

Booked jobs are also on the calendar (the Jobs layer: whose and where, by
technician when filtered by assignee), in a "Jobs today" card at the top of
My day, and in a Today card at the top of the home page with the quotes
waiting for an answer and the overdue invoices.

* Maintenance visits

=/jobs/visits= (Visits due, under Customers) lists the plans whose next
visit is in the next 30 days or overdue, soonest first, with the customer
and the property's address. *Book the visit*
(=POST /api/v1/maintenance_plan/:id/actions/book=) makes the job -- the
visit as its title, the plan's customer and property, the equipment in its
problem -- and moves the plan's next visit on by its interval from the day
it was due, so a visit booked late does not push the later ones back. The
job's time is set on the job. A plan that has ended is refused. How the
plan is billed (a subscription, an invoice per visit) is separate.

* For a home-services business

=data/examples/home-services.yaml= switches off the modules such a
business never uses -- repositories and releases, trading and crafting,
holding companies, idea boards -- so the sidebar is customers, jobs,
quotes, invoices, reminders and the books. Nothing is deleted.

* Not yet

There is no dispatch board of technicians against hours, and a
maintenance plan is not linked to the subscription that bills it.

* Lists on a phone

The ordinary Jobs list stacks each job's facts below its title on narrow
screens: customer, service address and arrival dates stay visible without
sideways scrolling. Sorting, paging and bulk selection still use the same
records. Addresses are multiline facts in the shared metadata renderer.
A new job linked to an estimate takes its explicit service address when
neither a typed job address nor a property's address is supplied.
