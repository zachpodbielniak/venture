#+title: Hosted control and evidence matrix
#+description: Enforced boundaries, evidence limits and outstanding launch controls

* Status and authority

This matrix accompanies [[file:post-parity.org][issue 125's delivery contract]],
[[file:tenant-operations.org][the platform lifecycle runbook]] and
[[file:tenant-recovery.org][the recovery contract]]. It records technical controls;
it is not a certification, legal retention determination or hosted-launch approval.
A passing small fixture does not satisfy the capacity or deployment gates.
Lightsite provisioning is excluded. Its separate capture contract is unaffected.

An implementation lane is not integrated evidence. Record the exact PR revision,
image digest, configuration, database version and test transcript before marking a
control qualified. Failed drills remain evidence of the failure until a corrected
artifact passes; do not silently replace their status with a unit-test result.

* Data and authority inventory

The supported topology is one application process, database/role, state tree and
provider-key domain per workspace. Legal organizations are subordinate business
scopes inside that workspace. A customer company is not an isolation boundary.
The record/resource classification registry supplies the exhaustive machine-readable
inventory; this table describes handling by class rather than maintaining a second
list of record types. Registry classification is still subject to integrated review.

| Class | Examples | Access and export | Retention/removal boundary |
|-------+----------+-------------------+----------------------------|
| Tenant business | Accounting, CRM, projects, delivery evidence | Local organization/record authority; complete encrypted operator snapshot includes attachments | Posted financial evidence and dependent references cannot be erased by generic soft delete. A separately authorized retention/erasure workflow is required. |
| Personal within a tenant | Private mail/calendar, conversations, personal notifications | Owner binding in addition to workspace and organization; snapshot is privileged platform data | Disconnect stops new access/import but is not historical erasure. Account disablement does not erase records. |
| Tenant administration | Memberships, invitations, scoped support grants | Verified administrative routes/actions; ordinary business authority does not imply administration | Revocation invalidates authority immediately. Restore quarantines old authority before reopening access. |
| Intentionally public/reference | Build health, published shared reference values | Explicitly classified route/resource; health carries no installation activity | Public classification does not authorize exposing business counts, secrets or neighbor identity. |
| Platform | Database/configuration, host execution, trusted plugins, recovery archives and keys | Local authenticated platform operation; no tenant filesystem/image selection or unrestricted in-process code | Operator access and destructive lifecycle actions require distinct evidence and retention policy. |
| Unclassified | New plugin types and undeclared hosted routes | Hosted exposure fails closed until explicitly classified | A plugin's installation is not approval to expose its data. |

Snapshots contain all classes in a workspace, including disabled identities and
historical secrets. They are recovery artifacts, not a sanitized customer-facing
export. Delivering one to a third party requires an explicit sensitive-export
authorization and a secure, separately keyed transfer channel.

* Control and evidence matrix

“Lane evidence” means exercised on the named component's revision and must be
repeated on the final combined artifact. “Deployment gate” requires evidence from
the actual operating environment, not a source-code claim.

| Control | Enforcement/evidence available | Remaining qualification |
|---------+--------------------------------+-------------------------|
| Physical workspace separation | Platform tool creates separate rootless networks, PostgreSQL volumes/roles and private config/state roots; validates labels/full IDs/mounts before mutation | Final combined adversarial A/B deployment, colliding identifiers, callbacks, files and support access; independent isolation review |
| Provider secrets | Encrypted organization bindings, owner-private connector policy, version/revocation guards; rotation/process-lease lane tests | Integrate all provider lanes; finish remaining ambient provider settings; review external processor inventory |
| Revoked authority after restore | Offline quarantine suspends and invalidates restored authentication authority; session secret rotates; explicit named recovery; fresh default-module physical drill passed | Final combined credential/MFA/member-reenrollment tests and deployment recovery qualification |
| Complete encrypted backup | Authenticated GnuPG archive, private files, payload hashes, immutable workspace/image binding and empty-target restore; real PostgreSQL lane drill | Final revision and actual deployment backup credentials, remote transport, access and retention evidence |
| Neighbor-preserving recovery | Recovery lane restored 214 tables/205 synthetic rows plus attachment/encrypted settings; 189 neighbor probes succeeded and neighbor rows were unchanged | Representative final Accounting/CRM/project tie-outs and production-sized recovery workload |
| Exclusive maintenance | Shared platform flock; server process-lease lane covers state/SQLite inode and database-scoped PostgreSQL advisory ownership | Integrate both layers and repeat competing-process, crash and upgrade tests on final image |
| Upgrade and rollback | Tool snapshots under the same lock, migrates candidate offline and refuses old-binary restart after failure | Distinct schema-version upgrade under traffic; measure outage; rehearse restoration to an empty original-image target |
| Audit | Application privilege/business events; hash-linked private platform operation log with explicit reason | Separately administered append-only sink, delivery failure alert, retention/restore policy and tamper investigation drill |
| TLS and encrypted storage | Backend pinned to configured HTTPS authority and loopback proxy mapping; provider transports use documented boundaries | Actual certificate/proxy configuration, disk/volume encryption, key custody and restore evidence. Loopback and rootless containers do not encrypt storage. |
| Residency and processors | Separate local roots and explicit configured integrations make placement enumerable | Record actual primary/replica/attachment/log/backup locations and each authorized processor. No residency claim before inventory and enforcement are verified. |
| Offboarding and holds | Offboard stops/suspends and retains data; operator hold is recorded; no implicit subscription deletion | Local archive catalog/explicit expiry now documented in backup retention; scheduler, offsite hold propagation, authorized erasure/anonymization and final media/key destruction evidence remain |
| Recovery targets | Selected RPO 15 minutes/RTO four hours; daily operational backup retention target 30 days | WAL/PITR capture/restore, offsite schedule, alerting and measured worst-case drill. Daily snapshots alone cannot meet a 15-minute RPO. |
| Limits and fairness | Per-workspace process/database resource defaults; bounded subsystem work and durable identities where documented | API/storage/job admission limits, heavy-neighbor fairness and restart/overload qualification |
| Capacity | Explicit 100/1,000/3,000 profiles and latency/error targets in delivery contract | Actual measured hardware, occupancy, active users, storage and rates. No extrapolation from a two-workspace drill. |

* Retention and erasure rules

Until an approved class-specific retention policy and execution mechanism exist,
offboarding retains the workspace and releasing a hold deletes nothing. This
fail-safe prevents accidental destruction; it does not satisfy a personal-data
removal request or justify indefinite retention. Hosted launch remains blocked on
that missing workflow.

A retention policy must distinguish financial evidence, operational history,
personal communications, authentication authority, derived indexes, logs and
recovery archives. Store its organization/workspace scope, applicable hold, expiry
basis and approving authority. Define how accounting references retain necessary
evidence while removable personal payloads are anonymized. A successful soft
delete or disabled user is never reported as erasure.

The 30-day operational backup target does not override a hold or a separate
financial-evidence policy. A deletion ledger must survive backup restoration so
later recovery cannot republish previously erased payloads. Credential quarantine
protects authority; it is not a substitute for replaying that deletion ledger.
The [[file:backup-retention.org][local archive catalog]] now enforces authenticated identity, explicit
plan/execute, holds and crash reconciliation for registered local files. Do not
claim automated or offsite expiry until scheduling, remote copies and failure
reporting are qualified together.

* Operator evidence packet

Before hosted launch, retain the final source revision/image digests, actual
workspace inventory and platform ownership, test reports, measured capacity
profile, independent review and resolved findings, TLS/storage/processor inventory,
key custody/rotation record, backup/PITR/restore and financial tie-outs, alert tests,
retention/hold/erasure drill, and an incident-response exercise. Keep sensitive
artifacts private; publish only redacted synthetic demonstration evidence.

An incident report identifies affected workspace UUIDs from verified platform
registrations and audit events, preserves original evidence, records containment
and credential changes, and reconciles delayed jobs/provider events before
reactivation. A shared provider or platform-host incident can affect multiple
workspaces even when their business databases are physically separate. Do not
infer “no neighbor impact” from database separation alone.
