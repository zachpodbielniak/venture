#+title: Vendor payables
#+description: Supplier bills, payments and dated liability evidence

* The supplier's bill

The =payables= module requires =finance=, =ledger= and =crm=. The last
requirement supplies the supplier company; switching the module off hides
its records and reports together. Bills belong to one organization and
numbering is unique inside that organization.

=vendor_bill= holds the supplier, number, bill and due dates, currency,
status and memo. =vendor_bill_line= holds description, exact decimal
quantity, unit price, expense account or category, and tax amount.
Quantity is a decimal string with at most three fractional digits, parsed
into an integer rational. Price multiplication rounds once, half to even;
tax is then added in the same currency. No floating-point arithmetic is
used. A line may name a =tax_code=. When that code is recoverable, approval
debits the net expense and Recoverable tax 1300 for the frozen tax-amount,
and credits AP for the total. Otherwise tax remains part of the expense
cost.

* One financial writer

=VenturePayablesService= belongs to the database and owns approval,
allocations, vendor credits, refunds and voiding. Generic status changes
are refused with the service named. Approval freezes the amount and due
date in =vendor_bill_event= and posts each line's expense account against
Accounts payable 2000. Approved lines, currency and dates cannot be edited.
An unpaid approved bill can be voided; its approval is reversed with a new
dated posting. Historical evidence cannot be removed through deletion,
restoration or purge.

=bill_payment= records money paid to a supplier. =bill_payment_allocation=
applies exactly one payment or vendor credit to a bill. A payment can cover
several bills in one transaction through the service's allocation-array
API. A single-bill payment applies up to that bill's remaining amount;
the excess is a =vendor_credit=. Remaining credit and bill status are
derived from retained allocations and refunds. A =bill_refund= records
cash returned by the supplier and reopens an allocated bill when applicable.
Payments debit AP and credit Cash 1000. Vendor credit notes debit AP and
credit General expenses 6900. Refunds reverse the cash movement.

Every financial operation shares one transaction with its audit entries,
allocations and journals. A failure after writing the payment leaves no
payment or partial journal. Dated events check the fiscal-period guard
inside that transaction; updating a derived current balance does not
rewrite its earlier accounting date. Currency differences are refused.

Account IDs on lines select active expense accounts within the bill's
organization. Service properties select the cash, payable and default
expense accounts. Zero selects conventional codes 1000, 2000 and 6900;
missing default accounts are created within the transaction.

* Scope

Bank matching and purchase orders/receiving are separate modules. No
historical approvals are inferred from an imported bill's current status.

* Actions and historical reports

The =/payables= workbench selects approved bills and pays them through
=VenturePayablesService= using a named adapter (=manual=, =transfer=,
=card=, =check=, =ach=). Adapters never write settlement tables themselves.
=POST /api/v1/payables/pay= accepts =bills=, =date=, =adapter=, =method= and
=reference=. One payment per vendor is allocated in a single transaction.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl bill pay-bulk 1,2,3 adapter=transfer date=2026-02-01
#+end_src

The bill page has Approve and Pay actions. REST accepts
=POST /api/v1/vendor_bill/:id/approve=, =/pay= and =/void= with ordinary
record fields in a JSON body; =?stage=1= queues the same request for
confirmation. The bill ID and organization come from the URL's bill.
An omitted action date means now. Pay defaults to the outstanding amount
and accepts =amount=, =date=, =method=, =reference=, =external_id= and
=bank_account_id=.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl bill approve 1 date=2026-01-01
venturectl bill pay 1 'amount=40 USD' date=2026-01-15 method=transfer
venturectl report payables 2026-01 organization_id=1 currency=USD
venturectl report vendor_statement 2026-01 vendor_id=1 currency=USD
#+end_src

The assistant's generated =venture_create= tool stages a =vendor_bill_event=
with =bill_id=, =vendor_id=, =kind=approve=, =state=approved= and =date=.
Approval invokes the service and saves the immutable event with its frozen
amount. Creating =bill_payment= or =bill_payment_allocation= through that
same staged tool invokes payment or credit allocation. No financial status
is assigned through a generic bill update.

Aging reconstructs approval events less dated allocations plus refunds
before the exclusive cutoff. It uses the frozen due date, with Current,
1–30, 31–60, 61–90, Over 90 and No due date buckets. The financial aging
report participates in period snapshots. Vendor statements require
=vendor_id= and show opening balance, dated movements and closing balance.
Both select one organization and currency, retain historical evidence and
accept =as_of=. A later payment never removes a bill from an earlier aging.

* Cash-report expenses

An expense with =external_id=bill_line:<line UUID>= requests conversion of
a fully paid bill line. The service derives amount, description, category,
venture and cash date from the line and its cash allocations. The projection
is immutable and organization-unique. It bypasses automatic expense posting:
approval already recognized the expense and payment already moved cash.
An explicit document repost is refused at the journal save boundary too.

This conversion is deliberately optional and narrow. One immutable expense
has one amount and one date. It can represent cash allocations at one effective timestamp,
without credit-note settlement or refunds; it cannot correctly represent
partial payments on different dates. Those conversions are refused instead
of assigning all earlier cash to the final payment date. Use the dated AP
subledger for such bills. Expense deductibility remains the conservative
ordinary default; the projection does not invent a tax classification.

* Schema and verification

Migration =000110_payables_schema.sql= is paired for SQLite and PostgreSQL.
It verifies the metadata-created bill columns and the complete seven-table
set, or the absent set of a never-enabled module. It creates no accounting
history. Re-enabling the module uses ordinary additive schema reconciliation.

=test-payables= covers registration, direct status refusal, approval and
frozen dates, partial and consolidated payments, credits, refunds, voiding,
external-ID refusal, rollback after a payment write, January close followed
by February settlement, historical reports, real web/REST/CLI and staged
MCP actions, and immutable cash expense conversion without duplicate posting.

After conversion, a later cash refund or repayment appends a negative or
positive expense adjustment on its own date, with a =bill_cash:= source
identity. Exact allocation across the frozen bill lines preserves cents;
the original expense is never edited. A line not converted to a cash-report
expense does not acquire these optional adjustments.

Default posting accounts come from =accounting_control_map= (cash, payables, expense). When a mapping is absent they still reuse the organization's legacy code or its =organization-id:code= chart entry.

* Workbench approval

Batch payments use the same second-actor approval checks as individual payments. A refused proposal remains pending for a second actor; no vendor is paid until all proposals are allowed. Execution and approval consumption are one transaction, so a failed vendor payment rolls back the entire batch.
