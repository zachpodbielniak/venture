* A calendar belongs to one organization

The =periods= module requires =finance=. It owns =fiscal_year=,
=fiscal_period= and =report_snapshot=; their tables, forms, REST resources,
CLI commands and audit records come from field tables. Disabling =periods=
hides those records and the =snapshot_vs_live= report together.

Create a fiscal year with its organization, name, =start_at= and
=period_length= (=monthly= or =quarterly=). The service generates twelve
months or four quarters in the same transaction. =end_at= is the date twelve
months after the start; supplying a different end is refused. Boundaries are
measured from the original start, so a January 31 start does not drift after
February. Starts are inclusive and ends exclusive.

A company's calendars cannot overlap or leave gaps. Another organization
can use the same dates independently. Saved boundaries, organization and
year membership are immutable; calendars cannot be deleted or restored.
Generic fiscal-year creation uses =VenturePeriodService=, so every entry
point gets the same complete calendar.

* Closing and reopening

Set a fiscal period's =state= from =open= to =closed= using any ordinary
record editor. Earlier periods must already be closed or locked. Closing a
year requires its periods to be closed; locking a year requires its periods
to be locked. The service stamps =closed_by= from the actor or named
approver and =closed_at= from the clock. Those stamps cannot be supplied or
edited by a caller.

The close checklist refuses unbalanced ledger transaction batches and draft
invoices dated in the period. With the =close= module on, unmatched bank
lines and an unsigned close workspace also veto the close. See
[[file:close.org][accountant close workspace]]. Failures are collected and named together in
the error, so fixing one does not merely reveal the next. Ledger amounts are
summed with =VentureMoney=; currencies and organizations are not netted
against one another.

Reopening requires the named =periods.reopen= permission. Active
administrators and owners hold it; a machine actor needs a named human
approval with that permission. The normal audit diff records who reopened
the period and when. The last close stamp and every earlier snapshot remain.
A closed fiscal year must be reopened before one of its periods. A locked
period cannot reopen or be edited.

Sales, expenses, invoices, invoice lines and ledger entries are checked at
the repository boundary. A migrated invoice or bill stamped with
=opening_at= by a cutover is judged at that instant instead of its source
issue date, which may lie in a period this organization never kept. Both the old accounting date and the proposed one
must be postable, so changing a date or organization cannot move a closed
record out of the restriction. Delete and restore are checked too. Web
forms, REST, CLI and approved AI proposals reach this same boundary; a
proposal staged while a period was open is refused if approved after close.

An organization without a calendar keeps its existing posting behavior.
Once it has a calendar, an undated write or a date outside that calendar is
refused. With the module disabled the default guard permits posting.

* Reports as of a date

Financial reports accept =as_of= as an ISO timestamp or a calendar date.
A date means the end of that UTC day. The reporting =period= still selects
the business-date range; =as_of= selects historical visibility. A row whose
=deleted_at= is later than the cutoff is included, while a row deleted at or
before it is excluded. Omitting =as_of= reads currently visible records.

#+begin_src sh
venturectl report pnl 2024-01 as_of=2024-01-31 organization_id=1
venturectl report receivables 2024-01 as_of=2024-01-31 organization_id=1
venturectl report snapshot_vs_live 2024-01 organization_id=1
#+end_src

REST accepts the same options on
=/api/v1/reports/pnl?period=2024-01&as_of=2024-01-31&organization_id=1=.
The report page accepts those query parameters too, and the assistant's
report tool describes them. Exact timestamps can select an instant within a
day; malformed cutoffs are refused rather than treated as live reports.

Historical receivables reconstruct balances from immutable issue events, allocations and refunds strictly before the selected cutoff. Later payments and refunds do not change a closed period's receivables snapshot. Invoice lifecycle updates performed by settlement may change a historical invoice's current status, while the journal guard validates the new transaction's date. Generic writes, soft deletion, restore and hard purge still enforce the original period's protection.

* What a close preserves

Closing a period generates each enabled report marked with the
=VentureReport:financial= property and saves its exact result in a
=report_snapshot=. Built-ins include P&L, venture and category performance,
inventory, deductions, campaigns, pipeline, monthly series, receivables and the authoritative trial balance.
The snapshot keeps the organization, fiscal period, report name, effective
cutoff, closing actor, metrics, table and explanatory notes. Money retains
its exact serialized representation.

The cutoff is immediately before the exclusive period end, capped at the
closing instant for a period closed early. Report generation, period state,
audit and snapshots share one transaction. A report failure leaves the
period open and stores no partial snapshots. Closing needs a reporting
context; an isolated repository cannot silently omit the reports.

Snapshots cannot be created, edited, deleted or restored through ordinary
record writes. Reopening and closing again appends another set of evidence.
The =snapshot_vs_live= report reruns the preserved periods against current
records and lists differences, with the saved and current results beside
one another. With no differences it has no rows. An unavailable report is
reported in a note rather than silently declared unchanged.

The cutoff is not a complete versioned database: later edits to amounts,
classification or descriptive fields are not generally reversed. Preserve
closed figures by keeping the period closed; use snapshots and the drift
report when a period is reopened for correction. Historical invoice status
without a payment date or audit evidence cannot recover missing history.

* Extension points and the posting boundary

=VenturePeriodGuard= is a GInterface. The database owns a registry containing
the default calendar guard. Additional guards run in registration order and
all must allow the date. Posting services should call this inside their own
posting transaction, before writing any lines:

#+begin_src C
gboolean venture_period_guard_is_postable(
    VenturePeriodGuard *self,
    VentureDatabase *database,
    gint64 organization_id,
    GDateTime *date,
    GError **error);

if (!venture_period_guard_is_postable(
        VENTURE_PERIOD_GUARD(venture_database_get_period_guard(database)),
        database, organization_id, occurred_at, error))
    return FALSE;
#+end_src

A refusal names the period and its state in =GError=. Closed and locked
periods use =VENTURE_ERROR_CONFLICT=; a missing or uncovered date uses
=VENTURE_ERROR_VALIDATION=. The registry remains available with periods off.
This module does not post journals.

=VenturePeriodCheck= is the checklist GInterface. Obtain the checklist with
=venture_period_service_get_checklist()= and register another implementation,
or connect to its =collect-checks= signal. The signal is =RUN_LAST=; handlers
append checks before every collected check is run. A failing plugin check
vetoes close without replacing the built-in checks. Financial report plugins
set =financial= to true and consume the same =as_of= and =organization_id=
options to participate in snapshots.

* Financial identifiers

Nonempty sale and expense =external_id= values are unique within an
organization, including soft-deleted records. Empty external IDs remain
available for manual entries. Re-importing an identity is refused; it does
not silently create another financial row. Account codes and invoice numbers
are also unique per organization, so two organizations may each use account
=1000= and invoice =1=.

The metadata-driven migration replaces legacy global constraints on account
codes and invoice numbers. SQLite preserves rows, identities, references,
indexes, triggers and its autoincrement watermark while rebuilding the
constraint; PostgreSQL drops only the old global constraint. Startup can run
the migration repeatedly. Existing duplicate external IDs cause migration to
fail; it does not choose which financial record to discard.

* Tests

=test-periods= covers organization uniqueness, populated-schema migration,
calendar generation and refusals, close order, reopening and audit, the
checklist, historical reports, snapshots and the generated surfaces.
=test-period-surfaces= exercises real HTTP forms and REST calls, the real
CLI subprocess and the assistant's approval store, including proposals
staged before close, locked periods, invalid cutoffs and the exclusive
snapshot boundary. No journal posting, payments, bank matching or vendor
bills are part of this module.
