* Market data

A market data source -- an auction house API, a bookmaker's odds feed, a supplier's price list -- returns far more than the main database is for. One World of Warcraft region is some 186 realms times ten thousand items, every hour, out of a hundred thousand listings per realm. VENTURE keeps all of it, reduced to compact figures as it arrives, in a *series store*: one SQLite file per data source, outside the main database.

This page documents the whole path market data takes. It opens with how the pieces fit together, then covers the store itself -- its layout, its schema, the arithmetic behind every figure it holds, retention and the threading contract -- then the feeds that fill it (data sources, providers, the worker thread, the transport rules, scheduling, the records sink), then the marketdata module that joins the stores to the books (venue and instrument records, watchlists, promotion and the price oracle), its alerts, and the Trading pages that browse it all. For the =market= module's own records (price observations, listings, sale rate), see [[file:market.org][Market]]; for what is done with the prices once they are read -- the arbitrage scan, trades and their legs -- see [[file:arbitrage.org][Arbitrage]].

** How it fits together

Market data crosses four modules -- =feeds=, =marketdata=, =arbitrage= and the ledger, with =market='s price observations beside them -- and two kinds of storage, and each step has one owner. Plugins add the parts that know a particular outside service; the =feeds= module decides when to fetch and records what happened; a worker thread does the fetching and writes a *series store*, a SQLite file of its own per data source; the =marketdata= module reads the stores on the main thread and turns what matters into ordinary records; the =arbitrage= module prices opportunities from the same stores and records the trades; and the ledger books what the trades moved.

- *Plugins* own knowledge of one outside service. A native =.so= or a crispy =.c= file calls =venture_plugin_register()= with the context and adds to its registries directly -- a data source provider (=venture_context_get_data_source_providers()=), a fee model (=venture_context_get_fee_models()=), a strategy (=venture_context_get_arbitrage_strategies()=), an export format (=venture_context_get_export_formats()=), a record action. An exec plugin is a program and can only offer what its manifest's =provides:= names; for market data that is =kind: data_source_provider=. A name is never registered twice, so a plugin cannot replace a built-in. See [[file:plugins.org][Plugins]], and the three feed examples below.
- *Feeds* (=src/feeds/=, module =feeds=, requires only =core=, off until =feeds.enabled: true=) owns the =data_source= and =data_source_run= records, the provider registry's built-ins, the worker thread and its schedule, and the records sink. Everything a fetch needs is frozen on the main thread into a =VentureFeedSource= before the worker sees it, which is what lets the worker never touch the database. See [[*Feeds][Feeds]] below, and [[file:security.org::*Market data feeds: where the server may reach][Security]] for where it may reach.
- *The series worker* owns the network and the store's writer handle. Fetching, parsing and aggregating happen there, one source's units one after another; what comes back to the main thread is plain data: the run's figures, its notes and each hook's payload. See [[*The worker thread][The worker thread]].
- *The series store* (=src/series/=) owns everything a source ever returned, reduced to compact figures as it arrives: the newest figures per venue and instrument, hourly and daily history, region figures, quotes and entries. It is one SQLite file per data source, never the main database, because its rows are derived and arrive by the million. A build without SQLite has no store, and then no feeds. See the store chapters that follow, and [[file:backup.org::*Series stores][Backups: series stores]] for how installation backups copy them.
- *Marketdata* (=src/marketdata/=, module =marketdata=, requires =market= -- and through it =sales= -- suggests =feeds=, on by default) owns the records the books can point at: venues and instruments promoted out of a store, watchlists, alert rules and their hits. It owns the price oracle every valuing report can ask through a =series:= price source, and the Trading pages, reports and widgets that browse the stores. With feeds off its records still work and its pages say there is no data. See [[*Venues, instruments and the price oracle][Venues, instruments and the price oracle]], [[*Alerts][Alerts]] and [[*Browsing: the Trading pages][Browsing: the Trading pages]].
- *Arbitrage* (=src/arbitrage/=, module =arbitrage=, requires =marketdata= and =ledger=, suggests =production= and =goods=, on by default) owns the scan -- strategies run over the stores and netted by each venue's fee model -- the saved questions (=arbitrage_strategy=), exports, and the trades and legs that record an attempt. See [[file:arbitrage.org][Arbitrage]].
- *The ledger* owns the money. An executed leg is a ledger source: it posts journals through the posting service, through the venue's cash (a location's holding, else the venue's account, else the organization's cash), stock legs move inventory at FIFO cost, and a memo currency moves a holding instead of posting. Nothing here posts around it. See [[file:arbitrage.org::*Posting][Arbitrage: posting]] and [[file:ledger.org][the general journal]].

Two rules hold the whole path together. Nothing on the worker thread reads or writes the main database, so every record -- a run, a price observation, an alert hit -- is written on the main thread as the system, through every validator. And nothing guesses a conversion: the store, the oracle, the Trading pages and the alerts never convert at all -- a price in another currency than the one asked for is no answer, said in a note -- and where the arbitrage scan and the ledger do value one currency in another, it is only through a recorded =exchange_rate=, never an inverted or guessed one.

** Why it is not entity tables

Everywhere else in VENTURE a record type is a field table and a registration, and the database table, the REST routes, the forms and the audit diff all derive from it. The series store is the one documented exception: it hand-writes its SQL. The rows here are not records.

- *Derived.* Every row is computed from what a source returned, and can be computed again. Nobody edits one.
- *Append-heavy and high-volume.* Ten thousand instruments a venue an hour is two million row writes an hour for one region. An audit entry, a version bump, a save validator and a signal per row would cost more than the data.
- *Closer to the audit log than to a record.* Nothing here is shown in a generic form, audited, staged or federated.

So the store owns its schema, prepares each statement once per connection, and writes one transaction per snapshot. It never touches =VentureDatabase=: the two are different files with different rules. What /does/ belong in the main database -- a venue or an instrument somebody watches, trades or links to a product -- is promoted into ordinary records by the marketdata module, which reads the store; the store never writes back.

** Layout

A store is the file =store.db= in a directory the caller names. The feeds module derives that directory as =<state_dir>/series/<data-source-uuid>/= every time it needs it; the path is never stored in a record, so moving a state directory moves its stores with it and no record can point a store at an arbitrary file.

The writer creates the directory (mode 0700) and the file. The file runs in WAL mode, so readers never wait on the writer; with =synchronous = NORMAL=, because everything in it can be fetched or derived again and losing the last snapshot to a power cut is a better trade than syncing a hundred times an hour; and with incremental auto-vacuum, so a purge hands space back to the file system.

** Threading

- A handle is one SQLite connection and belongs to the thread using it. Connections are opened without SQLite's own mutexes: one thread per connection makes them pointless.
- There is one *writer*, =venture_series_store_open()=, owned by whatever drives ingestion -- the feeds worker thread. A second writer would only wait on the first's transactions.
- Any number of *readers*, =venture_series_store_open_reader()=, can run at the same time on other threads; the web pages read from the main thread. A reader sees whole snapshots: WAL gives it the last committed state, and every cached statement is reset after use so a reader never pins an old one.
- Nothing in the store touches =VentureDatabase=, the entity registry, the configuration or anything else that is not thread-safe. Limits arrive as arguments (=venture_series_store_set_max_bytes()=, the purge's day counts), read from the configuration on the main thread by the caller.
- The store logs with =g_debug()= and =g_message()= only. =g_warning()= is fatal in the test harness from any thread, and a store failure is a =GError= for the caller to record in its run.

** Opening and versions

The schema is a list of append-only steps embedded in the server; =PRAGMA user_version= records how many have run. Opening a writer runs the missing steps, each in its own transaction with its version bump, so a crash between steps leaves a store at a version that exists. A shipped step is never edited.

| The file                                   | Writer                                         | Reader                                           |
|--------------------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------|
| missing                                    | created at the current version                 | =VENTURE_ERROR_NOT_FOUND=; nothing is created     |
| at an older version                        | upgraded                                       | =VENTURE_ERROR_MIGRATION= (the writer upgrades)  |
| at a newer version than this build knows   | =VENTURE_ERROR_MIGRATION=, nothing written     | =VENTURE_ERROR_MIGRATION=                        |
| not an SQLite database                     | =VENTURE_ERROR_DATABASE= naming the file       | =VENTURE_ERROR_DATABASE= naming the file         |
| an SQLite database with tables, version 0  | =VENTURE_ERROR_DATABASE= ("not a series store") | =VENTURE_ERROR_MIGRATION=                        |

The last row matters: a store pointed at some other database by mistake is refused before anything -- not even the journal mode -- is written into it.

A reader is opened read-write without =CREATE= and then made =query_only=: a WAL database opened strictly read-only cannot create its shared-memory index, and the first reader after the writer closed would fail for that alone.

** Units and conventions

- *Times* are Unix seconds, UTC. A *day* is days since 1970-01-01 UTC, so a day starts at midnight UTC like every other period boundary in VENTURE.
- *Prices* are integer minor units, never a double, with the currency beside them. Currency codes are checked with =venture_currency_is_valid()= -- pure string work, safe on the worker -- and stored upper-cased.
- *Odds* are decimal odds times =VENTURE_SERIES_ODDS_SCALE= (1 000 000): 2.15 is 2150000. Odds are a ratio, not money, but an integer keeps two sources quoting the same odds equal.
- *Nothing* is =VENTURE_SERIES_NONE= (the most negative 64-bit integer) in the API and NULL in the file -- never zero, which is a real price.
- *Ratios* (percent of the region median, sale rate) are doubles; NAN means none.
- *Keys.* A venue or instrument is addressed by the source's key, unique within the store, at most 512 bytes of UTF-8. A store belongs to one data source, so the source's namespace is recorded on each row (for promotion to form =namespace:key=) but is not part of the key; a source with two kinds of thing whose keys collide prefixes them.

** Schema

Version 4 (=venture_series_store_schema_version()=); the steps are =series_schema_steps[]= in =src/series/venture-series-store.c=.

|------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
|    1 | the price data: =meta= (with =created_at=), =venues=, =instruments=, =snapshots=, =current=, =hourly=, =daily=, =region=, =venue_state= and their indexes |
|    2 | =quotes= (indexed on =instrument_id=), =quote_history= (indexed on =taken_at=) and =entries= (indexed on =COALESCE(published_at, fetched_at)= for the purge) |

*** =meta=

Source-level state: a cursor, a quota. =key= (primary), =value=. Step 1 writes =created_at=.

*** =venues=

| Column       | Meaning                                                                 |
|--------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =id=         | row id, referenced by every other table                                 |
| =key=        | the source's key, unique                                                |
| =namespace=  | the source's namespace for it                                           |
| =name=       | readable name                                                           |
| =kind=       | free-form: realm, marketplace, bookmaker                                |
| =group_key=  | the group it is priced against (a region); =''= for none                 |
| =currency=   | its currency, used when a snapshot names none                           |
| =attrs=      | a JSON object of anything else                                          |
| =first_seen=, =last_seen= | when the store first and last heard of it                  |

Indexed on =group_key=.

*** =instruments=

=id=, =key= (unique), =namespace=, =name=, =name_fold= (the name case-folded, for search), =kind=, =category= (a =/=-separated path), =parent_key= (an event for an outcome), =attrs=, =first_seen=, =last_seen=. Indexed on =category= and (partially) =parent_key=.

An upsert with a =NULL= member keeps what is stored, so a listing that names only a key never blanks a name a lookup filled in. A bare key only moves =last_seen=, and only forward.

*** =snapshots=

The log of applied snapshots, and what makes re-applying one a no-op. Primary key (=venue_id=, =taken_at=); also =fetched_at=, =currency=, =listings=, =instruments=, =complete=, =late=. Indexed on =taken_at= for the purge.

*** =current=

The latest figures for each venue and instrument -- what browsing sorts and filters. One row per (=venue_id=, =instrument_id=), unique.

| Column            | Meaning                                                                                  |
|-------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =currency=        | the currency of every price in the row                                                  |
| =taken_at=        | the snapshot the row reflects                                                           |
| =seen_at=         | the last snapshot that listed the instrument here                                       |
| =quantity=        | units offered; 0 out of stock; NULL when a source's figures did not say                  |
| =listings=        | sell listings                                                                           |
| =min_price=       | lowest price                                                                            |
| =market_value=    | market value (below)                                                                    |
| =median=, =p15=, =mean=, =stddev= | unit-price statistics (below)                                           |
| =bid_price=, =bid_quantity= | best buy order and units wanted                                               |
| =tiers=           | the 64 cheapest prices with their quantities, packed (below)                            |
| =region_median=   | the group's median of minimums, from the last region recompute                          |
| =deal_price=      | the group's deal price, from the last region recompute                                  |
| =pct_vs_region=   | =min_price= as a percent of =region_median= (80 is 20% cheaper); NULL out of stock       |
| =stock_changed_at= | the snapshot at which =quantity= last crossed zero, either way; NULL when it never has   |
| =sale_rate=       | units sold ÷ (sold + expired) over the last 14 days, from the last region recompute; NULL when nothing sold or expired |
| =sold_per_day=    | units sold ÷ days with history over the same 14 days, from the last region recompute     |

Indexes: (=instrument_id=, =min_price=) for "other venues"; (=venue_id=, =min_price=), (=venue_id=, =market_value=), (=venue_id=, =pct_vs_region=), (=venue_id=, =sale_rate=) for one venue's page; =pct_vs_region=, =deal_price= and =sale_rate= alone (partial, non-NULL) for deals and sorts across every venue.

=pct_vs_region= is refreshed against the stored =region_median= at every snapshot, so a sort by it is right between recomputes; the median itself only moves when the region is recomputed.

=sale_rate= and =sold_per_day= (schema step 4) are what the browse page sorts "how fast it sells" by. The server answers pages on its main thread, and a sort computed from two weeks of =daily= history per request is a sort over millions of rows, so the figures are kept on the row: the region recompute, which already runs every half hour on the worker, writes them with two set-based statements after the region figures -- the same arithmetic as =venture_series_store_reference()= for one venue, over the days in the row's own currency -- and clears the rows whose history has aged out of the window. They are not counted in the recompute's =current_updated=, which says how many rows' region figures moved. A store upgraded to step 4 has them NULL until its next recompute.

=stock_changed_at= (schema step 3) is written by the statement that makes the change: the snapshot upsert stamps it when the stored and the new quantity sit on different sides of zero, and leaves it alone when either side is unknown (a stats row that did not say how many); marking an absent instrument out of stock stamps it when the row had units. A new row leaves it NULL -- the first sighting of an instrument is not "back in stock", or the first sync of a source would say so about everything. Equal to =taken_at= exactly when the newest snapshot took the instrument out of or back into stock, which is what the stock alerts read.

*** =hourly=

One row per (=venue_id=, =instrument_id=, =day=), unique, with =currency= and =points=: a packed blob of up to 24 points. Each point is the *last snapshot taken in its hour*: minimum price, quantity, market value and listings. Indexed on =day= for the purge. An instrument absent from a snapshot gets no point; a day whose currency changes starts again, because two currencies' prices in one series would read as a price movement.

*** =daily=

One row per (=venue_id=, =instrument_id=, =day=, =currency=) -- the currency is in the key, so a mid-day currency change is two rows, never a mixed one.

| Column             | Meaning                                                                       |
|--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =snapshots=        | snapshots folded into the day (0 for a row that holds only sales)             |
| =min_price=        | the lowest price seen all day                                                 |
| =max_quantity=     | the most units on offer in any one snapshot                                   |
| =price_at_max=, =market_value=, =mean=, =listings= | that fullest snapshot's figures                 |
| =sold_estimate=    | units estimated sold (below), or reported sold by a source                    |
| =sold_value=       | what they sold for, summed                                                    |
| =expired_estimate= | units that vanished when they may have expired                                |
| =sale_avg=         | an average sale price a source reported outright                              |

The day keeps the snapshot with the most units on offer, as Undermine Exchange does: the fullest market of the day says most about its prices. A strictly larger quantity takes over; a tie keeps the first. Sums saturate at the 64-bit maximum rather than letting SQLite turn an overflowing integer sum into a floating-point one.

The reported average sale price is =sold_value / sold_estimate= rounded half to even when there were sales, else =sale_avg=.

*** =region=

Per (=group_key=, =instrument_id=, =currency=): =computed_at=, =venues_offering=, =total_quantity=, =median_min=, =p33=, =deal_price=, =market_avg=. Indexed on =instrument_id=. Rebuilt whole for the groups a recompute covers.

*** =venue_state=

Per venue: =last_taken_at=, =last_fetched_at=, =gaps= (the last 36 gaps between snapshots, packed), =interval_seconds= (learned from them), =listing_set= (the listings of the last complete snapshot, packed) with =listing_set_at= and =listing_set_currency= (the currency its prices are in), and =cursor= (where the venue's next fetch resumes).

*** =quotes= and =quote_history=

=quotes= holds the latest quote per (=venue_id=, =instrument_id=, =side=): =taken_at=, =value= (odds times the scale, or a price in minor units), =currency= (NULL for odds), =liquidity=. A quote older than the stored one does not replace it. =quote_history= keeps every quote, keyed by (venue, instrument, side, =taken_at=), so the same quote twice is written once. Sides: back, lay, bid, ask.

*** =entries=

Non-price items, matched on =key= (an RSS guid): =venue_id=, =instrument_id=, =published_at=, =fetched_at=, =title=, =url= (http or https only -- only a web address is a link), =summary=, =attrs=. =fetched_at= is the first arrival and is never updated, so =venture_series_store_list_new_entries(store, since, count)= -- on the =entries_fetched= index from schema step 3 -- is "what arrived since", whatever the entries' publication times.

** Packed blobs

Every blob starts with a format byte (1 today) and stores integers as unsigned LEB128: seven bits a byte, so a quantity of 20 is one byte and a price of 300 000 000 is five. A blob that does not decode is reported as a corrupt store, never guessed at.

- *Hourly points:* per present hour, ascending: the hour (0-23), the seconds into the hour of the snapshot it came from, then minimum, quantity and market value each as 0 for none or value + 1, then listings.
- *Tiers:* the count, then per tier the rise in price from the tier before (prices ascend) and the quantity.
- *Gaps:* the count, then each gap in seconds.
- *Listing set:* the count, then per listing, sorted by id: the rise in id from the one before, the instrument's row id, the unit price, the quantity, and the expiry hint plus one.

** Ingesting a snapshot

A snapshot is assembled in memory and applied in one transaction:

1. =venture_series_store_begin_snapshot(store, venue, currency, taken_at, fetched_at, complete)=. With no currency, the venue's. =complete= says the snapshot lists everything at the venue, so an instrument missing from it is out of stock there.
2. =venture_series_snapshot_add_listing()= per listing -- instrument key, optional listing id, unit price, quantity, side, and the least time the listing has left (=expires_in_min=, in *seconds* despite its name -- "min" is minimum -- and -1 when unknown) -- or =venture_series_snapshot_add_stats()= per instrument a source has already summarised. One instrument cannot have both in one snapshot. A listing whose quantity would take its instrument's total past 64 bits is refused alone, leaving the snapshot as it was.
3. =venture_series_store_commit_snapshot()=.

The commit:

- *Finds a duplicate first.* The same venue and =taken_at= applied twice is reported as =duplicate= and writes nothing -- a retried fetch must not count a snapshot twice or double its sales.
- *Resolves instruments*, creating unknown ones -- unless the store is past its cap (below).
- *Computes each instrument's figures* (below) and upserts =current=, folds the hour into =hourly= and the day into =daily=.
- *Marks what a complete snapshot left out* as out of stock in =current=: quantity 0, prices NULL. Rows already at nothing are left alone, so an item that sold out a week ago is not rewritten every hour since.
- *Estimates sales* between this complete snapshot and the venue's last complete one (below), and stores this one's listing set.
- *Learns the interval* from the new gap.

A snapshot older than the venue's newest is *late*: its hourly and daily history is written, and nothing a newer snapshot already decided -- =current=, the listing set, the gaps -- is touched. A backfill must not roll a browse page back an hour.

=venture_series_store_begin()= / =_commit()= / =_rollback()= batch many writes (ten thousand instrument upserts are ten thousand transactions otherwise). Calls nest; inner levels are savepoints, so a snapshot that fails inside a batch takes back only its own writes.

** The figures

All of these are pure functions in =src/series/venture-series-math.c=, with no database, and pinned by =tests/test-series-math.c=. "Rounded" always means half to even, applied once, to an exact integer quotient.

*** Per snapshot, per instrument

The listings are sorted by price and merged into tiers. With Q units in total:

- *Minimum, maximum*: the first and last tier's price.
- *Mean*: Σ pᵢqᵢ / Q, rounded. The sum is formed in 128-bit arithmetic: a commodity of forty million units at three hundred million copper is past 64 bits, and the result still fits.
- *Standard deviation*: the quantity-weighted population deviation around the exact mean, computed in long double (it is a statistic, not money) and rounded once to minor units.
- *Median*: the price of the middle unit; with an even Q, the mean of the two middle units, rounded.
- *p15*: the price of the unit at nearest rank ⌈0.15 Q⌉.
- *Market value*, the way TradeSkillMaster's AuctionDB computes one snapshot's:
  1. Take the first max(1, ⌈0.15 Q⌉) units unconditionally.
  2. Keep taking, up to ⌊0.30 Q⌋ units, until a unit's price is at least 20% above the unit before it (5(p − p_prev) ≥ p_prev); that unit and everything after are left out. A jump inside the first 15% cuts nothing.
  3. Over the units taken, compute the mean m and population standard deviation s, and drop every unit with |p − m| > 1.5 s.
  4. The market value is the exact mean of the units left, rounded.
- *Tiers kept*: the 64 cheapest (=VENTURE_SERIES_MAX_TIERS=). A buyer walking deeper than that is past where anyone prices from, and the bulk calculator says it ran out rather than guess.

Precomputed figures from a source are stored as given; what it did not give is NULL.

*** Bulk cost

Walk the tiers from the cheapest, buying min(tier, remaining) at each price until the units are bought or the tiers run out. The answer is the units filled, the cost, the worst price paid, the average (cost / filled, rounded) and whether it was complete. A cost past 64 bits is refused.

*** Sale estimate

The listing set of each complete snapshot is kept: every listing with an id, sorted by id. Between two complete snapshots of a venue, elapsed seconds apart, a merge over the two sorted lists finds:

- *Vanished, and could not have expired* -- its =expires_in_min= exceeds the elapsed time, or the source gives no expiry at all (-1): *sold*, all of its quantity at its price. =expires_in_min= is the *least* time the listing had left, in *seconds*: "min" is minimum, not minutes. A source that gives a bucket gives its lower bound (a World of Warcraft LONG auction, two to twelve hours, is 7200), because the question is whether the listing could have run out -- the upper bound would count expiries as sales.
- *Vanished, and might have expired*: *expired*.
- *Still there with less quantity*: sold the difference.

The sales land on the later snapshot's day in =daily=. A cancelled listing looks exactly like a sale; that is the estimate's known bias, and why it is called one. Sales are estimated only when both snapshots are complete (an incomplete one's missing listings say nothing), and only when the later one carries ids at all -- otherwise a source that stopped sending ids would read as every listing at the venue selling at once. And only within one currency: a sale is valued at the earlier set's price, so the set records the currency it was priced in (=listing_set_currency=, schema step 5) and a complete snapshot in another currency starts a new set instead of diffing -- nothing is booked across the switch, and the next diff is within the new currency. Step 5 fills the column for an existing set from the snapshot it was taken from; a set whose snapshot was already purged keeps NULL, read as unknown, and restarts once. A source whose ids are text uses =venture_series_listing_id_from_string()=, a 64-bit FNV-1a hash that is never zero.

*** Interval learning

The venue's last 36 gaps between snapshots, oldest first, gaps of zero or less ignored. The interval is the median, over every window of three consecutive gaps, of the window's maximum -- so one early double update does not halve it and one outage does not double it -- capped at two hours (=VENTURE_SERIES_INTERVAL_CAP=). With fewer than three gaps it is their maximum; with none, an hour. The feeds module schedules the venue's next check from it.

*** Region

=venture_series_store_recompute_region(store, group, now, deal_min_value, deal_min_currency)= streams =current= sorted by group, instrument and currency, and for each combination takes the venues *offering* it -- a minimum price, and a quantity that is not known to be zero:

- =venues_offering=: how many.
- =total_quantity=: their quantities summed (saturating).
- =median_min=: the median of their minimums.
- =p33=: the minimum at index ⌊n / 3⌋ of the sorted list, counted from zero.
- =deal_price=: =median_min=, or =p33= when at least 15 venues offer it -- Undermine Exchange's rule: with that many, a third of the market being cheaper is a fact about the market rather than one venue. An instrument whose median is below =deal_min_value= gets no deal price (a 40% discount on something worth a copper is not a deal); the bound is in one currency and says nothing about rows in another.
- =market_avg=: the mean of their market values, rounded.

Then, in two set-based statements, every =current= row gets its group's median, deal price and percent, and rows whose group no longer offers the instrument lose them. Only rows whose figures changed are written. One transaction; readers see the old figures or the new, never half.

The pass is linear in =current=: a million venue-instrument rows take about three seconds in a debug build.

*** Reference figures

=venture_series_store_reference(store, venue or group, instrument, now)= reads the last 60 days of =daily=, keeping only the newest day's currency. For a group, each day's market value is the mean of its venues' and sales are summed across them. With d the days before today:

- *market_14d*: Σ w_d v_d / Σ w_d over the days 0-13 that have a value, rounded, where the weights are round(1 000 000 × 0.5^(d / 2.1)) -- a half-life of 2.1 days -- fixed as the table 1000000, 718873, 516779, 371499, 267060, 191983, 138011, 99213, 71321, 51271, 36857, 26496, 19047, 13692. A missing day is left out of both sums, never read as zero.
- *historical_60d*: the unweighted mean of the daily market values over 60 days, rounded.
- *sale_avg*: Σ sold value / Σ sold units over 14 days, rounded.
- *sale_rate*: Σ sold / (Σ sold + Σ expired) over 14 days.
- *sold_per_day*: Σ sold over 14 days divided by the days in that window that have data.

*** Heat map

=venture_series_store_heat()= buckets the hourly minimum prices from a time on by the weekday (Monday first) and hour they fall on in a zone, given as seconds east of UTC, and averages each cell, rounded. Only points in the newest point's currency count.

** Browsing

=venture_series_store_list_current(store, filter)= returns plain rows (=VentureSeriesRow=) with the venue's and instrument's names. The filter narrows by a set of venues (at most 512), a group, one instrument, a search (case-folded, matching the name or the key), a category and the ones beneath it (=herbs= matches =herbs= and =herbs/rare=, never =herbsx=), a minimum value (the market value, else the minimum, in a named currency -- a bound with no currency is refused), in-stock only, deals only (in stock at or under the row's =deal_price=), and a percent of the region median at most (=max_pct_vs_region=, NAN for none; a negative or infinite bound is refused). Every value reaches SQL as a parameter, and a search for =%= finds a literal percent sign. =venture_series_filter_init()= is the only correct way to start a filter: two of its "no bound" values are not zero.

Sorts come from an allowlist -- =min_price=, =market_value=, =quantity=, =listings=, =pct_vs_region=, =deal_price=, =region_median=, =name=, =updated=, =venue=, =sale_rate=, =sold_per_day= -- so a sort named in a query string never reaches SQL as text. Unknown values sort last whichever way the sort runs, ties break on venue and instrument so paging is stable, and a page is at most 1000 rows (50 by default). =venture_series_store_count_current()= counts the same filter.

Also: =_get_current()=, =_other_venues()= (every venue's row for one instrument, cheapest first), =_hourly()=, =_daily()=, =_get_tiers()=, =_bulk_cost()=, =_get_region()=, =_venue_index()= (per venue: instruments compared with the region, how many are cheaper, equal and dearer, the mean ratio, listings, last snapshot and learned interval), =_get_venue_state()=, =_list_venues()=, =_get_instrument()=, =_list_quotes()= (by instrument or by parent), =_list_entries()=, and the two bounded walks the arbitrage scan takes over odds -- =_list_quoted_parents()= (the events whose outcomes carry quotes) and =_list_quoted_instruments()= (the instruments quoted on one side, e.g. lay), each the most recently quoted first, with a =LIMIT= over the current quotes table (one row per venue, instrument and side, never the history).

** Retention and the cap

| Setting               | Default | Meaning                                                                                   |
|-----------------------+---------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =series.hourly_days=  | 14      | days of hourly history, quote history and snapshot log kept, today included; 0 forever      |
| =series.daily_days=   | 0       | days of daily history and entries kept; 0 forever -- it cannot be fetched again               |
| =series.max_store_mb= | 0       | size past which a store refuses new instruments; 0 for no cap                               |
| =series.include_in_backup= | true | copy every store beside each installation backup (see "Backups" below)                  |

The store reads none of these: the feeds service reads them on the main thread, freezes the first three into each source (=venture_series_store_set_max_bytes()= takes the cap in bytes) and asks the last itself when a backup runs; the configuration refuses a negative day count or cap at startup. =venture_series_store_purge(store, now, hourly_days, daily_days)= deletes everything before day /today − N + 1/ -- keeping N days counting today -- for good, then runs an incremental vacuum.

The cap counts pages that hold data. Past it, snapshots of known instruments carry on -- the history being paid for keeps arriving -- but an instrument the store has never seen is refused and counted in the commit's =instruments_refused= and =listings_refused=, which the feeds run records. Hourly history is what grows: as measured, one snapshot of a million venue-instrument rows (100 venues × 10 000 instruments, 50 000 listings each) makes a 350 MB store, of which =current= and its indexes are about 190 MB, a day's =daily= and =hourly= rows with their indexes about 120 MB and the hundred listing sets about 40 MB; each further hour adds roughly ten to fifteen bytes per row to =hourly=. At a full World of Warcraft region that is gigabytes a fortnight, which is what =hourly_days= is for.

** Backups

A store is history its source will never serve again, so installation backups copy it: with =series.include_in_backup= on (the default) and the =feeds= module on, every installation copy is followed by one copy of each store on disk, recorded as a =backup_run= of scope =series= naming the installation run (=parent_run_id=) and the data source (=store_uuid=). [[file:backup.org][Backups]] has the whole of it -- retention per store, failures, verification and restoring by hand; what matters here is how a copy is taken.

=venture_series_store_backup(directory, destination, pages_per_step, cancellable, &sha256, &size, error)= copies a store with SQLite's online backup API on a connection of its own -- never the writer's, which may hold a batch open -- inside one read transaction, so the copy is the store as of one commit while the writer carries on in WAL; and stepping inside that pinned read is what lets a copy finish at all under a busy writer, because without it SQLite restarts the copy at every commit. It writes =destination.partial=, switches it to a rollback journal so it is one self-contained file, renames it into place, and hashes it, all on the calling thread; nothing half-written survives a failure or a cancel. It reads no version: a copy is bytes, and a store an older build wrote is still worth keeping. =venture_series_store_check_file(path, &check, error)= opens a copy immutable -- not one byte written, so its recorded digest still matches -- and reports its schema version, =integrity_check= and row counts; verification uses it.

The copy is queued through the feeds worker (=venture_series_worker_backup()=, answered on the main context as =VentureSeriesWorker::backup-finished=) and taken on a short-lived thread of its own (a GTask), because a store can be gigabytes, the main thread serves requests and the worker's loop serves every fetch. That thread touches nothing but its own two SQLite connections -- never =VentureDatabase=, never the writer -- so fetches, syncs and commands go on while it runs; it used to run on the worker's loop, and a large or busy store held every sync behind it (=/backup-series/sync-during-copy= holds one store locked and records a sync of another meanwhile). A stop cancels it between steps of 4096 pages, and the worker's join waits for it within its ten-second bound.

** Builds without SQLite

A =SQLITE=0= build has no store: =venture-series-store.c= is left out by the Makefile and its header is not included by =<venture.h>=, so nothing can call it. The math (=venture-series-math.c=) has no database and is always built. =tests/test-series-store.c= becomes an empty plan.

* Feeds

The =feeds= module (=src/feeds/=) fetches market data on a schedule and writes it into each source's store. It is off unless the operator sets =feeds.enabled: true=, because a source makes the server call outside hosts. It requires only =core=, and it exists only in a build with SQLite: everything but its two record types is compiled under =VENTURE_HAVE_SQLITE=.

** Configuration

The defaults are in =data/default-config.yaml= and =src/config/venture-config.c=, and an out-of-range value is refused at startup rather than making every fetch fail in a way that looks like the far end's fault.

| =feeds.allowed_origins=     | empty   |              | exact origins a source may fetch from, comma-separated (=https://api.example.com,https://eu.api.example.com=); empty denies every HTTP source |
| =feeds.file_roots=          | empty   |              | absolute directories a file source may read under, comma-separated; empty denies every file source; a relative entry is ignored |

A =data_source= record says where the data comes from. Fields are given in their wire spelling, as =venturectl= and the JSON API take them; the GObject property is the same name with dashes.

*** Settings every provider shares

Whatever else a provider reads, these keys mean the same thing in every source's =settings=, because the feeds service reads them itself when it freezes the source:

| Key                 | Meaning                                                                                                                                       |
|---------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =units=             | the units one pass fetches, one fetch each (=list_units='s default: this list, else one unit called =default=); ={unit}= in a template is the unit's name |
| =instruments=       | with =track: known=, the instrument keys to keep                                                                                              |
| =requests_per_hour= | the source's own request budget, 0 to 1000000; absent is the provider's schema default (0 for the built-ins, 36000 for Blizzard), 0 is no budget |
| =record_types=      | the main-database types the source may write through the records sink: =price_observation= and =exchange_rate=, nothing else                  |
| =origins=           | a list that narrows =feeds.allowed_origins= for this source; it can never widen it                                                            |

Writes need an administrator, through the same =venture-access-admin-write= type data the currency uses: which host the server calls is configuration. The save validator (=feeds_validate_source()=) parses the settings, checks them against the provider's schema -- a setting the schema marks =x-sensitive= is refused outright, it belongs in the sealed store -- checks declared types and required settings, the schedule, the currency, the namespaces, =track: known= with something known (with marketdata off; with it on the source's instrument records count, and an empty list is accepted), =record_types= against what a feed may write, and that the provider can list its units from them.

A source's store is always =<state_dir>/series/<uuid>/=, derived by =venture_feeds_store_dir()= every time it is needed. No record holds a path.

** Credentials

A provider's sensitive settings are sealed in the integration store under the provider key =feed-<uuid>=, per organization, through =/feeds/ID/credentials= (organization owners and administrators; write-only, nothing saved is shown back). They are resolved on the main thread when the source is frozen and travel to the worker inside the frozen source, never in a record, an argv or an environment. A template names one as ={secret:NAME}=; a template naming one the source has not been given is refused rather than sent empty. The page seals through =venture_feeds_set_credentials()=, which binds the credentials to the origins the source's =x-endpoint= and =x-endpoint-override= settings name at that moment; a run whose settings now name another origin withholds them and fails, and an endpoint override is honoured only with =feeds.allow_endpoint_overrides= on ([[file:security.org::*Where a credential is sent][Where a credential is sent]]). Which names exist is the provider's schema: =http_json= and =csv= have two slots, =token= (for a header, =Authorization: Bearer {secret:token}=) and =api_key= (for an address); an exec provider has one, =token=, handed to its program in the request's =secrets=; the Blizzard plugin's is =client_secret= and the-odds-api's =api_key=.

Every string the worker records -- an error, a note -- and every name, attribute, title and summary a provider hands over is passed through the source's redaction, which replaces each credential value of six bytes or more with =[redacted]=, before it reaches a run, the store or a log. Errors are built to name only an origin, never a full address, because a query string may carry a key.

** Providers

=VentureDataSourceProvider= is a GInterface: =get_name=, =get_label=, =dup_settings_schema= (a JSON schema, =x-sensitive= on credentials; each scalar =default= it declares fills a setting the source leaves out when the source is frozen, so the program, the budget and the provider all see it -- an explicit =null= is kept, and a sensitive setting is never defaulted), an optional =freeze= (main thread), an optional =list_units= (default: the settings' =units= list, else one unit called =default=), and =fetch_async= of one unit into a =VentureFeedBatch= (with an optional =fetch_finish=). Providers register on the context's registry (=venture_context_get_data_source_providers()=, with =venture_data_source_provider_registry_add()=, =_lookup()= and =_list()=) whether or not the module is on, so a plugin loaded while feeds are off is still there when they come on; a held name is never replaced -- a second registration is refused with =VENTURE_ERROR_ALREADY_EXISTS=, so a plugin cannot quietly override a built-in.

The threading contract is the point of the interface. Names, schemas and units are pure. =freeze= runs on the main thread and may read anything there -- an exec plugin's program and stored settings -- returning immutable data handed to another thread. =fetch_async= runs on the worker (or on a private context, for a test fetch) and touches nothing but its =VentureFeedRequest=. A provider object is shared by every source and both threads, so it holds no per-fetch state.

=venture_func_data_source_provider_new(name, label, schema_json, fetch, user_data, destroy)= builds one from a blocking function, for a crispy script or a small native plugin: the function runs on a GTask thread and may call the blocking =venture_feed_request_http_get()= or =venture_feed_request_http_send()=. A provider that needs a freeze step or units of its own writes the GObject; =plugins/blizzard-auctions/= is the worked example of that, =plugins/scripts/odds-api.c= of the function form (see [[file:plugins.org::*The reference plugins][Plugins: the reference plugins]]).

The batch is plain data: venues, instruments, snapshots of listings or figures, quotes, entries, record upserts, a cursor, not-modified, notes, a refused count and an optional error that makes it partial. Prices go in as minor units (=venture_feed_decimal_to_minor()=, exact: more places than the currency has is refused, never rounded); odds as decimal odds times =VENTURE_SERIES_ODDS_SCALE= (=venture_feed_decimal_to_odds()= reads decimal, American and fractional, rounding half to even at the sixth place). =venture_feed_batch_add_jsonl()= reads the JSON-lines protocol of [[file:plugins.org][Plugins]] into a batch; =file_jsonl= and exec plugins share it.

*** The ones that ship

- =http_json=: =url= (={unit}= and ={secret:NAME}= filled in, percent-escaped), =headers= (a mapping of templates; a value with a line break is refused), =items= (a dotted path to the list), =record_as= (=listing=, =stat= or =quote=), =fields= (target to dotted path: =instrument= required; =venue= defaults to the unit; =name=, =category=, =kind=, =parent=, =price=, =quantity=, =id=, =side=, =expires_in_min= (seconds, the least time left), =currency=; for =stat= the figures =min=, =market=, =mean=, =median=, =sale_avg=, =listings= and =sold=; =odds= and =liquidity= for =quote=), =price_units= (=major= decimals or =minor= integers), =odds_format=, =complete= (true: each fetch lists everything at its venue), =cost=. A JSON number with a fraction is spelt back in its shortest exact decimal form and parsed as a decimal -- no arithmetic happens on the double. The snapshot is dated by =Last-Modified= when the far end sends one.
- =csv=: the same mapping over an RFC 4180 CSV (quoted fields, doubled quotes, =delimiter=, =header=), from =url= or from =file=. A row with no header uses column numbers as names.
- =file_jsonl=: =file=, a JSON-lines file in the plugin protocol. A line that breaks the protocol fails the unit, as it fails an exec run; an =error= message makes the batch partial.
- exec plugins: a manifest entry =kind: data_source_provider= with =name=, =label=, =command= (default =fetch=), =description=. The program gets =params.unit=, =params.cursor=, =params.if_modified_since= and =params.source= (the settings), and its credentials as =secrets=; =plugins.allow_exec= is frozen with the source and checked at every run.

** Files

A file provider resolves its path with =venture_feed_request_resolve_file()=: realpath()'d and compared against each of =feeds.file_roots= plus a separator, so =../x=, a sibling named =<root>-evil= and a symlink out of the root are all refused. No roots configured means no file is readable. A file is refused before it is read if it is larger than =feeds.max_response_mb=.

** Transport

Every HTTP fetch goes through the request's helper (=venture_feed_request_http_get_async()=, or the blocking form on a GTask thread), so a plugin's provider gets all of it by calling one function:

- *An allowlisted origin.* The origin (scheme, host, port) must be on =feeds.allowed_origins=, deny by default, narrowed -- never widened -- by the source's own =origins= setting. Plain http only for a loopback host (=127.0.0.1=, =::1=, =localhost=). Both sides are compared as =scheme://host[:port]=, lower case, with the scheme's default port dropped; an entry carrying a path, a query or user info is not an origin and is ignored, and an address with credentials in it is refused. Frozen with the source, so a configuration change refreezes every source.
- *No redirects.* The message carries =SOUP_MESSAGE_NO_REDIRECT=; a 3xx is a failure naming the status, and the final address is compared with the one asked. A redirect would otherwise carry the source's =Authorization= header to a host the answer chose.
- *A byte cap*: =Content-Length= first, then what actually arrives, chunk by chunk.
- *One deadline* for the whole exchange (=feeds.request_timeout=), through a cancellable of its own, so a timeout is told apart from the worker stopping.
- *If-Modified-Since* from the unit's last =Last-Modified= (kept in the store's meta as =ims:<unit>=); a 304 is a not-modified unit. A manual sync sends none: whoever asked wants the data.
- *Retry-After* on a 429 (seconds or an HTTP date, at most a day) is kept and the unit waits at least that long.
- *A request budget*: the settings' =requests_per_hour=; each request costs what the provider declares (=cost=, default 1). The window is an hour from the first request spent in it, not a clock hour. A unit that would overspend is deferred to the window's end, not failed, and its back-off is left where it was -- a spent budget is not the far end's fault. The budget is kept in the store's meta (=quota_window=, =quota_spent=), so a restart is not a fresh hour. The cost is spent *before* the request goes out and never refunded, which is right for a far end that charges a 304 too (Blizzard's commodity market costs 25 either way).

The general form is =venture_feed_request_http_send(request, method, url, headers, body, content_type, cost, flags, error)= (and =_send_async= / =_send_finish=), under the same rules, for a provider that has to POST -- an OAuth client-credentials token -- or make requests that are not the unit's own answer. Only GET and POST exist, and a body only on a POST. The flags:

| Flag                              | Means                                                                                                                                                                                                                         |
|-----------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =VENTURE_FEED_HTTP_DEFAULT=       | The unit's own request: the unit's If-Modified-Since goes out, and the answer's Last-Modified and status become the unit's.                                                                                                    |
| =VENTURE_FEED_HTTP_UNCONDITIONAL= | A side request -- a token, an index, an item's name. No If-Modified-Since is added (the provider may send its own header), and its Last-Modified and status are left out of the unit's: an item endpoint's date must not become the question the next fetch asks of the auctions. |
| =VENTURE_FEED_HTTP_ANY_STATUS=    | Hand back any final answer that is neither a redirect nor a 429 as a response, body included, so the provider can say what a 401 means ("the client credentials were refused") instead of "HTTP 401". |

Failures come back in the =VENTURE_FEEDS_ERROR= domain, so the worker can tell how to reschedule: =VENTURE_FEEDS_ERROR_ORIGIN= (not on the allowlist), =_QUOTA= (the budget is spent: the unit is deferred, not failed), =_RATE_LIMITED= (a 429; its =Retry-After= is kept), =_HTTP= (any other status the provider did not ask to see), =_REDIRECT=, =_TOO_LARGE= and =_FILE= (outside =feeds.file_roots=, or unreadable); a deadline is =VENTURE_ERROR_TIMEOUT= and a connection that failed =VENTURE_ERROR_NETWORK=.

A response carries every header, names folded to lower case, read with =venture_feed_http_response_get_header()= -- how the-odds-api's quota headers are read. =venture_feed_request_redact(request, text)= applies the source's redaction to a message a provider builds, so a message is safe wherever else it goes; the worker redacts again regardless.

A provider that reads the far end's own count of its quota (x-requests-used and x-requests-remaining) hands it over with =venture_feed_batch_set_remote_quota(batch, used, remaining)=. The run then records that count as its quota used and limit in place of the source's own budget -- it is the one the far end enforces -- and notes it per unit.

A main-thread action that must reach the far end -- the Blizzard plugin's recipe import -- asks the service for a request with =venture_feeds_service_open_request(service, data_source_id, unit, error)=: the source frozen exactly as for a run (credentials, allowlist, deadline, cap, the provider's own frozen data), with no session, no cursor, no If-Modified-Since and no budget, used only with the blocking =venture_feed_request_http_send()=. Each call blocks the main loop for at most the deadline, on a private main context, so nothing else of the main loop runs nested inside it; bound how many one action makes.

** The worker thread

=VentureSeriesWorker= is one thread with its own =GMainContext=, its own =SoupSession= and the writer handle of every source's store. It starts lazily -- when a source is enabled and runs on its own schedule, or when somebody asks for a sync -- and is joined when the context is disposed or the module switched off. Stopping cancels every fetch and waits at most ten seconds for them to come back; a provider that ignores its cancellable past that is left behind -- its pass, its source and the session deliberately never freed, and the worker's context never iterated again so its completion is never dispatched -- rather than holding the join, and the main thread with it, forever. A message says so. The worker's own references to a request's settings and secrets are dropped before the fetch starts, because a provider may take and drop references on the same JSON objects from its GTask thread and JSON-GLib's counts are not atomic.

- *What it never touches*: =VentureDatabase=, the entity registry, the configuration, a plugin's settings. Everything it needs is frozen first, on the main thread, into a =VentureFeedSource=: the record, its credentials, the provider's frozen data, every limit, and every hook's frozen copy. The JSON in it is kept as text and parsed by each thread for itself, because JSON-GLib's reference counts are not atomic.
- *Commands* reach it through =g_main_context_invoke_full()= on its context; *runs* come back through the *default* main context and are emitted as =VentureSeriesWorker::run-finished= there -- the context the service's own retries (a run waiting for a transaction, a store copy's answer, a refresh) are on too, never whatever context the caller had pushed when the worker started, which nobody might iterate again (=/feeds/runs-reach-the-default-context=). A store copy for an installation backup is a command too, taken on a thread of its own and answered as =VentureSeriesWorker::backup-finished= (see Backups above). The only shared state is the published status (as JSON text), a live count, the deliveries not yet emitted and the runs kept at stop, under a mutex.
- *A source's units are fetched one after another*, never at once: one quota, one writer, one far end. Different sources interleave on the loop. Parsing and exec programs run on GTask threads so a large answer never stops another source's timers.
- *It logs with =g_message()=*, never =g_warning()=, which the test harness makes fatal from any thread; a failure is recorded in the run.

*** Scheduling

| Schedule | When a unit is next checked                                                                                                               |
|----------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =auto=   | After new data: 45 seconds before the store expects its venue to update (its newest snapshot plus its learned interval), or in a minute if that has passed, or in an hour if nothing has been learned. After nothing new (a 304, a snapshot already held, a failure): 1, 5, 15, then 30 minutes. Never sooner than a Retry-After. |
| =hourly= | An hour after the last pass began.                                                                                                        |
| cron     | Looked at each minute with =venture_report_pack_schedule_due()=, the report packs' catch-up-once rule.                                   |
| =manual= | Never on its own.                                                                                                                         |

The rule is =venture_feeds_schedule_next_auto()=, a pure function the tests drive directly. The worker sleeps until the earliest due unit, at most a minute.

*** After each pass

Region figures are recomputed when the pass wrote something and half an hour has passed (or the sync was asked for), with the source's =min_value=; retention is purged once a day with =series.hourly_days= and =series.daily_days=; the quota is written to meta.

** Runs

A =data_source_run= is written once, when a run ends, by the service on the main thread, as the system actor, from the plain figures the worker handed back:

| Field                        | Meaning                                                                                                   |
|------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =data_source_id=             | which source ran                                                                                          |
| =status=                     | =ok= (the zero value), =partial=, =failed= or =deferred=                                                   |
| =trigger=                    | =schedule= (the zero value), =manual= or =automation=                                                     |
| =started_at=, =finished_at=  | when its first fetch began and its last ended                                                             |
| =units=, =not_modified=      | fetches made, and how many answered that nothing had changed                                              |
| =rows=, =new_instruments=    | rows written to the store, and instruments it had not seen before                                         |
| =refused=                    | listings and instruments refused: malformed, or past the store's cap                                      |
| =records=                    | main-database records the records sink created or updated                                                 |
| =requests=, =bytes=          | HTTP requests made and bytes of answers read                                                              |
| =quota_used=, =quota_limit=  | the budget spent this hour and the hourly limit (0 for none) -- or the far end's own count, when a provider reported one |
| =http_status=                | the last status other than 200, else 200; 0 when nothing went over HTTP                                   |
| =error=, =notes=             | why it failed, and what else it noticed (refusals, skipped records, deferrals, alert overflow), credentials redacted |

The generic write routes refuse it (=venture_web_type_accepts_writes()=). Webhooks hear about it like any other record: a run is publishable, and is not on the quiet list.

A manual sync, an automation's and the API's are each a run of their own. Scheduled passes are gathered into one run per =feeds.run_window_minutes= (default 15; 0 records every pass), so a source polling a hundred venues a minute apart writes a few rows an hour rather than a hundred; a failing pass closes its run at once so the failure is seen. A window still open when its source comes off the worker -- it can no longer be frozen, or was deleted -- is closed and handed back then rather than dropped (a deleted source's run still cannot be written: a run may not name a deleted source). When the server stops on SIGINT or SIGTERM it calls =venture_feeds_stop()= while the loop still runs, which joins the worker and writes every window still open and every run handed back but not yet written; =venture_feeds_service_stop()= is the same for one service. At the context's dispose and when the module is switched off a run can no longer be written -- the context is going, or the feeds types are masked -- so those are dropped with a message saying how many; the store has their data. The status follows from the units: all deferred is =deferred=, none failed is =ok=, none succeeded is =failed=, the rest =partial=.

A run that arrives while a transaction is open on the main thread waits for it to close -- it is queued and retried every 50 ms -- rather than joining somebody else's transaction and being rolled back with it. A source that cannot be frozen (its provider gone, exec switched off) gets a failed run at once when a sync is asked for, saying why.

After the run is written the service emits =feed_synced= (or =feed_failed= for a failed run) to automation, with =data_source_id=, =run_id=, =organization_id=, =status=, =rows= and =error=, and calls every hook's after-run function.

** The records sink

A batch may carry =record= upserts. The worker keeps those whose type the source accepts (its settings' =record_types=, from what a feed may write: =price_observation= and =exchange_rate=), up to =feeds.max_records_per_run=, and the main thread writes them as the system, through every validator, before the run: matched in the source's organization on the record's =match= fields, or the type's declared keys (=product_id=, =source=, =observed_at= for an observation; =from_currency=, =to_currency=, =effective_at=, =source= for a rate), deleted rows included. A match updates the row, restoring it first if it was deleted -- a deleted row would otherwise be shadowed by a duplicate that fails a unique index on every later run. A record refused is a note on the run, never a failure of it.

** Hooks

=venture_feeds_add_hook(context, name, freeze, commit, run, user_data, destroy)= lets a later module (alerts, promotion) ride every run without the feeds module knowing it:

- =freeze= runs on the main thread when a source is frozen and returns that module's frozen copy -- alert rules, watched instruments -- read on the worker through =venture_feed_source_get_hook_data()=.
- =commit= runs on the worker after each unit's batch is committed, with the store's writer handle, and may attach plain JSON to the run with =venture_feed_run_set_payload()=.
- =run= runs on the main thread after the run record is written, outside any transaction, and reads the payload back with =venture_feed_run_get_payload()=.

A hook added while the worker runs refreezes every source. A module whose frozen copy is made of its own records calls =venture_feeds_queue_refresh(context)= when one of them is saved or deleted: a coalesced refreeze after the transaction, and nothing while no feeds service exists. On the worker a hook may add a line to the run's notes with =venture_feed_run_add_note()= and read when the run began with =venture_feed_run_get_started_at()=.

=venture_feeds_service_count_pending()= counts a sync from the moment it is asked for until its run is written: the queued command, then the sync waiting for the worker's timer, then the pass, then the delivery, each counted before the one before it stops being. A gap between the command and the pass once let a caller read zero and look for a run that did not exist yet.

** Surfaces

- Actions on =data_source=: =sync= (queues, never waits; editors), =test= (fetches one unit on a private main context and reports what came back, writing nothing, not counted against the budget; editors; it waits on the request's thread, so its wait is bounded: the fetch is cancelled at =feeds.request_timeout= plus five seconds, and a provider that ignores the cancellation for two seconds more is abandoned -- its completion left undispatched on the private context, as the worker abandons one at stop -- and the action answers =VENTURE_ERROR_TIMEOUT=), =purge_history= (deletes the store on the thread that holds its writer; administrators). None is stageable: a sync reaches outside, a test writes nothing, a purge cannot be taken back.
- Automation: =feeds_sync("ID")= queues and returns; =feed_synced= and =feed_failed= events.
- =/feeds=, under *Trading* in the sidebar: the organization's sources, each one's last run, next check and quota, an upload timer per store (each venue's last update, learned interval and expected next update, read through a read-only store handle on the main thread), and the recent runs.
- =/api/v1/feeds= (sources with their last run and the worker's view), =/api/v1/feeds/due= (every unit's next check, soonest first), =/api/v1/feeds/ID/runs?count=N= (newest first; 1 to 500, 20 by default), =POST /api/v1/feeds/ID/sync= (202, ={"status": "queued"}=). Reading is a viewer's, a sync an editor's. Each takes =organization_id= and answers for that organization, else the active one; a source of another organization than the one asked about is NOT_FOUND. See [[*Which organization][Which organization]] below.
- =/feeds/ID/credentials= (GET and POST; organization owners and administrators) and =POST /feeds/ID/sync= (editors) behind the page.
- =venturectl feeds sync ID [--wait] [organization_id=N]= (=--wait= polls for the run for up to five minutes and prints it), =feeds runs ID [organization_id=N]=, =feeds due [organization_id=N]=; =venturectl act data_source ID sync|test [unit=U]|purge_history= for the actions.
- =venture_feeds_service_open_reader()= opens a read-only handle on a source's store from the main thread, for pages and the price oracle.

* Venues, instruments and the price oracle

The =marketdata= module (requires =market=, suggests =feeds=, on by default, no switch of its own) joins the series stores to the books. It owns six record types -- the four below, and the alert rules and hits of the next chapter -- and one lookup: the price oracle, which answers "what is this worth" from a store, and which every valuing report can ask through a =series:= price source.

** Records

All six are tenant records, filed under an organization, and work through the generic routes, forms and =venturectl= like any other type -- except that an =alert_hit= is evidence the generic routes refuse to create or edit. Fields are given in their wire spelling (=group_key=, =data_source_id=), as the JSON API and =venturectl= take them; =venturectl describe venue= prints each type's fields, what each reference points at and what each enum accepts.

*** =venue=

A place things are priced and traded: one realm's auction house, a bookmaker, an exchange, a supplier, your own shop.

- =name=, and =kind=: =other= (the zero value, deliberately -- a venue nobody classified is not silently a marketplace, and a later fee model or strategy keyed on the kind must not apply a marketplace's cut to it), =marketplace=, =auction_house=, =bookmaker=, =exchange=, =supplier=, =store=.
- =namespace=, =key= and =group_key=: the venue's row in its store, and the group (a region) it is compared within.
- =external_ref=: =namespace:key= (=:key= with no namespace), derived at every save, unique in the organization with deleted rows included (=VENTURE_COLUMN_FLAG_UNIQUE_ORGANIZATION=; the partition is the integer organization id). A record with no key has no reference and nothing to keep unique. A namespace may not contain a colon, or ="a:b" + "c"= and ="a" + "b:c"= would be one reference.
- =data_source_id=: optional; the source whose store has this venue.
- =location_id=, else =account_id=: where money moves when you trade there -- a location's holding, or an account when the venue has no location.
- =currency=: optional, a currency code, stored in capitals.
- =fee_model= and =fee_params=: a fee model's name and its YAML parameters. The names come from the arbitrage engine's registry (=percent=, =commission=, =none=, and whatever a plugin adds, such as =wow_auction=); while the =arbitrage= module is on, a save validator the arbitrage engine registers judges the model and its parameters when either is written (with the module off they are kept as text), and a model kept is never judged again, so a venue saved under a plugin's model stays editable after the plugin is gone. See [[file:arbitrage.org::*Fee models][Arbitrage: fee models]].
- =transfer_cost= (money, not negative) and =transfer_hours= (not negative): what moving one lot to or from the venue costs and how long it takes.

*** =instrument=

A thing that is priced: =kind= =other= (the zero value, for the same reason), =item=, =outcome=, =event=, =asset=, =sku=. It has the same =namespace=, =key=, derived =external_ref= and optional =data_source_id= as a venue, and:

- =product_id=: the product it is. This is what joins outside prices to recipes, stock and goals: the oracle answers for a product through every instrument that names it.
- =category_id= and =parent_id=: where it is filed, and what it belongs to (an outcome's event). The tree is held to the category rules -- no loop, one organization, bounded depth -- by =venture_category_check_tree_node()=.
- =attrs=: what the source said about it, as YAML or JSON.

*** =watchlist= and =watchlist_entry=

A named list of instruments, optionally in one venue group and for one venture, shared in the organization rather than owned by a person: alerts read watchlists, and a record with a personal owner is one webhooks and automation are kept quiet about. An entry names a watchlist and an instrument of its own organization, once per list, with an optional =target_buy= and =target_sell= that are not negative and, when both are given, in one currency.

*** Listings

The market module's =listing= gained an optional =venue_id=, held to the listing's organization, so an undercut alert has a venue to compare an open listing with. The reference is to a type of this module: with marketdata off it is refused when written, like any reference into a hidden module, and listings work as before. The column is additive; reconciliation adds it, and no migration script is needed.

** Promotion

=venture_marketdata_promote_instrument(context, organization, data_source_id, key, actor, &record, &error)= and =venture_marketdata_promote_venue(...)= turn a row a store has seen into a record:

- The source must be the organization's and not deleted; its store is read through =venture_feeds_service_open_reader()=, so promotion needs the feeds module (and a build with SQLite). A store with nothing in it yet, or a key it has not seen, is =VENTURE_ERROR_NOT_FOUND=.
- The namespace is the row's, else the source's =instrument_namespace= (or =venue_namespace=). The reference is looked up *deleted rows included*: a live record is returned as it is, edits kept; a deleted one is restored, never shadowed by a duplicate that would fail the unique index on every later attempt.
- A new record takes the row's name, its kind (free text read as a nick: "Auction House" is =auction_house=, anything unknown is =other=), its group and currency (venues) or its attributes (instruments). An instrument's category is the existing category whose computed path matches the row's ="Materials/Ore"=, if any, and that may group instruments; categories are never made here, because a store's taxonomy would otherwise grow a branch per string a provider ever sent. An instrument's parent is promoted first; a parent the store does not know is left out, and a loop in the store's own data ends at =VENTURE_CATEGORY_MAX_DEPTH=.

=POST /api/v1/market/promote= ={"data_source_id": N, "kind": "instrument"|"venue", "key": "..."}= (editors) and =venturectl market promote SOURCE_ID instrument|venue KEY= call it.

** Known instruments

A data source with =track: known= stores only the instruments its settings list under =instruments= and, with marketdata on, the instrument records filed under the source -- promoted, or typed in with their key. The feeds service reads those keys (=venture_marketdata_known_keys()=) when it freezes the source, on the main thread, so the worker only ever sees a frozen set; saving or deleting an instrument queues a coalesced refreeze. With marketdata on, a known-only source may therefore be saved with an empty list.

** The price oracle

=venture_marketdata_reference_price(context, &question, &price, &evidence, &error)= answers one question; =venture_marketdata_reference_number()= answers the three number bases. A =VentureMarketdataOracle= object (=venture_marketdata_oracle_new()=) does the same and keeps the store handles it opens for as long as it lives, so a report that asks about a hundred products opens each store once; make one per report or request and drop it after, so a purged store is never read through a stale handle. Every read is on the main thread.

The question (=VentureMarketdataQuestion=, always cleared with =venture_marketdata_question_init()= first):

- =organization_id=; =instrument_id=, or =product_id= to ask through every instrument naming the product, oldest first, until one answers.
- =venue_id= (a venue record) or =where= (a key or group in the instrument's store; a key the store knows as a venue is a venue, anything else a group), not both. A venue record answers only for its own store.
- =basis=, =at= (%NULL for now), =currency= (only that currency answers), =prefer_currency= (among several instruments, the first in it wins), =allow_fallback= and =fallback_source=.

Its evidence (=VentureMarketdataEvidence=, also JSON through =venture_marketdata_evidence_to_json()=) says where the answer came from: =origin= =series= or =observation=, the data source, instrument, venue or group, when the figure was taken, the observation and its source, and a note -- why nothing answered, when nothing did.

*** Bases

| Basis | At a venue | Across a group (or every venue, with none named) |
|-------+------------+------------------------------------------------|
| =market= (the zero value) | the venue's market value | the group's =region_market_avg= |
| =min= | the cheapest unit on offer | the cheapest venue's minimum, in one currency |
| =market_14d= | the 14-day weighted market value | the same over the group's per-day means |
| =historical_60d= | the 60-day mean market value | likewise |
| =region_median=, =region_p33=, =region_market_avg= | the venue's group's figure | the group's figure |
| =sale_avg= | the average estimated sale price, 14 days | sales summed across the group |
| =sale_rate= (number) | sold / (sold + expired), 14 days | likewise |
| =sold_per_day= (number) | units sold a day | likewise |
| =quantity= (number) | units on offer now | units on offer at every venue, summed |

The formulas are the store's (see "The figures" above); the oracle only chooses which and reads it. A group-wide figure with no group named uses the store's only group, and a store whose venues sit in several groups refuses (=VENTURE_ERROR_INVALID_ARGUMENT=, naming them): guessing would price an EU recipe from US realms. The cheapest across venues is in the currency asked for, else the preferred one when a venue offers in it, else the cheapest venue's.

*** Time

With =at= set, a current figure answers only when it was taken at or before =at=; otherwise the newest hourly point at or before it (14 days back), then the newest day that began at or before it (60 days back). The daily-history bases are computed as of the day =at= falls in. Region figures are current only: a region computed after =at= does not answer for it.

*** Never a conversion

A figure in another currency than =currency= is no answer; the evidence names both currencies and says nothing was converted. Nothing observed is not an error: the call succeeds with no price, exactly as =venture_market_latest_price()= does. Feeds off, a source with nothing stored, an instrument with no data source and key, and the marketdata module off are all "nothing observed", with the reason in the note. Only a store that cannot be read (a newer schema) is an error.

*** The fallback

Only when =allow_fallback= is set, only for prices, and only when no store answered: the newest =price_observation= of the product at or before =at= (=fallback_source= matched exactly), strictly in =currency= when it is set and otherwise preferring =prefer_currency=. The evidence's origin is then =observation=. Numbers never fall back: observations carry no rates.

** Price sources: the =series:= grammar

A valuing report's =price_source= is parsed by =venture_marketdata_parse_price_source()= *before* it is matched against observation sources:

#+begin_example
series:<basis>[@<venue key or group>]
#+end_example

- Text that does not start with =series:= is an exact observation source, as before; the market module refuses to save an observation whose source starts with =series:=, which could never be asked for.
- The basis must be a price basis; =series:quantity= is refused as "a number, not a price", and an empty basis or an empty place after =@= is refused.
- A series source needs the marketdata and feeds modules in a build with SQLite (=venture_marketdata_series_available()=); without them the report refuses, naming what is off, rather than reading every product as unpriced.

=recipe_margin= and =goal_materials= accept it (=venture_marketdata_oracle_source_price()=, shared so the two cannot disagree): each product is priced through its instruments on that basis, at that place, as of the report's =as_of=, strictly in the =currency= option when one is given and otherwise preferring the book currency. Never an observation in its place -- =recipe_margin='s never-fall-back rule holds: a product the feeds have not priced is named and its figures left blank.

** Surfaces

- =GET /api/v1/market/quote= (viewers): =instrument_id= or =product_id=, =venue_id= or =where=, =basis=, =at=, =currency=, =prefer_currency=, =fallback= (=true=), =source=. Answers ={"basis", "found", "price" (money or null), "value" (number or null), "evidence"}=.
- =POST /api/v1/market/promote= (editors), above.
- =venturectl market quote ID|product=ID [basis=B] [venue=KEY|GROUP] [venue_id=N] [at=DATE] [currency=C] [prefer_currency=C] [fallback=true] [source=S] [organization_id=N]= (=venue= is sent as =where=, =product= as =product_id=) and =venturectl market promote SOURCE_ID instrument|venue KEY [organization_id=N]=.
- =venturectl market browse|deals|venues|instrument|watchlist|alerts=, the Trading pages' twins (see /Browsing: the Trading pages/).
- The records themselves through the generic routes and =venturectl list|get|create|update|delete venue|instrument|watchlist|watchlist_entry=. The Trading pages (see /Browsing: the Trading pages/) browse the stores and price the watchlists.
- All of it is gone with the module off: the routes answer 404, the types are hidden.

* Alerts

An =alert_rule= says what somebody wants to hear about the market data -- a price under a line, a venue running out, a listing of theirs undercut, a news entry naming an item -- and an =alert_hit= is one time it fired. Both belong to the =marketdata= module and are tenant records. The evaluation follows the feeds module's threading exactly: rules are frozen on the main thread, candidates are found on the worker against the store it just wrote, and hits are written back on the main thread by the system.

** Rules

| Field | Meaning |
|-------+---------|
| =name= | what it watches for |
| =kind= | one of the kinds below; =below= is the zero value |
| =enabled= | evaluated after every feed run; a new rule starts on (the column's zero value, off, is the safe reading of a row nobody wrote it on) |
| =watchlist_id=, =instrument_id=, =category_id= | what it watches: the watchlist's instruments, one instrument, or every instrument filed under the category or beneath it. Any of them; together they are a union. Required for every kind but =undercut= and =entry_match=, which they only narrow |
| =data_source_id= | optional: only this source's data; empty is every source of the organization |
| =venue_id= or =group_key= | optional, not both: only at that venue, or at venues in that group. A watchlist's own group applies when the rule names neither |
| =threshold= | a price, not negative, for =below=, =above= and =spread= |
| =threshold_number= | a number, for =pct_vs_reference= (a percent of the reference, above 0 and at most 1000), =shortage= (units, at least 1) and =spike= (a percent change other than 0, at most 100000 either way); 0 for every other kind |
| =pattern= | plain text, for =entry_match= |
| =basis= | the reference price for =pct_vs_reference= and =spread=; the series a =spike= reads |
| =window_hours= | =spike= only: 1 to 336 |
| =cooldown_minutes= | quiet time after a hit about the same thing; a new rule starts at 60; 0 to 43200 |
| =notify_username= | whose inbox a hit reaches; empty is whoever created the rule |
| =notes=, =result= | free text; =result= is transient -- the =evaluate= action's answer, shown once and never stored |

The save validator holds each kind to exactly the fields it reads, and refuses the ones it does not when they are set -- a price left on an =out_of_stock= rule would read as if it mattered. =basis= is an enum with no empty value, so a kind that does not read it ignores it. References are held to the rule's organization, and a venue in another data source than the one the rule is narrowed to is refused. A category reference is judged against the tree's =applies_to= as an instrument's would be: a rule names the category its instruments are filed under, and the class says so with the =VENTURE_CATEGORY_APPLIES_AS_QDATA= qdata rather than the validator listing it.

A rule has no personal owner: a record with one is kept from webhooks and automation, and a hit is exactly what those are for. Whose inbox a hit reaches is =notify_username= (refused when written unless it is an active user who may read the rule's organization -- a member there of any role, or a global owner or administrator; the refusal is the same as for a name nobody has, so it says nothing of who exists elsewhere -- and judged again at every hit, so a membership revoked since stops the delivery while the hit is still written), or, left empty, the actor of the rule's creation in the audit log -- an API token stands for its owner. Nobody resolvable is nobody told; the hit is still written and published.

** Kinds

Every kind but =spread= and =entry_match= is judged per instrument per venue, on the venue's newest snapshot only: a row that snapshot did not touch was judged when it was new. Prices are compared in minor units of one currency and never converted -- a row in another currency than the threshold says nothing about it.

| Kind | Fires when | Hit's =observed= / =reference= | Numbers |
|------+------------+-------------------------------+---------|
| =below= | the venue's cheapest unit is at or below =threshold= | the minimum / the threshold | |
| =above= | the venue's cheapest unit is at or above =threshold= | the minimum / the threshold | |
| =pct_vs_reference= | 100 x minimum / reference is at or below =threshold_number= (80 fires 20% under the reference) | the minimum / the reference | the percent / the threshold |
| =spread= | within the group (the rule's, else the venue's), the best other venue's =basis= figure is at least =threshold= above the cheapest venue's minimum | the buy venue's minimum / the sell figure | |
| =out_of_stock= | the newest snapshot took the quantity to zero (=stock_changed_at= is that snapshot) | | 0 |
| =back_in_stock= | the newest snapshot brought it back above zero; never a first sighting | the minimum | the quantity |
| =shortage= | fewer units are on offer than =threshold_number= | | the quantity / the threshold |
| =spike= | the =basis= figure (=market=, =min= or =quantity=) moved by =threshold_number= percent or more -- up for a positive one, down for a negative one -- against the newest hourly point at or before =window_hours= earlier | the price now / then (prices) | the change / the threshold |
| =undercut= | an open listing at a venue with prices here asks more than the cheapest unit that venue now offers of its product, in the listing's currency | the minimum / the listing's price | |
| =entry_match= | an entry's title or summary contains =pattern=, both case-folded | | |

- *Reference prices.* =pct_vs_reference= reads =market= (the venue's market value), =market_14d=, =historical_60d=, =sale_avg= (the venue's daily history) or a region figure of the venue's group, in the row's currency; =min= is refused, being the figure itself. =spread= reads the same bases for each other venue; a region basis is the group's one figure.
- *Spread* is net of nothing: fees and transfers belong to the arbitrage engine. The buy side is the cheapest in-stock venue in the threshold's currency, the sell side the best figure among the *other* venues, and the hit is about the buy venue.
- *Stock* is read from =stock_changed_at=, which the store writes in the statement that makes the change, so the worker never has to remember the previous snapshot.
- *Undercut.* Your own listing is among the units on offer, so a cheapest unit below it is somebody else's. The organization's open listings that name a =venue_id= are frozen with the source: the venue's key when its prices are in this source, and the store keys of the instrument records naming the listing's product. Up to 1000.
- *Entries* are matched on what a run brought (=fetched_at= at or after the run's start); an evaluation by hand looks back a day. A pattern is plain text of at most 200 characters, never a regular expression, so nobody's rule can make the worker backtrack. A scoped =entry_match= rule matches only entries about an instrument in scope.

** Evaluation

1. *Freezing, main thread.* The alerts register a feeds hook (=VENTURE_ALERTS_HOOK=, "alerts"). Whenever the feeds service freezes a source, every enabled rule of its organization (oldest first, up to 500) is reduced to plain data for that source: the store keys its scope comes to, the category's store path ("Materials / Ore" becomes "Materials/Ore"), the venue's key, the group, the threshold in its currency's minor units, the case-folded pattern. A rule that says nothing about the source -- narrowed to another, its venue in another, its scope naming no instrument there -- is left out. Saving or deleting a rule, a watchlist or its entries, a venue, a listing or a category queues a refreeze (=venture_feeds_queue_refresh()=); instruments already do.
2. *Finding, worker thread.* After each unit's commit the hook takes the run's venues it has not judged at their newest snapshot (=venture_feed_run_get_venues()=, remembered as "venue@time"), and the entries that arrived since the run began, and evaluates every frozen rule against the writer handle. Candidates are plain JSON on the run's "alerts" payload, keyed by rule and subject so a later unit's sighting replaces an earlier one; past 1000 they are only counted. A failed read is a note on the run. The worker never touches the database.
3. *Writing, main thread.* After the run record is written, outside any transaction, each candidate is held to its rule as it is now (deleted, moved or switched off since the freeze: dropped), to the cooldown, and to the cap of 100 hits a run; then it is saved as an =alert_hit= by the system actor ("alerts") and told to the recipient's inbox as an =alert= notification. A run that ends while an automation handler is running waits for the handler to return: a hit written inside one would raise no =on_created=.

Nothing is ever evaluated from inside an automation handler. The run hook waits, and the =evaluate= action is refused there (=VENTURE_ERROR_CONFLICT=).

** Cooldown and the cap

The cooldown is kept per rule and *subject*: the venue and instrument, plus the listing for =undercut= or the entry for =entry_match= (the hit's technical =subject= field). A candidate is dropped when the rule's newest hit about that subject was observed at the same snapshot or later -- always, so an evaluation by hand after a run says nothing twice -- or less than =cooldown_minutes= before it. Deleted hits count: deleting one is not asking to be told again at once. With a cooldown of 0 every new snapshot that matches is a hit.

A run writes at most =VENTURE_ALERTS_MAX_HITS_PER_RUN= (100) hits; the rest are counted, and the run record's notes say how many, as they do for candidates past =VENTURE_ALERTS_MAX_CANDIDATES= (1000). A loose rule on a large store's first sync would otherwise write a hit, a notification and a webhook delivery per instrument.

** Hits

An =alert_hit= carries =rule_id=, =kind=, =observed_at= (the snapshot's time, or the entry's arrival), =message= (one sentence, prices formatted on the main thread), =data_source_id=, =instrument_key= and =instrument_id= (when a record names that key in the source), =venue_key= and =venue_id= likewise, =listing_id= (=undercut=), =entry_key= and =url= (=entry_match=), the money =observed= and =reference=, the numbers =observed_number= and =reference_number=, and =subject=. Its display name is its message, cut to a line, so ={event->label}= and a webhook body's =label= say what happened. Instruments and venues the store saw but nobody promoted have their key and no record; a hit never promotes.

A hit is evidence: =venture_web_type_accepts_writes()= refuses creating or editing one through the generic routes, as it does a data source run, and it must belong to an organization. Its creation is the event. There is no separate alert event:

- *Webhooks* publish =alert_hit.created= -- subscribe with =alert_hit.created= or =alert_hit.*= (see [[file:webhooks.org][Webhooks]]). The system actor's creation is published like anybody's: only its updates are kept from the inbox.
- *Automation* hears =on_created= with =event->type= ="alert_hit"= (see [[file:automation.org][Automation]]).
- *The inbox* gets one notification of kind =alert=, titled with the rule's name and the message.

Forwarding to a phone or a chat is an automation rule, with podomation's =ntfy= or =discord= module configured:

#+begin_src
pod alerts = venture->new();
alerts->on_created where event->type == "alert_hit"
	=> ntfy->publish("trading", "{event->label}", "Market alert");
alerts->on_created where event->type == "alert_hit"
	=> discord->send_message("123456789012345678", "{event->label}");
#+end_src

** Evaluating by hand

=venture_marketdata_alerts_evaluate(context, rule, record, &report, &error)= and the record action =evaluate= on =alert_rule= (editors; not stageable) hold one rule against the newest snapshot of every venue in the store of each source it covers, now, on the main thread through a read handle -- the same evaluation the worker makes. =record= false says what would fire and writes nothing; true writes the hits under the cooldown and the cap, as a run would. A disabled rule is evaluated too: trying one before switching it on is the point. The action's answer is the rule's transient =result=; the function's report is ={"candidates", "written", "cooled", "over_cap", "failed", "sources", "matches": [{"message", "subject", "venue_key", "instrument_key", "observed_at", "data_source_id"}], "hits", "notes"}=.

** Modules off

- *Feeds off* (or a build without SQLite): no store, no worker, no evaluation; =evaluate= refuses with =VENTURE_ERROR_CONFIG=. Rules can still be written.
- *Marketdata off*: the types are hidden like any module's, the freeze contributes nothing, a run writes no hits and =evaluate= refuses.

** Surfaces

- The records through the generic routes and =venturectl list|get|create|update|delete alert_rule= (and =list|get alert_hit=); =venturectl act alert_rule ID evaluate [record=true]=.
- =/market/alerts= lists the recent hits and the rules, with an /Evaluate now/ button per rule (=POST /market/alerts/ID/evaluate=, editors; it records, as a run would); its twins are =GET /api/v1/market/alerts= and =POST /api/v1/market/alerts/ID/evaluate= (=?record=false= for a dry run). The =market_alerts= dashboard widget shows the latest hits.
- =venturectl market alerts [count=N]= (the rules and the hits) and =venturectl market alerts evaluate RULE_ID [--dry-run]= (=--dry-run= sends =record=false=; without it the hits are written under the cooldown).

* Browsing: the Trading pages

The pages under =/market= are how a person reads the stores: the catalogue, an instrument at every venue with its history, the deals, how cheap each venue runs against its region, the watchlists and the alerts -- what Undermine Exchange, FlippingPal and Saddlebag show for one game, for any venue and instrument. They are the =marketdata= module's, drawn under *Trading* in the sidebar.

** One answer per page

Each page is one function in =src/marketdata/venture-marketdata-browse.c= that answers in plain JSON, and every door reads that answer:

| Page | Function | API twin |
|------+----------+----------|
| =/market/browse= | =venture_marketdata_browse()= | =GET /api/v1/market/browse= |
| =/market/i/SOURCE/KEY= | =venture_marketdata_instrument()= | =GET /api/v1/market/i/SOURCE/KEY= |
| =/market/deals= | =venture_marketdata_deals()= | =GET /api/v1/market/deals= |
| =/market/venues= | =venture_marketdata_venue_index()= | =GET /api/v1/market/venues= |
| =/market/watchlists=, =/market/watchlists/ID= | =venture_marketdata_watchlists()=, =venture_marketdata_watchlist_view()= | =GET /api/v1/market/watchlists[/ID]= |
| =/market/alerts= | =venture_marketdata_alerts_overview()= | =GET /api/v1/market/alerts= |

The page and its twin are the same handler: the request is parsed into the function's question the same way, and the twin returns the answer untouched, so the page cannot show what the API would not say. The reports (=market_deals=, =venue_index=, =watchlist=) and the dashboard widgets (=watchlist=, =market_alerts=, =source_health=) read the same functions. The web layer only draws.

Every handler checks its own session or token and the =marketdata= module, and reads one organization: another organization's data source, watchlist or rule is NOT_FOUND, never FORBIDDEN, so whether it exists is not told. Reading is a viewer's; promoting and evaluating are an editor's.

** Which organization

A page answers for the sidebar's pick, as every page does; the pick "all" answers for the default organization, as the scan and the Trading widgets do, because a Trading page reads one organization's stores and files what it promotes under one (left as "every organization" it listed them all while every link and action on the page answered NOT_FOUND). Somebody who may not read the default organization is told to pick one. An =/api/v1/market=, =/api/v1/feeds= or =/api/v1/arbitrage= call answers for the organization its =organization_id= names -- the query string's, or a JSON body's member (=POST /api/v1/market/promote=, =POST /api/v1/arbitrage/record=), a number or its digits -- and for the active organization when it names none, which for an API token is always the default one. Without the parameter a second organization's market data could not be reached from a token at all. The organization is judged first (=venture_web_request_organization()=): its own row is read under the caller's access scope, which every member may read and nobody else may, so a stranger is told NOT_FOUND, the same thing reading any of its records tells them. That check is not a formality: these routes read the series stores, which are files, and the row-by-row record policy never sees them. Everything after it is the record policy as usual -- a viewer member reads only what is assigned to them, and a data source is nobody's -- and a promotion or a recorded trade is judged again at its save or its action. A value that is not a positive whole number is refused (400).

The command line follows the API: every =venturectl feeds= and =venturectl market= verb takes =organization_id=N= (after its positional words), and reports take it as an option like any other, so a second organization's market data is reached the same way as the first's.

** What a store read may cost

The server is single-threaded. Every list a page asks a store for is a page of an indexed sort on a precomputed column of =current= -- which is why the sale rate became a column (schema step 4) rather than a figure computed per request -- and every history read is one instrument at one venue: fourteen days of hourly points, sixty days of daily rows, the 64 cheapest tiers. Nothing here sorts or filters store rows in C. Pages are at most 200 rows, deals at most 500, and a page number past a million is refused.

** No data sources

With feeds off, or in a build without SQLite, every page is still a page: the answer's =available= is false, its =notes= say why ("No data sources: market data feeds are off (feeds.enabled)."), and its lists are empty. A source that has stored nothing yet says so by name. Watchlists, rules and hits are records and are always listed. An instrument page whose store is empty, whose key it does not know, or whose =?venue= never listed it, is NOT_FOUND with the reason.

** Browse

=/market/browse?source=&search=&category=&venue=&group=&stock=1&sort=&dir=&page=&per_page== lists one source's current rows (the organization's first source by name when none is chosen), a row per venue and instrument. The columns:

| Column | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| Instrument | the store's name for it (else its key) and category path; links to its page at that venue |
| Venue | the venue's name, else its key |
| Lowest | =min_price=, the cheapest unit on offer in the newest snapshot |
| Quantity | units on offer; 0 out of stock; blank when the source did not say |
| Market value | the TSM-style market value of the book (see /The figures/) |
| Region median | the median of the lowest prices across the venue's group, from the last region recompute |
| Of region | Lowest ÷ Region median × 100: 80% is 20% under the region |
| Sale rate | sold ÷ (sold + expired) over 14 days, from the last recompute |
| Updated | when the row's snapshot was taken |

Every heading is a link that sorts by that column (=aria-sort= marks the active one; a second click turns it round), keeping the rest of the question; the sorts are =venture_marketdata_browse_sorts()= -- =min_price=, =quantity=, =market_value=, =region_median=, =pct_vs_region=, =sale_rate=, =sold_per_day=, =deal_price=, =listings=, =name=, =updated=, =venue= -- and anything else is refused with a 400, never ignored or passed on. Unknown values sort last either way. Search matches the name or key, case-folded; category matches the path and everything beneath it.

** An instrument

=/market/i/SOURCE/KEY?venue=&units== is Undermine's item page. =KEY= is the store's key, escaped as one path segment in every link (=venture_marketdata_instrument_path()=); the server hands handlers the path already unescaped, so a key holding a slash arrives as two segments, and the route takes the whole rest of the path as the key. With no =?venue= it opens at the cheapest venue that has it in stock, else the first.

- *Now* (base stats): the venue's lowest price, quantity, last seen, the median of each day's lowest price over 14 days at this venue, the region median, the region's deal price, "of region" and the market value. The region figures are the group's =region= row in the venue's currency.
- *Sales*: units sold a day, the sale rate and what units sold for on average over 14 days, the 14-day weighted market value and the 60-day historical one -- =venture_series_store_reference()= for the venue.
- *Snapshots*: the lowest price and the quantity every hour for 14 days, one line against each axis (quantity on the right). An hour in another currency is a gap on the price line, not a number in the wrong unit.
- *Heat map*: two weekday-by-hour maps of the last seven days, lowest price (cheapest darkest) and quantity (most darkest), in the configured =locale.timezone= at its offset now (a daylight-saving change inside the week shifts the hours before it by one). Only points in the newest point's currency count. Each cell is the mean of the hourly figures that fell in it.
- *Daily history*: per day for 60 days, the most units on offer in any one snapshot and the lowest price in that snapshot (the fullest market of the day, which is the one Undermine keeps), the day's lowest, and the units estimated sold and expired; as a chart, and as a table newest first.
- *Bulk pricing*: =?units=N= walks the venue's book from its cheapest tier (=venture_series_store_bulk_cost()=): units filled, total cost, average and the price of the last unit, and says when the book runs out first. The 20 cheapest tiers are listed under it.
- *Every venue*: the instrument's current row at every venue, cheapest first -- Undermine's "other realms" -- each linking to the page at that venue.
- *Act*: view at another venue; *Promote to a record* (or a link to the record once there is one); *Add to watchlist* with a list to choose; *Create an alert*. These are forms that POST to the page's own address with =action=promote=, =watch= or =alert= (editors). Each promotes the instrument (=venture_marketdata_promote_instrument()=, idempotent), then goes back to the page, or to the generic =/e/watchlist_entry/new= form with =instrument_id= and =watchlist_id= filled in, or to =/e/alert_rule/new= with =instrument_id=, =data_source_id=, =kind=below= and the venue's =group_key=. The action is a form field rather than a path segment because the key is the rest of the path.

** Deals

=/market/deals?source=&venue=&group=&category=&min_value=&max_pct=&top== lists every instrument in stock at a venue whose lowest price is at or under its group's deal price -- the median of the group's lowest prices, or their 33rd percentile when 15 or more venues offer it (=venture_series_math_deal_price()=), withheld when the median is under the source's =min_value=. Every source of the organization is read unless one is named; each answers its best =top= + 1 from the deal index and the merge keeps the best =top=, cheapest against the region first, and says when it cut some.

| Column | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| Lowest | the venue's lowest price |
| Deal price | the group's deal price |
| Region median | the group's median |
| Of region | Lowest ÷ Region median × 100 |
| Under by | Deal price − Lowest, exact, in the row's currency |
| Quantity, Sale rate | as on Browse |

=min_value= is an amount with its currency ("10.00 GOLD"); the row's market value (else its lowest price) must be at least that, compared in that currency only, so a gold bound lets no dollar row through. An amount with no currency is refused rather than read in the install's default (=venture_marketdata_parse_amount()=). =max_pct= is a percent of the region median ("80").

** Venues

=/market/venues?source=&group== is FlippingPal's realm index with Saddlebag's upload timer, per venue of every source:

| Column | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| Compared | instruments in stock at the venue that have a region median |
| Cheaper, Equal, Dearer | the shares of those under, at and over the region median (they add to 100%) |
| Price to region | the mean over those instruments of lowest ÷ region median |
| Listings | listings across every instrument |
| Last snapshot | the venue's newest snapshot |
| Updates every | the interval the store learned from the venue's last 36 gaps (median of 3-gap maxima, capped at two hours) |
| Next expected | last snapshot + interval |
| Data age | now − last snapshot; /late/ past twice the interval |

** Watchlists

=/market/watchlists= lists the lists; =/market/watchlists/ID= prices one now. Each entry's instrument record names its data source and key; the venues are the instrument's current rows in the list's =group_key= (every venue when it has none). The cheapest in-stock one is /Cheapest at/; /Against buy/ and /Against sell/ are that price minus the targets, exact, and only when the target is in the same currency -- nothing here converts. /Now/ says /buy/ when the price is at or under the buy target and /sell/ when at or over the sell target. The sparkline is the cheapest venue's lowest price each day over 14 days. An entry whose instrument has no data source says so.

** Alerts

See /Alerts/ above: the recent hits newest first (each with the rule, the message, a link to the instrument's prices and, for an entry, its source link when it is a web address) and the rules with when each last fired and an /Evaluate now/ button.

** Reports

| Report | Options |

| =market_deals= | =data_source_id=, =venue=, =group_key=, =category_path=, =min_value= ("10.00 GOLD"), =max_pct= ("80"), =top= (1 to 500, 50) |

** Widgets

- =watchlist= (=record_id=, =limit=): one list priced now -- the first by name with no id -- instrument, cheapest venue, price and signal.

** Charts

The pictures are drawn by =src/web/venture-web-chart.c= on the server as inline SVG: a line (two series, the second on its own axis), bars, a sparkline and a heat map. No script is needed and they print. Colour comes from the stylesheets' tokens through =currentColor= and classes (see [[file:interface.org][The interface]]); every chart has a =<title>=, a =<desc>=, an =aria-label= and -- except the sparkline, which is named by its label -- a visually hidden table of every figure. Every label is escaped, because an instrument's name is whatever a feed said it was. Series figures arrive as integer minor units and are formatted as the currency reads them.
