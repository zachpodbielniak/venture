#+title: Durable follow-up sequences

* Enrollment and exits

The =sequences= module requires =crm=. Its five records are =sequence=,
=sequence_step=, =sequence_enrollment=, =sequence_delivery= and =suppression=.
Their fields drive the ordinary record surfaces. A sequence belongs to one
organization; its contacts, steps and optional deal must belong there too.
The optional =lead_id= is an external identifier, with no dependency on a
lead module.

=VentureSequenceService= owns enrollment and the database save hook routes
ordinary enrollment creation through it, including approved assistant writes.
Active and paused enrollment duplicates are refused. Suppressed addresses
cannot enroll. Addresses are trimmed and ASCII case-folded, unique per
organization. Suppression records are retained.

A sequence declares its IANA timezone, a same-day send window with an
inclusive start hour and exclusive end hour, and comma-separated ISO weekdays
(1 is Monday, 7 Sunday). Calendar-day delays use that timezone; hour delays
are elapsed hours. The stored next-run timestamp is UTC. An enrollment keeps
its reason and the actor who enrolled it.

An inbound interaction exits matching reply-sensitive enrollments; a meeting
can meet a meeting goal. A deal changing to won exits matching enrollments
without relying on a pipeline service. Suppression stops the email's
enrollments regardless of their unsubscribe switch. Each exit retains the
current step and an exit reason and cancels pending deliveries in the same
transaction. Already sent messages remain evidence.

* Mail adapter seam

A mail adapter picks up =sequence_delivery= rows with =channel=email= and
=state=pending=, sends, and sets state/external id. The sequence engine does
not send mail or depend on a mail implementation. The adapter must recheck
pending state before sending, since an exit can cancel a queued delivery.

* Running and inspecting steps

Call =venture_sequence_service_run_due()=, =venturectl sequence run= or
=POST /api/v1/sequences/run=. Execution is lazy and bounded to 1000 due
enrollments per call; an operator or scheduled automation must invoke the
sweep. There is no database worker thread. Each enrollment transition is
one transaction. A failed transaction rolls back its delivery, inbox message,
progress and audit records; other enrollments already committed by a sweep
stay committed.
A due enrollment whose contact or sequence has been deleted exits with =contact_deleted= or =sequence_deleted= and cancels pending deliveries; retained historical references never authorize new outreach.

Every enrollment/step identity has at most one delivery, enforced by a
per-organization unique key. A second sweep or a process restart reads that
history. A completed step moved later is still complete. Active steps found
before the current position without history get =skipped= deliveries with
an explanation. New steps after that position remain eligible. Editing a
pending step does not rewrite a delivery already produced. The saved
=next_run_at= is retained until the next transition; new delays apply when
that transition schedules another step.

Templates accept ={contact.name}=, ={company.name}=, ={deal.name}= and
other readable fields from those records; an unqualified name means a
contact field. Underscores and hyphens are accepted in field names.
Unknown or unavailable placeholders fail the delivery visibly. Sensitive
fields never enter the rendering context. A call or SMS step creates one
plain inbox notification for the enrollment's =owner_id=; it performs no
call or SMS. A missing owner fails the step. A wait step records its
completion as skipped because no delivery is required.

Email completion means queued for the adapter. The enrollment may reach
=completed= while its last email is still pending. The adapter may update
only a pending delivery's state, execution time, error and external message
identifier. Failed completion requires an error and stops further steps.
Resetting a finished delivery to pending or rewriting its payload is refused.
Automatic provider retries are outside this engine; no failure silently
re-enqueues a completed step.

* Operator and assistant actions

The contact page's *Enroll in sequence* action opens the ordinary generated
enrollment form with the contact filled in. Contact and enrollment pages
show the next position and scheduled time, enrollment reason, exit reason,
and delivery history. Pause, resume and exit buttons call the same service
as REST. Pausing preserves the checkpoint. Resuming never clears delivery
history. Terminal enrollments cannot resume; a deliberate new enrollment
is a separate journey.

=POST /api/v1/sequence/:id/enroll= takes the enrollment fields. Append
=?stage=1= to propose it. The assistant's ordinary staged create tool can
create =sequence_enrollment= with =sequence_id= and =contact_id=; approval
rechecks suppression and duplicates and calculates the schedule. It writes
nothing until approved. =enrolled_at= can be supplied for deterministic
imports; omitting it uses the approval/enrollment time.

=POST /api/v1/sequence_enrollment/:id/pause=, =/resume= and =/exit= perform
manual controls. The =/goal= action marks a custom goal met and exits.
A meeting interaction satisfies a meeting goal regardless of its outbound
flag; an inbound interaction satisfies a reply goal. A won deal satisfies a
=deal_won= goal. Goal satisfaction stops future steps even when the
corresponding optional exit switch is false.

* Reporting and upgrades

=sequence_performance= reports current outcomes for the cohort enrolled in
the selected period: enrolled, completed, exited by reason, replies, and
met goals for each sequence. It is not a reconstruction of past mutable
statuses. =sequence_failures= lists errors from failed deliveries whose
scheduled timestamps fall in the period. Both use one exact organization
and ordinary report rendering/export.

The paired =000080_sequences.sql= migrations verify the organization-scoped
suppression, delivery and active-enrollment indexes made from the field
metadata. They permit absent tables when the module is off and do not
invent historical enrollments. Enabling the module later reconciles its
field tables normally. SQLite upgrade/restart tests preserve existing
contacts and delivery history. PostgreSQL SQL ships with the same contract;
a live PostgreSQL integration run is separate from the SQLite suite.

=test-sequences= covers enrollment windows, duplicate and suppressed
refusals, reply/deal/meeting exits, repeated sweeps, edited steps, rollback,
manual controls, rendering failures, adapter evidence, report counts,
staged approval, real HTTP and CLI calls, task notifications, module enable
and file-backed restart.

Sending or enrollment times beyond the representable calendar range are refused as validation errors; the sweep retains no delivery or progress from that failed transition.
