#+title: SaaS billing
#+description: Plans, subscription terms and recurring customer revenue

* Selling Lightsite

=billing= requires =invoicing= and =receivables=. A =plan= describes Starter
or Pro; a =plan_price= describes one version of what it costs for one
period -- every month, every 3 months, every 6 months or every year.
The customer's commitment is a =customer_subscription=. The example
plugin's =subscription= remains a recurring cost paid by the business.

The field tables supply ordinary lists, forms, REST resources, CLI records,
assistant schemas and audit history. Plans and prices are edited through
those generated surfaces. Subscription state is changed through
=VentureBillingService=, including when an instruction comes from a form,
the CLI, REST, automation or an approved assistant proposal.

| Record | Meaning |
|--------+---------|
| =plan= | Organization, optional venture, name, organization-unique code, description, active flag |
| =plan_price= | Plan, currency, interval (=month=, =quarter=, =half_year=, =year=), exact amount, per-seat flag, trial days, optional tax rate (=tax_code_id=), active flag |
| =plan_discount= | Plan, name, whole percent off or amount off, number of periods (0 = every), optional code, offered-until date, active flag |
| =customer_subscription= | Customer/contact, price, state, seats, period, trial and cancellation dates, external identity; pending terms and adjustment; discount and how many invoices it has covered |
| =subscription_event= | Immutable effective-dated change, old/new terms and seats, proration, invoice and frozen MRR |
| =dunning_step= | Active notice, retry, pause or cancel action at an overdue-day offset |
| =billing_notice= | Durable dunning-notice record; the dunning step itself sends no email |
| =billing_request= | Stageable instruction (including a start's =discount_code=) and, after execution, its immutable result |

Plan codes and nonempty subscription external IDs are unique within their
organization, including retained history. Empty external IDs are optional.
A price used by a subscription or event is immutable except for its active
flag: make a new price version rather than changing an existing customer's
terms underneath them. Customer, contact and price must belong to the same
organization, and the contact must belong to the customer company.

For example, with organization 1, Lightsite venture 1 and customer company 1
already present (use the IDs returned by each create):

#+begin_src sh
venturectl create plan organization_id=1 venture_id=1 name=Starter code=starter active=true
venturectl create plan organization_id=1 venture_id=1 name=Pro code=pro active=true
venturectl create plan_price organization_id=1 plan_id=1 currency=USD interval=month amount='30 USD' per_seat=true trial_days=14 active=true
venturectl create plan_price organization_id=1 plan_id=1 currency=USD interval=year amount='300 USD' per_seat=true active=true
venturectl create plan_price organization_id=1 plan_id=2 currency=USD interval=month amount='60 USD' per_seat=true active=true
venturectl create plan_price organization_id=1 plan_id=2 currency=USD interval=year amount='600 USD' per_seat=true active=true
venturectl billing start organization_id=1 company_id=1 plan_price_id=1 seats=2 external_id=lightsite-customer-1 --as-of 2026-01-01
venturectl billing renew --as-of 2026-01-15 --dry-run
venturectl billing renew --as-of 2026-01-15
venturectl billing change 1 plan_price=3 at_period_end=true --as-of 2026-01-20
#+end_src

A non-trial start issues its first period's invoice immediately. A trial
start issues nothing until its trial ends; renewal then activates it and
issues its first paid period. Seats default to one and must be positive.
Non-seat prices ignore seat count when calculating charges.

* Actions and transactions

=billing start= creates a subscription. The other single-record actions take
its ID: =renew=, =change=, =change-seats=, =pause=, =resume=, =cancel=,
=mark-payment-failed= and =recover=. =change= accepts =plan_price= as a CLI
alias for =plan_price_id=. =change= and =cancel= accept
=at_period_end=true=. Seat changes are immediate.

A scheduled plan change keeps today's price until renewal. Scheduled
cancellation keeps the subscription active until its boundary, when a
renewal sweep cancels it without another period invoice. Immediate
cancellation ends the commitment at the action date. Events are
chronological; a request pinned to an older subscription version is refused.

Each instruction holds one transaction across subscriptions, events,
invoices, invoice lines, settlement, journals, notices and audit entries.
An injected failure after an invoice draft is saved rolls everything back.
The service works on detached request data and only copies results back
after success. Generic subscription, event, notice and completed-request
writes, deletion, restoration and purge are refused naming the service.

The =changing= signal runs after the tentative subscription write and
before event persistence, inside the transaction. It receives a detached
event snapshot. Handlers run before the class closure and may return an
owned =GError= to veto; the whole transaction rolls back. Handlers must not
send mail or perform another external effect before commit.

Invoices use the subscription's company and contact, and the plan's venture.
Their lines carry exactly the price currency; there is no conversion. A
stable subscription/period invoice number also prevents a period being
invoiced twice. Every invoice is issued through =VentureSettlementService=.
The fiscal-period guard checks its issue date. A late sweep dates invoices
at the supplied effective date while retaining the service period in the
stable invoice identity. The organization needs the settlement accounts
configured in [[file:receivables.org][receivables]].

* Proration

Periods are half-open. Changes allocate the full-period price difference
with =VentureMoney= over its calendar days, then sum only the remaining
days. Remainder cents stay on their originally assigned days. Monthly
boundaries retain their original day: January 31, February 28, March 31.

Worked example: two Starter seats at $30 per seat were billed $60 on
January 1 for January 1 through February 1. A third seat is added January 17.
The full-period difference is $30, or 3,000 cents. Allocating over 31 days
gives 97 cents to the first 24 days and 96 cents to the last seven. The
remaining fifteen days receive =8 * 97 + 7 * 96 = 1,448= cents: *$14.48*.
The subscription's MRR becomes $90 immediately. Its next invoice charges
$90 plus the $14.48 pending adjustment: *$104.48*.

A decrease of one seat on the same day produces *-$14.48*. Renewal issues
its ordinary $30 invoice, creates a $14.48 customer credit through
settlement, and applies it, leaving *$15.52* outstanding. Credit beyond an
invoice's amount remains customer credit. Adjustments accumulate exactly
until renewal. Trials and scheduled changes add no immediate adjustment.

Price versions may use different minor-unit exponents in the same currency; credit allocation compares their monetary values with checked rescaling.
Changing currency is refused. A change between monthly and yearly terms
normalizes both prices to the service period already in progress before
allocation, including repeated interval changes within that period.

* Cancelling now

=billing cancel ID= without =at_period_end=true= credits the unused days of
the period being served, prorated exactly as a downgrade to nothing would
be: the period's charge -- the price, less its discount when the discount
covered this period, scaled to the period's months -- is negated, spread
over the period's days with =VentureMoney=, and only the days from the
cancellation date to the period's end are summed. Worked on the example
above: $60 billed for January, cancelled on January 17, spreads -6,000
cents over 31 days (-194 on the first 17, -193 on the rest); the remaining
fifteen days are =-194 - 14 * 193 = -2,896=: *$28.96* is credited.

Only a period that was actually invoiced is credited: the service finds the
start or renewal event that billed the current period, so a trial, a
period nobody invoiced, and a cancellation dated on or after the period's
end give nothing. A part-period difference still carried from a change
this period is folded in, because no renewal will ever settle it, and the
carried amount is cleared. When the period's invoice charged tax, the
credit carries the same share of tax (=tax-amount= and the line's
jurisdiction on the credit note), so the customer gets back the tax on the
days they will not use and the sales tax return shows it credited.

The credit is a =customer_credit= of kind =credit_note= issued through
settlement, applied to whatever is still owed on the period's invoice; the
rest stays on the customer's account. A past-due customer is therefore not
paid out for days they never paid for. The amount credited, tax included,
is recorded negative as =proration_amount= on the cancellation's
=subscription_event= and on the =billing_request= result. Because it posts
money, an immediate cancellation runs under an accounting operation like a
renewal. There is no payment-provider refund; paying the credit out is a
deliberate =refund=.

=venture_billing_service_cancel_credit()= answers the same figure without
writing anything, which is what the subscription page's button shows.
Cancelling at renewal credits nothing and bills nothing more.

* Renewal and dunning sweeps

#+begin_src sh
venturectl billing renew --as-of 2026-04-01 --dry-run
venturectl billing renew --as-of 2026-04-01
venturectl billing dunning --as-of 2026-04-05
#+end_src

Omitting the date uses the server's clock. Sweeps select one organization
(the configured default unless =organization_id= is supplied). Renewal
catches up every due period, capped at 1,200 periods per subscription in
one instruction; repeating a completed sweep creates no invoices. Dry run
returns the number of operations without writing records or audit entries.
Schedule the CLI or POST the sweep endpoint from the installation's
scheduler; no separate billing thread touches the database.

A =plan_price= may name a =product_id= so issued invoice lines qualify for
hosted Checkout. =customer_payment_method= stores explicit authorization
(=card=, =ach=, =direct_debit= or =manual=). =billing collect ID= records an operator-confirmed receipt only with an authorized =manual= method, then recovers a =past_due= subscription. Card, ACH and direct-debit mandates do not prove collection and are refused by this command; they require verified processor settlement.
Invoice creation, cash collection and recognized revenue remain separate
events.

=mark-payment-failed= records the failure date and moves active to
=past_due=. Dunning measures whole overdue days from that date. Each
subscription/failure episode/step has one durable =billing_notice= with
=channel=email=. Notice and retry actions record that intent only; pause
and cancel also call the subscription lifecycle service. Steps run by day
offset, then ID; escalation continues after a dunning pause until cancellation.
A late sweep catches up all eligible steps in that order. =resume= clears
the failure episode. =recover= moves
=past_due= back to active. The operator or the verified Stripe collection adapter
reports failure and recovery; an unpaid invoice alone does not fabricate a
failed provider attempt.

The mail lane must wire the =billing_notice= adapter to =VentureMailer=;
a dunning step queues no mail. The two customer notices the service does
queue -- a trial ending and a price change -- are in [[*Telling the customer][Telling the customer]]. The optional
[[file:stripe.org][Stripe adapter]] consumes its immutable invoice events, obtains hosted reusable
customer permission and reports verified payment failure/recovery through this
lifecycle. Neither module stores raw card data. A manual payment-method row alone
does not authorize a Stripe charge; hosted Setup evidence is required.

* Surfaces and approval

The Money sidebar links to the subscriptions list. Subscription pages have
renew, pause, resume, cancel and change actions. Cancelling is two buttons,
each saying what it does to money: "Cancel now: $X.XX credited for the N
days left" (or "nothing is credited") and "Cancel at renewal: nothing more
is billed". REST uses
=POST /api/v1/customer_subscriptions/:id/renew|change|cancel|pause|resume=;
additional service verbs use the same shape. Start and sweeps use
=POST /api/v1/billing/start|renew-sweep|dunning-sweep=. Bodies use underscores.

Every action accepts =?stage=1=; CLI actions accept =--stage=. The assistant
can stage a =billing_request= through its ordinary generated create tool.
The request's fields show the proposed action rather than granting a generic
writer permission to set a derived subscription status. Staging pins the
subscription version; approval rechecks version, state, currency and fiscal
period. Approval after another action is refused as a conflict.

* Report definitions

All three reports live in the report registry and accept =organization_id=.
=mrr= and =churn= select one =currency= (the configured default unless
supplied); they never add currencies. Their notes carry these definitions:

- =mrr=: contracted active and past-due MRR immediately before the exclusive
  period endpoint, per plan and in total. Trials, pauses, cancellation and
  expiry contribute zero. Annual prices divide by twelve with half-even
  rounding; ARR is twelve times rounded MRR. Frozen event amounts preserve
  the answer after later changes. =as_of= can narrow the endpoint.
- Movements within the period: =new= is first positive MRR, =reactivation=
  a subsequent return from zero, =expansion= and =contraction= changes
  between positive amounts, =churn= a fall to zero, including pause.
  All movement metrics are positive magnitudes.
- =customers= are the companies whose combined MRR is positive at the
  endpoint (two subscriptions of one company are one customer); =arpa= is
  MRR over them, half-even, and absent when there are none.
- =quick_ratio= is =(new + expansion + reactivation) / (contraction +
  churn)= within the period, to hundredths half-even; absent when nothing
  was lost, because there is no denominator.
- =churn= (titled /Subscription churn (billing)/, to tell it apart from the
  headline module's =customer_churn=, which counts paying customers going
  quiet): opening companies with positive MRR are the cohort. A logo is
  lost when its combined closing subscriptions have zero MRR. Revenue loss
  sums positive opening-minus-closing differences per opening company,
  including contraction, excluding new companies and gains. Rates are
  also returned in basis points (10,000 = 100%); an empty denominator has
  no rate metric. Numerators and denominators remain visible.
- =expansion_mrr= sums positive closing-minus-opening differences of opening
  companies only: a company that arrived during the period is new business,
  not retention.
- =grr_bps= (gross revenue retention) is =10,000 - revenue_churn_bps=, so the
  two always sum to 100% exactly. =nrr_bps= (net revenue retention) is
  =(opening_mrr - lost_mrr + expansion_mrr) / opening_mrr= in basis points,
  half-even. Worked through: opening Alpha $100 and Beta $50; Alpha grows to
  $150, Beta cancels, Gamma starts at $50. Revenue churn 33.33%, GRR 66.67%,
  NRR 100.00%, quick ratio (50 + 50) / 50 = 2.00, ARPA $200 / 2 = $100.
- =subscriptions_due=: the current operational renewal queue from the
  period endpoint through the next =days=, default 30, inclusive. It
  excludes scheduled cancellations and paused/past-due subscriptions.
  It is a forward-looking queue, not reconstructed historical schedules.

These are contracted revenue metrics, not cash receipts or accounting
revenue recognition. Usage metering, entitlements, payment integration and
revenue recognition schedules remain separate follow-ups.

* Discounts

A plan offers discounts -- "20% off the first 3 periods", "$10.00 off every
period". One is chosen when a customer is started on the plan
(=discount_id= on the start instruction); =VentureBillingService= refuses a
discount of another plan, one retired or past its offered-until date, and
one that names both a percent and an amount or neither. Each invoice the
subscription issues -- the first and every renewal -- takes the discount off
the plan's charge while it still covers invoices, counts it on the
subscription, and says so on the invoice line; a part-period adjustment is
added after the discount, not discounted. An amount off larger than the
charge makes the invoice free, never a credit. Moving to another price of
the same plan keeps the discount; moving to another plan ends it.

MRR counts the discount. An event's MRR is what the period being served is
billed at: the price less the discount while the discount covers that
period, divided by the period's months (a percent off, or an amount off
per period, so $120 off a $600 yearly price is $40.00 a month). Invoices
are counted from the start, and a discount covers the first N, so the
period's invoice was discounted when no more than N have been issued; the
discounted-invoice counter on the subscription stops at N and cannot tell
the Nth invoice from the next. MRR returns to the list price at the
renewal the discount no longer covers, which the report reads as
expansion.

A start can also skip the price's free trial (=skip_trial=true=): the first
period is billed on the day, as for a price with no trial. The invoice line
names the plan, the price, the seats, the period and any discount, rather
than "renewal".

* Starting a subscription from a quote

A quote line may name a plan price (=plan_price_id=); its =quantity= is the
seats. The line is priced at the plan price, takes no discount or tax of its
own (the subscription's invoices would not charge them), a flat price takes
one seat, and a quote proposes one subscription. Accepting the quote leaves
that line out of the acceptance invoice -- the subscription's first invoice
bills it -- and a quote that is only a subscription issues no acceptance
invoice at all.

On the accepted quote, *Start subscription* (quote action
=start-subscription=; =POST /quotes/:id/start-subscription=,
=POST /api/v1/quotes/:id/start-subscription=, =venturectl quote
start-subscription ID=) runs this service's own =start= through
=venture_billing_service_execute()= for the quote's customer and contact,
with the line's price and seats, now. The start instruction carries
=quote_id=; the service refuses a quote that is not accepted, is for another
customer, or already started a subscription -- by its own =subscription_id=
or because a subscription already names it -- so a second start fails
whether it comes from the quote or around it. The subscription records
=quote_id=, the quote records =subscription_id= and the action its
=result_subscription_id=. The quote page says "Started subscription ..." and
the subscription page "From quote ...". A plan discount is not carried from
a quote; add it on the subscription's plan instead. Whole-operation approval
is taken before the quote's transaction when the start will issue an invoice.

* Usage-based pricing

A plan price is metered when it names a =usage_unit= ("API calls"); it then
needs a =unit_amount= per unit in its own currency, and may include
=included_units= each period. The base =amount= is still charged each period
(it may be zero). A =usage_record= reports use: =subscription_id=, a positive
whole =quantity=, =occurred_at= (now when empty) and an optional
=idempotency_key=, unique per organization including deleted rows. A repeated
key is refused as a conflict naming the first record, so a sender retrying a
lost response is not counted twice. Usage is refused for a flat price, an
ended subscription, or a time before the current period -- that period is
already invoiced -- and billed usage cannot be edited, deleted or restored.

At renewal the invoice charges the new period's base price in advance and
the ended period's usage in arrears, as its own line on the same invoice:
"240 API calls x $0.01 (1,240 used, 1,000 included), 2026-01-01 to
2026-02-01". Usage is counted in [start, end), so a report at exactly the
renewal instant belongs to the new period and is billed once. The charge is
the whole count times the rate, rounded once, half to even, to the currency
(1,235 calls at $0.001 is $1.24). The rate is the one in force at the end of
the period, even when a scheduled change moves the price at that renewal.
Usage within the included units adds no line; usage during a free trial is
not charged; the plan discount applies to the base charge only.
When a subscription ends -- cancelled now, or at renewal -- the usage of
its last stretch, from the period's start to the moment it ends, is billed
beyond the included units on an invoice of its own, numbered
=USAGE-<subscription uuid>-<date>=. The billing request reports it; the
cancel event never names it, because a subscription is looked up by the one
event naming an invoice. A trial is not charged.

Record usage with =POST /api/v1/usage_records= (or =usage_record=) or
=venturectl billing usage SUB quantity=N [key=K] [at=DATE]=, which sends the
numbers as JSON integers. The subscription page shows the period's usage so
far and what it adds up to.

The next invoice a customer is told about -- in the trial reminder, the
price-change notice and the customer portal -- is worked out as the
renewal will issue it: the price, the discount while it covers invoices,
a carried part-period charge, and the price's tax unless the customer is
exempt; a carried credit comes off after tax.

* Periods

Every period is a whole number of calendar months --
=venture_billing_interval_months()= answers 1, 3, 6 or 12 -- so a renewal
stays on the customer's billing day and MRR divides a price by its months
exactly. A change part-way through a period prorates over that period's
days, with each price scaled to the period first, so a monthly and a yearly
price compare over the same days. =quarter= and =half_year= were appended
to the enum; a stored price reads back by value, so their order is fixed.

* In the web interface

*Plans* are under Money in, next to Subscriptions. A plan is made at
=/plans/new= with its prices on the same sheet: one row per period it is
sold by, each with its amount (the business's currency is assumed), a
per-seat box and a free trial. The plan and its prices are saved in one
transaction, and a plan with no price is refused, because nobody could be
put on it. A plan's page lists its prices with how many customers are on
each, adds a price for another period, and retires or re-offers one --
retiring stops a price being offered without moving anybody off it.
Each price can carry a tax rate -- a Tax select on the sheet and on "Add a
price", "No tax" first, then each active rate with its percent. The
invoice line a price issues carries that =tax_code_id=, so settlement
taxes it exactly; "No tax" leaves the line to the customer's address, as
any invoice line without a rate. A customer marked tax exempt stays
exempt: settlement freezes the exemption from the company at issue.
Existing prices have no rate (migration =000650=), and a price in use is
immutable, so taxing an existing plan is a new price and a move.

A retired price that still has customers offers "Move its N customers
to..." with the plan's offered prices and the two timings a single change
has: at renewal, or right now with the difference prorated. =POST
/plans/:id/prices/:price/move= calls
=venture_billing_service_move_customers()=, which runs one ordinary
=change= =billing_request= per trialing, active or past-due subscription
through =venture_billing_service_execute()=, all inside one transaction.
Any refusal rolls every move back, and the page says which customer
stopped it and why. Paused subscriptions cannot change terms and stay.

A plan belongs to a venture -- the sheet asks whose, and picks it when
there is only one -- or is shared by every venture. A customer is put
only on their own venture's plans or shared ones: =VentureBillingService=
refuses anything else at start and at a plan change, from every surface.
A customer with no venture may take any plan, and changing seats keeps
the terms already agreed. The new-subscription page narrows the plan list
to the chosen customer's venture; the change-plan list is narrowed on the
server.

A plan's page has a Discounts card beside its prices: each discount with
its code and how many customers have it, retire and re-offer, and a line to
add one -- "20%" is a percent, "10" an amount in the business's currency,
and an empty period count is every period. The new-subscription page offers
the chosen plan's discounts, and, when the price has a free trial, a box to
skip it; it says when the first invoice will be issued. A subscription's
page lists the invoices it has issued and what is still owed on each.

The subscription pages offer every active price of every active plan,
grouped under the plan's name, and name any active plan that has no price
yet with a link to price it. A new record's "Active" box starts ticked on
every generated form: a plan or price saved inactive was never offered,
and nothing said why.

New invoice asks "How often?" first, with three answers that each say what they mean:

- *Just once* -- one invoice, composed on the sheet and sent now.
- *Same invoice, on a schedule* -- the same sheet in repeat mode. Its customer and lines become a =recurring_schedule= template (weekly, monthly or yearly from a first date, optionally to a last), issued as drafts or automatically. Editing the schedule changes invoices still to come; nothing is prorated.
- *A customer on a plan* -- =/billing/subscriptions/new=, which starts a =customer_subscription= through =VentureBillingService=.

A subscription's page leads with "What happens next": when it renews and at what price, a trial's end, a scheduled switch, and any part-period difference carried to the next invoice. Changing the plan offers the two choices with what each does -- at renewal (today's price stands until then) or right now (the difference for the days left is added to the next invoice or credited, as in [[*Proration][Proration]]).

* Telling the customer

=VentureBillingService= queues two plain-text messages through the mail
outbox, in the instruction's own transaction -- a notice exists exactly
when its change committed, and delivery is the outbox's after commit. They
go to the subscription's contact, else the company's email; with neither,
or with mail off, nothing is queued and nothing fails. See
[[file:mail.org][Subscription notices]] for the wording.

- A renewal sweep tells a trialing customer, =billing.trial_reminder_days=
  (3) before the trial ends, the date and what the first invoice will be.
  Keyed by the subscription, so once. The subscription's "What happens
  next" says whether it was queued or sent, and to whom, or when it will be.
- =change= and =change-seats= tell the customer what changed, when it takes
  effect and the next invoice, keyed by the change's =subscription_event=
  uuid, so each change once. =billing.price_change_notices: false= turns
  them off.

"What the next invoice will be" is =venture_billing_service_next_invoice()=:
the price the subscription switches to at renewal, the discount while it
still covers invoices, and the part-period difference carried to it; a
credit larger than the charge makes it zero. Nothing is written to work it
out. It is the same number the customer portal shows.

* Discount codes

A =plan_discount='s =code= is what a customer quotes. A start may give
=discount_code= instead of =discount_id=: the service looks among the chosen
price's plan's discounts, comparing without regard to case or surrounding
spaces, and refuses in words a code nobody offers ("the code WINTER is not
one this plan offers"), a retired one ("is no longer offered") or one past
its offered-until date ("expired on 2026-03-31"). A matching live code
becomes the start's =discount_id= and is then held to every rule a chosen
discount is. Naming both is refused. The new-subscription page has a "Have
a code?" box beside the discount list; the CLI takes
=billing start ... discount_code=SPRING25=, REST =discount_code=. Migration
=000660_billing_discount_codes.sql= checks the new request column and
invents no codes for earlier starts.
